Slayer Block & Skip: The Decision Framework by Master & Level 2026

Every Slayer guide eventually hands you a list of tasks to block. This isn't that. This is the method: a repeatable decision you can run on any task, for any master, at any Slayer level, with whatever gear you own. Block the wrong thing and you starve your point economy; skip blindly and you waste the best money-makers. Here's how to decide.

🛡️ Slayer Level: 50+
🆕 NEW

Before you touch a single block, read the companion Slayer block & skip list for the concrete task names — but treat that list as output, not input. The input is your account. If you are still choosing a master, start with the Slayer beginner guide; this framework sits on top of it and tells you what to actually do with each assignment.

💡 Quick Verdict

Run one question on every task: "Is this task both unprofitable AND outside my goals?" If yes → block it (once you have the slot). If it is profitable but annoying → skip it occasionally, never block it. If it is profitable and on-goal → always do it. The masters and your Slayer level only change how many you can block and which master feeds you the most points — they do not change the core rule.

1. 🎚️ Block vs Skip vs Do — The Three Levers

Slayer gives you exactly three ways to handle an unwanted task. Players confuse them constantly, and that confusion is where point economies die. Learn the difference first.

① Block — Remove It From the Pool Permanently

Blocking a task removes it from your master's assignment rotation until you unblock it. You get a limited number of block slots (unlocked by Slayer points — see Section 2). Blocking is the strongest lever: the task simply stops appearing. Use it only for tasks that are both low-value and off-goal, because a block slot is a scarce resource you can't waste on something that might become profitable later.

② Skip — One-Off Removal, Keeps the Slot

Skipping discards the current task without doing it, but the task stays in the pool (it can come back). Skips come from two sources: a skip token (bought or earned) or the Turael free-skip trick (cancel at Turael before killing anything — costs no token and no points). Skips are for "this task is annoying right now but I might want it later" moments. Never skip a task you should have blocked.

③ Do — Just Complete the Assignment

Sometimes the answer is boring: do it. If a task is profitable, gives good XP, or advances a goal (boss unlocks, clues, charms), you do it — even if it's tedious. The framework exists to stop you from skipping or blocking tasks you should be grinding. Plenty of "annoying" tasks are secretly your best GP or XP.

💡 The One-Line Rule

Block = never want it. Skip = want it later, not now. Do = it serves you. If you can't sort a task into one of those three in five seconds, you don't understand your own goals yet — go define them first.

2. 💎 The Block-Slot & Point Economy

Block slots are not free and not infinite. Understanding how they unlock is what separates a clean Slayer setup from a blocked-up mess.

① How Block Slots Unlock

You earn your first block slot at 50 Slayer points. After that, additional slots unlock at roughly every 200 points: 200, 400, 600, 800, 1,000, and beyond — reaching double-digit block slots only at very high point totals (1,500–2,000+). The chart shows the growth curve.

② How Many Slots You Really Have

At a typical mid-game point bank (a few hundred points) you'll hold 2–4 blocks. That is not enough to block everything you dislike — only what you truly never want. Players who block on impulse run out of slots and then can't block the task that's actually ruining their farm. Reserve slots for the worst offenders.

③ The "Never Block a Profitable Task" Law

The most expensive Slayer mistake is blocking a task that prints GP. Tasks like Greater Demons, Suqahs, or Konar-assigned high-value monsters can look tedious but pay extremely well. Block them and you permanently cut your income. If a task makes good money, it goes in the "do" or at most "occasionally skip" bucket — never the block bucket. The 70–95 money-makers guide shows which assignments to protect.

A practical guardrail: before blocking any task, check its average GP/hr and drop table once. If it clears even a modest money bar, it stays unblocked. The point of blocking is to remove dead weight, not to purge anything mildly annoying that still funds your account.

⚠️ Block Slots Are Recoverable, Reputation Isn't

You can unblock a task later, but you can't undo the weeks of point-starved farming you did while a good task was blocked. When in doubt, skip (cheap, reversible) instead of block (scarce, semi-permanent). Only block when you are certain the task serves no goal at any account stage.

3. 🧭 Decision Rule by Master

Your master changes two things: the point reward per task and the task pool. The decision framework is the same, but which masters you should even be using depends on your level.

① Low Masters — Turael, Mazchna, Vannaka (0–50 Slayer)

At low Slayer you have no block slots yet (first unlocks at 50 points). So the lever is skip, not block. Use Turael's free-skip trick liberally: get a task, and if it's bad, cancel at Turael and re-roll. Mazchna (20+) and Vannaka (40+) give 2 and 4 points per task respectively and feed easier monsters — good for building your first points toward that 50-point block unlock. Don't stress about blocking yet; just farm points.

② Mid Masters — Chaeldar, Konar (70–75 Slayer)

Chaeldar (70+) offers 6 points per task with a solid mid-level pool. Konar (75+) gives ~7 points per task and assigns a location, which often pairs with a slayer dungeon boost — generally higher value but more variance. By now you have 1–3 block slots. Start blocking only the genuinely dead tasks (e.g., a cave-task you can't reach, or a monster with zero profit and no unlocks). Keep the profitable Konar assignments.

③ High Masters — Nieve, Duradel, Krystilia (85–100+ Slayer)

Nieve/Steve (85+) gives 8–10 points per task and the broadest useful pool. Duradel (100+) gives the highest points (~10–15/task) and the best money tasks — the end-game standard. Krystilia (60+, Wilderness) gives the most points but carries PK risk. At this stage you may hold 4–6 blocks, so you can finally block several unwanted tasks. Decision: run Duradel for points+money, block the 4–6 worst tasks, skip the rest occasionally.

Most new players should skip her entirely until they are comfortable with PK risk or are specifically farming Wilderness content. The point bonus is not worth a lost bank at low levels.

4. 📊 Decision Rule by Slayer Level

Your Slayer level gates which monsters you can even be assigned and how many blocks you hold. Apply the framework differently at each band.

① Level 1–49 — No Blocks, Pure Skipping

You have zero block slots. Every unwanted task is handled by the Turael skip or a skip token. Focus on racking points toward 50 so you can start blocking. Do every task that's profitable or gives good XP; skip only the truly unreachable ones (e.g., a task needing gear you don't own yet). This band is about building the economy, not curating it. Until you hit 50 points, treat Slayer as pure point farming — the tasks you dislike most now are exactly what you'll block later, so just survive them and bank the points.

② Level 50–84 — First Blocks, Careful Curation

You now have 1–4 blocks depending on points. This is where the framework earns its keep. Block the 1–3 tasks that are both unprofitable and off-goal. Everything else: do it, or skip it occasionally if it's a one-off annoyance. Resist blocking "meh" tasks — you'll want those slots later for genuinely bad ones as your pool grows at Chaeldar/Konar.

③ Level 85–99+ — Full Curation

At Nieve/Duradel you hold 4–6+ blocks. Now you can aggressively curate: block every task that fails the core rule, keep a tight profitable rotation, and skip the few borderline cases. Your point income is high enough that skipping a task costs little. This is the "comfortable Slayer" stage the whole framework is building toward.

💡 Level Band Cheat

1–49: skip everything bad. 50–84: block only the worst 1–3. 85+: block freely, skip the rest. The number of blocks you hold should equal the number of tasks you are 100% certain you never want — no more.

5. ⚙️ Decision Rule by Gear & Preference

Two final filters turn the framework from generic to yours: what gear you own, and what you actually want from Slayer.

① Gear-Gated Tasks

If a task needs gear you don't own (e.g., a magic-heavy task with no mage setup, or a task in a dungeon you can't access), it's a candidate for skip now / block later. Early on, skip it — you may buy the gear and want the task later. Once you've permanently decided against that gear path, it becomes a block. The Slayer unlocks priority guide helps you plan which gear paths are worth it.

② Money vs XP Preference

Your goal flips the decision:

  • Money focus: Block low-GP tasks even if they give XP. Do every high-GP task. Skip mid tasks only when burned out.
  • XP focus (getting 99): Block low-XP tasks; do high-XP assignments; skip only unreachable ones.
  • Boss-unlock focus: Never block tasks that progress a boss unlock (e.g., Cerberus, Abyssal Sire pre-reqs); they are "do" forever.

Concrete example: a player chasing 99 Slayer should block low-XP tasks and do every high-XP assignment, even tedious ones. A player building a bank should do every task above ~200K GP/hr and only skip the rest. The same task — say, a mid-tier demon — is "do" for the moneymaker and "skip if bored" for the XP chaser. Your goal, not the task, decides.

③ Annoyance vs Profit Trade-off

The trap: blocking a task because it's annoying, even though it pays. Annoying + profitable = skip occasionally, never block. Annoying + unprofitable + off-goal = block. Train yourself to separate "I dislike this" from "this wastes my time" — they're different inputs. A disliked task that funds your account is a "do."

② What to Block (Confident Examples)

Block only tasks that are consistently unprofitable AND serve no unlock or XP goal for your account — the kind you'd never choose. These are rare; that's the point. The exact names live in the companion list, but the rule is what matters: confident "never," not "meh."

A good test before blocking: ask whether the task could ever serve a future goal. If you might one day want the drops, the unlocks, or the XP, skip it instead. Blocks are best reserved for tasks that are dead at every account stage — unreachable content you'll never gear for, or monsters whose drops you'll never use. That bar is higher than most players assume.

③ What to Always Do / Never Block

Protect at all costs: any task that unlocks a boss, any high-GP assignment on your money route, and any high-XP task if you're chasing 99. These are the engine of your Slayer account. Blocking them is the single most common way players kneecap their own progression.

🎯 10-Second Framework Recap

  1. Profitable or on-goal? → DO.
  2. Unprofitable AND off-goal? → BLOCK (if you have a slot).
  3. Profitable but annoying? → SKIP occasionally.
  4. No slot / unsure? → SKIP, never block.
  5. Master & level only change slot count and points — not the rule.

7. 🚫 Framework Mistakes to Avoid

Even players who know the theory break the framework in these predictable ways.

① Over-Blocking at Low Points

With only 1–2 slots, blocking three tasks means you've locked yourself out of future curation. Early on, skip; block only the one task you'd stake your account on never wanting. Save slots for when your pool actually includes bad high-level tasks.

A simple habit solves this: keep a notes list of tasks you dislike, and only act on it the moment a real block slot opens. Writing it down costs nothing; blocking early costs a slot you'll need later for a genuinely terrible high-level task. Patience here pays off directly in point income.

② Blocking for Profit, Then Regretting It

The classic: block a task that looked weak, then a gear upgrade or a new money route makes it great. Because unblocking is possible but point-disruptive, you've created avoidable friction. When a task has any income path, skip it, don't block it. The 1–99 Slayer guide maps which "weak" tasks become strong with gear.

③ Ignoring the Turael Free Skip

Players burn skip tokens (or just suffer bad tasks) when Turael offers a free re-roll. As long as you haven't killed a single monster of the assignment, you can cancel at Turael and get a fresh task with zero point cost. This is the cheapest lever in the game and the backbone of low-level Slayer. Use it before spending a token.

💡 Token Discipline

Reserve skip tokens for when you're already mid-task and Turael can't help (you've already started killing). For a fresh unwanted assignment, always try Turael first. Tokens are for emergencies, not routine re-rolls.

8. ❓ Slayer Block & Skip FAQs

When do I unlock my first block slot?

At 50 Slayer points. Before that, you can only skip (via Turael or tokens), not block. Once you hit 50 points, one slot opens; more slots unlock at roughly every 200 points after that (200, 400, 600, 800, 1,000…). Most mid-game accounts hold 2–5 blocks.

What's the difference between blocking and skipping?

Blocking removes a task from your rotation permanently (until you unblock it) and consumes a scarce block slot. Skipping discards only the current task; it stays in the pool and can return. Skip is reversible and cheap; block is semi-permanent and slot-limited. Use skip for "not now," block for "never."

How does the Turael free-skip trick work?

If you've been assigned a task you don't want, go to Turael (or Spria after the quest) and request a new task before killing any monster of the current assignment. The old task is cancelled with no point penalty and no token spent. It's the cheapest way to re-roll at low levels.

Which master should I use for Slayer?

It depends on your Slayer level and goals. Low level (under 70): Mazchna or Vannaka for steady points. Mid (70–85): Chaeldar or Konar for better points and value. High (85+): Nieve, and at 100+ Duradel for maximum points and money tasks. Krystilia is Wilderness-only with PK risk but high points.

Can I unblock a task later?

Yes. You can free a block slot and return the task to your pool at any time through the same block management interface. Unblocking doesn't refund anything special, but it's instant. That's why the framework says: when unsure, skip — blocking is only for confident "never" calls.

Should I block a task just because it's slow XP?

Only if it's also unprofitable and off-goal. Slow-XP tasks often still pay GP or progress unlocks, which makes them "do" or "skip," not "block." Blocking on XP alone wastes a slot you'll want for a truly dead task. Define your goal first, then apply the rule.

Do block slots carry across masters?

Yes. Blocks are account-wide, not per-master. A task you block is removed from every master's pool. That's why curation matters: a block you set at Duradel also affects what Nieve or Konar can assign you. One block list, all masters.

How many tasks should I block at 99 Slayer?

As many as you're certain you never want — typically 4–6 at high point totals. Block the dead-weight tasks (unprofitable, no unlocks, no XP value for your goals) and keep a tight profitable rotation. Don't block tasks that still serve any goal; those belong in "do" or "skip."

Where do skip tokens come from?

Skip tokens are earned from certain activities and can be bought from other players, but for a fresh unwanted task the Turael free-skip is always cheaper. Only spend a token when you've already started killing and Turael can't cancel it. Tokens are a reserve, not a routine tool — burning them on re-rolls you could do free wastes a scarce resource.

Is the companion block list enough on its own?

No — and that's the point of this guide. The block list names specific tasks, but your account, master, and goals decide whether each name applies to you. Run the framework on the list: a task one player blocks, another player grinds for profit. The method beats the list.
