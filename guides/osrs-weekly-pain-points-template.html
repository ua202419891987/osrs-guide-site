🔥 Pain Point #1 — Lost Authenticator? Players Locked Out and Worried About Their Banks

Source: r/2007scape community discussions

What players are complaining about:

Every week a new wave of players reports being locked out after switching phones, reinstalling their authenticator app, or wiping their device. The recovery process works, but it takes time, and the wait makes people nervous about what is happening to their accounts. A common mistake is losing the backup codes that were saved when the authenticator was first set up — that single error turns a quick fix into a slow support ticket. Set up your recovery details the moment you add two-factor authentication, not after you lose them.

Why it matters: A locked account is an account you cannot protect. If you cannot log in, you cannot spot unusual activity early, so securing your recovery details before anything goes wrong is the real defense. Most players who lose an account lose it to a recovery detail they ignored, not to a hacker they saw coming.

Quick fix checklist:

  • Check your email and saved files for the backup codes from when you set up the authenticator.
  • Keep the Jagex account recovery page open in a tab before you switch devices.
  • Write down your recovery questions and answers somewhere safe, outside the game.
  • If you still have access, add a secondary email and review your linked devices now.

🔥 Pain Point #2 — Bank Drained? Scam and Phishing Reports Keep Coming

Source: r/2007scape community discussions

What players are complaining about:

The classic tricks keep working: the double-death lure near the Wilderness ditch, "trust me and I will double your money," fake clan invites, and lookalike login pages that steal your password the moment you type it. Players post screenshots of losing endgame gear in seconds to lures that look friendly at first, and the scam reports follow the same patterns week after week. Knowing the exact wording of these lures is the fastest way to stay safe.

Why it matters: Scams cost players items, accounts, and trust in the community. Most of them are preventable, but only if you recognize the pattern before you see it on your own screen. If you can name the trick before the scammer uses it, the entire lure stops working.

Quick fix checklist:

  • Never trade anyone who asks you to drop items to prove trust — real trades go through the trade screen.
  • Bookmark the official login page and always check the address bar before you type your password.
  • Ignore "doubling money" offers in any world; no stranger doubles your gold for free.
  • If you were lured once, change your password and review your linked emails before doing anything else.

→ Guide priority: High — publish a "Scam and Phishing Watchlist" that breaks down the current lure patterns and what to do if you have already been hit.

🔥 Pain Point #3 — Items Lost to Bugs and Rollbacks After Updates

Source: r/2007scape community discussions

What players are complaining about:

After certain updates, players occasionally report losing items to game bugs, disconnects during boss fights, or server rollbacks that undo progress. The frustration is not just the lost value — it is that support feels slow and the rules for what gets restored are unclear, so people are not sure whether it is worth filing a report at all. File the report early, because screenshots and timestamps lose their value the longer you wait.

Why it matters: Knowing what is recoverable, how to submit a request, and what evidence to keep turns a hopeless situation into a solvable one. A little preparation before an incident makes the recovery process far smoother, because support can only work with the information you actually recorded.

Quick fix checklist:

  • Take a screenshot of your inventory and bank value before risky activities like bosses or quests.
  • Note the world, time, and in-game event if something disappears after an update.
  • Submit a report through the official support page with the evidence attached.
  • Avoid logging in during peak rollback windows right after a major update if you can.

→ Guide priority: Medium — write a "Recover Items Lost to Bugs or Rollbacks" checklist with the information you need to gather before you file a report.

🔥 Pain Point #4 — Sailing XP Feels Slow and the Bounty Change Confuses Players

Source: r/2007scape community discussions

What players are complaining about:

With Sailing still finding its balance, players argue every week about which methods are worth training. The recent cut to bounty XP made some players feel their favorite method was nerfed, while others are unsure whether to switch to cargo runs, treasure voyages, or island exploration. New sailors keep asking the same questions: what is the fastest route, and what should I do first with my ship. Old guides go stale fast here, so always check the latest patch notes before you copy a setup.

Why it matters: Sailing is a long skill. Picking the right early activities saves hours, and understanding how the bounty system works stops you from grinding a method that no longer pays off. Choosing the right activity from the start saves you an entire evening of wasted playtime.

Quick fix checklist:

  • Compare bounty missions against cargo runs before you commit to one activity.
  • Unlock the early ship upgrades first; they make every later voyage faster.
  • Keep a bank preset of the supplies your chosen method needs.
  • Read the in-game update notes for your version before following an older guide.

→ Guide priority: High — publish a "Sailing Leveling Guide" comparing the current best activities and explaining how the bounty XP changes affect each method.

🔥 Pain Point #5 — DPS Lower Than the Calculator? Gear Mismatch Is the Usual Cause

Source: r/2007scape community discussions

What players are complaining about:

A recurring complaint is that real kills take much longer than DPS calculators predict. The usual causes are the same small list every time: using the wrong attack type against a monster's weakness, adding strength bonuses at the cost of accuracy, forgetting to turn on the right prayer, or missing an attack-style bonus check. Players paste their setups and the answers always point to one of these mistakes. Once you fix them, most monsters melt noticeably faster.

Why it matters: DPS is the difference between comfortable trips and constant banking. Fixing the top causes costs nothing and can roughly double your kill speed on monsters you are attacking with the wrong style. Gear swaps are free to test, and one practice kill tells you within minutes whether the setup is working.

Quick fix checklist:

  • Check the monster's weakness and switch your weapon to the matching attack style.
  • Verify your attack bonus screen shows the style you think you are using.
  • Turn on the correct offensive prayer before every fight.
  • Bring the right stat-boosting potion for your style if your levels are borderline.

→ Guide priority: Medium — write a "DPS Checker" guide walking you through the gear, prayer, and attack-style checklist before you blame your stats.

🔥 Pain Point #6 — Prayer Training Costs Climb and Players Hunt for Cheaper Routes

Source: r/2007scape community discussions

What players are complaining about:

Prayer is one of the most expensive skills, and when bone prices rise on the Grand Exchange, the weekly posts start: is it worth training now, or should I wait? Players compare altar methods, ensouled heads, and the Wilderness Chaos Altar, weighing cost against the risk of being killed with a stack of bones in your inventory. Price swings can be sharp, so a little patience and market watching goes a long way.

Why it matters: Prayer directly boosts your combat, so it is worth training early. Overpaying by buying at the wrong time, or dying in the Wilderness with your bones, are both mistakes you can avoid with a simple plan. Comparing methods before you buy is worth the extra minutes, because the cost gap between routes can be significant.

Quick fix checklist:

  • Watch bone prices for a few days and buy when the market settles instead of at the peak.
  • Use ensouled heads from Slayer if you already train the skill.
  • If you use the Chaos Altar, bring only what you can afford to lose and use a world with less traffic.
  • Check the price history before you buy a full stack of any offering material.

→ Guide priority: Medium — publish a "Cheapest Prayer Training Methods" breakdown comparing cost, speed, and risk for each altar.

🔥 Pain Point #7 — Mobile Crashes and Clunky Menus Ruin Boss Trips

Source: r/2007scape community discussions

What players are complaining about:

Mobile players report app crashes during boss fights, freezes on crowded worlds, and interface elements that are too small to tap reliably. Dying to a disconnect because a menu was hard to close, or losing a kill because the game stuttered at the wrong moment, is a pain point that comes up every single week on the subreddit. Most of these issues respond well to a few minutes of setup work.

Why it matters: A stable setup is a big part of PvM on the go. Small changes — turning off player rendering, closing extra tabs, and enlarging your tap targets — make mobile bossing dramatically safer. A crash during a boss fight is not bad luck if it happens every session — it is a settings problem you can fix.

Quick fix checklist:

  • Turn off player rendering or choose a less crowded world for heavy boss areas.
  • Close unnecessary tabs and keep only your inventory and prayer open during fights.
  • Update the app and restart before longer sessions to clear memory.
  • Run a few practice kills in instanced practice modes before risking real gear.

→ Guide priority: Medium — write a "Mobile PvM Settings Guide" covering stability tweaks and the best layout options for boss fights.

🔥 Pain Point #8 — Flipping Losses: Grand Exchange Margins Disappear Fast

Source: r/2007scape community discussions

What players are complaining about:

New flippers buy at the shown price, watch the item crash, and panic-sell at a loss before the market recovers. Others complain that buy limits and the exchange tax eat into thin margins, making some items not worth trading at all. The advice in the comments is always the same: trade items with real demand, never chase a margin you saw in a video, and always account for the tax before you commit. The market punishes impulse trades faster than anything else.

Why it matters: Flipping looks easy until you understand liquidity and spread. Avoiding the beginner mistakes separates steady profit from daily losses, and it protects your bank while you learn the market. Beginners usually lose money not because they picked wrong items, but because they sized their first trades too large.

Quick fix checklist:

  • Check an item's trade volume, not just its margin, before you buy.
  • Remember the exchange tax when you calculate your expected profit.
  • Set a stop-loss in your head and sell if the price drops past it.
  • Trade items you understand — if you cannot explain why an item is worth something, skip it.

→ Guide priority: Low — publish a "Grand Exchange Flipping Basics" guide covering buy limits, the tax, and how to read a margin before you commit.
