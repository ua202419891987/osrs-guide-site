OSRS Tree Runs Guide 2026 — Tree Levels, Patch Order and the Daily Farming Loop

🆕 NEW
Published September 20, 2026  |  16 min read  |  By Yanchun Wu

Tree runs are the highest-value routine in Old School RuneScape Farming, because the tree does the training while you are logged out. Almost all of the experience lands on one action, checking the tree's health, so a full run is five minutes of running for tens of thousands of XP. This guide lists every tree seed with its real check-health value, every patch on the map with its fastest teleport, the patch order that makes each route quick, and how to weld tree runs onto herb and birdhouse loops.

1. What a Tree Run Actually Is

A tree run is a short circuit around the map. At each patch you check the health of the tree that finished growing since your last visit, chop it down, dig up the roots, plant a fresh sapling, and teleport to the next one. Six patches takes four to six minutes with the right teleports.

The experience curve is why this routine dominates Farming. Trees award almost nothing for planting and almost everything for checking health: a magic tree gives 145.5 XP when planted and 13,768.3 XP eight hours later. Nothing else in the skill pays that much for one click, and the growth timer runs while you are offline or doing something else.

The timers set your schedule. Regular trees mature in 3 hours 20 minutes to 8 hours, so two or three resets a day are realistic. Fruit trees take exactly 16 hours, which locks them to once a day. Mahogany hardwood takes about 85 hours, making it a twice-weekly layer. Bring a spade, an axe, a rake unless you own auto-weed, one sapling per patch, the protection items you plan to use, and coins for the gardener.

One structural point saves a lot of confusion: regular tree patches and fruit tree patches are different types in different places. Tree Gnome Village and Lletya, often listed alongside the gnome patches, are fruit tree patches and are not on the regular tree list.

Read that as a cost ladder, not a level ladder. Willow more than triples oak's XP per patch, maple does it again, and magic is worth nearly thirty oaks. Seed prices climb faster than XP in the same direction, which is the whole economics of tree runs in one sentence. Levels are boostable, so a garden pie or spicy stew can push you into the next tier early.

The two numbers worth memorising: six magic trees is about 82,610 XP from check-health alone for five minutes of movement, and six yews is about 42,419 XP. Regular tree seeds are not sold in shops in any quantity. They come from bird nests, which come from birdhouse runs, the giant mole, Woodcutting and Farming Guild seed packs. That loop funds itself: see our birdhouse runs guide and the Farming Guild contracts guide. Trees never need watering, and paying the gardener makes disease impossible for that tree's whole cycle.

Spirit trees do double duty: planting one adds a teleport destination to your spirit tree network, which is what makes the fruit route fast. You may only have one planted at a time until level 91, when it rises to two, and the cap disappears at 99 Farming. Redwood is the biggest single Farming XP drop in the game at 22,450 for one check, but on a four-and-a-half-day timer it is a background task, not part of a daily loop. Pair it with a Forestry-aware Woodcutting session so the logs have somewhere useful to go.

Hardwood is the cheapest high-level XP in the skill. Teak unlocks at 35 Farming for 7,290 XP, mahogany at 55 Farming for 15,720 XP, in the three Fossil Island patches unlocked by Bone Voyage. Growth is 74h 40m and 85h 20m respectively, and mahogany costs 25 yanillian hops. Three mahogany patches is about 47,160 XP per cycle, twice a week, for very little gold.

Lletya is the gate that matters most for mid-level accounts. You do not need to finish Mourning's End Part I, only to have started far enough to reach the village, and then a teleport crystal to get there quickly. Skipping it costs you a seventh of your daily fruit tree XP. The five plantable spirit tree patches are in Etceteria, east of Port Sarim, north of Brimhaven, south-west of Hosidius and the Farming Guild's northern section; the guild patch needs 85 Farming to reach even if you boosted to plant. The elite Falador Diary makes the Falador Park tree patch permanently disease-free, which is one of the few permanent upgrades aimed squarely at this loop.

5. The Optimal Route and Patch Order

Route design comes down to one rule: never teleport twice when one teleport and a short run will do.

① Regular tree route — six patches, four to six minutes

  1. Farming Guild west wing at 65 Farming. A skills necklace lands you inside, and the guild bank chest makes it the natural start and finish.
  2. Gnome Stronghold. A guild spirit tree goes straight there, and the patch sits beside the slayer cave. Without one, use a slayer ring.
  3. Taverley. A Taverley tablet drops you at the gates, with the patch a few steps south.
  4. Falador Park. A ring of wealth lands inside the park, next to the patch. No walking at all.
  5. Varrock Palace courtyard. Varrock teleport, then the eastern courtyard entrance.
  6. Lumbridge. Lumbridge teleport, then west through the castle back gates.

② Fruit tree route — seven patches, five to eight minutes

  1. Farming Guild north wing at 85 Farming. Same necklace, same bank chest, and a guild spirit tree unlocks the rest of the route from one spot.
  2. Tree Gnome Village. Spirit tree in, then follow Elkoy out of the maze.
  3. Tree Gnome Stronghold. Spirit tree across.
  4. Catherby. Camelot teleport and run east, which also puts you beside the Catherby herb patch.
  5. Brimhaven, then the calquat. A house portal or charter ship reaches the fruit patch; run south from Brimhaven to the calquat so it never costs a second teleport.
  6. Lletya. Teleport crystal, then the patch inside the village.
  7. Kastori. Quetzal transport or a pendant of ates, then a short run south-west.

The spirit tree network is the spine of the fruit route, which is why planting spirit trees early repays itself immediately: every patch added to the network is one fewer teleport item to carry. Keep one preset with your saplings, the matching payments and your tools, batch your saplings in the bank while doing something else, and use the tool leprechaun to note harvested fruit. The order of operations at every patch is fixed: check health, chop, dig the roots, plant. Roots make supercompost, so bag them rather than dropping them.

Health-check XPThe entire point, one click per patchNothing to optimise except running more patches
Logs and rootsYew and magic logs hold real value; roots become supercompostChop and dig every tree instead of paying to have it cleared
Fruit harvestsPalm, papaya and dragonfruit keep producing after maturityHarvest on every visit, no replanting needed

Seed prices move with the Grand Exchange, so any guide quoting a hard figure for a magic sapling is quoting a snapshot. Work from the structure instead: regular tree seeds are the scarce input because they only come from nests and seed packs, and magic is the scarcest of all, while fruit tree seeds are farmed in bulk by contract runners and pay part of their own cost back in fruit.

Three value plays hold regardless of the market. Calquat at 72 buys 12,096 XP for eight poison ivy berries and rides along on the fruit run for free. Mahogany at 55 gives 15,720 XP for 25 yanillian hops across three patches, with a long timer as the price. Papaya at 57 is cheap to plant and its fruit pays gardeners elsewhere. The other half of the equation is what funds the trees: a herb run every 80 minutes is the highest gold-per-run Farming activity in the game, and the herb run mastery guide covers patch order and yield stacking. The patch disease and compost guide explains why ultracompost matters when you are not paying for protection. Before committing to a tier, multiply the sapling price by the patches you can reach and compare that total against the XP: many players find running willow and papaya indefinitely is not a compromise at all, just the cheapest route to 99.

7. Stacking Tree Runs With Herb and Birdhouse Loops

A tree run on its own is an errand. The routine is what happens when you chain the repeatable Farming activities by their timers so each is ready when you next log in.

Birdhouse runs50 minutesUp to about tenFunds everything: nests supply tree seeds and crushed nests

Fruit trees16 hoursOneOnce-a-day XP drop plus a rolling fruit harvest

Hardwood74h 40m to 85h 20mTwo per weekCheap bulk XP on a long timer

A workable rhythm: on login, run birdhouses first because the 50-minute timer recovers soonest, then herbs while it ticks, then the regular tree run, which resets two or three times a day depending on tier. Fruit trees and the calquat happen once whenever it suits you, and hardwood is handled twice a week. Farming Guild contracts slot in naturally, since the requested crops usually match patches you are already working and the seed packs hand you the tree seeds you want; keep the guild patches planted and contracts often complete themselves as you pass through. The whole structure also survives a phone session, because a tree run is short, teleport-heavy and needs almost no inventory management, as covered in our mobile farming guide. Short weeklies can be folded into the same session: a Tears of Guthix visit takes two minutes between patches.

The most common planning error is ignoring fruit trees to fund regular trees. Regular trees cost the most per XP and pay nothing back; fruit trees cost less and hand you a harvest. An account running papaya or palm daily, and planting regular trees only from birdhouse seeds, will reach 99 Farming for a fraction of the gold. The mature version of the routine is six magic trees, seven fruit trees, one calquat, one celastrus and three mahogany patches: roughly 82,610 XP from the regular trees, up to 121,345 from seven dragonfruit patches, 12,096 from the calquat, 14,130 from the celastrus and 47,160 from the hardwood cycle.

⚠️ The timer trap

Growth only advances on the patch tick, so a tree planted just before a tick effectively loses a stage. Plant straight after a tick when you can, but do not panic when you cannot: over a day the difference is minutes, not a missed run.

10. Tree Runs FAQs

Q: How many tree patches are there in OSRS?

Six regular tree patches: Varrock Palace courtyard, west of Lumbridge Castle, Falador Park, Taverley, Gnome Stronghold and the Farming Guild west wing at 65 Farming. Separately there are seven fruit tree patches, three hardwood patches on Fossil Island, and five plantable spirit tree patches.

Q: What level do I need for each tree?

Regular trees: oak 15, willow 30, maple 45, yew 60, magic 75. Fruit trees: apple 27, banana 33, orange 39, curry 42, pineapple 51, papaya 57, palm 68, dragonfruit 81. Specials: calquat 72, crystal 74 with Song of the Elves, spirit 83, celastrus 85, redwood 90. Hardwood: teak 35, mahogany 55. Levels are boostable at planting.

Q: Regular trees or fruit trees?

Fruit trees are cheaper per point of XP and pay part of the cost back in fruit, so they are the better default. Regular trees give the biggest drop per click and reset two or three times a day, so they are faster if gold is no constraint. The cheapest reliable plan is fruit trees daily plus regular trees from free birdhouse seeds.

Q: Do I need to pay the gardener to protect trees?

No, but it removes disease risk for that tree's entire growth cycle, so it is worth it on magic, palm, dragonfruit and celastrus. On cheap trees the payment often costs more than the seed, so skip it and rely on ultracompost. Trees never need watering either way.

Q: How much XP is a full tree run worth?

Check-health only: six magic trees is about 82,610 XP, six yews 42,419, six maples 20,420 and six willows 8,739. Six dragonfruit is about 104,010 and a full seven-patch run 121,345. A run covering regular trees, fruit trees, a calquat and the guild specials clears 200,000 XP in under fifteen minutes of movement.

Q: How often should I run trees?

Regular trees whenever they finish, roughly two or three times a day since growth runs from 3h 20m for oak to 8h for magic. Fruit trees exactly once a day at 16 hours. Calquat about daily at 21h 20m, hardwood twice a week at about 85 hours, and spirit, celastrus and redwood whenever they finish.
