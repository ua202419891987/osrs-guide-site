OSRS Fletching 1-99 Standard Training Guide (2026)

That is the promise of Fletching in Old School RuneScape. It is the most bank-friendly artisan skill in the game, it can be trained almost entirely from materials you buy at the Grand Exchange, and unlike most skills it does not demand your full attention for every single action. This is the skill you train while watching a movie, while chatting with clanmates, or while waiting for your Slayer task to reset. In this guide we take you from the very first arrow shaft at level 1 all the way to the 13,034,431 experience points required for level 99, with a standard, low-drama route that any member account can follow, plus the faster arrow and bolt shortcuts you can switch to once your bank allows it. Whether you are a brand-new account or a returning player chasing that final cape, this is the only Fletching page you need to bookmark for 2026.

1. Why Train Fletching (Value, AFK, Gold Synergy)

Fletching sits in a small, privileged group of OSRS skills that players describe as "buyable" — meaning you can reach the max level purely by purchasing raw materials and clicking them. That label is usually said with a hint of jealousy, because buyable skills let you convert spare gold directly into levels without the grind, the travel, or the RNG of a gathering skill. But Fletching is more than just a gold sink you click through. It is one of the few artisan skills whose outputs feed directly back into your combat power, your money making, and your achievement diary progress.

Best return on effort of any artisan skill

Compared with Herblore, Construction, or Prayer, Fletching is uniquely forgiving on attention. Almost every action is a bank-standing click that you can queue with the Make-X system, and the animation does not require you to watch the screen. A single inventory of magic longbows takes only a few seconds to fletch and a few more to string, and you can keep the queue running while you do something else. That makes Fletching the default "second monitor skill" for thousands of players. If you have ever wanted a 99 that does not feel like a chore, this is it.

The AFK and semi-AFK advantage

Most mid- and high-level Fletching methods are semi-AFK by design. You open your bank, choose Make-X on a log, and the game keeps fletching even while the bank interface is open. You only need to re-bank every inventory. At lower levels the click cadence is faster, but from willow shortbows upward you can comfortably look away between inventories. During Double XP weekends this becomes even more relaxed, because you need roughly half the materials to hit the same XP. For players who dislike intensive skilling, Fletching is the gentlest path to a skill cape in the entire game.

Synergy with your gold making

Fletching pairs beautifully with the money you earn from bossing and Slayer. Because it is a buyable skill, the smart play is to fund it with GP from PvM gear swaps and high-value kills rather than trying to skilling your way to the gold. A single profitable bossing session can bankroll several levels of Fletching. In the other direction, Fletching reduces your recurring Ranged costs: once you can make your own broad arrows, dragon arrows, and amethyst bolts, you stop paying the Grand Exchange markup on every stack of ammo. Over a long account lifespan that saving is enormous.

It unlocks content, not just a cape

High Fletching is a quiet requirement behind a surprising amount of endgame content. Broad arrows need 55 Slayer to unlock the arrowheads but are fletched at a Fletching level that scales with the ammo. Dragon arrows need level 90 Fletching. The +4 Ranged strength bonus of the twisted bow is irrelevant to Fletching, but the ability to craft your own high-tier ammo for raids, slayer, and Yama gear requirements is a real, practical upgrade. Achievement diaries across the game also reward Ranged ammo you can produce yourself. In short, Fletching is an investment that keeps paying you back every time you draw a bow.

💡 Members vs F2P

Free-to-play accounts can make bronze arrows and basic short and long bows, but the real training, oak through magic bows, all arrows, and all bolts, is members-only. If you are F2P, treat Fletching as a tiny head start and switch to a member world to actually level it. Our F2P training efficiency guide shows how to squeeze the most from the limited F2P options before you subscribe.

2. Prerequisites — Quests, Bank Presets, F2P vs Members

One of the best things about Fletching is how little setup it needs. There is no skill you must train first, no dangerous boss you must beat, and no expensive unlock you must buy. But a little preparation turns a messy grind into a smooth one. This section covers the quest shortcuts, the bank presets, and the membership decision that shape your route.

Quest experience rewards worth grabbing first

Fletching has a handful of small quest rewards that shave a little XP off the early grind. They will not skip the skill, but they are free progress you should not leave on the table:

  • Animal Magnetism — rewards the Ava's accumulator, which passively recovers arrows. It is not Fletching XP directly, but it saves thousands of arrows you would otherwise fletch or buy, and it is a near-mandatory Ranged item.
  • Perils of Ice Mountain — grants a small Fletching XP lamp-style reward and is a very short, low-requirement quest.
  • Defender of Varrock and a few others — each hands over a modest XP drop; check the OSRS Wiki "Quest experience rewards" page before you grind, because the exact numbers change as quests are added.
  • Playing the Sectional role in Temple of the Eye — not Fletching XP, but it unlocks the Lunar spellbook shortcut that helps with related activities.

None of these replace the core grind, but stacking them lets you start the real training a level or two ahead. If you want the full picture of which quests gate which skills, our quest requirement wall guide maps every lock in the game.

Set up bank presets before you start

The single biggest quality-of-life improvement for Fletching is a clean bank preset. Before you begin a session, save a preset that contains only the logs and bowstrings for your current method, plus your knife and any stamina or teleport tabs you use. A tight preset means every bank open shows exactly what you need, and you waste zero time searching. If your bank is a mess of unrelated items, Fletching will feel sluggish even though the clicks are fast. Presets also let you swap instantly between, say, willow shortbows and maple shortbows the moment you hit the next level.

F2P vs members: what changes

Free-to-play Fletching is essentially a tutorial. You can cut arrow shafts, fletch bronze arrows, and make normal short and long bows, but you top out almost immediately because oak logs and everything above are members content. Members unlock the entire tree of bows from oak to magic, every arrow type, every bolt type, and the high-XP dragon and broad methods. The cost difference is also dramatic: F2P methods are nearly free but painfully slow, while members methods are fast but can be expensive. If you are on the fence about subscribing, read our new player skill priority guide to see where Fletching should sit in your early plan.

💡 Knife vs chisel vs nothing

You need a knife to fletch bows and arrow shafts from logs, and you need arrowtips plus shafts to make arrows. Always keep a knife in your preset. The chisel is only needed for bolts and javelins. If you ever "cannot fletch" a log, nine times out of ten you simply are not holding a knife or you are standing too far from a bank interface.

4. Phase-by-Phase 1-99 (Seven Level Bands)

This is the heart of the guide. Each band below gives you the method, the approximate cost, the expected XP per hour, and a practical tip drawn from how real players actually train. Stick to the standard stringing path unless you have a specific reason to sprint.

Phase 1 — Levels 1 to 10 (Arrow shafts and normal bows)

The opening ten levels are cheap and quick. Start by fletching arrow shafts from normal logs if you want pure speed, or jump straight to bronze arrows which are F2P-safe. Most players move to shortbows almost immediately because the XP per item is higher. Cut the unstrung shortbow, then string it with a bowstring for the bonus XP. The whole band costs well under a million GP and takes maybe twenty minutes of clicking.

  • Method: Arrow shafts to 5, then shortbow (u) + string to 10.
  • Cost: ~200k GP buying normal logs and bowstrings.
  • XP/hr: ~150k.
  • Tip: Buy normal logs in bulk; they are dirt cheap and the bank standing click is identical to every later method, so this is where you learn the Make-X rhythm.

Phase 2 — Levels 10 to 20 (Longbows from normal logs)

At level 10 you unlock the normal longbow, which doubles the XP per item compared with the shortbow. This is still pocket change in GP terms, and it is a good place to settle into the semi-AFK cadence. You will outgrow normal logs fast, so do not over-buy; a few hundred logs covers the whole band.

  • Method: Longbow (u) + string.
  • Cost: ~500k GP.
  • XP/hr: ~200k.
  • Tip: This is the last band on free-to-play-friendly materials. If you are F2P, this is roughly where you stall.

Phase 3 — Levels 20 to 40 (Oak bows)

Oak is where members training properly begins. The oak shortbow is cheap, the XP per item jumps to 33 when strung, and you can comfortably AFK it. Many players consider 20-40 the most relaxing part of the whole 1-99 because the materials are so inexpensive that mistakes barely matter.

  • Method: Oak shortbow (u) + string.
  • Cost: ~3M GP.
  • XP/hr: ~280k.
  • Tip: If you are an Ironman, oak logs are easy to bank from nearly any oak tree, which makes this band nearly free. See our Ironman self-sufficient skills guide for log gathering routes.

Phase 4 — Levels 40 to 55 (Willow bows)

Willow shortbows are the classic "set and forget" method. The logs are still cheap on the Grand Exchange, the XP per item is solid, and the XP per hour clears 400k with reasonable clicking. This band is where most casual players spend their Fletching hours because it is the best value-for-attention in the skill.

  • Method: Willow shortbow (u) + string.
  • Cost: ~8M GP.
  • XP/hr: ~400k.
  • Tip: Around level 55 you unlock broad arrows if you also have 55 Slayer. Hold that thought for the routes section; for the standard path, keep stringing willows until 55.

Phase 5 — Levels 55 to 70 (Maple bows)

Maple shortbows push XP per item to 100 and XP per hour past half a million. The cost starts to bite, but maple logs are still a liquid Grand Exchange item. This band is the last "comfortable" stretch before the expensive yews and magics.

  • Method: Maple shortbow (u) + string.
  • Cost: ~14M GP.
  • XP/hr: ~550k.
  • Tip: If GP is tight, you can substitute yew shortbows from 65, but the standard path stays on maples to 70 for the smoother XP curve.

Phase 6 — Levels 70 to 85 (Yew and magic shortbows)

Yew shortbows carry you through 70-80, then magic shortbows take you to 85. This is where the bill climbs sharply: yew and magic logs are among the most expensive tradeable logs in the game. The XP per hour is good, but your bank will feel it. Many players begin considering the arrow shortcut here to save both time and gold on the final push.

  • Method: Yew shortbow (u) + string (70-80), then magic shortbow (u) + string (80-85).
  • Cost: ~22M GP.
  • XP/hr: ~700k-750k.
  • Tip: Magic shortbows can be crushed into magic stock for Construction, so the "waste" is not total if you also train Construction. Otherwise sell them back to recover a fraction of the cost.

Phase 7 — Levels 85 to 99 (Magic longbows or the arrow sprint)

The home stretch. The standard route uses magic longbows from 85 to 99. The XP per item is 183 when strung and XP per hour sits around 850k with good clicking. If you have the gold and want it done faster, switch to dragon arrows at 90 — more expensive per XP but it can cut several hours off the grind. Broad arrows are the cheaper fast option if you have the Slayer requirement.

  • Method: Magic longbow (u) + string (standard), or dragon arrows (90+) / broad arrows (speed).
  • Cost: ~45M GP standard; add 25-50M for the arrow sprint.
  • XP/hr: ~850k standard, ~1.2M-1.5M with arrows.
  • Tip: Do not start magic longbows until your bank is stocked. Running out mid-inventory at this level wastes the most expensive clicks in the skill.
💡 Use Make-X, Not One-At-A-Time

Right-click a log and choose "Make-X", type a big number, then click your bank open. The fletching continues while the bank interface is up. This is the single biggest quality-of-life trick for the whole skill and it works identically from level 1 to 99.

5. Arrows vs Bows vs Bolts — Deep Route Comparison

Beyond the standard bow path there are two other families of Fletching method, and understanding them lets you optimize the endgame. Each has a different cost profile, XP rate, and relationship to the rest of your account.

Bows (the standard route)

Bows are the safest, most predictable method. You fletch a log into an unstrung bow, then string it. The outputs are tradeable (except where noted), so you can sell them back to recover some cost, and the magic shortbow in particular has Construction value. Bows are the recommended route for a first 99 because the clicking is uniform and the materials are always available. The downside is that bows are the most expensive per XP at the very top end because magic logs are pricey.

Arrows (the speed route)

Arrow fletching combines arrow shafts with arrowtips. Bronze through rune arrows are slow and barely worth it, but two methods dominate the late game. Broad arrows require 55 Slayer to buy the untradeable broad arrowheads from a Slayer master; they give 10 XP each and are among the fastest XP in the skill at roughly 1.2M XP per hour. Dragon arrows unlock at level 90 Fletching; you cut dragon arrowtips onto shafts for very high XP per hour and they are fully tradeable, which means you can sell the finished arrows to claw back a chunk of the cost. Arrows are the choice when you want 90-99 finished in a hurry and have the bank for it.

Bolts (the hybrid route)

Bolts are made by attaching bolt tips to unfinished bolts, and at higher levels you can gem-tip them for profit. Amethyst bolts (level 80+) and diamond bolts are solid middle-ground methods that also feed general PvM mechanics where bolt procs matter. Bolts are slower than dragon arrows for pure XP but they can be profitable when margins are good, and they serve players who want both training and a sellable product. For most pure 1-99 speedruns, bolts are a side option rather than the main line.

💡 When to Switch to Arrows

If you have the GP and want 90-99 done fast, buy dragon arrowtips at 90 and fletch dragon arrows. It is more expensive per XP than magic longbows but can cut hours off your grind. If you are on a budget, just ride magic longbows to 99.

6. Cost Optimization — Grand Exchange Timing & Bulk Buying

Fletching is a buyable skill, which means your biggest variable is not time, it is how cleverly you buy materials. A player who buys at the wrong moment can pay double what a patient player pays for the same 99. This section is where good Fletchers save tens of millions of GP.

Total cost reality check

Total XP to 99 is 13,034,431. Your cost depends entirely on whether you string bows and which late method you pick. A pure unstrung-bow route that sells the results back can land around 10-40M and sometimes turns a small profit. The standard stringing route runs roughly 50-100M. Finishing with dragon arrows adds another 25-50M on top but saves hours. The chart below shows how the cumulative cost concentrates in the expensive upper bands.

Grand Exchange timing

Log and bowstring prices swing with supply and demand, and they spike whenever content creators publish a "fast 99" video or whenever Double XP weekend approaches. The cheapest time to buy is usually a week or two after a DXP event, when the market is flooded with sellers. Avoid buying the day a popular streamer features Fletching. Set buy limits patiently: a single Grand Exchange slot only fills a limited number of items per four hours, so open several slots across multiple item types (logs, bowstrings, arrowtips) to build your stock without moving the price against yourself.

Bulk buying without moving the market

If you dump a huge buy order on magic logs, you will eat through the cheap sell offers and pay progressively more per log. Spread purchases over days, use multiple alts or multiple worlds if you manage them, and never panic-buy the top offers. Watch the "sell" column on the Grand Exchange interface: if only a few items are listed cheaply, wait. Patience here is worth millions across a 99.

Make your own bowstrings

Bowstrings are a quiet cost multiplier. Picking flax at the Gnome Stronghold, banking at the nearby spinning wheel, and spinning bowstrings is slow but free versus buying thousands of them. Most players only do this for early levels or when GP is tight, but if you are training Fletching on a tight budget it is the highest-leverage free activity in the skill. Flax is also a gentle self-sufficient loop for Ironmen who cannot buy materials at all.

Fund it, do not grind for it

The smartest cost optimization is not coupon-clipping on the Grand Exchange, it is earning the GP elsewhere. Bossing and high-value Slayer bankroll buyable skills far better than skilling for gold. Pair your Fletching push with a money-making method from our PvM gear swap guide so the skill feels free.

💡 Buy-price monitoring

Check the live Grand Exchange mid-week, not on weekends. Use the in-game "view price" graph and watch the 30-day trend. If the price is at a 30-day high, wait. If it is at a 30-day low and stable, that is your buy window. This single habit saves more GP than any "cheap method" gimmick.

7. Milestones & Boss-Prep Gear Linkage

Fletching is not an island. The levels you gain translate into concrete combat advantages, and planning your Fletching around boss preparation makes the grind feel purposeful. Here is how the milestones connect to the rest of your account.

Level 55 — Broad arrows and Slayer synergy

Once you have 55 Slayer and the Fletching level to match, broad arrows become available. They are a top-tier Ranged ammo for Slayer tasks and many bosses, and making them yourself removes a recurring expense. This is the first milestone where Fletching actively improves your money making rather than just costing you.

Level 80 — Amethyst bolts and mid-game PvM

At 80 you can make amethyst bolts and arrows, which bridge the gap between mid-game and end-game Ranged. They pair well with the budget gear setups many players use before they can afford bis equipment, and they are a step up from broad arrows for bosses weak to Ranged.

Level 90 — Dragon arrows and raid-ready Ranged

Dragon arrows are the gold-standard Ranged ammo for high-level content. Hitting 90 Fletching means you can produce your own supply for raids, slayer, and Yama gear requirements instead of buying them at a premium. For players chasing completionist goals, this milestone alone justifies the grind.

Level 99 — the cape and the credibility

The Fletching skill cape is a clean, fast, and relatively cheap 99 compared with Construction or Prayer. It also unlocks the cape's emote and, more practically, signals to clans and teams that you have the account maturity to handle endgame content. When you plan your 90-plus skill planning, Fletching is usually one of the first capes you should grab because it pays you back immediately through cheaper ammo.

💡 Link Fletching to your gear swaps

Keep a bank preset that pairs your fletched ammo with the specific weapon it feeds. When you build PvM gear swap presets, include a dragon-arrow or broad-arrow tab so you never waste a trip because you forgot ammo. The gear-swap discipline you learn here transfers to every boss in the game.

8. 10+ Common Mistakes & Traps

Most Fletching "failures" are not about clicking slowly, they are about avoidable errors that cost GP or time. Here are the traps to dodge.

  1. Over-buying at a price peak. Dumping a massive buy order right before DXP doubles your cost. Buy during lulls, not hype.
  2. Forgetting the knife. You cannot fletch without a knife in your inventory. Keep one in every preset.
  3. Using one-at-a-time instead of Make-X. This triples your clicks for zero extra XP. Always use Make-X at a bank.
  4. Stringing when you meant to save gold. If your goal is cheap, make only unstrung bows and sell them. Do not accidentally string and lose the margin.
  5. Running out mid-session at magic level. The most expensive clicks in the skill happen at 85-99. Stock before you start, not after you stall.
  6. Ignoring broad arrows at 55. Players who keep stringing willows past 55 leave free XP on the table if they have the Slayer requirement.
  7. Buying bowstrings instead of picking flax. On a tight budget, free bowstrings from flax are a massive saving you should not skip.
  8. Mis-pricing the sell-back. Magic shortbows crush into Construction stocks; selling them raw loses value if you also train Construction.
  9. Training Fletching before you can afford it. A half-finished 99 is worse than a planned one. Save the GP first, then grind.
  10. Chasing "fastest method" clickbait. Dragon arrows are fastest but priciest; the "fastest" route is only fastest if your time is worth more than your gold. Know which you have less of.
  11. Not using bank presets. A cluttered bank turns a semi-AFK skill into a chore. Preset everything.
  12. Forgetting Ava's accumulator. Without it you lose fired arrows; Fletching ammo only saves money if you recover what you shoot via PvM mechanics and Ava's.

9. Synergy With Woodcutting, Combat & Firemaking

Fletching does not exist in a vacuum. Three other skills feed it or are fed by it, and smart players train them together.

Woodcutting supplies the logs

In theory you can chop every log you fletch. In practice, buying is almost always faster than chopping your own at 99-scale volumes, because Woodcutting is far slower per XP than Fletching consumes logs. The exception is Ironmen, who must gather, and self-sufficient Ironman planning treats Woodcutting and Fletching as a single loop. For mains, the synergy is loose: train Woodcutting separately for its own rewards, buy logs for Fletching.

Combat consumes your ammo

Every arrow and bolt you fletch exists to be shot at something. Fletching and Ranged combat are a closed loop: you make ammo, you use it in PvM, Ava's accumulator returns part of it, and you top up the rest. Training Fletching right before a big Slayer or bossing push means your ammo is fresh, free (to you), and exactly the tier you need.

Firemaking meets Fletching at the log

When you cut a log into an unstrung bow, you do not get the burnt log, but the broader wood economy connects them: many players burn the lowest logs for Firemaking while saving higher logs for Fletching. More directly, the flax-to-bowstring loop pairs with the calm, repetitive rhythm of Firemaking training, making both good "second monitor" skills you can run in the same session.

Herblore is the sibling buyable

Both Herblore and Fletching are buyable artisan skills, so train them back-to-back when you have a GP buffer. The mental model is identical: buy materials, click at a bank, repeat. Players who batch their buyable skills spend less time context-switching and more time gaining levels.

10. Frequently Asked Questions

Is Fletching members only?

Full Fletching is members-only. F2P accounts can only make bronze arrows and basic short and long bows, which caps progress very early. To reach high levels you need a membership, and our F2P training efficiency guide covers what little you can do without one.

What is the fastest Fletching method?

Broad arrows (after 55 Slayer) and dragon arrows (level 90) are the fastest XP methods, reaching well over 1M XP per hour with good clicking. The standard magic longbow route sits around 850k XP per hour. If you want the absolute fastest clock, arrows win; if you want the cheapest, unstrung bows win.

How much does 99 Fletching cost?

A standard stringing route costs roughly 50-100M GP if you buy all materials. Making only unstrung bows and selling them can drop that toward 10-40M, sometimes with slight profit. Finishing with dragon arrows adds another 25-50M but saves hours. Buy smart and you land at the low end of every range.

Can I make money with Fletching?

Some methods break even or profit slightly, such as flipping unstrung bows or making amethyst and dragon arrows when margins are good, but Fletching is generally treated as a GP sink funded by other money makers rather than a profit skill itself. Think of it as upgrading your account, not filling your bank.

Should I make my own bowstrings from flax?

If GP is tight, absolutely. Picking flax at the Gnome Stronghold and spinning it at the nearby wheel is free versus buying thousands of bowstrings. It is click-heavy, so most mains only do it for early levels or when gold is scarce, but for Ironmen it is mandatory.

Do I need any quests before Fletching?

No quest is required to start, but Animal Magnetism (for Ava's accumulator) and a few small XP-reward quests give free progress. Our quest requirement wall guide lists every relevant reward so you can grab them first.

Bows or arrows for my first 99?

For a first, low-drama 99, bows are the better teacher: uniform clicking, tradeable outputs, and predictable cost. Switch to arrows only when you specifically want to sprint the 90-99 band and have the bank for it. Both reach 99; the difference is time versus gold.

How long does 99 Fletching take?

Active clicking time is roughly 15-25 hours on the standard route, longer if you AFK heavily, shorter if you use dragon arrows. Because it is semi-AFK, many players spread it across a week of casual sessions rather than one sitting.

Is Fletching worth it for bossing?

Yes. Dragon arrows at 90 and broad arrows at 55 directly reduce your Ranged ammo costs for raids, slayer, and bosses like Yama. The Yama gear requirements in particular assume you can supply high-tier ammo. The cape also signals account maturity for teams.

Where should Fletching sit in my skill priority?

It is a high-value, low-effort 99, so it belongs early in your skill priority plan once you have the GP. Pair it with Herblore as your two buyable artisan skills and knock both out in the same wealthy stretch.

🎯 Final Tips Before You Start

  • Use Make-X at a bank — it is the entire skill's quality of life.
  • Bank-stand in a popular Fletching world so the queue never stalls.
  • Decide early: budget (unstrung) vs speed (string + dragon arrows).
  • Pair Fletching with Herblore when you have GP to burn on buyables.
  • Save GP with flax-to-bowstring if you do not mind the clicks.
  • Buy materials during price lulls, never during Double XP hype.
  • Stock magic logs before you hit 85 — do not stall at the most expensive clicks.
