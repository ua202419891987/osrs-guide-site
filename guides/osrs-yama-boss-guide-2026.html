Yama 2026: All Phases, Glyphs & Counter Play in One Guide

Updated August 19, 2026  |  15 min read  |  By Yanchun Wu
🆕 NEW
🛡️ Level: 85+

Yama is the fight that finally rewards players who read the room instead of just clicking a weapon. Across three escalating phases you will juggle color tiles under your feet, read glyph walls before they fire, dodge shadow pillars that sweep the arena, and — in the final push — survive attacks that punch straight through your protect prayers. This master guide compresses every phase, every counter, and the full contract/pact scaling system into one page, then hands you off to the focused deep-dives where needed. Start with our Yama gear requirements breakdown if you just want to know whether your account can even enter, then drill into the full Yama deep-dive for the rotation-level detail.

If you have already fought Yama a few times and only came here for one mechanic, use the table of contents to jump straight to it. If you are brand new, read top to bottom once — the phase structure is interlocking, and the mistakes players make in phase 3 almost always come from not understanding the color logic introduced in phase 1.

💡 Quick Verdict

Yama is a mechanically dense but fair boss. The single most important skill is footwork on the color tiles: if you stand on the wrong tile during a Ward Surge you eat a hit that no prayer blocks, and if you stand on the right one you take zero. Learn the four colors, learn which phase introduces glyphs and shadow walls, and learn that phase 3 ignores your protect prayers entirely. Master those three ideas and the contract scaling — solo, duo, trio, and the three pact tiers — becomes a pure loot-and-difficulty dial you turn up only when your mechanics are clean.

1. 🔥 Yama at a Glance — Overview, Requirements & Loot

Yama, the Temperate King of the Chasm of Fire, is a three-phase demon boss reached through the Varlamore firelands. Unlike the sprawling raid-style bosses, Yama is a contained, arena-based duel: one boss, one room, and a mechanic set that grows in complexity as his health drops. The reason so many players bounce off their first attempt is not the damage — it is the fact that every phase adds a new rule you must internalize, and the rules stack rather than replace each other. By phase 3 you are simultaneously managing color tiles, glyph reads, shadow-wall dodges, and prayer-piercing damage.

① Requirements & how to reach the arena

Before you can even stand in the room you need a few things squared away. The numbers below are the practical floor, not the theoretical minimum — bring more combat if you can.

RequirementValueWhy it matters
Prayer level85Unlocks the Chasm of Fire prayer gate and the Piety-tier uptime you need to out-sustain phase 3
Combat level110+ recommendedYama hits hard in phase 2 and 3; under-leveled accounts run out of food
Quest progressChildren of the Sun startedOpens the firelands travel route to the Chasm
Gear tierMid-tier melee + ranged swapSee gear requirements guide
Food15+ sharks or betterPhase 3 pierces prayer, so eating replaces praying

Travel is straightforward once unlocked: teleport to the firelands hub, run south into the Chasm, and step onto the summoning pad. From there you open the contract interface (covered next) and begin the fight. There is no dangerous wilderness or risk zone on the way, so you can repeatedly attempt Yama without losing your bank.

② Core stats & phase thresholds

Yama has 600 HP split across three phases at fixed percentage breaks. Knowing the exact breakpoints lets you plan your biggest hits and your safest positioning windows in advance.

PhaseHP rangeNew mechanic introducedPrimary threat
Phase 1100% → 67%Color tiles & Ward SurgeOff-style AoE if mistiled
Phase 267% → 33%Glyphs & Shadow WallsLine attacks + glyph bursts
Phase 333% → 0%Prayer-piercing BurnoutUnblockable chip damage

The thresholds are percentage-based, so they do not shift with contract scaling — a 600 HP solo fight and a 600 HP trio fight both flip phases at the same marks. What scales is the incoming damage, the loot multiplier, and the enrage of secondary attacks, which is exactly why the contract system is the lever you pull to make the fight harder and richer.

③ Loot & why players farm Yama

Yama drops a tight, high-value loot table built around fire- and prayer-themed gear. The headline uniques are the contracted weapons and the prayer-scaling armor pieces that pair naturally with the fight's 85 Prayer gate. Because the table is small, your expected value per kill is consistent and the grind feels less punishing than a wide, diluted drop table. The pact tier you choose (see section 2) directly multiplies unique drop rates, so the "best" Yama kill for profit is almost always a max-pact kill once your mechanics are solid.

💡 Farm Smart, Not Hard

If your goal is profit, do not jump to Supreme pact on day one. A clean Common-pact kill that you survive every time out-earns a Supreme-pact attempt where you die and lose the trip. Scale the pact up only as your death rate drops below roughly one in ten.

2. 📜 The Contract & Pact System (How Difficulty Scales)

The contract and pact system is Yama's version of difficulty dials, and it is the mechanic that lets one boss serve both casual learners and maxed speedrunners. Two independent axes exist: the contract size (how many players) and the pact tier (how punishing the fight is). Understanding both is the difference between a comfortable learn and a needless wipe.

① Contract size — solo, duo, trio

When you step on the summoning pad you choose how many players the contract is written for. Solo is the baseline; duo and trio scale Yama's effective HP and damage so the fight stays challenging with more people, while also improving the loot split per participant.

ContractYama HPDamage to each playerLoot per playerBest for
Solo600Full (you take it all)100% of tableLearners, speedrunners
Duo~840~70% per player~60% eachFriends, steady clears
Trio~1,050~55% per player~43% eachClans, tank splits

The key insight: duo and trio do not make the fight easier per se — they make the damage you personally eat lower because it is spread across bodies, which is a huge help for learning phase 3's prayer-piercing hits. If you are struggling solo, bring one friend before you crank the pact tier up.

② Pact tiers — Common, Greater, Supreme

The pact tier is the real difficulty dial. Each step up adds enrage: faster Ward Surges, more glyphs firing at once, thicker shadow walls, and a larger prayer-pierce component in phase 3. In exchange, unique drop rates climb sharply.

PactMechanic enrageUnique drop multiplierRecommended before
CommonNone (baseline)1.0×First 20 kills, learning
Greater+20% surge speed1.6×Clean phase 3, <10% death
Supreme+45% surge + extra glyph2.4×Consistent no-death clears

Our phase 3 prayer-pierce guide breaks down exactly how the Supreme enrage changes your eat timing, because the difference between Greater and Supreme is felt most in the final phase where you can no longer lean on Protect prayers.

③ How to pick your settings

The rule of thumb is boring but correct: start Common solo, learn the color logic, then move to Common duo for phase 3 confidence, then Greater solo, then Supreme only once you can clear Greater without dying. The pact multiplier is meaningless if you never bank the loot. Many players also run a "learning duo" at Common pact specifically to watch phase 3 patterns safely before attempting it solo.

⚠️ Don't Overscale Early

Supreme pact triples your chance of death compared to Common if your mechanics are shaky, and a dead attempt yields zero loot. The loot multiplier only pays out on completed kills — there is no consolation drop for a good fight that ends in a gravestone.

3. 🟥 Phase 1 (100–67%): Color Tiles & Ward Surge

Phase 1 is where Yama teaches you the language of the fight. The arena floor is divided into a grid of four colored tile types — Crimson, Azure, Verdant, and Amber — arranged in a repeating pattern. Yama periodically channels a Ward Surge: he becomes warded in one of those four colors, and a matching colored shockwave expands from his position. If you are standing on the tile that matches the active ward color, you take no damage; if you are on any other color, you eat a hit that ignores your protect prayer entirely.

① Reading the Ward Surge color

Yama telegraphs the surge in two ways: a colored ring forms around him (matching the safe tile color) and a chat/visual cue names the color a beat before it fires. The safe tile is always the same color as the ring around Yama, never the opposite. Players who instinctively dodge to "any other tile" consistently die here — you must move onto the matching color, not away from it.

Ring colorSafe tileWrong tilesTelegraph time
Crimson (red)Red tilesBlue/Green/Amber~2.5s
Azure (blue)Blue tilesRed/Green/Amber~2.5s
Verdant (green)Green tilesRed/Blue/Amber~2.5s
Amber (yellow)Amber tilesRed/Blue/Green~2.5s

At Common pact the surges are slow and predictable. At Supreme they fire faster and occasionally back-to-back in different colors, which is why footwork — not panic — is the skill to build now. Drill the color recognition in phase 1 so it is automatic by phase 3.

② Breaking the ward to deal damage

While Yama is warded you deal reduced damage to him, so the fight has a rhythm: survive the surge on the correct tile, then attack during the vulnerable window that opens immediately after the surge lands. The deeper mechanic (covered in our glyph & color mechanics guide) is that standing on the correct tile during the surge also "charges" your next hits, so good positioning directly increases your DPS — you are not just avoiding damage, you are enabling it.

③ Phase 1 positioning map

The diagram below shows the four-color tile layout and where Yama stands when he surges. Keep a tile of each color within one step so you can always reach the safe color without crossing a dangerous one.

💡 Phase 1 Habit to Build

After every surge, return to a neutral central tile rather than hugging a corner. Corners trap you when the next surge is the opposite color, and Yama loves to chain surges in phase 2. Central positioning keeps every color one step away.

4. 🟦 Phase 2 (67–33%): Glyphs & Shadow Walls

Phase 2 keeps everything from phase 1 and adds two new layers: glyphs that spawn on the floor and detonate after a delay, and shadow walls — vertical pillars of darkness that sweep across the arena on fixed lines. This is the phase where most deaths happen, because players who are still thinking about color tiles get caught flat-footed by a glyph burst or a shadow sweep.

① Glyph mechanics

Glyphs appear as glowing runes on specific tiles. Each glyph has a short fuse (about 3 seconds at Common pact) and then erupts in a small radius. Standing in the eruption deals damage that, like the Ward Surge, ignores protect prayers. The trick is that glyphs often spawn on the very color tile you want to stand on for the next surge, forcing a timing decision: do you take the surge hit to avoid the glyph, or reposition and accept the glyph?

Glyph typeFuseRadiusCounter
Fixed glyph3.0s1 tileWalk off before it pops
Chaining glyph2.5sSpreads to neighborLeave a 2-tile gap
Pulse glyph (Supreme)2.0s3×3 burstFull reposition, no shortcut

Our glyph & color guide has frame-by-frame timings for each pact tier. The headline takeaway: glyphs are a positioning puzzle layered on top of the color puzzle, and the only way to solve both is to keep moving with intent rather than camping one tile.

② Shadow walls

Shadow walls are the signature phase 2 threat. A wall of darkness spawns at one edge of the arena and sweeps horizontally or vertically across it over roughly 4 seconds. Any player standing in the wall's path when it arrives takes a heavy, prayer-ignoring hit and is briefly rooted. The counter is simple to say and hard to do under pressure: be on the correct color tile and outside the wall's lane.

③ Stacking the three threats

The hard part of phase 2 is that Yama will sometimes trigger a Ward Surge (color) while glyphs are live (positioning) and a shadow wall is mid-sweep (lane). When all three overlap, you prioritize in this order: (1) be on the correct color tile so the surge doesn't kill you, (2) be outside the shadow wall lane, (3) minimize glyph exposure as a secondary concern. You will eat some glyph chip damage in those moments — that is fine. Dying to the surge or the wall is not. Build this priority mentally in phase 2 so phase 3's added pressure doesn't overload you.

⚠️ Root = Death in Phase 3 Prep

The shadow wall roots you briefly. If you get rooted on the wrong color tile right before a surge, you cannot move to safety. Always keep one tile of buffer between you and the wall's leading edge so a root never traps you off-color.

5. 🟪 Phase 3 (33–0%): Prayer-Piercing & Burnout

Phase 3 is the reason Yama has a reputation. Everything from phases 1 and 2 is still active, and now Yama gains Burnout: a prayer-piercing attack that deals a flat chunk of damage no protect prayer can reduce. Your entire defensive plan shifts from "pray the right style" to "eat the chip and out-heal it." This is the phase our dedicated phase 3 guide covers in full, but the essentials belong on this page too.

① What prayer-piercing actually means

In phases 1 and 2, the only unblockable damage comes from standing on the wrong color tile, a glyph, or a shadow wall. In phase 3, Yama's Burnout attack ignores protect prayers entirely — even with the correct Protect prayer active, you take a set amount of damage. The number scales with pact tier: Common ~8 per Burnout, Greater ~12, Supreme ~16. That sounds small until you realize he casts it every few seconds and you are also still dodging surges, glyphs, and walls.

PactBurnout damageFrequencyEat strategy
Common~8Every ~5sSharks cover it easily
Greater~12Every ~4sMix in brews
Supreme~16Every ~3sBrews + restores, tight

Because Burnout is unblockable, your prayer points are now spent on offensive prayers (Piety/Augury/Rigour) and on blocking the non-piercing attacks, not on defense against Burnout itself. The defensive layer against Burnout is purely your food and your lifesteal if you bring it.

② The Burnout + surge overlap

The dangerous moment is when a Ward Surge (color) lands at the same time as a Burnout tick. You still must be on the correct tile or you take the surge hit on top of the Burnout. This is where duo and trio contracts shine: the damage is spread, so a single Burnout tick is a smaller fraction of your HP pool and you have more room to fix a footwork mistake. Solo Supreme phase 3 is genuinely one of the tighter DPS-check-meets-mechanics fights in the game.

③ Out-healing and the kill window

Phase 3 rewards aggression. Because you cannot pray away the Burnout, your best defense is to end the fight fast — more DPS means fewer Burnout ticks total. Bring your strongest weapon, keep Piety (or the style-appropriate offensive prayer) up, and prioritize damage during the vulnerable windows after each surge. Our full deep-dive includes a sample phase 3 rotation that banks the kill in under 60 seconds of phase 3 time on a clean Supreme solo.

💡 Phase 3 Food Math

At Supreme, ~16 Burnout every 3s = ~5.3 damage per second from that source alone, plus surge/glyph/wall mistakes. A shark heals 20. You can sustain roughly one shark every 4 seconds and stay even — so your DPS must be high enough that phase 3 ends before your inventory empties. Count your sharks before phase 3 starts.

6. ⚔️ Gear, Inventory & Positioning

Yama is a mixed-style fight, so your loadout is a balancing act between melee DPS for the vulnerable windows and enough ranged/magic swap to handle any phase where Yama forces a style change. The full slot-by-slot breakdown lives in our gear requirements guide; here is the condensed version.

② Inventory template

A standard Yama inventory is roughly: 16 sharks (or 10 sharks + 4 brews + 2 restores for Supreme), 1 teleport, 1 spec weapon (Dragon Claws or a DWH for defense drops), and the rest prayer potions or a holy wrench + prayer restore. Do not over-bring teleports — Yama is not a risk fight, so your one emergency teleport is just for leaving if you misclick, not for PK escape.

③ Positioning cheat sheet

Print this loop in your head: (1) read the surge ring color, (2) step onto that color, (3) attack in the vulnerable window, (4) check for glyphs and shadow walls, (5) return to center. Repeat for all three phases. The only thing that changes is that phase 2 adds glyph/wall awareness and phase 3 adds "eat the Burnout, don't pray it." If you internalize the five-step loop, the fight becomes a rhythm rather than a panic.

🧭 Central Tile Rule

Always reset to a central, neutral tile between mechanics. Corners are death traps when the next surge is the opposite color or a shadow wall sweeps your lane. Center keeps every color and every safe lane one step away.

7. ❓ FAQs — Yama 2026

What Prayer level do I need for Yama?

You need 85 Prayer to pass the Chasm of Fire gate and to sustain the offensive prayers that make phase 3 a kill rather than a stall. Below 85 you cannot enter at all, so treat 85 Prayer as a hard requirement, not a suggestion.

Which color tile is safe during a Ward Surge?

The safe tile is always the same color as the ring that forms around Yama before the surge fires. Red ring means stand on red tiles, blue ring means blue tiles, and so on. Do not dodge away from the color — move onto it. Standing on any other color takes an unblockable hit.

Can Protect prayers block the phase 3 Burnout?

No. Burnout is prayer-piercing by design — it deals a flat amount of damage regardless of which Protect prayer you have active. Your only defense against Burnout is food and, indirectly, killing Yama faster so fewer Burnout ticks land. This is why phase 3 shifts your prayer points toward offensive prayers instead of defense.

Is Yama easier solo or duo?

For learning, duo is easier because the damage is spread across two players, especially the prayer-piercing Burnout. For profit per hour once you are consistent, solo Common or solo Greater is usually better since you keep the full loot table. Scale to duo only while you are still dying in phase 3.

What does the pact tier change?

The pact tier (Common, Greater, Supreme) controls mechanic enrage — surge speed, number of simultaneous glyphs, shadow wall thickness, and Burnout size — while multiplying unique drop rates (1.0×, 1.6×, 2.4×). It does not change Yama's HP thresholds; those stay at 67% and 33% regardless of pact.

What is the best weapon for Yama?

A high-accuracy crush or slash melee weapon for the vulnerable windows — Abyssal Bludgeon or Inquisitor's Mace are the mid-tier favorites, with the Scythe of Vitur or Soulreaper Axe at the top end. Because phase 3 is a DPS race against Burnout, prioritize raw strength and accuracy over style-specific perks. See the gear guide for slot details.

How do glyphs and shadow walls interact?

Glyphs are stationary timed explosions on tiles; shadow walls are sweeping lanes of damage that root you. They frequently overlap with Ward Surges. Your priority is always: correct color tile first, out of the wall lane second, avoid glyphs third. Eating a little glyph chip is acceptable; dying to a surge or wall is not.

Why do I keep dying in phase 2?

Phase 2 deaths almost always come from treating it like phase 1 — players focus on color tiles and forget the new glyph and shadow wall layers. Practice reading the wall's lane early and keeping a buffer tile so a root never traps you off-color. Once glyph/wall awareness is automatic, phase 2 becomes manageable.

Is Yama worth farming in 2026?

Yes, for mid-to-high level accounts. The loot table is small and high-value, the unique drop multiplier scales hard with pact tier, and there is no risk route to the arena. Once your mechanics are clean at Greater pact, Supreme solo becomes one of the better focused boss moneymakers for a maxed-ish melee account.
