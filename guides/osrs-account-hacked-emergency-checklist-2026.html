Account Hacked? The 1-Hour Emergency Checklist (2026)

Updated August 19, 2026  |  15 min read  |  By Yanchun Wu

Someone just logged into your Old School RuneScape account. Every minute counts. This is the exact, minute-by-minute action list for the first 60 minutes after you realize you have been hijacked — the window where you can still lock the hacker out before your bank, bonds, and membership are gone.

You noticed the logout, the missing items, or the "your email was changed" email. Stop scrolling — start the clock. This checklist is about containment in the first hour, not the full restoration process. Pair it with our OSRS Account Hijack Prevention Guide so this never happens again, and keep our After-the-Hack Recovery Walkthrough open for the steps that come once the hacker is locked out.

⏱️ Quick Summary — 30-Second Read

  • 📌 End every session from jagex.com account settings — this boots the hacker off your account instantly, even if they changed the password.
  • 📌 Change the password on both your Jagex account AND the linked email — a hacker who still owns your email owns the reset link.
  • 📌 Reclaim or remove the Authenticator — 2FA is the lock the hacker needs next; recover it before they lock you out permanently.
  • 📌 File the Jagex recovery ticket now — recovery is manual and queued; the earlier you submit, the earlier you get the account back.

1. 🔥 Why the First Hour Is the Only Hour That Matters

When a hijacker gets into your account, they are not browsing — they are executing. The typical OSRS account theft plays out in a predictable sequence: log in, move or liquidate valuables, strip the bank, buy and convert bonds to GP (or vice versa), change the registered email, attach their own Authenticator, then log out and leave you a cleaned shell. Most of that can happen in well under an hour if the account has no Bank PIN or 2FA protecting the valuables.

The reason this guide exists is simple: you can win the account back after the hour, but you usually cannot win the items back. Jagex can and does restore account access, but item and GP restoration is limited, slow, and never guaranteed. Your job in minute one is not to recover — it is to interrupt the hijacker before the liquidation completes. Every step below is designed to pull the rug out from under an active intruder.

① The hacker's timeline vs. yours

Understand what you are racing. A skilled hijacker who already has your password and an open session will often: (1) immediately change the password so you cannot log back in, (2) within minutes attempt to change the registered email, (3) attach their own Authenticator to lock you out of recovery, and (4) spend the next 20–40 minutes emptying the bank and buying bonds. If you act in the first 5–10 minutes, you can often catch them mid-step — and the "End all sessions" button is your single most powerful tool because it kills their active login regardless of what password they set.

② What you can and cannot undo

Be realistic so you spend the hour wisely. You can almost always regain account access through Jagex recovery, even if the email and Authenticator were changed. You cannot always get the GP and items back — Jagex's rollback and item-return policy is narrow and reserved for specific loss types. So the hour is about access and interruption, and the days after are about restoration. Keep our After-the-Hack Recovery Walkthrough ready for that second phase.

💡 The One Button That Beats a Password Change

In your Jagex account settings there is an option to "Log out of all devices" / "End all sessions." This does not require you to know the new password — it forces every active login (including the hacker's) to re-authenticate. If you can reach account settings at all, hit this first. It is the fastest way to flip the board.

2. ⏱️ Minutes 0–5: Stop the Bleeding

Do not panic-post on Reddit yet. The first five minutes are purely about cutting the hacker's access and resetting the keys. Order matters.

① Change your Jagex account password immediately

Go to jagex.com → Account Settings → Sign-in & Security → Change password. If you are already logged in, do it from there. If you have been logged out and the password was changed, skip to the recovery ticket (Section 6) — but first try the "End all sessions" path described below. A fresh password does two things: it stops the hacker from setting a new one that sticks, and combined with session termination it forces them to start over from zero knowledge.

Use a password you have never used on this account or any related gaming account. Reusing an old password is how accounts get re-hijacked weeks later. Aim for 14+ characters; a short passphrase like correct-horse-battery-osrs style strings beats a complex 8-character password every time.

② End every active session

In the same Sign-in & Security area, look for "Manage devices" or "Log out everywhere." Click it. This invalidates every current login token — desktop client, mobile app, and the hacker's session. Even if the hacker changed the password seconds ago, ending sessions can still boot them if your recovery/login still holds. This is the single highest-impact action in the entire hour.

③ Write down exactly what you saw

Before you start changing things, take 60 seconds to record evidence: the time you noticed, what was missing, any emails from Jagex (subject lines and timestamps), and the worlds/servers you were on. Jagex support asks for a clear timeline, and "I noticed around 9pm" is far weaker than "I was logged out at 21:14, received the email-change notice at 21:16." Screenshots of the missing items or the email notices belong in your recovery ticket later.

⚠️ Do NOT click "reset password" links in emails

If you got an email saying your account was changed, that email is fine to read — but never click "recover your account" buttons inside unexpected emails. Hijackers send phishing copies that look identical. Always type jagex.com into your browser manually. Jagex will never ask for your password by email, and real recovery is done through the official account-recovery form only.

3. 🔐 Minutes 5–15: Lock Down 2FA / Authenticator

Two-factor authentication is the lock the hacker needs next. If they already attached their own Authenticator, your window to block permanent lockout is narrow — but recoverable. Here is the decision tree.

① If YOUR Authenticator is still attached

Great — that means the hacker likely could not pass 2FA, which is probably why you are reading this. Keep it. Open Sign-in & Security → Authenticator and confirm the device listed is yours. If you have backup codes saved (see our Authenticator Migration & Recovery Guide), store them somewhere safer than your desktop. Consider adding a second trusted device so a lost phone never strands you.

② If the hacker attached THEIR Authenticator

You will see an Authenticator you do not recognize, or you will be unable to log in because it demands a code you cannot generate. Do not try to guess codes. Instead, open the account recovery request and select the option that you cannot access your Authenticator. Jagex will verify your identity through original account details (creation date, historical passwords, payment/transaction IDs) and, once approved, strip the unauthorized Authenticator. This is exactly the scenario our Authenticator lost-access recovery guide covers in full — but the emergency version is: submit recovery now, do not keep guessing, and stop trying to log in (failed attempts can trigger lockouts that slow you down).

③ Verify the recovery codes are yours

Modern Jagex accounts issue one-time backup/recovery codes when you enable 2FA. If you still have access, regenerate and download them to a password manager or printed paper kept offline. If the hacker already regenerated them, that is another sign they are deeper in than you thought — escalate to a recovery ticket immediately. Recovery codes are the difference between a 10-minute fix and a 5-day wait, so treat them like the spare key they are.

4. 💳 Minutes 15–25: Check Email & Payment Methods

The registered email is the skeleton key to every game account you own. If the hacker owns it, they own your reset links, your recovery confirmations, and your other accounts too. This is also where unauthorized charges show up.

① Confirm the registered email is still yours

Log into your Jagex account settings and check the registered email. If it still shows your address, change its password anyway (next subsection) and move on. If it shows an address you do not recognize, the hacker changed it — that triggers a mandatory change-notice email and usually a short cooling-off window. Immediately open a recovery ticket; Jagex can revert an unauthorized email change once your identity is confirmed. Do not "confirm" anything from the hacker's notification email.

② Change the email password + enable email 2FA

Even if the registered email was not changed, assume the hacker has seen it. Change that email's password now and turn on its own two-factor authentication (Google, Outlook, etc. all support it). A Jagex account protected by 2FA is useless to a hacker who can still read your email and intercept the reset code — so the email itself must be the hardest door. This single step prevents the "second hit" more than any other.

③ Scan payment methods and recent charges

Open Account Settings → Payment & Billing (or the equivalent Jagex Store billing page). Look for: newly added payment methods, bonds purchased you did not buy, membership applied to the wrong account, or "free trial" conversions. Unauthorized bond purchases are the most common monetization path for hijackers because bonds are liquid — they buy bonds, trade them to a mule, and cash out. Note every transaction ID and timestamp; Jagex support will ask for them in the recovery ticket.

💡 Where the "email changed" notice lives

Jagex sends a confirmation email to both the old and new address when the registered email is changed. If you still have the old inbox, that message contains the change request and often a way to flag it as unauthorized. Save it — the subject line and timestamp are exactly what the recovery team wants to see.

5. 🏦 Minutes 25–40: Secure the Bank & Valuables

You may or may not still have access to the character at this point. If you do, act fast. If you do not, this section becomes "what to ask Jagex to check" — but the Bank PIN is your secret weapon.

① If you can log in: move nothing, change the PIN

If you managed to boot the hacker and get in, do not start shuffling items around — that can look like the hijacker's own activity and complicate recovery. Instead, open your bank, go to Bank PIN settings, and either set a fresh PIN or confirm the existing one is intact. Critically, make sure "Require a 7-day waiting period before the PIN can be changed or deleted" is ENABLED. A Bank PIN with the 7-day delay is the single best theft deterrent in the game: a hijacker who gets back in cannot remove or change it for a week, which is usually longer than they are willing to wait.

② If you cannot log in: rely on the 7-day PIN

This is why the 7-day Bank PIN delay exists. If you had it enabled before the hijack, the hacker cannot strip your bank layout or disable the PIN quickly — any attempt to delete or change the PIN starts a 7-day countdown that you (or Jagex) can cancel. If you never set one, treat this as the lesson for next time and read our Bank PIN Delay Window Guide the moment you recover access. For now, note in your ticket that no PIN was set, so support knows the bank was fully exposed.

③ List what is missing for the recovery ticket

If the bank was already emptied, build the most accurate list you can: approximate GP lost, notable items (with quantities), and any bonds. You do not need perfect numbers — Jagex can see the account's history — but a clear, honest list helps the recovery team assess. Do not inflate the claim; false item reports slow legitimate recoveries for everyone. Keep the list in the same document as your timeline evidence from Section 2.

⚠️ Do not "trade items to a friend for safety" right now

Moving wealth to another account during an active hijack can look like RWT (real-world trading) or muling and can get both accounts flagged. If you truly still have access and want to protect items, the Bank PIN with 7-day delay is safer than shuffling to a separate account. Let Jagex's recovery process handle item questions.

6. ☎️ Minutes 40–55: Contact Jagex the Right Way

Recovery is a manual, queued process. The earlier and cleaner your ticket, the earlier you get the account back. Most delays come from vague tickets and missing proof — so do this properly.

① Open the official account recovery request

Go to jagex.com/account-recovery (or "Can't access your account?" on the login page). Choose the option that matches your situation — usually "I think my account has been hijacked" or "I cannot access my Authenticator." Fill in the original account details, not what the hacker changed them to. Original creation date, original password(s), original email, and any historical payment/transaction IDs are the strongest proof you have.

② Provide transaction IDs, not just "I paid once"

If you ever bought membership, bonds, or Runecoins, find the transaction ID from your email receipts or bank statement and paste it into the ticket. Jagex weights verifiable purchase history very heavily in recovery decisions. A single transaction ID can be the difference between approval and denial. If you used a third-party reseller, the ID from your payment provider (PayPal, credit card) still helps.

③ Explain the hijack clearly and briefly

Write a calm, factual summary: when you noticed, what changed (email, Authenticator, items), and that you have already ended sessions and changed passwords where possible. Attach your timeline and missing-items list. Do not threaten, do not spam, and do not submit ten tickets — multiple tickets on the same account can merge or deprioritize. One complete ticket is better than five frantic ones. Our Suspicious Activity & Locked Account Guide explains the lock states you might see while waiting.

④ Ignore "recovery services" that DM you

Within minutes of posting about a hack, you will get messages offering to "recover your account for a fee" or "boost your recovery priority." These are almost always scams that steal more from you. Jagex does not sell priority recovery, and no third party can access Jagex's systems. Only the official recovery form works. If your ticket was denied, follow our Recovery Denied — How to Reapply guide rather than paying a stranger.

7. 🛡️ Minutes 55–60: Prevent the Second Hit

You may have stopped this hijack, but the cause is usually still on your machine or in your habits. Spend the last five minutes (and the next day) closing the door the hacker walked through.

① Scan your devices for malware and keyloggers

Most OSRS hijacks start with a keylogger, a fake client, or a "free GP/bot" tool. Run a full antivirus scan on every device you use to play. Remove any unofficial RuneScape clients, "item generators," or "macro" software — these are the most common infection vectors. If you typed your password on an infected machine, the new password is already compromised, so change it again after the scan comes back clean.

② Enable 2FA everywhere that matters

Turn on two-factor authentication for: your Jagex account, your email, your password manager, and any linked payment account. Use an authenticator app (not SMS where avoidable — SIM-swap attacks are real). If you have multiple OSRS accounts, our Multi-Account 2FA Management Guide shows how to track them all without losing your mind or your codes.

③ Review where you typed that password

Think back: did you log in through a link from a Discord "giveaway," a "survey," or a "trade confirmation" site? Those are phishing pages that captured your credentials. Bookmark jagex.com and only ever log in there. Add a unique, strong password per service so one leak never cascades. Our Anti-Scam & Phishing Guide lists the exact fake-app and fake-site patterns circulating in 2026.

💡 The 60-minute mark is not "done"

You have contained the bleeding. You have not finished. The recovery ticket can take days, and item restoration is separate and limited. Use the waiting time to harden every account you own — the second hit is far more common than players expect, and it almost always uses the same open door.

8. 📋 The Post-Hour Recovery Roadmap

With the hour behind you, the work shifts from interruption to restoration. Here is the order of operations for the next few days, and where each step lives.

① Watch the recovery ticket

Check the email you used on the recovery form (not necessarily the registered game email) for Jagex's response. Reply only through the official channel, and only if they ask for more proof. Do not "bump" the ticket daily — that does not speed it up and can push it down the queue.

② Expect a lock, then access

Once approved, Jagex typically returns the account to you with the unauthorized email and Authenticator stripped, and a temporary password. Log in, set a fresh password, re-add your Authenticator, and regenerate backup codes. Then immediately enable the 7-day Bank PIN delay (Section 5) so a future hijack hits a wall.

③ Pursue item restoration the right way

If items or GP were lost, there is a separate process — usually a "missing items" or "rollback" request — with strict eligibility. It is not guaranteed and is not the same as account recovery. Our After-the-Hack Recovery Walkthrough covers exactly what Jagex will and will not restore, and how to phrase the request so it is not auto-denied.

④ Harden for good

After recovery, treat the account as "burned once" and over-protect it: unique password, app-based 2FA, 7-day Bank PIN delay, secured email, and a malware-free machine. Run through our Prevention Guide checklist one more time as a final gate before you log in for normal play.

9. ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

I was logged out and the password was changed — can I still act in the first hour?

Yes. If you can still reach the Jagex account-recovery or "log out everywhere" flow, you can interrupt the hacker even without the new password. The moment you cannot access settings at all, shift straight to filing the recovery ticket (Section 6) — that is still "acting in the first hour" and timestamps your claim early, which helps.

The hacker changed my registered email. Is the account gone?

No. An unauthorized email change can be reverted by Jagex once your identity is confirmed through the recovery process. The change also usually sends a notice to your old inbox — save it as evidence. File recovery immediately and explicitly state the registered email was changed without your permission.

They attached their own Authenticator. Can I get back in?

Yes, via the account recovery request selecting "cannot access my Authenticator." Jagex verifies your original account details and strips the unauthorized 2FA. Do not keep guessing codes — that wastes time and can trigger lockouts. Our Authenticator lost-access recovery guide walks through the exact form steps.

Will Jagex give my items and GP back?

Sometimes, but not automatically and not always. Item/GP restoration is a separate, narrow process with strict eligibility — it is not the same as getting the account back. That is exactly why the first hour focuses on interruption. For the restoration path and what Jagex will not return, see the After-the-Hack Recovery Walkthrough.

How long does Jagex account recovery take?

It varies with queue length and proof quality — from under a day with solid transaction IDs to over a week when details are thin or conflicting. Strong original details plus a transaction ID is the fastest route. Submitting multiple tickets does not speed it up.

Should I buy bonds back or just wait?

Do not buy replacements expecting Jagex to refund them. Wait for the recovery outcome first. If items are restored, duplicates you bought are yours to keep but were unnecessary spend. If they are not restored, buying more does not improve your case — it just costs you more.

My Bank PIN had the 7-day delay on. Did that help?

Likely yes. With the 7-day delay enabled, any attempt by the hacker to change or delete the PIN starts a week-long countdown they cannot short-circuit, which protects your bank layout and items longer than they usually stay interested. If you did not have it, enable it the instant you recover — see the Bank PIN Delay Window Guide.

Someone DMed me offering to recover my account for a fee. Real?

No. These are recovery scams that take more money and often your remaining credentials. Jagex does not sell priority recovery and no third party can touch Jagex's systems. Only the official jagex.com/account-recovery form works. If a ticket was denied, use the reapply guide instead of paying anyone.

What if I play on mobile and cannot reach a browser in time?

Use your phone's browser, not the game app, to go to jagex.com and end sessions / start recovery. The mobile app itself cannot change account-security settings, so a browser is required regardless. Our Mobile Security & Fake-App Guide shows how hijackers target mobile players specifically.

After I recover, what is the single most important thing to set up?

In order: a unique strong password, app-based 2FA on the Jagex account and email, and the 7-day Bank PIN delay. The PIN delay is the cheapest, highest-impact anti-theft setting in the game and the one most players skip. Do it before you log in for normal play again.
