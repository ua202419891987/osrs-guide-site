OSRS Quest Requirement Wall 2026 — Plan DS2, Song of the Elves & Rigour

Updated August 19, 2026  |  15 min read  |  By Yanchun Wu

The "requirement wall" is the gate every mid-game account eventually hits: a famous unlock you want, blocked by a chain of quests and stats you never trained. Here is how to plan it in advance so you never stall halfway.

🗺️ Quest Planning Series · Total Level 1500+ · 18 min read · Updated for 2026
🛡️ Level: 1500+

At some point every OSRS account hits a "requirement wall" — a famous unlock you desperately want, but it is gated behind a chain of other quests and skills you never trained. The three most common walls for established accounts are Dragon Slayer II (unlocks the Vorkath money-maker and the Twisted Bow progression), Song of the Elves (unlocks Prifddinas, the best all-round training hub in the game), and Rigour (the best Ranged prayer, locked behind 70 Prayer plus a long Camelot-style chain). This guide shows you how to plan the wall in advance so you never get stuck halfway. If you are still mapping out your first quests, start with our best quests for new members before tackling the gates below.

The wall is rarely about talent or even about time. It is almost always about sequencing. Players who train blindly often reach level 80+ in their combat stats, then discover they cannot touch the content they wanted because one forgotten quest — usually buried at the end of a long chain — is missing. The fix is not to grind harder; it is to map the dependency tree before you invest a single training hour, then weave the required quests and stats into the progression you were already doing. Do that, and you arrive at each gate already qualified.

⚡ Quick Verdict — The 30-Second Read

  • 📌 The wall is a planning failure, not a hard cap — map prerequisites from day one and you never stall.
  • 📌 About 80% of the pain comes from 20% of prerequisites: high Prayer, high Construction/Crafting, and the long Elf and Fremennik chains.
  • 📌 Budget 90–120M GP for the three gates; almost all of it goes to Prayer (70), Construction (70), and Crafting (70).
  • 📌 Do Dragon Slayer II first — Vorkath funds the rest of the wall.
  • 📌 Use quest XP lamps to push expensive 70s; quests are the highest XP-per-hour content in the game.

1. 🧱 What Is the Quest Requirement Wall?

In OSRS, quests are not isolated stories — they form a dependency tree. Dragon Slayer II requires you to have finished Dragon Slayer I, partially completed the Fremennik quest line, and trained a pile of high stats. Song of the Elves requires the entire Elf quest line plus 70 in almost every skill. Rigour needs 70 Prayer and a long chain of Camelot-related quests. When you look at any single "big" unlock, you are really looking at the tip of an iceberg: dozens of smaller prerequisites that must be satisfied first, most of which you will only discover by reading the requirement list after the fact.

The wall is not a hard cap — it is a planning failure. If you know the prerequisites from day one, you can weave the required quests and stats into your normal progression and arrive at the gate already qualified. Think of it like the planning behind a long skill route: the fastest path is the one you mapped before you started, not the one you improvise while standing at the locked door. The players who clear the wall painlessly are not stronger or richer — they simply started the chain earlier, when the requirements were cheap.

This matters more in 2026 than it did years ago, because the "endgame" unlocks have multiplied. Prifddinas, Rigour, Augury, the Twisted Bow path, the various boss teleports, and the elite diary rewards all sit behind overlapping prerequisite trees. A single well-planned account can unlock most of them with one efficient sweep of the quest log; a poorly planned one ends up re-grinding stats it could have trained for free via quest lamps months earlier. The difference between those two accounts is almost entirely the document you are reading now.

💡 The 80/20 of the Wall

About 80% of wall pain comes from 20% of prerequisites: high Prayer, high Crafting/Construction, and the long Elf and Fremennik chains. Handle those early and the rest is trivial. Everything else — the combat stats, the gathering skills — tends to arrive naturally as you play.

2. 🚪 The Big Three Gates: DS2, Song of the Elves, Rigour

Most accounts that hit the wall are chasing one of three unlocks. Understanding exactly what each one demands is the first half of planning — the second half is ordering them so their prerequisites overlap. Below is the full requirement list for each, so you can audit your account against it today.

🐉 Dragon Slayer II

DS2 is the gateway to Vorkath (one of the best money makers in the game) and the Twisted Bow progression. It is also one of the most quest-heavy unlocks in OSRS. Key requirements:

  • Completion of Dragon Slayer I, Dream Mentor, Lunar Diplomacy, The Fremennik Exiles, Horror from the Deep, The Eyes of Glouphrie, and more.
  • Skills: 200 quest points and 70+ in a long list of stats — Attack, Strength, Defence, Magic, Hitpoints, Ranged, Prayer, Crafting, Smithing, Mining, Herblore, Agility, Thieving, Slayer, Construction, Hunter, and Fishing.
  • A Dragon hunter lance or a strong ranged setup for the fight itself (the lance is also obtainable during the quest).

The good news: the DS2 chain overlaps heavily with the early game. Most of its sub-quests are ones you would want anyway for XP and unlocks, so the "cost" of DS2 is mostly just reaching the 70 skill floors.

🧝 Song of the Elves

Song of the Elves unlocks Prifddinas, the best skilling city in the game, plus the Trahaearn mine, the crystal teleport seed, and a pile of training shortcuts. Key requirements:

  • The full Elf quest line: Regicide, Roving Elves, Mourning's End Part I & II, Within the Light, and the entire Plague's End chain.
  • Skills: 70+ in 13 different skills (Smithing, Crafting, Construction, Agility, and more at 70). This is the notorious wall inside the wall.

Song of the Elves is where most players stall, because the 13-skill floor forces you to train Construction and Crafting — two of the most expensive skills — right at the end if you left them for last. The planning lesson is obvious: never leave Construction and Crafting to the end.

🏹 Rigour

Rigour is the best Ranged prayer in the game (it boosts both accuracy and damage at every Ranged boss and Slayer task). Requirements:

  • 70 Prayer (expensive — plan your GP early).
  • Completion of King's Ransom, the Knight Waves training ground, and Preferences (which needs Holy Grail as well).
  • Also requires 70 Defence and several Camelot-related quests.

Rigour is the cheapest of the three in skill terms (only Prayer and Defence at 70) but the most GP-hungry per skill, because Prayer 70 is one of the most expensive single levels in the game. If you are an Ironman, the bones for Prayer 70 are a project in themselves — start Chaos Altar runs early.

⚠️ Don't Rush the 70s Blind

Song of the Elves needs 70 in 13 skills. If you leave Construction and Crafting to last, you will be stuck with two of the most expensive skills as your final gate, right when you most want to be done. Train them gradually alongside your GP-making from the very start of the wall.

3. ✅ Pre-Quest Skill & Quest Checklist

Use this checklist to audit your account before you start the wall. Tick everything off and the three gates open smoothly. The fastest way to fill the "must-have quests" column is to work through our best quests per skill, which links each skill floor to the quests that train it for free.

Gate Must-Have Quests Key Skill Floors
Dragon Slayer II DS1, Dream Mentor, Lunar Diplomacy, Fremennik Exiles, Horror from the Deep, The Eyes of Glouphrie 200 QP, 70s across 15+ skills
Song of the Elves Regicide, Roving Elves, Mourning's End I & II, Plague's End, Within the Light 70 in 13 skills
Rigour King's Ransom, Preferences, Knights' Waves, Holy Grail 70 Prayer, 70 Defence

Quest Points (QP): Several wall quests require a high QP total (DS2 needs 200). Do the easy QP quests early — they are also free XP and often hand you useful unlocks. Our ranked quest rewards guide shows which low-effort quests give the most QP and XP, so you can hit 200 QP almost as a side effect of normal play.

How to audit honestly: open your quest list and your stats panel side by side. For each gate, write down the gap — "Song of the Elves needs Construction 61→70, Crafting 58→70, Agility 64→70." Then total the GP and time those gaps represent. Most players underestimate the wall by 30–40% because they only count the famous quest, not the quiet stats behind it. The checklist forces you to see the whole tree.

4. ⚖️ The Three Gates Compared (GP, Time, Priority)

Not all gates are equal. DS2 is the best return on effort because it pays for itself via Vorkath. Song of the Elves is the widest utility unlock. Rigour is the smallest skill grind but the heaviest GP sink per level. This comparison table tells you what to do first and why.

Gate Approx. GP Cost Time to Skill Floors Payoff Do It…
Dragon Slayer II ~20–40M (mostly supplies) Medium (long quest chain) Vorkath + Twisted Bow path First — it funds the rest
Song of the Elves ~60–80M (Constr + Craft) Long (13× 70s) Prifddinas, best hub Second — widest utility
Rigour ~40–60M (Prayer 70) Short (only Prayer/Def) Best Ranged prayer Any time after Prayer 70

The pattern is consistent: do the gate that pays you back first. DS2's Vorkath is a 1–2M GP/hour boss on a well-geared account, which means the 20–40M you "spent" to qualify is recouped in a few weeks of casual bossing — and then it keeps paying. Song of the Elves is slower to pay back in raw GP but saves you time on every future training session through Prifddinas shortcuts. Rigour is pure combat power with no GP return, so it slots in whenever your Prayer happens to hit 70.

💡 One Number to Remember

If you only remember one thing: bank 90–120M GP before you start. That single buffer is the difference between a wall you clear in a few months and a wall you stall on for a year because you ran out of cash at Construction 68.

5. 🧭 Recommended Quest Order (Least Resistance)

The smartest order front-loads the long chains so their sub-requirements overlap. This is the path most efficient ironmen and mains use, because it turns "three separate walls" into "one shared climb."

  1. Early game: Waterfall Quest, Knight's Sword, Tree Gnome Village, The Grand Tree. These give a huge combat and QP head start for almost no effort.
  2. Fremennik chain: Fremennik Trials → Heroes' Quest → Fremennik Isles → Fremennik Exiles. This feeds DS2 and also hands useful unlocks along the way.
  3. Elf chain: Big Chompy Bird Hunting → Temple of Ikov → Regicide → Roving Elves → Mourning's End I & II → Within the Light → Plague's End. This feeds Song of the Elves.
  4. Camelot chain: Merlin's Crystal → Holy Grail → King's Ransom → Knights' Waves → Preferences. This feeds Rigour.
  5. Finish the three gates once your skill floors are met, in the DS2 → Song of the Elves → Rigour priority from Section 4.
💡 Overlap Your Chains

Many quests appear in two chains (for example, Temple of Ikov is both a QP quest and an Elf-chain step). Doing them in the right sequence means one quest counts toward multiple gates at once — you are not doing extra work, you are doing the same work in the order that unlocks the most.

The reason to finish DS2 before Song of the Elves is purely economic: Vorkath is the income engine that pays for Construction and Crafting. If you reversed the order, you would be grinding the most expensive 70s with no premium boss income behind you, which is exactly how accounts stall. Rigour can come last because its only real gate is Prayer 70, which you will have trained anyway to support the other two.

6. 💪 Efficient Training & GP Budget to Clear the Wall

The skill floors are where most players burn out. Here is how to hit each 70 efficiently without over-grinding, and how to fund it. For the full 1–99 routes behind each floor, our complete skill training guide covers every method.

The expensive 70s

  • Prayer 70: Gilded Altar + Dragon bones, or Chaos Altar (Wilderness) for cheaper XP. Budget ~40–60M GP. On an Ironman, this means hundreds of Chaos Altar runs.
  • Construction 70: Mahogany homes / oak dungeon doors. Budget ~30–50M GP. The fastest method (oak dungeon doors at the servant) is also the most GP-efficient.
  • Crafting 70: Glassblowing or dragonhide bodies. Budget ~10–20M GP. The cheapest of the three by a wide margin.

The free / profitable 70s

  • Agility 70: Rooftop courses — slow but free, and the shortcuts pay off forever.
  • Mining / Smithing 70: Motherlode Mine + Blast Furnace. Both can be near-profit or profitable depending on prices.
  • Hunter / Thieving 70: Birdhouses + Pyramid Plunder. Birdhouses are semi-passive and stack with herb runs.

Make your GP from Slayer, Vorkath (post-DS2), and bossing while training the free skills. The wall is mostly a GP problem disguised as a skill problem — keep your bank growing and the stats take care of themselves. Our 90+ skill planning guide shows how to sequence the high skills so the expensive 70s arrive exactly when you need them, not years late.

📋 GP Buffer Rule

Before starting the wall, bank at least 90–120M GP. That covers Prayer + Construction + Crafting to 70 with margin for supplies, temple repairs, and the inevitable price moves. Without it, the wall stalls — and stalled walls are how players quit mid-game accounts.

7. 🎁 Using Quest XP Rewards to Skip Grinds

Many wall quests hand out large XP lamps. Used strategically, they can push a skill to its 70 floor without a single manual training hour — turning a 2M-GP grind into free XP. The players who clear the wall fastest are the ones who check the XP reward table before they ever click a training method.

  • Recipe for Disaster (sub-quests): Multiple lamps — great for Prayer or Herblore, two of the most expensive skills.
  • The Fremennik Exiles: Gives XP in several stats and is a hard DS2 requirement anyway, so it doubles as progress and free levels.
  • Plague's End: A huge XP reward that can push a lagging skill over 70 in one quest.
  • Lunar Diplomacy / Dream Mentor: Magic and other lamps, plus they gate DS2.

Always check the OSRS Wiki "Quest Experience Rewards" table before training a skill manually. Sometimes one quest gives you the last 50k XP you need for free. This is the same synergy principle behind efficient route building — quests are the highest-XP-per-hour content in the game, and they cost nothing but the time to play them.

💡 Lamp Order Matters

Save your biggest lamps for your most expensive or slowest skill (usually Prayer or Construction). Don't waste a 15k lamp on a skill you can train profitably like Mining or Thieving — those will hit 70 on their own through money-making. Put the free XP where it saves you the most GP.

8. 🚫 Common Mistakes That Stall the Wall

Knowing what not to do is half the battle. These are the five mistakes that turn a planned 3-month wall into a year-long stall, drawn from the accounts that quit right at the door.

① Leaving Construction and Crafting for last

Song of the Elves needs both at 70. Train them in the background from the moment you start the wall, funded by boss income. Players who leave them to the end face the most expensive skills as their final gate, with no income engine yet running.

② Not banking the GP buffer first

The wall is a GP problem. Starting it with an empty bank means you halt at Prayer 60 or Construction 65 and can't continue. Bank 90–120M first; everything else flows from having that cushion.

③ Ignoring Quest Points until DS2

DS2 needs 200 QP. If you only start caring about QP at the gate, you face a scramble through low-value quests. Weave the easy QP quests into your early game and 200 arrives naturally.

④ Wasting lamps on profitable skills

A 15k XP lamp on Mining is wasted — Mining would have hit 70 while you made money. Put lamps on Prayer and Construction, where they save real GP.

⑤ Quitting the chain at the boring middle

The Elf and Fremennik chains have slow middle quests (Mourning's End, Temple of Ikov). Players who stop there never reach the payoff. Push through — the gate is closer than the boredom suggests.

⚠️ The Stall Is Quiet

Unlike a death or a ban, a stalled wall gives no dramatic warning. You just stop logging in because "the next step is too expensive." The GP buffer and background training exist precisely to remove that moment. Build them before you feel the stall, not after.

9. ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Can I do DS2 before Song of the Elves?

Yes. DS2 and Song of the Elves share almost no direct prerequisites, so you can do either first. Most players do DS2 first because Vorkath (unlocked by DS2) is a top GP maker that funds the expensive 70s needed for Song of the Elves. The order in this guide reflects that logic.

How much total GP do I need for the whole wall?

Roughly 90–120M GP for the three gates, almost all of it spent on Prayer (70), Construction (70), and Crafting (70). Combat stats and the free gathering skills cost little. If you boss or do Vorkath along the way, you can self-fund most of it and the real cash outlay drops toward the lower end.

Is Rigour worth the 70 Prayer grind?

For any Ranged account, absolutely. Rigour adds a large accuracy and strength boost that improves DPS at every Ranged boss and Slayer task. It is one of the highest-impact unlocks in the game and pays for itself through faster kills and fewer supplies wasted on failed attempts.

I'm an Ironman — does the plan change?

The quest order is identical, but you must self-supply the GP for Prayer and Construction, which is much harder without the GE. Prioritize farming bones (Chaos Altar runs) and teak/mahogany early, and treat the wall as a longer, self-sufficient project. The skill floors are the same; only the economy differs.

What's the single biggest mistake players make?

Leaving Construction and Crafting to last, then discovering Song of the Elves is gated behind two of the most expensive skills right at the finish line. Train them in the background from the start and the wall is a short final step instead of a wall inside a wall.

How do quest lamps fit into the plan?

Lamps are free XP you should point at your most expensive skills (Prayer, Construction). Check the Wiki quest XP table before manually training anything — one quest can hand you the last chunk of a 70 for zero GP. Never waste a big lamp on a skill you already train profitably.

Do I need 200 Quest Points, and how do I get them?

DS2 requires 200 QP. The easy route is to weave the high-QP, low-effort quests (Waterfall, Tree Gnome Village, The Grand Tree, Fight Arena, etc.) into your early game. By the time you reach the DS2 chain you will likely already be there, with no scramble required.

Can I skip Song of the Elves if I only care about bossing?

You can, but Prifddinas saves time on almost every future grind (Trahaearn mine, crystal teleport, training shortcuts), so skipping it trades a one-time cost for permanent inefficiency. Most bossers still do it eventually — better to fold it into the wall now than redo the chain later.

How long does the whole wall take in practice?

For a player who banks the GP buffer and overlaps the phases, roughly 2–4 months of casual play. For a player who stalls on Construction or runs out of GP, it stretches to a year or more. The timeline is almost entirely a function of planning, not of raw playtime.

10. 🏁 Final Tips — Clearing the Wall Without Pain

  • Map it: Write down all prerequisites for DS2, Song of the Elves, and Rigour before you train anything.
  • Bank 90–120M GP first: The wall is a GP problem more than a skill problem.
  • Overlap chains: Many quests count toward two gates — do them in the right sequence.
  • Train Construction & Crafting early: Never leave the expensive 70s to the end.
  • Use quest lamps: Save big lamps for Prayer/Construction, your most expensive 70s.
  • Do DS2 early: Vorkath funds the rest of the wall.
  • Weave in QP quests: Hit 200 QP as a side effect of normal play.
  • Keep phases parallel: Bank GP, run chains, and train 70s at the same time.
