OSRS Raid Group Finding & Communication Guide 2026

Updated August 19, 2026  |  24 min read  |  By Yanchun Wu

Raids are the most rewarding PvM in Old School RuneScape, but they are also the most social. This guide shows you how to find a team, speak the same language as your group, take a clear role, and — just as importantly — stay in the team instead of getting kicked.

⚔️ Raid Teamwork · Group PvM · 18 min read · Updated for 2026
🛡️ Level: 90+

Solo bosses test your mechanics. Raids test your teamwork. The three endgame raids — Chambers of Xeric (CoX), Theatre of Blood (ToB), and Tombs of Amascut (ToA) — are built around groups of two to eight players who must coordinate prayers, damage, and special attacks under a tight timer. A single quiet or confused player can wipe an entire attempt, which is why group leaders are quick to kick. This guide is written for players who already understand basic boss mechanics and now want to break into the raid scene: where to actually find groups, how to read a "looking for group" post, the communication rules that keep a raid alive, how roles are divided, and the etiquette that keeps you from being removed mid-run. If you are completely new to fight content, start with our PvM beginner guide first, then come back here once you can survive a solo boss.

The mistake most players make is treating raid recruitment like a shout in a busy world. It is not. Finding and keeping a raid group is a skill of its own — part networking, part preparation, part social awareness. The players who "always seem to get into good teams" are rarely the best mechanically; they are the ones who are reliable, communicative, and easy to group with. Every section below is built to make you that player. Pair this with our boss gear checklist so you know what each raid expects before you apply, because walking in with the wrong loadout is the fastest way to get skipped.

⏱️ Quick Summary — 30-Second Read

  • 📌 Find groups in Clan Chats, Friends Chats, and Discord — not just the chaotic public LFG worlds.

1. 🔎 Where to Find Raid Groups

The fastest way to waste an evening is to stand in a busy world shouting "LFT ToA" with no plan. OSRS raid groups form in a handful of reliable places, and each has its own culture, pace, and trust level. Understanding where groups actually form — and which channels are worth your time — is the first half of raid recruitment.

Clan Chats and Friends Chats (FC)

The single best source of consistent groups is a raiding clan or a busy Friends Chat. Join a clan that advertises raids, then simply type in the FC when you are ready. Regular clans often run "learner nights" where experienced players carry newcomers through ToA or ToB for free — these are the safest, most forgiving place to learn, because the goal is teaching, not speed. Popular public FCs rotate over time, so ask in our boss hub or check the official OSRS Discord's LFG channels for currently active chats. The advantage of an FC is continuity: the same people raid together week after week, so your reputation compounds.

Discord Communities

Discord servers dedicated to PvM (WDR — "We Do Raids" — is the most well-known) let you queue for groups with a bot, set your roles, and filter by raid, size, and invocation level. Discord is where most serious learners find a steady team. The advantage is structure: the bot records your completions, tracks no-shows, and builds a visible raid reputation you can reference when applying to stricter groups. For high-invocation ToA and most ToB, Discord voice is the default, so install the app and a working microphone before you queue.

In-Game LFG Worlds

Worlds 378 and 379 are traditionally the "raids" worlds where players advertise groups in the public chat. This works, but it is chaotic and full of scammers and leeches. Use it for chill, low-invocation ToA, but never drop items to a "host" and never pay upfront for a spot. If a post looks too good to be true, it is. Public LFG is where most first-timers get burned, which is exactly why the sections below spend so much time on reading posts and protecting yourself.

Friends List & Regulars

After a few good runs, add the competent players you met. A reliable trio or quad you can message directly is worth more than any LFG world, because you skip the audition every time. Long-term, your friends list becomes your private raid queue — message three people, get a team in five minutes, no strangers, no drama. This is the endgame of raid networking: you stop looking for groups and groups start looking for you.

💡 Start With ToA Learner Groups

Tombs of Amascut has the gentlest learning curve for grouped content because the invocation system lets hosts scale difficulty. A "ToA 0 invocation learner" group is where most players get their first raid completions. Move up only after you know every room cold — the room knowledge matters more than your gear early on.

2. 📋 Reading LFG Posts & Knowing the Requirements

Before you type "inv", read the whole post. A typical LFG line looks like this:

"ToA 300s, 4man, 150+kc, rigour+, bring thralls, FC 'osrs soa'"

Every token in that line is a filter. Hosts write them to avoid exactly the kind of player who slows a run or rage-quits. Here is what each part means and why it matters.

KC (Kill Count)

Hosts ask for a minimum personal completion count to filter out players who will slow the team. A "150+kc" requirement means you should have at least 150 solo or grouped completions. If you are under it, either find a learner group or be honest — say "80 kc but I know all rooms" and let the host decide. Lying about KC and then failing a room is the #1 way to earn a bad reputation that follows you across every FC and Discord you join. Honesty about being new is coachable; dishonesty is not.

Invocation Level (ToA)

ToA uses an invocation slider from 0 to 500+ that increases difficulty and reward. "300s" means around 300 invocation. Higher invocations demand tighter DPS and more supplies, so only join what your gear and skill support. Our gear checklist maps gear tiers to the invocation range you can handle — do not apply to a 400+ group on a budget setup, because you will be the reason the timer fails. The invocation number also signals the group's seriousness: low numbers are learner-friendly, high numbers assume you already know the raid blindfolded.

Gear & Prayer Abbreviations

"Rigour+" means you must have the Rigour prayer unlocked (a key ranged DPS boost). "Augury" and "Piety" appear for magic and melee teams. "Bowfa" or "Shadow" names the expected weapon. If you do not own the listed item, do not apply — the host is telling you the run's floor, and showing up without it wastes everyone. When a post says "tank gear" or "max melee," it means exactly that; bring the real thing, not a close substitute.

Size & Role Notes

"4man" or "5man" sets the group size, which changes DPS checks and supply splits. Smaller groups mean each player covers more, so requirements are stricter. "Bring thralls" (for ToA) or "bring brews" signals what you are personally responsible for supplying. Read these as a contract: if the post says bring brews and you arrive with none, you have already broken the agreement before the first room.

⚠️ Never Pay to Join

Legitimate groups never charge an entry fee. Anyone demanding GP or items up front to "let you in" or offering a "guaranteed carry for a price" is running a scam. Leave, block, and report the FC or player. Real carries happen inside clans for free on learner nights — you never pay to raid.

3. 📡 Communication Protocols That Keep Raids Alive

Raids fail in silence. The teams that clear consistently are not always the most skilled — they are the ones that talk. Good communication turns four average players into a unit that beats content meant for experts. Here is the protocol used by groups that actually finish.

One Caller Per Room

During a fight, exactly one person calls the plan — usually the most experienced player or the designated "caller." Everyone else listens and executes. If three people shout different instructions at once, the room falls apart. When you are not the caller, your job is to acknowledge ("g," "on it," "copy") and act. Quiet agreement is communication too; it tells the caller the team is with them. Assign the caller before the raid starts, not mid-fight.

Use Tick-Conscious Callouts

In ToB and CoX, timing is measured in game ticks (0.6 seconds). Callouts like "freeze now," "step in 1," or "vigora left" give the team a shared clock. Keep them short — long sentences get lost mid-fight. Agree on a vocabulary before the raid: which corner is "north," what "rotate" means, and how you signal a special attack is coming. The teams that never miscommunicate are the ones that spent two minutes agreeing on words first.

Voice vs Text

Many groups use Discord voice for ToB and high-invocation ToA because reactions must be instant. Others prefer in-game chat to keep it casual. Whatever the group uses, match it. If the host says "voice only," do not sit in text — you will miss callouts and slow the team. If the group is text-only, keep your typing fast and your callouts concise. Mismatching the medium is a silent form of not listening.

Pre-Raid Brief

Spend two minutes before entering: confirm roles, confirm the route (which ToA path, which CoX rooms), confirm supplies, and confirm the plan for the hardest room. This brief prevents the "wait, what do I do here?" moment that kills runs. A good host runs this automatically; a good guest asks for it if the host forgets. The brief is where confusion goes to die before it can cost a death.

🎯 Standard Callout List

  • "Stack" — group up on a tile (used for AoE or shared mechanics).
  • "Split" / "Spread" — move apart to avoid shared damage.
  • "Pray swap" — change Protect prayer to the named style.
  • "Spec" — use your special attack weapon now (DWH, BGS, Scythe).
  • "Heal" — a support player tops up the caller or tank.
  • "Reset" / "Re-group" — fall back to a safe tile and re-form.

4. 🎭 Role Assignment Across the Three Raids

Every raid divides labor. Knowing the standard roles means you can drop into almost any group and immediately know your job — which is exactly what hosts want to see when you apply. Below is the role split for each raid, plus what to study before you claim a role.

Tombs of Amascut (ToA) — 4 Mini-Bosses + Warden

ToA splits into four pillars, each with a boss: Ba-Ba (melee, boulders), Kephri (scarab, insects), Akkha (magic/melee, mirrored), and Zebak (ranged, blood). A common 4-man split is one damage dealer per pillar plus a dedicated support who brings thralls (from the Arceuus spellbook) and heals. The Warden fight at the end needs everyone to know the core and beam mechanics. Smaller groups mean each player covers two pillars, so flexibility matters more than in larger teams. Practice the mechanics solo first with our ToA solo guide so you are not learning on the team's clock.

Theatre of Blood (ToB) — 6 Rooms

ToB roles are stricter because of the shared HP pool and the "blood" supply system. Typical roles: a tank who holds the boss's attention (Verzik, Sotetseg), one or two damage dealers, and support/healers who heal the tank and manage blood spills. The Nylocas wave room needs a coordinated DPS order, and Xarpus requires precise poison management. ToB is the most role-rigid raid, which is why communication and knowing your exact job matter most here. Our Theatre of Blood guide breaks each room down room-by-room.

Chambers of Xeric (CoX) — 1 to 3 Paths + Olm

CoX is the most flexible. A team of 3-5 splits into "paths" that clear rooms in parallel, then regroups for the Great Olm. Roles are loose: everyone deals damage, but one player usually leads navigation and another handles the Olm head/tail split. A completed raid scales loot to team size, so smaller teams get a bigger personal share at higher risk. Because roles are fluid, CoX rewards players who can adapt — the "flex" player is king here.

💡 Know Two Roles, Not One

The players groups want most are "flex" — they can DPS or support depending on who else shows up. Learn both a damage role and a support role for your preferred raid and you will never struggle to find a spot, because you fill whatever the group is missing.

5. 🤝 Raid Etiquette & How to Avoid Getting Kicked

Getting kicked stings, but it is almost always predictable. Hosts do not remove players for being new — they remove them for being unreliable. Here is what gets players kicked, and how to make sure it is never you.

Be On Time and Ready

When the host says "meeting at bank in 2 minutes," be there with supplies already in your inventory. Players who show up five minutes late or have to shop for brews waste everyone's time. The first kick reason is almost always lateness — it signals you do not respect the group's time, and that reputation spreads.

Prepare Your Supplies

Bring exactly what the post asked: the right food-to-potion ratio, thralls if requested, your special-attack weapon, and a teleport. Arriving with an empty inventory or the wrong gear signals you did not read the post — an instant kick in strict groups. Our boss inventory prep guide shows the exact supply loadouts each raid expects, so there is no excuse for showing up short.

Know Your Room

You do not need to be flawless, but you must know the basic mechanics of the room you are in. Silence during a fight is suspicious; a player who says nothing and does nothing looks like a leech or a bot. Even a simple "on it" keeps the host confident you are engaged. Confused is fine; absent is not.

Listen, Then Speak

Follow the caller. If you think the plan is wrong, save it for after the room — arguing mid-fight gets people killed. Toxic chat, blaming teammates, or raging after a wipe is the fastest route to a kick and a block. The group would rather lose one attempt with a calm team than three with a loud one.

Don't Leech

Leeching means contributing no damage or healing while collecting a share of loot. Hosts watch DPS meters and healing contributions. Contribute your fair share every room or you will be removed and likely banned from that FC. In raids, effort is the price of admission.

⚠️ New Is Fine — Unprepared Is Not

Most groups welcome learners who say "first ToA, I know the rooms, tell me what to do." That is honest and coachable. What gets you kicked is pretending to know and then failing silently. Confidence from preparation beats experience every time, and preparation is something anyone can do before the raid even starts.

Kick Reason What It Looks Like How to Avoid It
Late or no-show Group waits 5+ min at bank; you "brb" Be in the bank, supplies ready, 2 min early
Unprepared Empty inv, wrong gear, no thralls/brews Read the post; pack the exact loadout asked
Leeching Zero damage or heals, still wants loot Contribute every room; pull your weight
Silent & lost Says nothing, misses mechanics Acknowledge callouts; ask before, not during
Toxic Blames, rages, argues with caller Stay calm; discuss after the room or run

The pattern is obvious: every kick reason is about reliability and attitude, never about raw skill. A new player who is on time, supplied, engaged, and friendly will get re-invited. A "pro" who is late, silent, and rude will not.

6. 🏆 After the Raid — Loot, Points & Reputation

The run is over; now protect the relationship that got you the loot. How you handle the aftermath is what turns a one-off group into a standing invite.

Loot Splitting

ToA and CoX drop a single chest split by the team (GP and items shared). ToB uses a "blood" point system where healing and damage earn points that decide who gets first pick. Learn the split method before the raid so there is no argument after. Disputing loot after a clear is a classic reputation-killer — groups remember the player who turned a win into a fight far longer than the player who just said "gg, thanks."

Clan Points & Contributions

Clan-based raids often track points for showing up, supplying, and succeeding. Contribute fairly and your points buy you priority invites. Skipping supply turns or leaving early burns those points fast. The players at the top of the clan board are not always the best — they are the most consistent, and consistency is what hosts filter for.

Build Your Reputation

Every good run is a reference. Thank the host, add competent players to your friends list, and show up reliably next time. In the raid world, a clean reputation is worth more than BiS gear — it is what gets you into the high-invocation groups that print GP. A stranger with 1,500 KC and a toxic log gets skipped; a known, friendly face with 200 KC gets the invite.

💡 The 24-Hour Rule

If a run goes badly through no one's fault, stay friendly and offer a rematch. Groups remember players who take a wipe in stride far more than players who quit angry. Your next invite often comes from the team you stuck with after a failed attempt — not from the team you carried to an easy win.

7. ⚔️ Raid Comparison: ToA vs ToB vs CoX

Each raid recruits and communicates differently. Knowing these differences before you apply saves you from walking into a group whose style you are not ready for. Use this table as a pre-application checklist.

Raid Best For Group Size Communication Style Typical Invite Requirement Loot Split
Tombs of Amascut (ToA) Learners & scalable difficulty 2–8 (often 4) Voice or text; invocation-based Know rooms; KC varies by invocation Single shared chest
Theatre of Blood (ToB) Strict role players 3–5 (often 4) Voice strongly preferred High KC; known role competency Blood point priority
Chambers of Xeric (CoX) Flexible all-rounders 3–5 (smaller = more loot) Text or voice; parallel paths Reliable DPS; navigation skill Scales to team size

Read the table top to bottom and you see the real pattern: ToA is where you learn, ToB is where roles are strictest, and CoX is where flexibility wins. Apply to the column that matches who you are today, not who you want to be next month.

8. ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

What raid should a first-time raider start with?

Tombs of Amascut at 0–150 invocation is the gentlest entry because the difficulty scales with the group's choice. Find a learner FC or Discord group, tell them it is your first run, and learn the four pillars before raising invocations. CoX is a reasonable second step once you are comfortable in a team, and ToB is best saved until you know a strict role cold.

How much KC do I need before applying to groups?

It depends on the post, but a good baseline is 50+ completions of the specific raid before joining strict groups, and any amount for learner groups. More important than raw KC is knowing every room's mechanics cold — a 60 KC player who knows the raid beats a 300 KC player who panics under pressure. Lead with room knowledge, not just the number.

Do I need voice chat to raid?

Not always. Many ToA and CoX groups run on in-game text. However, ToB and high-invocation ToA usually expect Discord voice for reaction speed. Match whatever the host specifies when you join — showing up in text to a voice-only ToB group is a quick way to get replaced before the first room.

Why do hosts kick players mid-raid?

The top reasons are: arriving late or unprepared, contributing no damage or healing (leeching), staying silent and lost during fights, arguing with the caller, or toxic behavior after a mistake. None of these are about skill level — they are about reliability and attitude. A new but reliable player is re-invited; a skilled but chaotic one is not.

Can Ironman accounts join raid groups?

Yes. Ironmen are welcome in most groups as long as they supply their own consumes and accept the same loot split rules. Some clans run Ironman-only raids so everyone shares the no-trade limitation fairly. Be clear in your application that you are an Ironman so the host plans supplies correctly.

How do I read a ToA invocation number in an LFG post?

The number (e.g. "300s") is the total invocation points, which scale difficulty and reward together. Low numbers (0–150) are learner-friendly; mid (150–350) assume competent DPS and supplies; high (350+) assume near-BiS gear and flawless mechanics. Only apply to a range your gear and skill can clear — our gear checklist maps setups to invocation tiers.

What does "bring thralls" or "bring brews" mean in a post?

"Bring thralls" means you are expected to cast Arceuus thralls (a support contribution, common in ToA). "Bring brews" means pack your own Saradomin brews rather than relying on the team's shared pool. These are personal-supply contracts — if the post says it, you are responsible for it, and arriving without it is a broken agreement.

Is it ever safe to pay for a raid spot or a "carry"?

No. Legitimate groups never charge entry, and "pay for a carry" is a common scam that ends with you down GP and still no loot. Learn for free on clan learner nights instead. If someone demands payment up front, leave and report them — it protects the next player too.

How do I build a reputation so groups invite me back?

Be on time, fully supplied, communicative, and friendly across many runs — and add the good players you meet to your friends list. Reputation is built one quiet, reliable run at a time. Thank hosts, take wipes in stride, and show up when you say you will. Within a few weeks your friends list becomes your private raid queue.

What should I do if I get kicked — can I rejoin?

Stay calm and do not argue in chat; that only confirms the kick was right. If it was a misunderstanding, send a brief, polite message after. If it was earned, fix the cause (supplies, punctuality, attitude) and apply to a learner group fresh. Most clans separate "had a bad day" from "is a problem" — how you react decides which column you land in.

✅ Final Tips — Your Raid Group Checklist

  • Join a raiding clan or Discord before you ever post in a public LFG world.
  • Read the full LFG post and only apply if you meet KC, gear, and invocation asks.
  • Learn one damage role and one support role so you fit any group size.
  • Show up on time, fully supplied, and quiet-until-called.
  • Follow the caller, contribute every room, and stay friendly after wipes.
  • Thank the host and add good players — your reputation is your ticket to better groups.
