OSRS Leviathan Enrage Guide 2026 — Faster Attacks, Pathfinder AoE & Coping

Updated August 19, 2026  |  21 min read  |  By Yanchun Wu

The normal Leviathan teaches you to bait and kite. The enrage phase takes those same mechanics and compresses them — faster attacks, a hungrier Pathfinder AoE, and slam windows so tight that hesitation means death. This is the coping strategy that turns enrage from a panic into a pattern.

🌊 Advanced · PvM — Desert Treasure II · 22 min read · Updated for 2026
🛡️ Level: 138

The Leviathan's enrage phase is where the Desert Treasure II water boss stops being a teaching fight and starts being a test of composure. The base fight already demands that you bait the ground slam and kite the tracking Pathfinder AoE — mechanics that punish standing still. Enrage tightens every window: the boss attacks faster, the Pathfinder locks on quicker and expands further, and the slam tells give you less time to react. This guide assumes you can already clear the normal Leviathan comfortably. If you cannot, start with our DT2 bosses overview and learn the base pattern first — enrage without the fundamentals is just a faster death.

What follows is the coping framework: how the enrage trigger actually works, why the Pathfinder becomes the real threat, and the movement-and-DPS rhythm that keeps you alive while still shortening the fight. Pair it with the right kit from our boss gear checklist so your accuracy does not fall off as you move. Before you commit to enrage, also skim our general PvM mechanics guide for the universal rules of prayer flicking, tick movement, and damage spacing that every end-game boss relies on.

⏱️ Quick Verdict — 30-Second Read

  • 📌 Enrage = a revive with a faster clock. Drop it to 0 HP and it gets back up stronger; every tell is shorter.
  • 📌 Pathfinder is the kill leader. It tracks your tile and ignores prayer — keep moving.
  • 📌 Slam windows shrink. Bait early, commit to the bait, don't double-back.
  • 📌 DPS through movement. A standing-still hit costs more than a lost attack.
  • 📌 Ranged > melee enrage. See the gear checklist for accurate ranged setups.

1. 🔥 What the Leviathan Enrage Mechanic Actually Is

The single most misunderstood part of this fight is the enrage trigger itself. Many players approach the Leviathan expecting a separate "hard mode" they opt into. In reality, enrage is built into the standard kill: you fight the Leviathan normally, you reduce its health to zero, and instead of the fight ending, the boss revives with boosted aggression. That revived state is the enrage phase. Understanding this changes how you prepare, because the fight is never "over" when the health bar empties the first time — it is only halfway done, and the second half is the harder half.

Enrage is a consistency test, not a gear check

A common misconception is that enrage demands better gear than the normal fight. In practice the gear that clears normal Leviathan cleanly is the same gear that clears enrage — the difference is execution. Players who blame their kit after an enrage death are usually masking a movement error. The honest question to ask after every failed attempt is not "do I need more stats" but "where did my anticipation break." If you can answer that specifically, you are improving; if you reach for a gear upgrade instead, you are avoiding the real lesson. This is why the practice plan later in this guide front-loads repetition on the normal fight before touching enrage at all, and why the prep tables below assume your normal-mode kit rather than a separate enrage-only setup.

Why the revive exists

All of the original Desert Treasure II boss trio (Vardorvis, the Leviathan, and the Whisperer) share a design language: a first health bar that teaches the pattern, followed by an enraged return that tests whether you internalized it. The Leviathan's enrage is not a new move set bolted on after launch — it is the intended second act of every kill. Treating it as a surprise is the number one reason players die at what should be the victory moment. Go in knowing that the revive is guaranteed, and you will have your movement plan ready the instant the boss gets back up.

What enrage actually boosts

Enrage is, at its core, a tempo and magnitude upgrade rather than a new kit. The boss keeps the same two phases and the same special attacks you already learned, but every dial turns up: attack speed increases so your safe gaps between actions shrink, the tracking Pathfinder projectile locks on and expands faster, the ground slam telegraph shortens, and any hit you do take lands harder. None of this changes what you do — only when and how cleanly. That is genuinely good news: if the base pattern is in your hands, enrage is a tempo drill, not a new fight. The danger is that the tighter tempo exposes every lazy habit you got away with on the normal version.

💡 Enrage Rewards Anticipation, Not Reaction

On the normal Leviathan you can react to the tell as it appears. In enrage you must anticipate it from the attack order. Start moving a beat before the telegraph shows — by the time it renders, you should already be relocating. Build that habit on the base fight and enrage stops feeling unfair.

The mental model that keeps you alive

Frame the whole encounter as two laps of the same track. Lap one (normal) is where you learn the corners. Lap two (enrage) is the same corners at higher speed. Players who "turn their brain off" after the first health bar empties are the ones who eat a Pathfinder to the face on the revive. Instead, the moment the boss drops, immediately re-center, confirm your prayer is active, and pre-walk to your phase-appropriate safe lane. The revive animation is itself a tell: use those few ticks to set up, not to celebrate. This single shift in mindset removes most enrage deaths before they happen.

2. 🌊 Phase 1 — Submerged & the Pathfinder

Phase 1 is the submerged portion of the fight. The Leviathan stays below the waterline and attacks from range while periodically surfacing its tracking attack. Your defensive prayer here is Protect from Missiles, because the boss's basic auto-attacks in this phase are ranged. The signature mechanic of phase 1 is the Pathfinder, and it is the mechanic that enrage makes lethal.

How the Pathfinder behaves

The Pathfinder is a ground marker that tracks your position and then detonates where you were standing. It does not care about your prayer — you cannot out-pray it, you can only not be there when it blows. When it spawns, it locks a target tile (your current position) and expands outward over a short window. If you stand still, it lands on you for heavy, unblockable damage. The only defense is continuous movement: keep walking so the detonation happens on empty ground. The trap is thinking you have "time to get one more attack in" — you do not, especially in enrage.

Kiting without losing DPS

The skill is attacking while you move. Range the Leviathan from a distance so your attacks land as you reposition, and let the Pathfinder expire behind you. This is why the Leviathan favors a ranged style — you can hit from afar while kiting, whereas a melee player must be in his face and has far less room to dodge. If you melee enrage, your Pathfinder windows are brutal; ranged is strongly recommended, and our DPS optimization guide explains how to keep your damage up while moving.

📋 Pathfinder Survival Loop

1) See the marker spawn on your tile. 2) Immediately walk perpendicular, not toward the boss. 3) Fire your ranged attack on the move. 4) Keep distance so the next marker also lands on empty ground. 5) Never stop moving during the window.

Reading the phase 1 attack order

Because enrage shortens tells, you cannot rely on reacting to each telegraph. You must learn the order in which phase 1 attacks arrive: the ranged auto-attacks come on a steady cadence, and the Pathfinder is woven in on a predictable beat. Once you know "a Pathfinder is due after every third auto-attack," you stop watching for the marker and start pre-moving on the count. This predictability is your friend — enrage does not randomize the pattern, it just compresses it. The player who counts beats survives; the player who waits for the sprite dies.

3. 🪨 Phase 2 — Surfaced & the Slam

At the halfway point the Leviathan surfaces and the arena's safe geometry changes. The boss is now in melee range of the platforms, so your protective prayer swaps to Protect from Melee for its basic hits. The headline mechanic of phase 2 is the ground slam: the boss telegraphs an impact zone and you must not be standing in it. In enrage the telegraph is shorter and the safe window after the slam is smaller, so the bait must be cleaner and earlier.

Bait early, commit fully

The correct play is to let the slam target a spot away from where you want to fight, then walk out. In enrage, hesitate and the slam lands on you mid-step. Bait the moment the tell appears, commit to the relocation, and do not double back to attack until the slam has fully resolved. Double-backing is how learners get clipped by the edge of the impact. The slam zone is a fixed area, so once you know its shape you can plan a path that keeps you on dry tiles the entire time.

Use the slam as a reposition opportunity

Advanced players turn the slam into free movement: when it telegraphs, walk toward your next kite position through the safe lane. You dodge the slam and set up your next Pathfinder dodge in one motion. This is the rhythm that makes enrage feel controllable instead of chaotic. The pillars you used as reference points in phase 1 become your hard safe spots in phase 2 — hug them when the slam fires, then peel off to re-open your ranged line.

💡 Keep an Exit Lane

Always fight with space behind you. If a slam telegraphs while your back is to a wall or the arena edge, your only escape is through the impact. Maintain a kite path from the first bait and enrage becomes survivable.

Phase 2 DPS discipline

Because the boss is surfaced, many players instinctively walk in for melee. Resist it. Ranged still out-kites the slam and lets you keep Protect from Melee up without ever being in his hitbox. The moment you step inside melee range during a slam tell, you have nowhere to go. Stay at range, use the pillars as your safe anchors, and let the enrage damage race work in your favor — the longer you survive, the more free ranged hits you land while he is committed to a slam animation.

4. ⚡ How Enrage Changes the Fight vs Normal

The single biggest adjustment in enrage is that the Leviathan's attacks land on a quicker cadence. Your defensive actions — prayer toggles, steps out of danger, food bites — now compete with a shorter cycle. The players who struggle most are the ones who try to keep their normal-rhythm routine and simply run out of time. Below is the side-by-side that makes the delta concrete.

Aspect Normal Leviathan Enrage Leviathan
Attack speed Standard cadence, comfortable gaps Faster cadence, shorter safe gaps
Pathfinder lock-on Tracks at a readable pace Locks faster and expands larger
Slam telegraph Long warning before impact Shorter warning, tighter safe window
Damage if clipped Significant Higher — punishes greed harder
Required composure React to the tell Anticipate from the attack order
Prayer sustain pressure Moderate over the fight Higher — longer fight, constant Protect
Recommended style Ranged preferred, melee viable Ranged strongly recommended

Why your old routine fails

In the base fight you might attack three times, then step, then eat, then step back. In enrage that same sequence overruns the next tell and you eat a hit you "should have" dodged. The fix is to compress your own actions: shorter commitment to each attack, earlier movement, and tighter eats. You will land slightly less DPS per click, but you will survive — and a living slower kill beats a dead fast one. The chart below shows the practical effect: each safe window shrinks, so your actions must be smaller and sooner.

⚠️ Don't Greed the Last Hit

The most common enrage death is "one more attack" before moving. That hit is never worth a heavy slam or a Pathfinder detonation on your tile. Click away the instant the next tell is due. Enrage is won by discipline, not by greed.

5. 🎒 Gear & Inventory Prep Tables

Enrage rewards gear that keeps your accuracy high while moving. Since you will be kiting constantly, a ranged setup with good range and a fast, accurate weapon is ideal. You also want enough sustain to absorb the occasional clip, because enrage hits harder when you do get caught. The tables below cover loadout priorities and a concrete inventory. 💡 Holy Wrench Is Non-Negotiable

The ammo-slot Holy Wrench adds prayer points per dose for free. In a long enrage fight that is the difference between finishing and running dry mid-Pathfinder. Always equip it, and pair it with Super Restores rather than plain Prayer potions so you also top up lost stats.

Do not skimp on food for enrage. Your first clean attempts are mistake-heavy, and the revived phase punishes every clip with higher damage. Bring more sustain than you think you need; the supply cost of one death usually exceeds the cost of a few extra fish. 6. 🗡️ Step-by-Step Kill Strategy

This is the repeatable script. Run it the same way every attempt so the enrage tempo becomes automatic. Each numbered step is a phase of the fight, not a one-off tip.

① Open the fight: positioning & prayer

Enter already on Protect from Missiles and standing near a pillar with an exit lane behind you. Do not start the fight in the open center — you want a reference point for your first kite. Pre-load a Ranging potion and confirm your weapon is in hand. The opening seconds set your whole rhythm: if you start calm and centered, the first Pathfinder is trivial.

② Phase 1: kite the Pathfinder

Count the attack order and pre-move before each Pathfinder spawns. Walk perpendicular to the boss, fire ranged hits on the move, and let the marker detonate on empty water. Keep Protect from Missiles pinned the entire phase. The goal is zero Pathfinder hits — if you eat even one in phase 1, your enrage margin shrinks before the hard part begins. Use the pillars as turn points so you never run yourself into a corner.

③ Phase 2: the slam dance

On the surface, swap to Protect from Melee and switch from kiting-open to hugging pillars. When the slam telegraphs, walk through the safe lane to your next pillar rather than backing into a wall. Bait early, commit, and do not double back. Re-open your ranged line only after the slam fully resolves. This is where anticipation beats reaction — you should already be moving on the count, not waiting for the sprite.

④ Survive the enrage revive

The instant the first health bar empties, re-center, confirm prayer, and pre-walk to your safe lane. The boss gets back up faster and hits harder. Expect the Pathfinder to lock sooner and the slam window to be tighter. Do not celebrate — treat the revive animation as a tell. Hold your rhythm, keep moving, and let your earlier DPS carry you through the shorter, sharper second act.

⑤ Close the DPS & collect

In the final stretch, resist the urge to greed. Trade the last few hits for safe positioning; a dead boss at 5% is still a kill, a dead you at 5% is a reset. Once he falls for good, grab the loot and bank. Review what broke down — almost every enrage death is a single missed anticipation, not a gear problem. Tightening your DPS through standard optimization helps you close the damage gap so you need fewer risky clicks.

7. 🏋️ Practice Plan & Where to Go Next

Enrage is a tempo skill, and tempo is learned by repetition, not by reading. But repetition on the hardest version burns supplies. The efficient path is to build the rhythm on the normal fight, then step up. Our full boss set lives in the DT2 bosses guide, which lays out the recommended kill order across Vardorvis, the Leviathan, the Whisperer, and Yama.

A graduated practice plan

  1. Normal Leviathan, no food pressure: drill pre-positioning and Pathfinder kiting until it is automatic.
  2. Normal Leviathan, speed runs: try to shorten the fight while staying flawless — this mimics enrage's DPS-under-pressure demand.
  3. Enrage, expect deaths: take what you learned and accept the first few attempts end early. Each death teaches the tempo.
  4. Enrage, clean: once deaths drop, optimize gear and DPS for consistency.

This progression keeps your supply bill sane while still teaching the exact skill enrage needs. If you plan to tackle Yama afterward, the movement discipline you build here transfers directly — see our Yama glyph guide for the next step up. For a different flavor of coordinated PvM, our Theatre of Blood guide is a strong sideways progression. The through-line is always the same: learn the pattern, then survive the faster version of it.

📋 Enrage Readiness Checklist

Can you clear normal Leviathan without taking a Pathfinder hit? Can you bait slams without double-backing? Can you keep the correct Protect prayer up the whole fight? If yes to all three, you are ready for enrage.

✅ Final Tips Before You Dive In

  • Clear the normal Leviathan cleanly before attempting enrage.
  • Expect the revive — it is guaranteed, not a surprise.
  • Pre-position; do not react to enrage tells.
  • Keep moving through every Pathfinder window — prayer will not save you.
  • Bait slams early and use them as free repositioning.
  • Favor a ranged setup so you DPS while kiting.
  • Equip the Holy Wrench; bring more food than you think you need.

8. ❓ Leviathan Enrage FAQs

What is different about the Leviathan enrage phase?

Enrage keeps the same mechanics but speeds them up: faster attack cadence, a quicker and larger Pathfinder AoE, and shorter slam telegraphs. You do the same actions on a tighter clock, and any hit you take deals more damage.

How is enrage triggered?

It is built into the standard kill. You reduce the Leviathan to 0 HP in phase 2, and instead of the fight ending, the boss revives enraged. The revive is guaranteed, so plan for a second, harder phase rather than treating the empty health bar as victory.

Can I melee the enrage Leviathan?

You can, but it is much harder. Melee puts you in his face with little room to kite the Pathfinder, which is the deadliest enrage mechanic. A ranged setup lets you DPS while moving and is strongly recommended, especially for the revived phase.

How do I survive the Pathfinder AoE?

Keep moving. The Pathfinder tracks your tile and ignores prayer, so the only defense is to not be standing where it detonates. Ranged attacks let you keep DPS up while you kite, and counting the attack order lets you pre-move before the marker even spawns.

Which prayer do I use in each phase?

In phase 1 (submerged) the boss's basic attacks are ranged, so use Protect from Missiles. In phase 2 (surfaced) its basic attacks are melee, so swap to Protect from Melee. Keep the correct prayer pinned the entire time and refresh with Super Restores.

Should I learn enrage before Yama?

Learning enrage builds the movement and composure skills Yama's glyph phase also demands, so it is good preparation. But clear the normal Leviathan first — enrage assumes the base pattern is already automatic. See the DT2 bosses guide for the recommended order.

What gear do I need for enrage?

An accurate ranged weapon, the correct Protect prayer per phase, strong food, and prayer sustain (Holy Wrench + Super Restores). Full recommendations are in the boss gear checklist and the packing framework in our inventory prep guide.

Why do I keep dying right after the first health bar?

That is the enrage revive, and most players are unprepared for it because they think the fight is over. The fix is to treat the revive animation as a tell: re-center, confirm prayer, and pre-walk to your safe lane the instant the boss drops. Anticipation there removes the most common death.

Is the Leviathan enrage worth doing for loot?

Yes, if you are already killing the Leviathan. The enrage phase is part of the same encounter, so you are not adding a separate trip — you are simply finishing the kill you already started. The reward is the same boss drop table, earned by surviving the second act rather than walking away at the first empty bar.
