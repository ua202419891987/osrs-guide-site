1. 🧭 Why Your Farming Patches Die (The 3 Killers)

Adsterra ad slot — paste your Adsterra banner code here

A dead patch is rarely bad luck. Farming in OSRS runs on a small set of deterministic rules, and when a crop fails it is because one of them was broken. The full training curve — from herbs to redwoods — is in our 1-99 Farming guide, but the failure rules below apply to every patch type.

The three killers are disease, misused compost, and unpaid farmer protection. Each is explained with its exact mechanic and fix.

① Disease — the silent yield thief

Every crop has a chance to become "diseased" on each growth stage if left unprotected. A diseased crop shows a wilted, browned sprite — and if you don't cure it before the next stage, it dies and yields nothing. This is the single most common reason players "got no herbs." Disease can strike even while you are away, so protection (or a cure in hand) is mandatory, not optional.

② Ultracompost "wasted" — the timing mistake

Players buy expensive ultracompost, pour it on… and still lose the crop. Nine times out of ten they applied it after planting. Compost must be added to the empty, cleared patch before the seed goes in. Applied after planting, it has no effect on that crop's disease roll or yield. The other 10% of "waste" cases misunderstood what compost does — it reduces disease chance and boosts yield, it does not grant immunity.

③ Forgot to pay the farmer — the protection fee lapse

Most patches can be protected by paying a nearby gardener a fee (usually a specific produce item). The gardener then prevents disease for that crop's life. The lapse: players pay once, harvest, replant — and forget to pay again. The new crop is unprotected from the first stage and dies. Payment is per crop, per planting, never permanent. For trees and fruit trees this is even more punishing because the growth timer is hours, not minutes.

2. 🦠 Disease Mechanics Deep Dive

Understanding the disease roll is what turns random losses into a controlled system. Here is how it actually works.

① How the disease chance is calculated

On each growth stage (crops advance in stages as the timer ticks), the game rolls a disease check unless the patch is protected. The base chance depends on crop type and whether compost was used: no compost gives the highest chance, compost lowers it, supercompost lowers it further, and ultracompost gives the lowest. Payment to a gardener makes the roll irrelevant — the patch simply cannot disease. So your two real options are: pay the farmer, or stack ultracompost + check often.

② Which patches can and cannot be diseased

Most patches — allotments, herbs, flowers, hops, bushes, trees, fruit trees — can disease. Some special patches (e.g. the Trollheim / Farming Guild herb patches with benefits, or the Hosidius disease-free patch) are exempt or reduced. Knowing your safe patches lets you route valuable crops there. The Farming Guild herb patch, unlocked at 85 Farming with Kourend favor, is a prime example of a lower-risk spot worth using for high-value herbs.

③ Curing — plant cure and the gardener

Always carry plant cure for unprotected runs

If a patch diseases and you have no gardener protecting it, apply plant cure (or pay the gardener to cure it) before the next growth stage. Caught in time, the crop recovers and still yields. Miss the window and it dies — a total loss. For long timers (trees), the safe play is simply paying the gardener so you never need the cure at all.

3. 🪣 Ultracompost Done Right

Ultracompost is the best compost tier, but only if used correctly. Most "wasted ultracompost" complaints are usage errors, not a bad item.

① Apply BEFORE planting, never after

The rule is absolute: rake the patch clean, apply ultracompost to the empty soil, then plant the seed. The compost binds to that planting. Applying it after the seed is in does nothing for the current crop. If you already planted and forgot, you cannot retroactively protect that crop — you can only pay a gardener (if the patch supports it) or watch and cure.

② Where ultracompost comes from

You can buy it from certain gardeners, or make it: start with supercompost (made from high-level produce in a compost bin) and add volcanic ash to upgrade it to ultracompost. Buying ready-made is convenient; making your own is far cheaper at scale if you already run a compost bin during herb runs. Either way, the cost is trivial next to the value of a saved high-tier herb or tree crop.

③ What compost actually does (yield numbers)

Compost cuts disease AND boosts harvest

Compost reduces the per-stage disease chance; supercompost and ultracompost reduce it further. Crucially, ultracompost also increases the minimum and average yield of the harvest — so even a crop that survives gives more. For herbs this can mean several extra Grimy herbs per patch, which compounds across a 5–9 patch run. It is not immunity, but it is the best non-gardener protection available.

4. 💰 Paying the Farmer (Protection Fees)

Paying a gardener is the single most reliable way to guarantee a crop survives. But the fee system has rules players constantly forget.

① The fee protects only the current crop

When you pay a gardener to "look after" a patch, they protect that specific planting from disease until harvest. After you harvest and replant, you must pay again. There is no permanent protection — every new seed is a new fee. This is the #1 reason "my patch died even though I paid last time": you paid the previous crop, not this one.

② Tree & fruit tree payments (the expensive ones)

Trees and fruit trees grow over hours and cannot be cured mid-growth easily, so paying the gardener is effectively mandatory. Each species wants a specific payment — get it wrong and the gardener refuses, leaving the tree unprotected. Common examples: an oak wants 1 tomato; a willow wants 1 apple; a maple wants 1 banana; a palm wants 15 papaya fruit; a dragonfruit tree wants 15 pineapples. Bring the exact item or the payment fails silently.

6. ❓ Farming Patch FAQs

Why did my patch die even though I used ultracompost?

Two likely reasons: you applied the compost after planting (it must go on empty soil first), or the compost simply reduced — not removed — the disease chance and the roll still failed. For guaranteed survival on valuable crops, also pay the gardener.

Does compost make a crop immune to disease?

No. Compost lowers the per-stage disease chance (ultracompost the most) and boosts yield, but a crop can still disease. Only paying a gardener grants effective immunity for that crop's life. Treat compost as risk reduction, not a shield.

I paid the farmer last time — why did it die now?

Payment protects only the crop you paid for. After harvest you must pay again for the replanted seed. Each planting is a fresh fee. Keep a payment kit so you never skip it.

What do I pay a gardener for a palm / dragonfruit tree?

A palm wants 15 papaya fruit; a dragonfruit tree wants 15 pineapples. Bring the exact item or the gardener refuses and the tree stays unprotected through its multi-hour grow. A stacked payment kit prevents this entirely.

My herbs survived but the yield was terrible — why?

You likely planted without ultracompost. Ultracompost raises both the minimum and average herb yield, so even a healthy crop gives noticeably less without it. Add ultracompost to the empty patch before every herb seeding.

Can I cure a diseased patch after it browns?

Only if you catch it before the next growth stage — apply plant cure or have the gardener cure it. Once it advances while diseased, the crop dies and yields nothing. For trees with long timers, prevention (paying) beats cure every time.

Are there patches that can't be diseased?

Yes — some patches are disease-free or reduced-risk, such as the Hosidius farm patch and certain Farming Guild patches unlocked with favor. Route your highest-value crops (especially herbs) to safe patches whenever possible.
