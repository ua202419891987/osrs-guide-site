🚫 OSRS Ban Types Explained: Temp, Permanent, Quash & Macro (2026)

Updated August 19, 2026  |  15 min read  |  By Yanchun Wu

Not every ban means the same thing, and not every ban is handled the same way. This guide breaks down the seven ban types OSRS players actually see — temporary, permanent, quashed, macro/botting, advertising, RWT, and linked-account chain bans — with what each one means, exactly what to do next, and the misconceptions that send players down the wrong path.

📅 Updated: August 2026 — Account & Bans · 19 min read
🛡️ Level: All
🆕 NEW

Before you write a single appeal, you have to name the right ban — the wrong label makes even a perfect message look like a copy-paste. Use the ready messages in our ban appeal template guide once you know your type, and gather the right proof with the ban appeal evidence checklist so your case matches the logs.

💡 Quick Verdict

Temporary bans expire on their own and need patience, not panic. Permanent bans are the intended end state but usually still allow one appeal. A "quashed" ban is already overturned — celebrate, don't re-appeal. Macro and RWT bans are the hardest to reverse when detection is solid. Advertising and linked-account bans have their own rules. Match your action to the type; the comparison table at the end makes it one glance.

1. ⏳ Temporary Ban (Temp Ban)

A temporary ban is a timed timeout — the account is locked for a set number of days and then unlocks automatically. It is Jagex's warning shot: serious enough to hurt, light enough to let you come back if you change behavior.

① What it means

The ban message shows a duration (for example, 7 or 14 days). When that clock runs out, the account returns to normal with no action needed from you. The offence behind it is usually a first or mid-level violation — minor macro use, a rule slip, or repeated smaller flags adding up.

② How to handle it

Do nothing but wait. Logging out, switching clients, or submitting appeals will not shorten it. Use the downtime to read the rules and, if you were using anything third-party, remove it. Appealing a temp ban is rarely useful and can sometimes extend scrutiny — save your energy for a permanent ban, which is the one that needs a real appeal.

③ Common mistake

Players panic and fire off five appeals, thinking it speeds things up. It does not; it just puts noise in the queue. A temp ban is a clock, not a decision pending — let it tick.

④ What pushes a temp ban toward permanent

The line between "you get a timeout" and "you are gone" is mostly repetition and severity. One minor slip might earn a week; the same slip a month later, or a clearly deliberate offence, earns a permanent. The system also weighs whether the behaviour continued after an earlier warning. If you got a temp ban and immediately did it again, do not expect a second timeout — expect the account to end. Treat every temp ban as the final warning it is: remove the cause, read the rules, and change how you play. The players who bounce back are the ones who treat the timeout as a real stop sign, not an inconvenience.

2. 🔒 Permanent Ban

A permanent ban is the account-level end state for a serious or repeated offence. The label says "permanent" because that is the intended penalty — but it is not a closed door. Most permanent bans still carry an appeal option in the ban message.

① What it means

The account is locked with no automatic unlock date. Common triggers: confirmed RWT, solid macro detection, repeated temp bans, or a severe single offence. "Permanent" describes the proposed outcome, not a refusal to listen.

② How to handle it

This is the ban that deserves your one best appeal. Name the exact offence from the message, match your story to the logs, and include proof of ownership. If you did it, lead with honesty and a clean history; if you didn't, lead with facts. The RWT and macro appeal guides give the per-type strategy.

③ Common mistake

Assuming "permanent = no point appealing" and walking away, or the opposite — assuming "I'll just make a new account and keep doing it." A new account linked to the same person often gets caught by the same detection and can pull others down (see linked bans).

3. ✅ Quashed Ban (Overturned)

"Quashed" is the good one: it means a ban that was put in place has been overturned and removed. If your ban status reads "quashed," you are already clear.

① What it means

A quashed ban is a ban that Jagex reviewed and lifted — typically a false positive, a hijack confirmed after the fact, or an error caught during review. The account is restored and the penalty record is cleared. You do not need to do anything else.

② How to handle it

Log in, secure the account (enable 2-step if you haven't), and move on. Do NOT submit another appeal for the same ban — there is nothing left to appeal, and re-pinging the system can create confusion. If something still looks off (missing items, a lingering flag), contact support about that specific issue, not the ban itself.

③ Common mistake

Players see "ban" in old emails after a quash and panic-appeal again. Check the live account status, not the old message. A quash means it is over.

4. 🤖 Macro / Botting Ban

Macroing — using bots, scripts, autoclickers, or any third-party input automation — is the most common ban and one of the hardest to reverse when detection is confident. The system logs client signatures and input patterns that are very hard to argue away.

① What it means

The detection flagged automated play: a botting client, a script, or input behavior no human produces. It can be temporary for a first slip or permanent for clear, sustained use. Many "I only used a lightweight plugin" cases still count if the plugin automates input.

② How to handle it

If you boted, the honest version in the template guide is your best shot, paired with a promise to use the official client. If you did not, fight it as a false positive with input facts and, if useful, a short video — see the false macro ban appeal guide. The banned botting account recovery guide covers the hijack variant.

③ Common mistake

Claiming "I was just AFK" or "it's a legit plugin" when the log shows a known botting client. The contradiction alone sinks the appeal. Match your claim to what the system recorded.

④ Plugins vs full bots — the gray line

Players argue "it was only a plugin, not a bot," but the rule turns on automation of input, not on the label. A plugin that highlights tiles is usually fine; a plugin that clicks, reacts, or plays for you is macroing. The gray area — auto-clickers, "mouse keys" scripts, and "assist" tools that take actions — is exactly where most bans happen, because the detection sees the automated input either way. If you are unsure whether a tool crosses the line, assume it does and stop before a ban. On appeal, do not split hairs about "bot versus plugin"; state plainly what the tool did. Honesty about a borderline tool reads far better than a denial the client log contradicts.

5. 📣 Advertising Ban

Advertising bans target in-game spam — posting links to gold sites, services, Discord servers, or real-money offers in chat, on forums, or via PM. It is treated as pollution of the community, not as seriously as RWT, but it still sticks.

① What it means

The account was used to broadcast unauthorized advertising — typically gold-selling or service promotion. It may be a temp ban for a first offence or permanent if it was aggressive or repeated. Sometimes a hacked account gets used to spam, which is really a hijack case (see the recovery guide).

② How to handle it

If it was you, a short honest appeal works; advertising is lower on the severity scale than RWT. If a hijacker used your account to spam, frame it as unauthorised use with recovery proof. Either way, stop the behavior — repeated advertising bans escalate fast.

③ Common mistake

Thinking "it was just a link, no big deal" and ignoring it. Each advertising offence is logged; the third one is far harder to walk back than the first.

④ Where advertising bans actually come from

Most advertising bans trace back to gold-selling or service promotion: posting "cheap GP" links in busy worlds, spamming Discord invites in PMs, or dropping site URLs in clan chat. Some players get flagged for sharing a "looking for group" link that happens to be a commercial server, not realizing the pattern reads as advertising. The ban is lighter than RWT because no in-game economy transfer is proven — but it still pollutes chat and annoys other players, so Jagex acts on it. If you were hacked and your account was used to spam, that is a hijack case, not an advertising one: prove the unauthorised access rather than apologizing for "your" spam.

6. 💰 RWT Ban (Real-World Trading)

RWT — trading GP, items, or accounts for real money outside the official Bonds system — is one of the most serious offences. Jagex views it as a direct attack on the game economy and usually issues a permanent ban.

① What it means

The logs show a trade or transfer tied to off-platform money: buying/selling GP, selling the account, or middle-manning. The ban is typically permanent on first offence because RWT is treated as intentional, not accidental.

② How to handle it

If you did it, an honest appeal citing a clean history and first offence is the only realistic path to a reduction. If you believe a normal trade was mislabeled as RWT, fight it with trade context — the false RWT ban appeal guide has the exact wording. Never attach off-site proof; that confirms the breach.

③ Common mistake

Denying RWT while the trade log shows the off-site deal. The log wins. And "but everyone does it" is not a defence Jagex accepts — it just confirms you knew the rule.

④ Account selling and buying is RWT too

Many players think RWT only means GP for cash, but buying or selling the whole account is the same offence and is treated just as seriously. Selling your maxed account, buying a maxed one to skip the grind, or "renting" an account all fall under Real-World Trading. The ban risk is higher here because account transfers also break the one-person-one-account expectation and feed chain bans across the new owner's other accounts. If you bought an account that later gets flagged, the original seller's offence can surface on your login and pull your clean accounts in. The only safe path is to never buy, sell, or share accounts — Bonds are the sole approved way to convert money into game value.

7. 🔗 Linked Account / Chain Ban

A linked (chain) ban happens when one account's offence pulls related accounts down with it — usually because they share an IP, a payment method, a recovery email, or were clearly the same person. This is the ban type that surprises players who thought only the "guilty" account would fall.

① What it means

Jagex links accounts it believes belong to one user. If Account A is permanently banned for RWT or botting, Accounts B and C on the same signals can be banned too, even if they never broke a rule themselves. The link is the evidence, not a second offence.

② How to handle it

Appeal each linked account separately, stating clearly that it is a different account you own but that it was NOT used for the offence. Provide ownership proof (payment receipt, recovery email) for the clean ones. The chain ban guide walks through the wording and the "I own both but only one broke a rule" framing that actually works.

③ Common mistake

Pretending the linked accounts "aren't mine" when they share your email and card. The link is already known; denying ownership just adds a lie on top of the original offence and kills the clean accounts' appeals too.

8. 🧭 Common Misconceptions Table

Players lose appeals because they believe a myth. The table below pairs each ban type with the misconception that hurts most and the reality that actually helps.

Temporary "Appealing speeds it up" It is a clock; appeals don't shorten it Wait it out, fix the cause
Permanent "Permanent = no appeal" One appeal is usually still allowed Send your best, honest message
Quashed "It might come back" It is overturned and cleared Log in, secure, move on
Macro "It was just a plugin" Input automation still counts Match claim to the client log
Advertising "A link is harmless" Each spam offence is logged Stop; short honest appeal if first
RWT "Everyone does it" That admits you knew the rule Honest + clean history, or fight mislabel

💡 The One Rule Behind All Seven Types

Match your message to the logs. The system already knows the offence, the date, and the evidence. Your job is not to argue the ban into nothing — it is to add the context the log cannot see: that the account is yours, that the session wasn't, or that this was a first and final mistake. Do that and you have used the right type the right way.

9. ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between a temp and a permanent ban?

A temp ban has an end date and unlocks on its own; a permanent ban has no automatic unlock and is the intended final penalty. Both can follow the same offence at different severities — repeat temp bans often become permanent.

What does "quashed" actually mean?

It means the ban was placed and then overturned — removed from your account. You are clear. There is nothing to appeal; just log in and secure the account.

Can a permanent ban really be appealed?

Usually yes — the ban message includes an appeal option even when labelled permanent. "Permanent" is the proposed outcome, not a closed door. Use your single appeal well.

Is a macro ban always permanent?

No. A first or light slip can be a temp ban; clear, sustained botting is typically permanent. The harder question is whether detection is solid — if it is, appeals rarely succeed regardless of duration.

Why did my other accounts get banned too?

Because Jagex linked them by shared signals (IP, payment, recovery email) and treated them as one user. That is a chain ban. Appeal the clean ones separately with ownership proof, and don't deny you own them.

Does an advertising ban become permanent?

It can, if you keep spamming after a warning. First offences are often temp; repeated advertising escalates and can go permanent. Stop the behavior and a first ban is usually survivable.

If I make a new account, will it get banned too?

If it shares the signals of a banned account (same person, same setup), it can be caught by the same detection and chain-banned. A new account is not a clean escape from an underlying offence.

My Ironman was banned — is that a different type?

Same ban types apply; Ironman just can't trade, so an RWT or trade-based ban is unusual and worth stating. Use the Ironman ban appeal guide for wording that fits a locked, untradeable account.

What if I was hacked and the hijacker caused the ban?

That is a macro/RWT/advertising ban committed by someone else. Frame it as unauthorised use with recovery proof and a dated hijack window. The account recovery after hack guide has the artifact list.

Can a chargeback cause a ban on its own?

Yes — reversing a membership payment is treated as a separate offence and can ban the account independently of any gameplay rule. Handle it on its own track via the chargeback ban guide, not inside another appeal.
