1. ⚓ Why The Red Reef Matters in 2026 Sailing

The Red Reef landed on 20 May 2026 as the second chapter of the Tortugan questline, and it arrived bundled with one of the most mechanic-heavy Sailing updates of the year. On its own the quest is a fun, character-driven Experienced-level adventure about Floopa's injury and a suspicious trading company. But the patch around it reshaped how boats fight, how they repair, and how you mine and smith for ship combat.

For most players the lasting value is not the quest XP — it is the infrastructure. The Bosun's Bench turns a boat from a thing you manually patch into a self-maintaining platform. The two new cannonballs give ship combat real crowd-control and damage-over-time options. Rubium opens a new Mining grind that feeds those cannonballs. And the trawling, sail and Medallion changes make Sailing broader and less click-intensive.

💡 A Sailing Milestone, Not a Side Quest

If you are training Sailing in 2026, The Red Reef is effectively mandatory. The Sacred Grove it unlocks is a stable Camphor source, the Bosun's Bench is the best boat facility for long trips, and the cannonballs are the new PvP-at-sea standard. Treat it as a core progression step, not optional flavor.

It also sets up content that follows. Boat enemies at sea launched alongside the quest, and later Sailing content (including a tougher Barracuda Trial) builds on the ship-combat systems The Red Reef introduces. Getting your boat kitted with a Bosun's Bench and a stock of Chainshot now pays off for the rest of the year.

2. 🔓 Quest Start, Requirements & The Sacred Grove

To begin, sail to the Great Conch and speak with Elder Raley in the south-east house of the Summer Shore. You need the first Tortugan quest, Troubled Tortugans, completed, plus 52 Sailing and 48 Smithing. The Smithing gate is small but real, so bank some bars or train a few levels before you go if you are short.

📜 Quest Requirements

  • Troubled Tortugans — the preceding Tortugan quest.
  • 52 Sailing — the main skill gate.
  • 48 Smithing — needed for the quest steps and the Bench materials later.
  • Start: Elder Raley, south-east house, Summer Shore (Great Conch).

The headline unlock is the Sacred Grove, a Camphor tree grove with an invisible Woodcutting boost of plus seven and a deposit box. Camphor is the wood the Bosun's Bench is built from, so the grove is the sustainable supply line for boat facilities. If you plan to fit out multiple ships, the Sacred Grove is where you will be chopping.

🎁 Quest Rewards

  • 2 Quest Points
  • 15,000 Sailing XP
  • 5,000 Smithing XP
  • Sacred Grove access — Camphor trees, +7 invisible WC boost, deposit box
  • Bosun's Bench schematic — the self-repair boat facility blueprint

💡 The Hardcore Warning

Part of the quest involves boat-to-boat combat, and dying on the ship is a dangerous death even though the boat sinking itself is safe. Jagex suggests at least 65 Combat to handle the encounters. Bring food and prayer, and do not treat the naval sections as free.

3. 🔧 Bosun's Bench: The Self-Repairing Boat

The Bosun's Bench is the reward players actually queue for. Assign a crew member (or yourself) to it and the bench periodically repairs the boat from your Cargo Hold's repair kits, without overhealing, scaling with your crew's Deckhandiness. For long Sailing trips it removes the single most annoying chore in the skill: manually patching hull damage.

⚙️ Build Requirements

  • 63 Sailing and 54 Construction to install it.
  • 6 Camphor Planks — from the Sacred Grove you just unlocked.
  • 24 Adamantite Nails
  • 2 Lead Bars
  • Bosun's Bench schematic — the quest reward.

It slots into any facility spot that supports a Salvaging Station, so you do not need to redesign your whole deck around it. Because it eats repair kits from the Cargo Hold, you still need to keep that hold stocked — the bench automates the clicking, not the supply. The upside is that a well-crewed bench keeps the boat healthy through extended journeys where you would otherwise be stopping to repair constantly.

💡 Crew Matters

The repair rate scales with your crew's Deckhandiness, so assign a high-Dechandiness crew member (or yourself) to the bench. A bench on a low-skill crew member still helps, but a skilled one meaningfully extends how long you can stay out before resupplying.

4. 💥 New Cannonballs: Chainshot & Incendiary

The Red Reef patch added two cannonball types with distinct combat effects, both smithable up to Rune. They are built at an anvil by combining regular cannonballs with a new component, and each pair produces two of the special ball that can be fired separately.

① Chainshot

Chainshot specializes in slowing enemy movement. It halves the target's movement speed for five seconds while dealing the same damage as a regular cannonball, just without the chain. You make it by combining a chain (smithed from Steel bars) with two cannonballs, producing two Chainshot of the same type up to Rune. Against fleeing or repositioning sea enemies, the slow is a meaningful control tool.

② Incendiary

Incendiary applies a burn damage-over-time effect. Sea enemies set ablaze take damage over time, and that burn ignores any flat armour bonuses, making it a noticeable DPS increase. You make it by combining ten Rubium Splinters with two cannonballs, again producing two up to Rune. The armour-ignore is the selling point: against heavily armoured ships, Incendiary pulls ahead of plain cannonballs.

🧪 How to Make Each (up to Rune)

  • Chainshot: 1 chain (from Steel bars) + 2 cannonballs → 2 Chainshot.
  • Incendiary: 10 Rubium Splinters + 2 cannonballs → 2 Incendiary.
  • Both: smithable to Rune; each pair fires twice separately.

💡 Pick by Target

Use Chainshot when you need to pin a mobile enemy or line up follow-up volleys. Use Incendiary against tanky, high-armour ships where the burn's armour-ignore pays off. Carrying both lets you adapt to whatever you meet at sea without swapping the whole loadout.

5. ⛏️ Rubium: The New Ore Behind It All

Incendiary cannonballs depend on Rubium, a new ore introduced in the same patch. It comes in two forms, and the split matters for both money and Ironman planning.

🪨 Rubium Forms

  • Rubium Splinters — require 48 Mining, mined from Rubium Rocks, and they are tradeable.
  • Rubium Geodes — require 68 Mining, mined from the Rubium Deposit, untradeable.
  • Geode breakdown — smash geodes into splinters with a hammer and a chisel.
  • Location: the centre of the Charred Dungeon on Charred Island.

Because splinters are tradeable, you can either mine them yourself at 48 Mining or buy them on the Grand Exchange to fuel an Incendiary stockpile. Geodes at 68 Mining give more per trip but are untradeable, so they are the Ironman route: mine geodes, crack them, and convert the splinters into cannonballs yourself. Your Mining level affects how many splinters you get when you break geodes, so higher Mining means a better yield.

💡 Mine or Buy?

On a main account, buying splinters is often cheaper than the time spent in the Charred Dungeon. On an Ironman, the geode grind at 68 Mining is the path, and the Sacred Grove Camphor plus Rubium loop makes a tidy self-sufficient Sailing-and-Smithing train.

6. 🛠️ Sailing QoL: Auto-Retaliate, Trawling, Sails, Medallion

Beyond the headline items, the Red Reef patch shipped a bundle of quality-of-life changes that make Sailing smoother across the board. None are flashy, but together they remove a lot of friction.

✨ The QoL List

  • Cannon auto-retaliate — on by default; toggled in Combat Options. Cannons fire back at attacking sea creatures with a short delay, so manual targeting stays best but long trips are less clicky.
  • Deep Sea Trawling buff — Sailing XP roughly doubled across all net types, gather rate dropped from 4 ticks to 3, and baited shoals now correctly give +20% catch.
  • Sail cosmetics — seven solid colors unlock at 41 Sailing, 10 matching dye each, swapped in the Shipyard.
  • Medallion of the Deep — now works as breathing gear across the Fossil Island underwater area, cutting oxygen drain by 80% in the Agility and Thieving section.
  • Boat teleports — Teleport to Boat and Summon Boat both gained a right-click "Last Boat" option.

The trawling buff is the big one for skillers. Roughly doubling XP while also speeding the gather rate makes Deep Sea Trawling a genuinely competitive Fishing-adjacent Sailing method, and the baited shoal fix means the +20% catch finally works as intended. The Medallion change is a quiet win for the Fossil Island underwater grind, turning a niche item into a real oxygen saver.

💡 Turn On Auto-Retaliate Deliberately

It defaults on, which is fine for relaxed trips, but remember there is a delay before the cannon responds. If you are optimizing DPS against sea enemies, keep manually targeting — auto-retaliate is convenience, not peak efficiency.

7. ⚔️ Boat Combat & Hardcore Cautions

The Red Reef shipped alongside NPC boat enemies at sea: full ships that can hold pirates inside them, just like your own. That means encounters where you attack the vessel and the crew on it at the same time, a real step up from fighting lone sea creatures. It is the foundation for the ship-to-ship fantasy Sailing has been building toward.

For the Hardcore crowd, the danger is specific. Your boat sinking is a safe event, but your character dying aboard the ship is a dangerous death — you risk losing unprotected gear. Jagex suggests at least 65 Combat to handle the naval encounters, and that advice applies to the quest's boat sections and to the roaming sea enemies afterward.

⚠️ Stay Safe at Sea

  • Pack food and prayer — ship crews hit hard and you cannot simply log out mid-fight cleanly.
  • Keep the boat repaired — a Bosun's Bench prevents a sinking that strands you in a bad spot.
  • Inventory for danger — on Hardcore, only carry what you can lose; the death is dangerous, not safe.
  • Chainshot control — slow enemy ships to control engagement range.

8. ❓ The Red Reef FAQs

Q: When was The Red Reef released?

20 May 2026, as the second quest in the Tortugan storyline, bundled with a large Sailing patch adding new cannonballs, Rubium, the Bosun's Bench and several quality-of-life changes.

Q: How do I start The Red Reef?

Speak to Elder Raley in the south-east house of the Summer Shore on the Great Conch. You need Troubled Tortugans completed, 52 Sailing and 48 Smithing.

Q: What do I get from the quest?

2 Quest Points, 15,000 Sailing XP, 5,000 Smithing XP, access to the Sacred Grove (Camphor trees, a +7 invisible Woodcutting boost and a deposit box), and the Bosun's Bench schematic.

Q: What is the Bosun's Bench?

A boat facility that assigns a crew member to periodically repair the boat from Cargo Hold repair kits, without overhealing, scaling with crew Deckhandiness. It needs 63 Sailing, 54 Construction, 6 Camphor Planks, 24 Adamantite Nails, 2 Lead Bars and the schematic.

Q: How do I make Chainshot and Incendiary cannonballs?

Chainshot: one chain (from Steel bars) plus two cannonballs makes two Chainshot, smithable to Rune. Incendiary: ten Rubium Splinters plus two cannonballs makes two Incendiary, also to Rune. Both are made at an anvil.

Q: Where do I get Rubium?

In the centre of the Charred Dungeon on Charred Island. Splinters need 48 Mining and are tradeable; geodes need 68 Mining and are untradeable but break into splinters with a hammer and chisel.

Q: Did Deep Sea Trawling get buffed?

Yes. Sailing XP from trawling roughly doubled across all net types, the gather rate dropped from 4 ticks to 3, and baited shoals now correctly grant a +20% catch bonus.

Q: Is boat combat dangerous?

The boat sinking is safe, but your character dying aboard the ship is a dangerous death. Jagex recommends at least 65 Combat for the naval encounters, and Hardcore players should only carry what they can afford to lose.

Q: What does the Medallion of the Deep do now?

It functions as breathing apparatus across the Fossil Island underwater areas, reducing oxygen depletion by 80% in the Agility and Thieving section, which makes the underwater grind far more forgiving.

Q: How does The Red Reef connect to later Sailing content?

Boat enemies at sea launched with the quest, and later content like a tougher Barracuda Trial builds on the ship-combat systems it introduces. A Bosun's Bench and a Chainshot stock now pay off for the rest of the Sailing roadmap.
