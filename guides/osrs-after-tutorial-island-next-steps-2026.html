OSRS After Tutorial Island: What to Do Next (2026)

You finished Tutorial Island. The screen fades to Lumbridge. And now… nothing tells you what to do. That empty feeling is normal — Old School RuneScape has no main quest line. This roadmap gives you the 5 first moves every new account should make, starting with the game interface guide so the buttons stop being a mystery.

🗺️ Beginner — New Player Roadmap · 11 min read · Updated July 30, 2026

⏱️ Quick Summary — 30-Second Read

  • 📌 There is no main quest. OSRS is open-world from minute one — you set the goal.
  • 📌 Step 1: learn the interface. Banks, inventory, spellbook, and prayers are your daily tools.
  • 📌 Step 2: earn first GP. Even 100K GP unlocks shops, teleports, and freedom.
  • 📌 Step 3: do quests. A handful unlock the whole map and huge XP rewards.
  • 📌 Step 4: train combat smart. One synced style beats grinding random monsters.
  • 📌 Step 5: pick a long-term goal — money, skills, or bossing — and chase it.

1. 🏰 The Lumbridge "Now What?" Problem

You are not lost because you are bad at the game. You are lost because Old School RuneScape is a 2007-style sandbox with no arrow pointing you forward. Modern games hold your hand for 20 hours; OSRS drops you at the Lumbridge fountain and wishes you luck.

This is a feature, not a bug — but it means your first sessions decide whether you fall in love or quit. The players who thrive pick a direction early. The players who quit wander aimlessly for a week and walk away thinking the game is "empty." It is not empty; it is wide open.

💡 Your First Rule

Do not try to "do everything." Pick one of the five steps below and commit for your first 10 hours. Momentum beats completeness. Our full New Player Guide expands every section here if you want the deep version.

2. 🖱️ Step 1: Learn the Interface & Controls

Before you chase anything, learn the dashboard. Half of "I'm confused" is not knowing where the buttons are.

① The panels you touch daily

Four things matter from day one: your Inventory (bottom-right), your Equipment screen, your Skills panel (the stats list, right side), and your Spellbook / Prayer tabs. Learn to open each with a hotkey (the icons along the bottom of the game frame). Fumbling these mid-fight wastes food and time.

② Bank and teleports are your freedom

Use the Lumbridge bank and the teleport spells

The Lumbridge castle bank is your first home base. Deposit everything you do not need. As soon as you can, unlock Varrock / Lumbridge / Falador teleport (Magic) — free teleports save more time than any single money method early on. The interface controls guide walks every panel with screenshots.

③ Settings that save your sanity

Turn on Mouse Keys if allowed in your region, set your camera to "fixed" or "mobile" per preference, and enable Shift-click-to-drop and Bank "place all". Small QoL toggles compound over hundreds of hours.

3. 💰 Step 2: Earn Your First GP

Money is freedom in OSRS. With GP you buy better gear, paid teleports, supplies, and eventually a bond for membership. You do not need much — your first 100K–500K transforms the game.

① Start with zero-requirement methods

The fastest legit early cash comes from collecting and selling things anyone can grab: cowhides (tan them or sell raw), flax bowstrings, ashes / bones from monotypes, and mining copper/tin → bronze bars. These need no levels and no quest unlocks.

② Pick one method and run it for an hour

One focused hour beats a scattered afternoon

New players leak time by switching methods every five minutes. Choose one from the no-requirement money guide and commit a full hour. A single focused hour at cows or flax earns more than three distracted ones. Target 100K GP, then move on.

③ What to spend your first GP on

Prioritise: a few food stacks (trout/salmon), runes for teleports, and one slightly better weapon (e.g. a steel / mithril scimitar). Do not blow it on cosmetics. Gear and teleports pay you back; dyes do not.

4. 📜 Step 3: Quests That Unlock the Game

If you do only one thing in your first week, do quests. A small list of quests hands you more progress than 50 hours of grinding — and unlocks whole regions.

① The "unlock everything" starter quests

Priority quests for a brand-new account

These open travel, gear, and content with modest requirements:

  • Waterfall Quest — huge Attack/Strength XP, near-instant combat boost.
  • Recipe for Disaster (subquests) — unlocks the Barrows Gloves, the best all-round gloves in the game.
  • The Grand Tree / Tree Gnome Village — unlocks Gnome gliders (free fast travel).
  • Fairytale II — unlocks the Fairy Ring network, the best teleport system in the game.
  • Priest in Peril — opens Morytania, home to many money methods.

Full ordering is in our questing beginner guide.

② Quests give XP you cannot grind as fast

Several quests dump 10K–30K XP into a skill instantly. Early on, that is weeks of grinding skipped. Stack your quest rewards and your account "feels" higher level than the clock says.

③ Use the Quest List as your to-do board

The in-game Quest List (icon near the skills panel) is the closest thing OSRS has to a mission log. Treat it as your roadmap when you are unsure what to do next.

5. ⚔️ Step 4: Train Combat Efficiently

Combat is how you survive quests, earn loot, and eventually boss. Train it with intent, not by clicking the first monster you see.

① Pick a style and commit (Attack / Strength / Defence)

Early melee training is fastest by syncing Attack and Strength (e.g. train Strength to your target, then Attack) and using the Controlled style to level all three at once when you want balanced stats. Don't spread across ranged/magic until you have a reason.

② Best early training spots

From chickens to cows to monsters you can safespot

Chickens (Lumbridge) → cows (north of Lumbridge) → safespotted monsters (e.g. Minotaurs in the Stronghold, or ranging goblins) keep you alive while you level. Our combat training guide maps the exact route by combat level, and the skills overview explains what each combat stat does.

③ Don't ignore Prayer and Defence forever

Protect Prayers (from Priest in Peril + a few bones) cut your food costs dramatically at bosses later. A little Defence now means fewer deaths later. Balance, don'tignore.

6. 🎯 Step 5: Pick a Long-Term Goal

Once the basics click, choose a lane. OSRS has three big endgames, and picking one gives every session a purpose.

① The three lanes

Money / Skills / Bossing — pick your magnet

  • Money lane: stack GP for gear, a bond, or a buyable 99. Great if you like optimisation.
  • Skills lane: chase 99s, achievement diaries, and the Completionist feeling. Calm and measurable.
  • Bossing lane: PvM for loot and adrenaline. Needs combat stats + gear but pays the most per hour at the top.

You will drift between them, but having a "main" lane stops the aimless wandering that makes new players quit.

② F2P vs P2P — the early decision

Free-to-play is a fine trial, but membership unlocks ~90% of the game (more skills, more quests, more money methods). When you can afford it, read the F2P→P2P guide to time the jump and even fund it with bonds you farm in-game.

③ Your first 7-day plan

💡 A Simple Week-One Loop

Day 1–2: interface + first 100K GP. Day 3–4: Waterfall Quest + a few unlock quests. Day 5–6: sync melee to ~40/40. Day 7: pick your lane and set a 30-day goal. The First Week Progression guide gives the hour-by-hour version.

7. ❓ New Player FAQs

Is there a main story or quest line I should follow?

No. OSRS has no central campaign. Quests are standalone stories, and the "progression" is the set of解锁 (unlocks) they grant. Use the Quest List as your loose to-do board and follow the starter quest order in our questing guide.

What should I do first in Lumbridge?

Open your bank, learn the interface hotkeys, then earn your first 100K GP with cowhides or flax. Money early removes more friction than anything else. Then start Waterfall Quest.

Should I buy membership right away?

Not required — F2P is a valid start. But membership unlocks most of the game. If you can, grab it after your first week once you know you enjoy the loop; you can even self-fund it with an in-game bond later.

Which skill should I train first?

Combat (melee) first — it lets you do quests and survive. Then let quests push your other skills via their XP rewards. Don't grind a gathering skill to 99 on day one; it is slow and aimless.

How do I make money with no stats?

Zero-requirement methods: cowhides, flax → bowstrings, ashes/bones, and low-level mining. Target 100K–500K, then reinvest in gear and teleports. The no-requirement money guide lists the current best ones.

I keep dying — is that normal?

Early deaths are normal while you learn safe spots and prayers. Use safe-spotable monsters, bring food, and turn on Protect Prayers as soon as you unlock them. Dying in F2P loses almost nothing, so treat early deaths as cheap lessons.

What is the single best early quest?

Waterfall Quest. Near-zero requirements, no combat, and it hands a huge Attack/Strength XP lump that effectively boosts your combat level for free. Do it as soon as you can reach the area.
