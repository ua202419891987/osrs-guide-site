1. 🔍 Why Public WiFi Breaks OSRS Login (3 Traps)

When you can load osrs.com in a browser but the game client won't log in, you've hit a network wall, not an account problem. Public and shared networks — hotels, airports, dorms, libraries, cafés, cruise ships — add layers between you and Jagex that a normal home connection doesn't have. There are exactly three traps, and they stack:

🎯 The Three Public-Network Traps

  • Captive portal — a redirect page that must be accepted before any real traffic flows. Until you accept it, the game client is silently blocked.
  • Port filtering — the firewall allows web browsing (port 443) but blocks the game ports OSRS actually uses to reach a world.
  • VPN / proxy flagging — routing through a VPN or the network's own proxy makes Jagex see a "new" location and forces extra verification or a soft lock.

One quick way to confirm it's the network: switch to cellular data (or your phone hotspot) and try once. If login works instantly on data but fails on the WiFi, you've proven the WiFi is the culprit — and you can stop worrying about your account being hacked or banned. From there, work the three traps top to bottom. This article is the public-network companion to our VPN login-blocked fix and the broader home router troubleshooting guide.

💡 Rule of thumb

If a normal website also fails to load on this WiFi until you "sign in" or "accept terms", it's a captive portal. If websites work fine but only the game fails, it's almost certainly blocked ports. If everything worked yesterday from home but fails on this trip, suspect the VPN/proxy first.

2. 🚪 Captive Portal: The Endless-Spinner Trap

A captive portal is the "Welcome to Hotel WiFi — click to accept" page. The trick is that the login page often loads, but the network holds all other traffic hostage until you acknowledge it. The OSRS client doesn't have a browser to "click accept", so it just hangs on "Connecting" or "Loading" forever. You never see the block because the client shows a spinner, not a web page.

② How to break out of a captive portal (desktop & mobile)

Step 1 — Trigger the portal page on purpose

  • Open any browser (Chrome, Safari, Edge) and go to a non-HTTPS site like http://neverssl.com or http://example.com.
  • The network will redirect you to its captive portal. Accept terms, enter the room code, or log in with your booking surname.
  • Once the browser shows a normal page, the portal has "released" your device.

Step 2 — Re-launch OSRS

  • Completely close the client (don't just minimize). On mobile, force-stop the app; on desktop, exit fully.
  • Reopen and tap Play. The spinner should now clear within a few seconds.

Step 3 — If there's no portal but it still fails

  • Some networks "register" by MAC address. Try forgetting and re-joining the WiFi, which forces a fresh DHCP + portal handshake.
  • On Android/iOS, toggle "Private MAC / Randomize MAC" off temporarily so the portal recognizes your device consistently across sessions.
⚠️ "I accepted it but OSRS still spins"

The portal may release browsers but still throttle or block game ports for a few minutes while it "provisions" your session. Wait 60 seconds after accepting, then retry. Also confirm you're not on a separate "guest" SSID that has no game access — ask the front desk which network actually allows gaming/streaming.

3. 🔌 Blocked Ports: OSRS Needs More Than 443

This is the trap most players and even some IT desks miss. OSRS loads fine in a browser because web traffic uses port 443 (HTTPS). But the game client connects to the live game world over different TCP ports. If the firewall allows 443 but blocks those, you get the classic symptom: websites work, the OSRS login screen appears, but hitting Play does nothing or drops you straight back.

③ Which ports OSRS uses

PortProtocolPurposeUsually Blocked On
443TCPWebsite, launcher, login handshakesRarely
43594TCPPrimary game-world connectionHotels, schools, corporate guest
43595TCPSecondary / world-select trafficStrict firewalls, some VPNs

④ How to test if a port is blocked

Desktop quick test

  • Open a Command Prompt / Terminal and run telnet world1.runescape.com 43594 (Windows may need "Telnet Client" enabled; macOS/Linux have it built in).
  • If the screen goes blank/black, the port is open. If you get "Could not open connection" or a timeout, the port is blocked.
  • No telnet? Use an online "open port check" tool aimed at world1.runescape.com:43594 from the same network.

What to do when a port is blocked

  • You usually can't change the firewall on hotel/school WiFi — it's managed by the venue. Don't try to "port forward" on their network; you don't have admin rights.
  • Ask the front desk / IT whether there's a "gaming" or "unrestricted" SSID — many hotels keep one network open for exactly this.
  • If you're on your own travel router or hotspot, open 43594/43595 in its firewall settings (see our router troubleshooting guide).
  • Otherwise, fall back to your phone hotspot (Section 5) — cellular carriers almost never block these ports.
🚨 Don't "fix" this with a random proxy

Free web proxies that claim to "unblock ports" route your login through a third party you don't control. That's how account credentials and 2FA codes get intercepted. If you need to tunnel, use a trusted VPN you control (Section 4) — never a mystery proxy.

4. 🛡️ VPN-Triggered Login Failure

A VPN is the right tool when a network blocks ports — but it creates its own problem. Jagex tracks the rough location and IP reputation of every account. When you suddenly log in from a VPN exit node in another country, or from an IP that other banned bots have abused, the risk engine may force a 2FA re-check, throw "Invalid credentials", or soft-lock the account pending verification.

⑤ Symptoms specific to VPN logins

  • You're asked for your Authenticator code twice or it's "suddenly wrong".
  • Login says "too many attempts" even though you typed it correctly once.
  • You get an email from Jagex about a new login location you didn't expect.
  • The client connects, then drops you at the character screen with a generic error.

⑥ How to use a VPN without locking yourself out

Step 1 — Pick a stable, reputable region

Connect to a server in your home country, not the cheapest or random one. A consistent region keeps Jagex's risk scoring calm. Avoid free VPNs with heavily abused shared IPs — they're the ones most often auto-flagged.

Step 2 — Split-tunnel the game (if your VPN supports it)

Many VPN clients let you exclude the OSRS client / Jagex Launcher from the tunnel. That way your browser stays protected but the game connects from your real IP — the best of both worlds on a port-blocking network.

Step 3 — If you're already locked, power-cycle the VPN

  • Disconnect the VPN entirely and try a plain login (if the network allows ports).
  • If plain login works, the VPN IP was the problem — switch to a different server or region and retry.
  • Still failing? Confirm your 2FA app clock is synced (an off-by-minutes clock makes codes invalid) and keep your backup codes handy.
💡 The "two countries in 5 minutes" red flag

Jagex's system especially dislikes seeing one account appear from two distant countries within minutes. If you must use a VPN, connect before you launch the client and stay on one exit node for the whole session. Our dedicated VPN login-blocked guide goes deeper on recovery after a lock.

5. 📱 Phone Hotspot: Your Reliable Backup

When the venue WiFi fights you, your phone's cellular hotspot is the fastest way to confirm the problem and usually the fastest way to just play. Cellular networks almost never block 43594/43595, and they give you a clean, consistent IP that Jagex trusts more than a crowded hotel firewall.

⑦ Get online in under a minute

  • iPhone: Settings → Personal Hotspot → Allow Others to Join. Connect your laptop/tablet to the "YourName's iPhone" WiFi.
  • Android: Settings → Network & internet → Hotspot & tethering → Wi-Fi hotspot. Toggle it on and use the shown password.
  • Launch OSRS on the tethered device. If it logs in instantly, you've proven the hotel/school WiFi was the blocker.

⑧ Keep the data bill sane

📊 OSRS is lighter than you think

Old School RuneScape streams very little data — a long play session rarely exceeds a few hundred MB, far less than a single video call. Turn off OSRS HD YouTube streams and you can raid for hours on a normal plan. If you're worried, set a hotspot data limit in your phone settings before you start.

🔋 Pro move for travelers

Keep one device (your phone) on the venue WiFi for browsing and tether only the game client. Or, better, run OSRS Mobile directly on the phone over cellular and skip the laptop entirely — see our background-disconnect fix so it doesn't drop while the screen sleeps.

6. 🚑 5-Minute Rescue Flow (Do This in Order)

When you're standing in a hotel room with a dead spinner, don't guess — run this ordered checklist. Each step takes under a minute and tells you exactly where the break is.

→ Step 1: Open a browser, visit http://neverssl.com. If a "accept terms" page appears, accept it. (Captive portal)
→ Step 2: With the portal accepted, try OSRS. Works? Done. Still spins? Go to Step 3. (Port check)
→ Step 3: Test telnet world1.runescape.com 43594. Blank screen = port open (problem is elsewhere). Timeout = port blocked.
→ Step 4: If using a VPN, disconnect it and retry. If that works, your VPN IP was flagged — switch region or split-tunnel.
→ Step 5: Tether your phone hotspot and log in. If this works, the venue network is the wall — play on hotspot or ask for the "gaming" SSID.
⏳ The 10-minute patience rule

If you've failed login 5+ times, stop for 10–15 minutes. Jagex rate-limits repeated failures per IP, and a flagged-hotel-IP hammered by many guests can temporarily block even valid logins. Step away, then resume at Step 1.

8. ❓ FAQ — Public WiFi & OSRS

Is it safe to play OSRS on public WiFi at all?

Yes, with one rule: the OSRS login is encrypted and Jagex never sends your password in the clear. The real risk on public WiFi is the captive portal phishing clone or a fake "OSRS login" page — always confirm you're on the official client/launcher, not a browser popup. A VPN adds a layer of safety on untrusted networks; just follow the VPN steps in §4 to avoid a lockout.

Why does the OSRS website load but the game won't connect?

Because they use different ports. The website is port 443 (almost never blocked); the game world is 43594/43595, which strict hotel/school firewalls commonly filter. Your browser works, the client hangs — that mismatch is the textbook sign of port blocking (§3).

My VPN fixed the connection but now Jagex wants 2FA again — normal?

Yes. A new IP region triggers a fresh verification. Enter your current Authenticator code (synced clock!) or a one-time backup code. If you're repeatedly forced to verify, your VPN is hopping regions — pin it to one stable server (§4, Step 1).

Will using my phone hotspot get me banned for "sharing accounts"?

No. Tethering your own device to your own phone is normal and won't trigger a ban. Bans come from real policy violations (botting, RWT, account sharing with strangers), not from which IP you play on. The only caveat: if your carrier NATs many users behind one IP, that shared IP can occasionally be rate-limited — hence the patience rule in §6.

The hotel says "gaming is allowed" but OSRS still won't load — what now?

Ask specifically whether 43594/43595 TCP are open, not just "is gaming allowed" (they may only unblock Xbox/PlayStation ports). If they can't or won't open them, your only reliable options are your phone hotspot or a trusted VPN with split-tunneling.
