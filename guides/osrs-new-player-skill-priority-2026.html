OSRS New Player Skill Priority 2026: What to Train First (and the Quest Order That Unlocks It)

🆕 NEW

New or returning to OSRS in 2026? You have 23 skills staring at you and no idea where to start. This guide gives you a simple priority order — what to train first, what to defer, and the quest sequence that unlocks the gear, prayers, and areas you need most.

📅 Published: August 9, 2026 ⏱️ ~28 min read 🎯 Beginner Roadmap
🛡️ Level: 1 (Fresh Account)

The single biggest mistake new and returning OSRS players make is treating all 23 skills as equally urgent. They grind Mining for hours, ignore quests, and wonder why they cannot wield the gear they just trained for. Skills in OSRS are not a to-do list — they are a dependency web. This guide gives you a priority order built around that web: train what unlocks the most, defer what costs the most, and run the quests that open the doors. If you want the full skill-by-skill breakdown first, our complete skill training guide covers every one of the 23 skills in depth, while the full new player handbook pairs this priority list with account-setup steps.

The reason a priority order matters so much is that Old School RuneScape is brutally non-linear. A skill you train today can hand you gear that makes five other skills faster tomorrow, while a skill you train today "just because" can quietly drain the millions of GP you needed for something far more important. Most veterans agree on one thing: the accounts that feel smooth and fast are the ones that sequenced their training correctly, not the ones that trained hardest. This article is that sequence, written for someone starting at level 1 with an empty bank and no assumptions about what you "should" be doing.

⏱️ Quick Summary — 30-Second Read

  • 📌 Tier 1 (do first): Combat 40-60 + Prayer 43 + a little Slayer. These let you survive and quest.
  • 📌 Tier 2 (fund your account): One money skill (Mining/Fishing/Thieving) + Agility to 60 (Graceful).
  • 📌 Tier 3 (quest-gated power): Run the quest order that unlocks gear, prayers, and whole areas.
  • 📌 Tier 4 (defer): Herblore, Construction, Prayer-to-99 — huge GP sinks, do them later.
  • 📌 Quests > grinding: One quest can hand you more XP than a day of training, for free.

1. 🧭 The Core Problem: Too Many Skills, Too Little Direction

A new OSRS account has 23 skills, and a returning player who last logged in years ago faces a game that has more than doubled in content since they left. The instinct is to "level everything evenly" or to chase the skill with the flashiest reward shown in a YouTube thumbnail. Both instincts waste enormous amounts of time, because skills in this game have wildly different return on effort, and almost none of them exist in isolation.

Consider what actually happens when you pick the "wrong" first skill. If you spend your first two weeks power-training Woodcutting to 60, you have a pile of logs, a slightly faster axe, and almost no new access. If you instead spend those two weeks on combat and quests, you have the Dragon Scimitar, Barrows gloves, Protect prayers, a Slayer task list feeding your bank, and the Agility to move between resources quickly. Same clock, completely different account. The Woodcutter is not "behind" in a moral sense — they simply trained a skill whose payoff is mostly cosmetic at low levels while skipping the skills whose payoff is structural.

The reason this happens is that skills have very different return on effort. Combat stats unlock content. Agility unlocks speed everywhere. Quests unlock gear and areas that would otherwise take millions of GP or dozens of levels. Meanwhile, Herblore and Construction mostly consume GP for small day-to-day benefits. A smart priority order front-loads the skills that unlock things and pushes the GP-sinks to later, when your bank can actually afford them. Think of it like building a house: you pour the foundation (combat, survival) before you argue about the paint color (a maxed artisan skill nobody needed yet).

💡 The One Question to Always Ask

Before training any skill, ask: "What does this unlock for me right now?" If the honest answer is "not much yet," defer it. If the answer is "the ability to do X content or wield Y weapon," do it now. This single habit will save you more hours than any tier list.

There is also a psychological trap worth naming up front: progress feels good even when it is the wrong progress. Watching a skill bar fill is satisfying, and the game is happy to let you fill any of them. But filling the bar on a skill that does not change your account is a distraction, not advancement. The framework below is designed to keep you on the bars that matter. The first-week progression guide turns this exact mindset into a day-by-day checklist if you want it handed to you.

2. 🧩 The Priority Framework: 4 Tiers

Every one of the 23 skills can be sorted into one of four tiers based on a single axis: does training this skill unlock new access, or does it mostly consume resources for a minor daily benefit? Your attention flows from Tier 1 down to Tier 4, and you do not seriously grind a lower tier until the tier above it is in a good place. This is not about "good" versus "bad" skills — every skill is useful eventually. It is purely about order.

The diagram below shows how the tiers connect. Notice that quests (Tier 3) are not a "skill" at all, yet they sit in the middle of the flow because they are the lever that moves you between tiers fastest. Combat unlocks the ability to do quests; quests unlock gear that makes money skills faster; money funds the expensive Tier 4 skills; Tier 4 skills then make the high-end content (bossing, end-game PvM) viable. It is a loop, but you enter it at Tier 1.

Tier 1 — Combat Foundation

Attack, Strength, Defence, Hitpoints, Prayer, Ranged/Magic (to ~40-60). These let you survive, complete quests, and start making money. Train first, always.

Tier 2 — Money & Unlocks

One gathering/money skill, Agility (to 60), and early Slayer. This funds your account and makes every later activity faster. You cannot skip this tier — an empty bank is what forces players into bad choices later.

Tier 3 — Quest-Locked Power

The quests that unlock gear, areas, and prayers. Not a "skill" per se, but the highest-leverage activity in the game. Done in the right order, quests hand you free levels and best-in-slot gear for almost no effort.

Tier 4 — Expensive Artisan Skills

Herblore, Construction, high-level Runecrafting, Smithing-to-99. Powerful but costly. Defer until you have a healthy bank — training them early starves you of the GP you need for gear and supplies.

3. ⚔️ Tier 1 — Combat Foundation (Train First)

Combat is the spine of every OSRS account. Even self-declared "skillers" eventually need it for quests, and almost every piece of unlockable gear is earned through combat or the quests that require combat levels. Get these stats to a useful floor before anything else — it is the prerequisite for Tier 3 (quests) and the fastest route to Tier 2 (money via Slayer).

① The 40-60 Melee Floor

Train Attack, Strength, and Defence together with the Controlled attack style at Chickens, Cows, then Rock Crabs (or Sand Crabs on the mainland). Aim for 40-60 in each early on. At 40 Attack you unlock the Rune Scimitar; at 60 Attack you unlock the Abyssal Whip (the best non-degradeable melee weapon for a very long time); at 60 Defence you unlock Barrows bodies and the ability to take far less damage in most content. Our combat training guide lays out the exact monster route and the gear swaps that keep your early training cheap.

A common question: should you train all three melee stats evenly, or rush one? For a new account, even training is correct because quests and Slayer reward you for having a balanced floor. The Abyssal Whip scales with your Attack and Strength, so a 60/60/60 account uses it far better than a 80/30/30 one. Keep them within a few levels of each other until the mid-60s.

② Prayer to 43 — Non-Negotiable

Prayer 43 unlocks the three Protect prayers (Protect from Magic, Missiles, and Melee), which make you nearly unkillable against most monsters and are required for every boss in the game. This single skill changes your entire game more than almost any other, because it turns "dangerous content" into "safe content." Train it by burying bones early, then via a Gilded Altar (members) for speed once you have a Player-Owned House. Details and the cheapest bone route are in the Prayer guide. If you only take one thing from this section: get Protect prayers before you do anything risky.

③ One Ranged or Magic Style to 40-60

You need a second combat style for monsters weak to it — most bosses have a preferred style, and some content (like certain quest fights) is close to impossible with only melee. Pick one and commit: Ranged (Rune Crossbow + bolts, later the Blowpipe) or Magic (trident / god spells, later the Tumeken's Shadow end-game). Train it alongside melee via Slayer so you are not grinding it separately — Slayer rewards all seven combat skills at once, which is exactly why it is the engine of Tier 1 and Tier 2 simultaneously.

💡 Hitpoints Trains Itself

Do not "train" Hitpoints on purpose — it rises automatically as you deal and take damage in combat. By the time your melee stats hit 60, your HP will usually be in the 50s on its own. Spend the time on Attack, Strength, and Defence instead.

⚠️ Do Not Chase 99 Combat First

Maxing combat before you have money, quests, and gear is a classic trap. High combat with no bank and no unlocks is a strong account that can do almost nothing useful yet. Hit the 40-60 floor, get Prayer 43, then move on. You will return to combat continuously through Slayer anyway.

4. 💰 Tier 2 — Money & Unlocks (Fund Your Account)

Once you can survive, your job is to build a GP buffer and unlock movement speed. Two things matter most here, plus a third that bridges into Tier 3. An empty bank is what eventually forces players to waste time on low-effort methods or to skip content they cannot afford — so funding yourself early is not "greed," it is what protects your priority order.

④ Pick ONE Money Skill and Actually Use It

Best beginner money skills

  • Mining Iron (15+): 50-100K GP/hr, trivial to learn, feeds Smithing later, and iron is always in demand.
  • Fishing Lobsters (40+): 50-150K GP/hr, also trains Cooking, and lobsters are a staple food for combat.
  • Thieving Cake Stalls (5+): 50-100K GP/hr, fast early cash with almost no requirements — the best "first 200K" method.

You do not need all three. Pick the one you find least boring and run it in short sessions while you do other things. The goal is a 5-10M GP buffer, not a maxed skill. That buffer is what lets you buy quest supplies, a Graceful set, and the occasional piece of gear without interrupting your progression. The beginner money making guide expands this list with methods that scale as your stats climb.

⑤ Agility to 60 — Get Graceful Early

Agility is a "force multiplier": higher Agility = faster run energy recovery = less time running between resources = more XP and GP everywhere you go. Push to 60 Agility and buy the Graceful armor set (it weighs nothing and restores run energy). It is the single best quality-of-life upgrade for a new account, and it pays for itself within days by cutting travel time. Train on rooftop courses: Gnome Stronghold (1-30), Al-Kharid (30-50), then any 50+ course such as the Varrock or Canifis rooftops. The Agility training guide covers the optimal course order and the shortcuts that matter.

⑥ Start Slayer (Combat 40-50)

Slayer is the best combat training method in the game — it grants XP in all seven combat skills at once and drops valuable loot, which is why it sits at the intersection of Tier 1 and Tier 2. Start with Turael in Burthorpe (no requirements) for fast, easy tasks, then move to a real master like Mazchna or Vannaka as your combat climbs. Even a few tasks build your bank and your combat stats together, and Slayer is what naturally carries you from "new account" to "mid-game" without a separate grind. See the Slayer beginner guide for task strategy and which masters to use when.

Tier 2 Daily Loop (≈1 hour)

10 min Agility course → 20 min money skill → 30 min Slayer/enhanced combat. Repeat. This funds Tier 4 later without ever feeling like a grind, because each part feeds the others.

5. 🗝️ Tier 3 — Quest-Locked Power (The Unlock Order)

Quests are the highest-leverage activity for a new account, and the part of the priority order that most players underrate. A single quest can grant tens of thousands of XP and unlock gear, areas, or prayers that would otherwise take weeks of grinding. The mistake is doing quests randomly — you should do the ones that unlock more quests and content first, because they compound.

⑦ Why Quest Order Matters

Some quests are prerequisites for others. Doing them in the right sequence means you are never blocked waiting on a requirement. More importantly, certain quests hand you free combat levels — Waterfall Quest alone gives 13,750 Attack and 13,750 Strength XP, enough to skip dozens of hours of crab-killing — and unlock gear like the Dragon Scimitar (Monkey Madness I) or Barrows gloves (Recipe for Disaster). A quest done at the right moment is effectively free levels and free gear; a quest done out of order is just a checkbox.

⑧ The "Free XP" Quests to Do Immediately

Quests that boost your account for almost no effort

  • Waterfall Quest — huge free Attack/Strength XP, zero combat requirement, do it the moment you can.
  • Knights' Sword — big free Smithing XP that saves a long grind.
  • Tree Gnome Village + Grand Tree — unlocks areas and some XP, plus Gnome gliders.
  • Monkey Madness I — unlocks the Dragon Scimitar, the best pre-70 melee weapon in the game.
  • Recipe for Disaster (subquests) — unlocks Barrows gloves, the best all-round gloves until late game.

The best quests for new members guide ranks these and dozens more by the unlock value they provide, which is the perfect companion to the order table later in this article. Treat that list as your quest "shopping list" once combat lets you start checking items off.

💡 Use the Wiki's Quest XP List

Before grinding any skill, check the OSRS Wiki "Quest Experience Rewards" page. You will often find a quest that hands you the exact levels you were about to spend a day training for — for free. This is the single highest-Efficiency habit in the game.

6. 🏗️ Tier 4 — Expensive Artisan Skills (Defer)

These skills are powerful but expensive. Training them early drains the GP you need for gear and supplies, and most of their benefits only matter at high levels or for late-game bossing. Defer until your bank is healthy — typically after you have finished the unlocks above and built a real cash pile.

Herblore — Defer until 10M+ GP

Potions are amazing for bossing, but training Herblore from scratch can cost 150-250M GP to 99. Grow your own herbs via Farming and only power-train it once you have surplus cash. It is a long-term project, not a week-one task. The Herblore 1-99 guide shows the cheapest training path when you are ready.

Construction — Defer until 20M+ GP

You mainly want a Player-Owned House for the Gilded Altar (fast Prayer XP) and jewelry box (teleports). Those unlock at 38-50 Construction. You do not need 99. Build to ~50 and stop — saving 150M+ GP versus maxing it. The Construction guide covers the altar and the cost-efficient route.

Prayer-to-99 & Smithing-to-99 — Defer

Both are massive GP sinks. You only need Prayer 43 (then 70 for Piety later, which is a real bossing upgrade). Smithing is useful but can be trained slowly via Blast Furnace as a side activity. Do not make these early priorities — they eat the GP that should be buying your unlocks.

The Defer Rule

If a skill costs more GP than your current bank, it is a Tier 4 skill — come back later. Your first 30 days should be about unlocking access, not about maxing expensive skills. An unlocked account with mid stats outperforms a maxed artisan with no gear every single time.

💡 Quest Points Are a Gate, Not a Goal

Some quest chains (notably Recipe for Disaster) require a certain number of Quest Points before you can start. Knock out a handful of easy F2P and low-requirement member quests early so you are never blocked when a high-value quest needs the points.

8. 🗓️ Your First 30 Days — Priority Plan

Translate the tiers into a month. This is a rhythm, not a strict schedule — adjust to your playtime. The chart below shows how your unlocks should accumulate week by week; the exact GP numbers will vary, but the shape is what matters.

Week 1 — Combat Floor

Melee to 40-50 (Controlled at Crabs), Prayer to 25-43, earn your first 200-500K GP from a money skill. Goal: survive most content and protect-pray.

Week 2 — Unlocks & Cash

Run the "free XP" quests (Waterfall, Knights' Sword), push Agility toward 50, start Thieving/Slayer. Goal: Dragon Scimitar path open, 1-2M GP bank.

Week 3 — Graceful & Slayer

Agility to 60, buy Graceful, commit to Slayer tasks. Goal: faster everywhere, combat stats climbing via Slayer, 3-5M GP.

Week 4 — Gear & Next Steps

Start Recipe for Disaster subquests for Barrows gloves, finish melee to 60-70. Goal: "mid-game ready" — 70/70/70 combat, 43+ Prayer, Graceful, 5M+ GP.

💡 Returning Players: Start at Week 2

If you are a returning player with some stats already, skip Week 1, audit your quest log for the unlocks above, and jump into the Slayer + Graceful loop. Your old stats likely already clear the Tier 1 floor, so your bottleneck is almost always missing quests, not missing levels.

10. 🆓 F2P vs P2P: How the Priority Shifts

Membership unlocks the quests that do most of the heavy lifting in this guide, so the priority order is not identical for free-to-play and pay-to-play accounts. Understanding the difference stops you from grinding the wrong thing while waiting to subscribe.

① Free-to-Play Priority

In F2P you have 15 skills and no quest log worth speaking of, so the plan compresses to: get Combat to the F2P cap (40 melee, 40 Ranged/Magic, Prayer 43 if you can afford the bones), then run the F2P money skills — Mining Iron, Fishing, and Woodcutting — to build a buffer. Save 500K-1M GP before you become a member so you can immediately buy quest supplies and a Graceful set. F2P is essentially a "prep phase" for the real priority order that begins at membership.

② Pay-to-Play Priority

Membership is where Tier 3 (quests) explodes in value. The moment you subscribe, pivot hard toward the quest unlock order in Section 7 — that is the fastest route from "new member" to "mid-game ready." Agility, Slayer, and the free-XP quests all require membership to shine, so they jump to the front of your list the day your membership activates.

💡 Don't Delay Membership Too Long

F2P skills are cheaper and give a head start, which is why a short F2P prep is smart. But membership unlocks the quests that do most of the heavy lifting in this guide, so don't camp F2P for months — the XP you "save" is dwarfed by the free quest XP you are missing.

11. 🚫 Common New Player Mistakes to Avoid

These are the patterns that quietly waste the most new-player time. None of them look like a mistake while you are doing it — that is exactly why they are worth calling out explicitly.

⚠️ The Five Traps
  • Grinding a skill just because the bar moves. Progress feels good; wrong progress still feels good. Always ask what the skill unlocks.
  • Skipping quests for "real training." You are leaving free levels and best-in-slot gear on the table. Do the unlock order.
  • Maxing one stat to 99 early. A 99 in anything does not help if you have no gear, no bank, and no unlocks to use it with.
  • Training Tier 4 skills on a fresh bank. Herblore and Construction eat millions you needed for gear. Defer until 10-20M GP.
  • Ignoring Agility. No Graceful means every trip is slower, every hour yields less. Get 60 Agility early — it compounds.

The throughline is simple: new players optimize for visible progress (a higher number) instead of structural progress (a new unlock). The number will come on its own once the structure is in place. Trust the tiers, run the quests, fund the bank, and the levels follow.

12. ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

What should I train first as a brand-new account?

Combat to 40-50 (Attack/Strength/Defence together via Controlled), then Prayer to 43. Those two unlock questing and survival, which everything else builds on. See the combat guide for the exact route from level 1.

Should I do F2P skills before becoming a member?

Yes, briefly, if you are undecided. F2P skills are cheaper and give a head start, and you should save 500K-1M GP before membership so you can buy quest supplies immediately. But membership unlocks the quests that do most of the heavy lifting, so don't delay it for months.

Which skill makes the most GP for a beginner?

The most reliable are Mining Iron, Fishing Lobsters, and Thieving Cake Stalls — each 50-150K GP/hr with minimal requirements. But the highest-value "GP" activity long-term is Slayer, because it trains combat and drops loot simultaneously, so it funds you while it levels you.

Why defer Herblore and Construction?

Both are enormous GP sinks (150M+ GP to 99) whose biggest benefits only matter at high levels or for late-game bossing. Training them early starves your account of the GP you need for gear and supplies. Build a 10-20M GP bank first, then approach them as projects, not priorities.

What quest order gives the best early power?

Waterfall Quest → Knights' Sword → Monkey Madness I (Dragon Scimitar) → Recipe for Disaster subquests (Barrows gloves). That sequence hands you free combat XP and the two best early weapons/gloves in the game, which then make every other activity easier and faster.

Do I need 99 in anything to be "mid-game ready"?

No. Mid-game ready means roughly 70/70/70 combat, Prayer 43 (later 70), Graceful, Barrows gloves, and a 5M+ GP bank — not a single 99. A balanced, unlocked account outperforms a one-trick 99 every time at this stage.

How important is Agility really?

Very. Agility has almost no flashy reward, but Graceful (at 60) restores run energy and weighs nothing, cutting travel time across the entire game. Faster travel means more XP and GP per hour everywhere. It is the quietest high-value skill in the game — train it early and never regret it.

Should I follow this order if I am a returning player?

Partially. Your old stats likely already clear the Tier 1 combat floor, so skip straight to auditing your quest log for the Tier 3 unlocks and the Slayer + Graceful loop. Your bottleneck is almost always missing quests, not missing levels — the new player guide helps you audit what you already have.

Is Slayer worth starting at low combat?

Yes, with the right master. Start at Turael in Burthorpe (no requirements) for fast, easy tasks, then move to Mazchna at Combat 50. Slayer is the most efficient combat training in the game because it rewards all seven combat skills and pays you in loot while it levels you.

What is the single biggest new-player time waste?

Training a Tier 4 artisan skill (Herblore, Construction, Smithing-to-99) on a fresh, low bank. It drains the GP you needed for gear and quests, and its benefits barely matter until late game. Defer them using the Defer Rule: if it costs more GP than your bank, it is a later problem.

🏁 Final Tips — Your First Three Sessions

You now know what to train and in what order. Make it concrete:

→ Session 1

Train melee to 40 and start Prayer toward 43. You are building the survival floor everything else depends on.

→ Session 2

Run Waterfall Quest and Knights' Sword for free XP, then a money skill for your first buffer of GP.

→ Session 3

Push Agility to 60 for Graceful and begin Slayer. From here the loop runs itself.
