1. Construction Basics & Your Player-Owned House

Construction is the skill of building and furnishing your very own Player-Owned House (POH). Unlike most skills where you simply click a rock or a tree, Construction requires you to teleport to your house, walk to a building hotspot, and assemble furniture from planks, nails, bolts of cloth, and a handful of other materials. The experience you gain is awarded the moment the object is built, and the same object can often be demolished and rebuilt to grind XP indefinitely. That loop is the heart of every fast Construction method in 2026.

Before you can build anything you need to actually own a house. Head to one of the Estate Agents — the most convenient one stands just south of the Varrock East Bank. Talk to him and purchase a basic house for 1,000 coins. You can later upgrade the exterior style (stone, whitewashed stone, fancy stone, tropical wood, and the expensive marble and Gothic styles), but for pure training the cheapest Basic Wood shell is perfectly fine and you should never waste millions on cosmetics. The house style does not change what you can build inside; it only changes appearance and the upfront cost.

Every POH is divided into a grid of rooms. You start with a single Parlour and a Garden, and you expand by paying the Estate Agent to add rooms in the available squares around your existing layout. Each room has one or more building hotspots (a glowing marker on the floor) where furniture can be placed. To actually build, you must be standing inside your house, have a saw and a hammer in your inventory (or tool belt), and carry the required materials. The Crystal saw, obtainable after the Icthlarin's Little Helper quest, is strongly recommended because it grants a invisible +3 Construction boost to your effective level while held. That +3 is the difference between being locked out of a high-XP object and being able to build it.

🎯 First-Hour Checklist

  • Buy a house from the Estate Agent (1,000 coins).
  • Complete Icthlarin's Little Helper for the Crystal saw (+3 boost).
  • Keep a normal saw, hammer, and teleport to house method ready.
  • Unlock the Teleport to House spell (requires 40 Magic, or use a house tablet / Portal Chamber).
  • Stock up on planks from the sawmill or the Grand Exchange before you begin.

The fastest way to reach the early levels before oak larders unlock is to repeatedly build and demolish cheap furniture. From level 1 you can build crude wooden chairs in your Parlour, but a far better early grind is oak chairs (level 8, one oak plank, 120 XP) in a Bedroom, or oak tables (level 16, four oak planks, 240 XP) in the Dining Room or Workshop. Because you demolish and rebuild, you only ever pay for the planks once and then recycle them. Even before you can afford a servant, this "build and remove" loop gets you to 33 quickly and cheaply.

💡 Why the crystal saw matters more than you think

The +3 boost is not a temporary potion effect — it applies for as long as the Crystal saw is in your inventory. Several key training objects sit right at a level boundary (for example oak larders at 33 and many teak/mahogany pieces later), so the boost lets you start a method three levels earlier than you otherwise could. It is the single highest-value free unlock for any Construction trainer.

Another concept you must understand before the methods below is the servant. Servants are hired from the Servants' Guild in Ardougne and, once paid, can fetch items from your bank and un-note stacks of planks for you. The two you care about are the Butler (7,500 coins per trip, brings 16 noted planks) and the Demon butler (10,000 coins per trip, brings 26 noted planks). The Demon butler is the gold standard for any plank-bulk method because he delivers the largest stack per trip, minimizing the number of times you interrupt your building to call him. You will see him referenced constantly in the oak larder section.

If your gold is tight, do not panic — Construction can still be trained on a budget. Our beginner money-making guide covers the safest low-level gold sources so you can fund your first few hundred thousand coins of planks without stress. And because you will be shuffling hundreds of planks, logs, and noted stacks in and out of your bank, good bank organization is essential — see our bank & inventory management guide for tab layouts that keep your building materials one click away.

2. The Oak Larder Method (Level 33+)

Once you hit level 33 Construction, the single most reliable training method in the game opens up: the oak larder. An oak larder is built in the Kitchen at the larder hotspot. It costs 8 oak planks and awards 480 XP per build. The magic of the method is that when you demolish the larder (right-click the object and choose "Remove"), the eight oak planks drop straight back into your inventory. You then rebuild, remove, rebuild, remove — and the same eight planks carry you through thousands of builds. Your plank supply is effectively infinite; the only thing you actually spend is the servant's un-noting fee.

To run oak larders efficiently you need a Demon butler (or Butler) standing by. The workflow looks like this: you hand the butler a stack of noted oak planks, he un-notes them into your inventory, you build an oak larder, you demolish it, and you repeat. Because each larder consumes eight planks and the Demon butler delivers 26 at a time, one butler trip fuels roughly three larders before you need to call him again. With practice and a clean inventory layout you can sustain roughly 200,000 to 250,000 XP per hour, which is among the best safe, non-combat XP rates in the entire game.

🎯 Step-by-Step Oak Larder Loop

  • Build a Kitchen in your house (any style) with a larder hotspot.
  • Hire a Demon butler from the Servants' Guild in Ardougne.
  • Fill your bank with noted oak planks and carry a saw, hammer, and Crystal saw.
  • Call the butler with "Send away" then "Bring planks" to un-note 26 at a time.
  • Build the oak larder, right-click "Remove" to get the 8 planks back, repeat.
  • Top up noted planks from your bank via the butler's "Fetch from bank" option.

The reason oak larders remain popular in 2026 is consistency. Mahogany tables are faster, but they burn through millions of coins of mahogany planks that are not returned. Oak larders, by contrast, only cost you the butler fee, which works out to roughly 5 to 7 gp per XP depending on how efficiently you cycle the butler. That is dramatically cheaper than any method that consumes mahogany, and it is far less tedious than the older "oak doors" or "gnome bench" loops that required precise clicking. For an intermediate player who wants a predictable, low-risk grind from 33 up toward the high 70s, oak larders are the safe default.

💡 Keep your butler alive and paid

Your servant only works while he is "present" in your house and you have the coins to pay him. If you run out of gold mid-session he stops delivering planks and your XP rate collapses. Always enter a training session with at least a few hundred thousand spare coins, and consider using the "repeat" right-click option on the larder hotspot to shave milliseconds off every build.

One subtle optimization: the demon butler is worth the extra 2,500 coins per trip over the regular butler because the larger 26-plank delivery means fewer interruptions. Every interruption is dead time where you are not gaining XP, so over a multi-hour session the upgraded servant pays for itself many times over. If you are an Ironman with no spare gold, oak larders are still viable using the Build and Demolish loop by manually banking your own planks, though your XP per hour will be lower without a butler feeding you noted stacks.

A word on oak larders vs. the alternatives at this level band: between 33 and roughly 74 Construction, oak larders are the sweet spot. Around level 74 the math starts to shift because higher-XP objects (teak benches, gnome benches, and eventually mahogany tables) become available, and the fixed butler cost per hour stays the same while the XP-per-build climbs. From 74 onward most players either switch to Mahogany Homes for cost-efficiency or push mahogany tables for raw speed. We cover the crossover in the cost tables below.

3. Mahogany Homes — Our Recommended Route

If we had to recommend one Construction method for the majority of players in 2026, it would be the Mahogany Homes minigame. Released by Jagex as a refreshing alternative to the plank-grind, Mahogany Homes casts you as a contractor repairing the houses of NPCs across Gielinor. You accept a contract from one of several contract givers (in Varrock, Hosidius, Ardougne, and Falador), travel to the assigned property, and build the specific furniture the contract lists. When the homeowners return and inspect the work, you are paid in Construction XP and Mahogany Homes points.

Why do we rate it above oak larders for most players? Three reasons. First, it is far less click-intensive and monotonous — you are moving between houses and solving small build puzzles rather than hammering the same larder thousands of times. Second, the XP rewards are generous and front-loaded, with contract completion bonuses that make the effective gp-per-XP excellent when you supply your own cheap planks. Third, the Mahogany Homes points can be spent on useful rewards like the Carpenter's outfit (a 2.5% XP boost when the full set is worn) and the Plank sack that lets you carry more planks per trip. Those rewards compound your gains on every future Construction session.

🎯 How to Run Mahogany Homes Efficiently

  • Talk to a contract giver and accept the highest-tier contract you can reach.
  • Read the contract: it lists exactly which furniture to build at that house.
  • Bring the correct planks (usually oak early, teak/mahogany later) and a saw + hammer.
  • Use the house teleport or a mounted glance to reach each contract fast.
  • Build all listed items, then wait for the homeowner to return and pay you.
  • Spend points on the Carpenter's outfit first for a permanent XP multiplier.

The contracts scale in tiers. Early tiers send you to simple one- or two-item builds using oak planks, while the highest tier sends you to elaborate mansions requiring teak and mahogany. Because you supply the planks yourself, your cost is entirely under your control — and this is exactly where the PLANK MAKE spell becomes a game-changer, because converting your own logs to planks can cut your material spend by roughly 30% versus buying planks from the sawmill. A player who combines Mahogany Homes with PLANK MAKE and the Carpenter's outfit routinely achieves the best gp-per-XP in the skill.

Experience rates at Mahogany Homes vary with tier and your efficiency, but a focused intermediate player typically sees 60,000 to 90,000 XP per hour at higher tiers, with the added benefit of being far more AFK-friendly than oak larders. You are not locked into a tight build-demolish rhythm; you travel, build a handful of items, and collect. For players who dislike the repetitive plank-loop or who are training Construction alongside other goals, this is the most sustainable path to 99.

💡 Prioritize the Carpenter's outfit early

The four-piece Carpenter's outfit (hat, top, trousers, boots) grants a cumulative 2.5% Construction XP boost when worn together. Because Mahogany Homes is where you earn the points to buy it, getting the outfit should be your first spending priority. A 2.5% boost may sound small, but across 13 million XP to 99 it saves you hundreds of thousands of XP worth of materials and time.

If you are an Ironman, Mahogany Homes is even more attractive because it does not require millions of liquid gold — you can farm your own logs, convert them with PLANK MAKE, and train almost for free apart from rune costs. The minigame also pairs beautifully with smart bank management since you will be shuttling oak, teak, and mahogany planks between contracts. And because it is a relaxed, low-risk activity, it is a great skill to train while you are also working toward gold goals from our money-making guide.

4. PLANK MAKE: Cut Your Plank Costs by 30%

The single biggest lever you have over Construction cost is the PLANK MAKE Lunar spell. Normally, turning logs into planks means either buying finished planks on the Grand Exchange (expensive) or paying the Sawmill operator a conversion fee (cheaper, but requires a trip to Varrock or the sawmill in the Woodcutting Guild). PLANK MAKE lets you convert logs directly in your inventory — including noted logs — for just 70% of the sawmill price. That single fact is what separates an overpriced Construction grind from a lean one.

To use PLANK MAKE you must be on the Lunar spellbook (unlocked by completing Lunar Diplomacy) and have 86 Magic. The spell consumes one Astral rune, one Earth rune, and one Nature rune per cast, which is inexpensive, and it converts a full stack of up to 28 noted logs at once. Because the rune cost is trivial next to plank savings, the spell pays for itself almost instantly. At 86 Magic you can convert oak logs; higher Magic simply means more casts per inventory and faster bulk processing.

🎯 Setting Up a PLANK MAKE Plank Pipeline

  • Unlock Lunar spellbook via Lunar Diplomacy and train Magic to 86.
  • Buy or chop bulk oak / teak / mahogany logs (noted) on the GE.
  • Cast PLANK MAKE on the noted stack — each log becomes a plank at 70% of sawmill cost.
  • Hand the noted planks to your butler (oak larders) or carry them into Mahogany Homes.
  • Repeat; the 30% saving compounds across every single plank you ever use.

The savings are concrete. A regular plank costs 100 at the sawmill but only 70 via PLANK MAKE. An oak plank costs 250 at the sawmill but 175 with the spell. A teak plank drops from 500 to 350, and a mahogany plank from 1,500 to 1,050. When you consider that a single mahogany table uses six mahogany planks (a 2,700-coin saving per table) or that oak larders cycle hundreds of oak planks per hour, the spell can save you millions of coins across a 1–99 journey. For Ironmen it is effectively mandatory, since it is the only convenient way to turn farmed logs into the planks training demands.

💡 PLANK MAKE works on noted logs

You do not need to un-note logs first. Cast PLANK MAKE directly on a stack of noted logs and the entire stack converts in one cast, provided you have the coins in your inventory to cover 70% of the cost for every log. Keep a healthy coin stack and a few Astral/Earth/Nature runes on you, and you can process thousands of planks between bank trips.

There is a small catch: PLANK MAKE requires 86 Magic, which not every Construction trainer has. If your Magic is lower, you have two options. You can either pause Construction to push Magic first (the Lunar grind doubles as decent progress toward the Dream Mentor and Ourania Altar routes), or you can simply buy planks from the sawmill and accept the slightly higher cost until you unlock the spell. For players who are only interested in Construction and have the gold, buying planks outright is perfectly fine — but for anyone watching their budget, 86 Magic is the unlock that transforms the economy of the skill.

Combining PLANK MAKE with the methods above creates powerful synergies. Running oak larders with PLANK MAKE means your eight planks per larder cost you 1,400 coins instead of 2,000 at the sawmill — but remember the planks are returned on demolish, so the spell's benefit there is mainly on the initial stack you buy. The bigger win is in Mahogany Homes and mahogany tables, where every plank is consumed and the 30% saving applies to each and every one. Whichever route you pick, PLANK MAKE is the highest-leverage money saver in this entire guide.

6. Must-Build POH Rooms for Trainers

Your Player-Owned House is not just a training ground — it is a toolkit that pays you back for every other skill and activity. As an intermediate Construction trainer, you should prioritize a handful of must-build rooms that make training faster and your account stronger. You do not need every room; you need the ones that remove friction from your daily routine.

6.1 The Kitchen (oak larders)

The Kitchen is non-negotiable because it hosts the larder hotspot at the core of the oak larder method. Build at least one Kitchen with a larder and, ideally, a stone space and a clockmakers bench for minor extras. The Kitchen is cheap to add and is the workhorse of your early-mid Construction grind.

6.2 The Chapel (Prayer synergy)

A Chapel is one of the highest-value rooms in any POH because it lets you bury or offer bones at a boosted XP rate. With the right icon and burner setup you can train Prayer efficiently using the bones dropped by your Slayer and boss kills, turning one room into a permanent XP multiplier for a second skill. For an intermediate account this is the single best "two birds, one stone" room you can build.

6.3 The Workshop (flatpacks & tools)

The Workshop unlocks flatpack making (build furniture that you can trade or use as furnishings later) and houses the Tool store (more tool storage) and the clockmaker. While not central to the XP methods above, it is where many players craft items for profit or for furnishing altars, and it rounds out a complete training house.

6.4 The Study (teleports &> utility)

A Study with a lectern lets you write magic tablets (including Teleport to House tablets) and mount your Construction cape for a free daily teleport. The Study is what keeps your training loops tight, because a house teleport on a single click eliminates the walk from a portal every session.

6.5 The Portal Chamber

The Portal Chamber lets you place teleport portals to key locations (Fish Guild, Camelot, Kourend, etc.). For a Construction trainer who is also doing money-making or bank runs,Portal Chamber teleports save enormous amounts of time and pair naturally with the house teleport.

🎯 Minimum viable POH for a 2026 trainer

  • Kitchen (larder) — for oak larders.
  • Chapel (boosted bones) — Prayer XP while you train.
  • Study (lectern + mounted cape) — cheap house teleports.
  • Portal Chamber — fast travel to money-making spots.
  • Workshop — flatpacks and tool storage.
💡 The Construction cape is your endgame perk

At 99 you unlock the Construction cape, which grants a one-time daily boost to 100 (handy for building anything) and a free teleport to your house. Mount it in your Study so every session starts with a single click teleport. It is the capstone of everything in this guide.

Building these rooms also grants a healthy chunk of XP on its own, so constructing your "dream training house" doubles as progress toward 99. Plan your layout on the room grid before you start placing walls — rearranging rooms later costs the Estate Agent a fee, so a little forethought saves both gold and frustration.

7. Construction FAQs

What is the fastest way to get 1–99 Construction in 2026?

The absolute fastest route is oak larders from 33 to the high 70s, then a switch to mahogany tables (level 86) for the final stretch, which can exceed 900,000 XP per hour with a Demon butler and PLANK MAKE planks. Expect to pay a premium in gold for that speed — typically 90+ million coins from 33 to 99. If you value engagement over raw speed, Mahogany Homes is the better all-round choice.

Do oak larders really give the planks back?

Yes. When you right-click an oak larder and choose "Remove" (demolish), the eight oak planks used to build it are returned to your inventory. That is the entire premise of the method: you pay once for the planks and then recycle them forever, with your only recurring cost being the servant's un-noting fee. This makes oak larders one of the cheapest sustainable trainers in the game.

Is PLANK MAKE worth getting 86 Magic for?

For almost any budget-conscious player, yes. PLANK MAKE converts logs to planks for 70% of the sawmill price and works on noted stacks, saving roughly 30% on every plank you consume. Across millions of planks that is tens of millions of coins saved. If you have the gold to buy planks outright and simply do not want to train Magic, you can skip it — but for self-sufficient and Ironman accounts it is effectively mandatory.

Which servant should I hire for plank methods?

The Demon butler is the best choice. He costs 10,000 coins per trip but delivers 26 noted planks, working out to about 385 coins per plank and fewer interruptions than the regular Butler (who brings only 16 for 7,500). Fewer interruptions means higher XP per hour, so the Demon butler pays for himself quickly during any long session.

Why is Mahogany Homes recommended over pure plank grinding?

Mahogany Homes is recommended because it combines decent XP rates with a much lower feeling of grind, awards Mahogany Homes points for the Carpenter's outfit (a 2.5% XP boost) and the plank sack, and keeps your cost low when you supply your own PLANK MAKE planks. It is also far more AFK-friendly and Ironman-friendly than the tight oak larder loop, making it the most sustainable path to 99 for most players.

Can I train Construction on a tight budget?

Yes. Start with free or cheap build-and-demolish loops (oak chairs, oak tables) up to 33, then run oak larders or Mahogany Homes while supplying your own PLANK MAKE planks from farmed or cheaply bought logs. Pair the grind with sessions from our money-making guide and keep your materials organized using our bank management guide. Construction is expensive, but it can be chipped away gradually rather than bought in one expensive burst.
