1. How We Define Boss Difficulty (Gear / Mechanics / Supplythreshold)

Before we drop a single boss into a tier, we have to agree on what "difficult" actually means in Old School RuneScape. Difficulty is not one number. A boss can be mechanically trivial but gear-gated, or mechanically brutal but accessible with a few million in gear. In this 2026 tier list we score every boss across three independent axes, and the highest of the three usually decides the tier. If you only read one section of this guide, read this one, because it explains why a fight sits where it does instead of just telling you to be afraid of it.

1.1 Gearthreshold (The Entry Cost)

Gearthreshold is the cheapest realistic setup that lets you survive and deal competitive damage. A boss with a low gearthreshold can be attempted the moment you hit the combat stats; a boss with a high gearthreshold demands hundreds of millions of GP or maxed melee/ranged/magic. Vorkath, for example, has a moderate gearthreshold because a blessed dragonhide swap plus an armadyl crossbow or blowpipe will do the job, whereas Nex expects near-maxed gear and a toxic blowpipe or better. We rate gearthreshold on a sliding scale from "void + rune" (almost nothing) up to "full ancestral, tormented bracelet, and a megarare" (endgame wealth).

🎯 The Three Axes, In Plain English

  • Gearthreshold: how expensive and how maxed does your equipment need to be before the kill is even plausible?
  • Mechanics: how many distinct attacks must you recognize, and how punishing is a single misstep?
  • Supplythreshold: how many food, potion, and prayer doses do you burn per kill, and how deep is your sustain?

1.2 Mechanics (The Skill Ceiling)

Mechanics separate the "click the thing until it dies" bosses from the "one wrong tick and you wipe your team" bosses. A mechanical score looks at telegraph clarity, the number of prayer switches, movement requirements, and whether failure cascades to other players. The Giant Mole has almost no mechanics: it digs, you follow, you hit it. Theatre of Blood has layered mechanics where the maiden's crabs, the bloat's tiling, and Verzik's reds and webs all demand frame-aware play. We weigh mechanics heavily because, unlike gear, you cannot buy your way past them — you either learn the fight or you die in it.

1.3 Supplythreshold (The Sustain Wall)

Supplythreshold measures how much you must bring and how much you lose when things go wrong. A solo entry boss like the King Black Dragon might cost a few sharks and a prayer potion per trip. A Chamber of Xeric raid team burns hundreds of brews, restores, and overloads across a single run, and a failed ToB can delete an entire inventory of supplies in seconds. Supplythreshold also captures death cost: a Wilderness boss death can cost your whole untradeable setup, while a safe boss simply sends you to Lumbridge. We fold risk into this axis because, in 2026, the scariest "cost" is often the one you cannot insure.

💡 How To Use This List

Find your current account on the gear axis first. If your bank cannot clear the gearthreshold of a tier, skip down a tier — you will learn faster and lose less. Then use the mechanics column to pick a fight whose complexity you are ready to study. New to bossing entirely? Our New Player Boss Survival Guide 2026 walks through the absolute basics before you attempt any tier here.

1.4 Why We Avoid A Single "Hardness" Score

Collapsing everything into one number hides the truth. A maxed main with unlimited wealth may find Zulrah easy but Corrupted Gauntlet miserable, while an Ironman with no money finds the opposite. That is why every boss below carries three mini-ratings (Gear / Mechanics / Supply) on a 1–5 scale. Treat the tier as a shortcut and the three numbers as the real answer. The 2026 meta also shifts these numbers: the Gauntlet meta changes in 2026 quietly moved Corrupted Gauntlet's gearthreshold down for practiced players, which is why it sits in A and not S this year.

2. S Tier — The Ultimate Bosses (CoX, ToB, ToA, Nex)

S Tier is where Old School RuneScape stops being a game you play alone and becomes a coordination puzzle with lethal consequences. Every boss here expects either a tight team or a near-maxed solo, deep knowledge of multiple mechanics at once, and supply piles that would bankrupt a mid-game account. If you are reading this as a fresh boss fighter, do not start here — treat S Tier as the destination, not the starting line. The bosses in this tier are Chambers of Xeric (CoX), Theatre of Blood (ToB), Tombs of Amascut (ToA), and Nex.

2.1 Chambers of Xeric (CoX)

CoX is the original raid and remains the most popular because it scales to your team. The gearthreshold is the lowest of the S Tier bosses — a twisted bow or a tumbleweed of melee gear helps, but a coordinated team with standard gear can clear it. The mechanics, however, are unforgiving: Vanguards require precise role assignment, Vasa Nistirio demands tick-perfect crystal shielding, and Olm's hands, auto-attacks, and special attacks must be managed with perfect communication. Supplythreshold is high because a full raid can last twenty minutes with constant brews and overloads. CoX teaches you the raid mindset: callouts, roles, and trusting your team.

2.2 Theatre of Blood (ToB)

ToB is widely regarded as the hardest content in the game for a reason. The gearthreshold is brutal — you want Scythe of Vitur, Masori, and maxed stats, and a failed run costs the entire team their supplies. The mechanics are the deepest in OSRS: the Bloat's tiling, Xarpus's reflections, Verzik'sthree phases with reds, webs, and ball phases, and the relentless healer management. Supplythreshold is the highest in the game because deaths are permanent within a run and a single mistake chains to everyone. If you can consistently clear a ToB, you have effectively mastered PvM.

🎯 S Tier At A Glance

  • CoX: scalable raid, lower gear need, communication-heavy mechanics.
  • ToB: hardest fight, maxed gear, permanent death chains, deepest mechanics.
  • ToA: invocation-scaled raid, build your own difficulty, puzzle-heavy.
  • Nex: solo or mass, prayer-swapping spectacle, stamina and DPS check.

2.3 Tombs of Amascut (ToA)

ToA introduced the invocation system, which lets you dial the difficulty from a gentle solo to a nightmare endurance run. At base invocations the gearthreshold is moderate, but pushing toward 500+ invocations demands maxed gear and flawless play. The mechanics reward puzzle-solving: the wardens phase, the Het path, and the Babi scarab sections all punish players who skip learning. Supplythreshold scales with invocation count, and the longer the run the more your sustain matters. ToA is the most flexible S Tier boss and the best entry point into raids if you have a patient team.

2.4 Nex — Angel of Death

Nex sits in S Tier because of the combined gear and mechanic pressure rather than team size. You can fight her solo or in a mass, but either way you need near-maxed gear, confident prayer switching (especially between soul, body, and blood phases), and the discipline to manage shadows and the smoke phase. The supplythreshold is high because the fight is long and the DPS check is real: if your team cannot burn her fast enough, the bleed phases overwhelm you. Nex is the boss that proves you have "arrived" as a PvMer without requiring a full raid group.

💡 S Tier Reality Check

Do not let the tier label scare you away from CoX or low-invocation ToA forever — both are learnable with a good teacher. But respect the supply cost: bring more brews than you think you need, and never enter an S Tier fight without a plan for every phase. If you want to test raid-adjacent pressure solo first, the 2026 Gauntlet meta guide is a great bridge before you commit to a raid team.

3. A Tier — High-Difficulty Solos (Vorkath, The Nightmare, Corrupted Gauntlet)

A Tier is the sweet spot for the ambitious mid-game player. These bosses are fought solo, they hit brutally hard, and a single mechanical error can end the trip — but they do not require a raid team or hundreds of millions of GP upfront. The three anchors of this tier are Vorkath, The Nightmare (NM), and the Corrupted Gauntlet (CG). Each teaches a different high-level skill: Vorkath teaches prayer switching and reflect handling, The Nightmare teaches positioning under a multi-phase specter, and the Corrupted Gauntlet teaches self-sufficiency with no supplies at all.

3.1 Vorkath

Vorkath is the classic "first real boss" for players graduating from entry content, yet it earns A Tier because of its unforgiving attack patterns. The gearthreshold is moderate: an armadyl crossbow with ruby dragon bolts (e), a blessed d'hide body, and an anti-dragon shield will get you there, though a blowpipe or Dragon hunter lance speeds things up. The mechanics are the real test — you must recognize and counter the acid phase, the fire breath, the undead spawn (kill it or eat through the explosion), and the reflect attack where you must step under him. Supplythreshold is moderate but the DPS race means sloppy play drains your brews fast. Master Vorkath and you have proven you can solo a high-pressure fight.

3.2 The Nightmare (NM)

The Nightmare of Ashihama is a multi-phase spectacle boss that can be fought solo or in a group, and solos land squarely in A Tier. The gearthreshold is higher than Vorkath — you want decent ranged or melee with a fair amount of food, and the fight rewards the Inquisitor's armour or a twisted bow for speed. The mechanics are layered: the sleepwalkers, the husks, the spiders, and the three totems you must disable, followed by the parasites and the final phase with her sweep and roots. Supplythreshold is moderate but the fight is long, so sustain matters. The Nightmare is the boss that first teaches you to manage multiple threats at once.

🎯 A Tier Learning Order

  • Step 1 — Vorkath: learn prayer switches and reflect counters.
  • Step 2 — The Nightmare: learn multi-target priority and phases.
  • Step 3 — Corrupted Gauntlet: learn to fight with zero supplies.

3.3 Corrupted Gauntlet (CG)

The Corrupted Gauntlet is unique: you enter with nothing and must gather resources, craft a weapon and armor, and then defeat the Corrupted Hunllef with no food beyond what you cook. The gearthreshold is effectively zero because you make your own, but the mechanics are among the hardest in the game — you must dodge tornadoes, crystals, and prayer-flipping attacks while managing your own prayer and hitpoints. Supplythreshold is replaced by resource management: a bad prep run means a weak setup and an almost certain death. The 2026 Gauntlet meta changes refined the optimal prep routes, nudging CG into A rather than S for players who study the new routing. CG is the best solo teacher of fundamentals because it removes every crutch.

💡 A Tier Mindset

All three A Tier bosses punish panic. The fix is the same for each: learn the attack tell, lock in one consistent counter, and never greed an extra hit when your health is low. If you can farm Vorkath comfortably, you are ready for The Nightmare; if you can clear CG, you are ready to look at S Tier raids with confidence.

4. B Tier — Mid-Difficulty Bosses (GWD, Zulrah)

B Tier is where most players spend their first hundred boss kills. The gearthreshold is approachable, the mechanics are real but readable, and the supplythreshold is gentle enough that a death stings but does not end your account. The headline bosses here are the God Wars Dungeon (GWD) generals and Zulrah, with room for others like the Kraken and Sarachnis. These fights teach you to actually read a boss instead of just tanking hits, and they are the natural bridge from C Tier into A Tier.

4.1 God Wars Dungeon (GWD)

The four GWD generals — Kree'arra (armadyl), General Graardor (bandos), Commander Zilyana (saradomin), and K'ril Tsutsaroth (zamorak) — are the textbook B Tier bosses. The gearthreshold is low: a set of god-aligned protecting gear, a decent weapon, and some food will do, though a zamorakian spear or armadyl helps. The mechanics are simple compared to raids — each general has one or two special attacks and a bodyguard squad — but the dungeon itself adds pressure because you must build killcount and survive aggressive minions. Supplythreshold is moderate: you will burn food to the minions and occasionally a brew to a special. GWD is the best place to learn sustained DPS and basic prayer switching at scale.

4.2 Zulrah

Zulrah is the boss that divides opinion, which is exactly why it belongs in B Tier. The gearthreshold is low — a trident of the seas or swap, some prayer potions, and food — but the mechanics are a step above GWD because Zulrah rotates through fixed phases (snake forms and locations) that you must memorize. There is no "react" here; you must know the rotation. Supplythreshold is low because the fight is short once learned, and a death costs little. Zulrah rewards pattern recognition over reflexes, making it the perfect bridge toward Vorkath and beyond. Many players farm Zulrah for their first big money and then graduate to A Tier.

🎯 B Tier Rotation Suggestion

  • Week 1: farm one GWD general you can kill reliably for cash and confidence.
  • Week 2: learn the Zulrah rotation from a fixed path guide.
  • Week 3: add Kraken or Sarachnis for variety and easy supplies.

4.3 Other B Tier Contenders

Beyond the headliners, several bosses sit comfortably in B Tier. The Kraken is a near-prayer-free money printer with one simple special. Sarachnis is a forgiving mass boss that drops the dragon hunter wand and teaches positioning. The Chaos Elemental is a quirky fight with weapon-swap and stat-drain mechanics that reward awareness. None of these demand maxed gear, and all of them build the habits — prayer management, movement, resource awareness — you will need when you step up to A Tier. Treat B Tier as your "comfort zone" where you sharpen the basics before the pressure ramps up.

💡 Don't Skip B Tier

It is tempting to rush from the Mole straight to Vorkath, but players who grind GWD and Zulrah first learn the muscle memory — prayer flips, gear swaps, tick awareness — that makes A Tier click instead of frustrate. B Tier is where "I can boss" becomes "I am a boss." Before you wander into riskier content, the new player boss survival guide covers the universal habits that keep you alive in every tier.

5. C Tier — Entry-Level Bosses

C Tier is where every bossing journey should begin. The gearthreshold is minimal — often just rune or dragon equipment and some food — the mechanics are gentle or nonexistent, and the supplythreshold is so low that dying simply means a trip back to the bank. These bosses exist to teach you the absolute fundamentals: how to turn on the right prayer, how to eat before you hit zero, how to move out of a danger tile, and how to actually enjoy the loop of "show up, kill, loot, repeat." Do not rush past C Tier; the confidence you build here pays off at every higher tier.

5.1 Giant Mole

The Giant Mole is the gentlest boss in the game. The gearthreshold is almost nothing — a whip or dragon scimitar, some food, and a spade for the tunnels. The mechanics are trivial: it digs and relocates, and you follow it. Supplythreshold is the lowest of any boss because it barely hits through protect melee. The Mole is the perfect first kill for a player who has never toggled a boss prayer before, and it drops the mole skin and claws that fund your first upgrades. If you can kill the Mole, you can kill anything — slowly, then faster.

5.2 King Black Dragon (KBD)

The King Black Dragon is the next logical step. The gearthreshold is still low: an anti-dragon shield (or better, an antifire potion plus shield), some food, and a decent weapon. The mechanics introduce the first real lesson — the KBD's breath attacks hit hard unless you manage antifire, and it can poison and freeze you. Supplythreshold is low but higher than the Mole because the fights last longer. KBD is where you learn to respect dragonfire and to keep your inventory organized, a habit that matters at every tier above.

🎯 C Tier Starter Path

  • 1. Giant Mole: learn to pray and to follow a moving boss.
  • 2. King Black Dragon: learn antifire and inventory discipline.
  • 3. Barrows: learn multiple brothers and loot tables.
  • 4. Scurrius: learn a modern, scalable low-level boss.

5.3 Barrows & Scurrius

Barrows is a C Tier rite of passage: six brothers, each with a simple attack, and a reward chest that can pay for your whole starter setup. The gearthreshold is low and the mechanics are minimal, but you learn to fight several enemies in one trip and to manage prayer across multiple kills. Scurrius, added in a later update, is a deliberately approachable boss designed for new players, scalable in difficulty and a great confidence builder. Both teach the "multiple kills per trip" mindset that B and A Tier bosses rely on. Obor and Bryophyta round out C Tier as quick, low-commitment bosses that drop useful early gear.

💡 C Tier Is Not "Easy Mode Forever"

The point of C Tier is repetition, not mastery. Kill the Mole until prayer switching is automatic. Kill KBD until antifire management is second nature. Then move to B Tier with habits already baked in. Players who skip C Tier often struggle at Vorkath because they never learned the boring fundamentals that C Tier drills for free.

6. Where Should New Players Start?

If you have read this far and you are a new player, the answer is simple: start at C Tier, stay until the basics are automatic, then climb one tier at a time. The single biggest mistake new bosses make is jumping straight to Vorkath or a raid because the loot looks good. The loot is good, but the death cost and the frustration are worse, and most players who skip the ladder quit bossing entirely. Build the habit first, then chase the profit.

6.1 A Concrete First-Month Plan

Week one: kill the Giant Mole until you can do it without thinking about prayer. Week two: add the King Black Dragon and learn antifire discipline. Week three: run Barrows for cash and multi-kill practice. Week four: try the Kraken or Sarachnis from B Tier to taste a "real" mechanic. By the end of the month you will have the reflexes that make Zulrah and Vorkath approachable rather than terrifying. This is not a slow path — it is the fast path, because you avoid the weeks of dying that skippers lose.

6.2 When To Attempt A Tier

You are ready for A Tier when a B Tier boss feels boring because you never get hit. That boredom is the signal: your fundamentals are solid, and now you need harder mechanics to stay engaged. Start with Vorkath, because its gearthreshold is the lowest of the A Tier and its lessons (prayer switching, reflect counters) directly prepare you for everything above. From there, The Nightmare and then Corrupted Gauntlet will feel like natural steps rather than walls.

🎯 The Ladder, Summarized

  • C: Mole → KBD → Barrows → Scurrius (learn the basics).
  • B: GWD → Zulrah → Kraken (learn real mechanics).
  • A: Vorkath → Nightmare → CG (learn pressure).
  • S: CoX → ToA → Nex → ToB (learn coordination).

6.3 A Word On Risk And Wilderness Bosses

Some of the most profitable bosses live in the Wilderness, where a death costs everything you carry. As a new player you should not rush there — but when you are ready, the Wilderness bosses guide 2026 explains exactly how to minimize risk with minimal gear and when the profit is worth the danger. Likewise, the 2026 Gauntlet meta guide is your best prep for the Corrupted Gauntlet before you attempt it. And if you want the universal survival habits that apply to every tier, the new player boss survival guide is required reading before your first kill.

💡 The Only Rule That Matters

Climb the ladder. Every tier teaches something the next one assumes you already know. Skip a rung and you will pay for it in deaths, GP, and motivation. The bosses are not going anywhere — OSRS has run for over a decade, and the S Tier will still be there when your C Tier habits are rock solid.

7. Boss Difficulty FAQs

What is the absolute easiest boss for a brand new account?

The Giant Mole is the easiest boss in Old School RuneScape for a new account. It has almost no mechanics, barely hits through Protect from Melee, and only requires a spade, some food, and any decent melee weapon. It sits in C Tier for exactly this reason and is the perfect first kill to learn prayer switching and movement. After the Mole, the King Black Dragon is the natural next step because it introduces antifire management without a punishing gear requirement.

Do I need a maxed account to attempt S Tier raids?

Not strictly, but it helps a lot. Chambers of Xeric and low-invocation Tombs of Amascut are completable with a coordinated team and solid (not maxed) gear, because the raids scale and good communication covers individual weakness. Theatre of Blood, however, effectively demands near-maxed gear and deep mechanic knowledge because deaths chain to the whole team and supply costs are enormous. If you are not maxed, start with CoX or low-invocation ToA alongside experienced players before touching ToB.

Is Vorkath actually harder than Zulrah?

In our 2026 tier list, yes — Vorkath is A Tier and Zulrah is B Tier. The reason is mechanical: Zulrah is a memorized rotation with no real reaction needed once learned, while Vorkath forces you to recognize and counter several distinct attacks (acid, fire breath, undead spawn, reflect) under a DPS timer. Zulrah's gearthreshold is also lower. That said, players who hate memorization often find Zulrah harder than Vorkath, which is why the three-axis rating matters more than the single tier letter.

What gear do I need before my first Chambers of Xeric raid?

For a learner CoX, you want protecting ranged and melee gear, a decent weapon (armadyl crossbow, blowpipe, or a godsword at minimum), a few prayer potions, and a stack of brews and restores. The gearthreshold is the lowest of the S Tier bosses precisely because CoX scales to your team, but you should still be comfortable with prayer switching and basic movement. Bring more supplies than you think you need — a failed raid wastes the whole team's food, not just yours.

Are Wilderness bosses worth the risk for a new player?

Generally no, not at first. Wilderness bosses offer excellent profit but a death there can cost your entire untradeable and tradeable setup with no insurance, which is a devastating setback for a new account. Once you have a comfortable bank and understand risk management, the Wilderness bosses guide 2026 shows how to farm them safely with minimal risk. Treat Wilderness content as a B-or-A Tier milestone, not a C Tier starting point.

How long does it take to learn the Corrupted Gauntlet?

Most players need anywhere from ten to thirty failed attempts before their first Corrupted Gauntlet (CG) kill, because the fight removes every supply crutch and forces you to craft your own gear and then survive the Hunllef with perfect prayer and movement. The good news is that the 2026 Gauntlet meta changes streamlined the optimal prep route, cutting early deaths for players who follow the new routing. CG is hard, but it is the single best teacher of self-sufficient PvM fundamentals in the game.
