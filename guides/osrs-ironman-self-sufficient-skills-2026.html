OSRS Ironman Self-Sufficient Skills 2026 — Herblore, Construction & Farming Resource Loops Without the GE

Updated August 19, 2026  |  15 min read  |  By Yanchun Wu

The Grand Exchange is a main account's crutch. On an Ironman, Herblore, Construction, and Farming must feed each other in a closed loop. Here is how to build that loop so you never stall waiting on a drop you cannot buy.

⚒️ Ironman Skills Series · 1000+ Total · 24 min read · Updated for 2026
🛡️ Level: 1000+

The hardest adjustment when you roll an Ironman is not the slower XP — it is the realization that three of the most important skills (Herblore, Construction, and Farming) cannot be rushed with cash. On a main, you buy herbs, buy planks, and watch the bars fill. On an Ironman, those bars only move if you have built a resource loop that manufactures the inputs yourself. This guide is for players who already understand the basics of each skill and want the intermediate strategy: how Farming grows the herbs Herblore needs, how Herblore's training and Farming's trees supply Construction's planks, and in what order to develop the loop so you never hit a dead end. If you are still building the foundation across all 23 skills, our complete skill training guide lays out where this loop fits into a first-month and first-year plan. Treat this article as the master plan for the three skills that punish GE dependence the hardest.

The core idea is simple but easy to forget in the moment: an Ironman account is a system, not a checklist. The player who trains Herblore in isolation, then panics because they have no herbs, then trains Farming in isolation, then panics because they have no planks, is fighting the account instead of using it. The players who thrive are the ones who ask, before every training session, "what does this produce that my other skills consume?" That single question collapses weeks of frustration into a tidy daily habit. The rest of this guide is the answer to it.

⚡ Quick Verdict — The 30-Second Read

Self-sufficiency on an Ironman is a speed multiplier, not a tax. Farm daily herbs and trees from early game, build the Gilded Altar at Construction 38, brew potions as herbs arrive, and use Plank Make for AFK planks that also train Magic. Do that and Herblore and Construction become nearly free — no GE required. The full plan, with numbers, is below.

1. Why Ironman Skills Must Be Self-Sufficient

On a main account, "self-sufficient" is optional — you can always buy your way out of a gap. Out of Ranarrs? Buy a stack. Out of planks? Buy a few thousand. The Grand Exchange absorbs every planning mistake you make. On an Ironman, the Grand Exchange is gone, and with it the easy exit. Every potion, every plank, and every secondary ingredient has to enter your bank through gameplay. That constraint is not a punishment; it is the entire design philosophy of the mode. The players who thrive are the ones who treat skill training as a system rather than a checklist, and who build the system early enough that they never feel the squeeze.

The three skills that expose the GE crutch

Herblore, Construction, and Farming are the trio that punishes GE dependence hardest, and they punish it in different ways. Herblore eats herbs and secondaries you cannot buy, and its cost on a main is measured in hundreds of millions of GP — on an Ironman that GP cost is simply impossible to pay, so the skill has to be fed by Farming instead. Construction eats planks by the thousand; a full Player-Owned House can consume tens of thousands of planks, each of which must be a tree you grew. Farming is the slowest to start but eventually supplies both herbs and trees — yet only if you invested in it early. Miss the Farming window and you will be farming herbs one patch at a time while your Herblore and Construction bars mock you. These three skills are where a sloppy main-account habit of "just buy it" turns into a multi-week stall.

Self-sufficiency is a speed multiplier, not a tax

It feels slower at first, but a working loop compounds in your favor. A herb run done daily from Farming level 32 produces a steady herb stream that funds Herblore for the rest of the account's life. Tree runs produce planks that fund Construction. Birdhouses produce seeds that fund more herb and tree runs. Once all three are running, the "expensive" skills become nearly free, and the only real cost left is the sawmill fee — a trivial GP expense covered by the PvM and Sailing loops you run anyway. The upfront cost is discipline, not GP, and discipline is the one resource an Ironman always has in unlimited supply.

Why most Ironmen stall at 80–90 instead of 99

If you look at a typical Ironman's stats, you will notice a pattern: combat and gathering skills climb steadily, but Herblore and Construction often plateau in the 70s or 80s. The reason is almost never a lack of XP methods — it is a lack of inputs. The player reached 80 Herblore by spending every herb they ever grew, then discovered there were no herbs left for 81–99. They reached 80 Construction by sawing every oak and teak they ever grew, then discovered there were no planks left for 81–99. The fix is not "train harder"; it is "start the farm earlier and let it run longer." A loop that has been spinning since Farming 32 arrives at level 99 Herblore and Construction with a full bank, not an empty one.

💡 The Ironman Mindset Shift

Stop asking "what is the fastest method?" and start asking "what method feeds my other skills?" The best Ironman training is almost never the fastest in isolation — it is the method whose byproducts you actually need. A tree run is "slow XP" but it is also tomorrow's planks. A herb run is "slow XP" but it is also next week's Prayer potions. Train for the byproduct.

2. Farming — The Engine of the Loop

Farming is the foundation because it is the only skill that produces both herbs (for Herblore) and trees (for Construction planks) on a timer you control. The genius of Farming is that the XP and the profit arrive while you do other things — you plant, you log off, you come back to a matured crop. No other skill in the game pays you for being absent, and that is exactly why it is the backbone of an Ironman resource loop. A disciplined Farming routine is the difference between an Ironman that cruises to 99 and one that grinds怨恨 at the herb patches forever.

Herb runs — your Herblore pipeline

From Farming level 9 you can plant Guam, but the real pipeline starts at Ranarr (level 32) and Snapdragon (level 62). A clean herb run across the available patches — Falador, Catherby, Ardougne, Port Phasmatys, Trollheim (after My Arm's Big Adventure), Harmony Island, the Cramped and Weiss patches, plus the Hosidius allotment once the Farming Guild contract is active — using Ultracompost every single time, yields dozens of herbs per cycle. Those herbs are exactly what Herblore consumes for Prayer potions, Super restores, Saradomin brews, and the high-tier combat potions you will need for late-game bossing. For an Ironman, a missed herb run is not a missed XP drop — it is a missed Herblore session you cannot replace by buying. The herb runs profit guide breaks down patch order, contract stacking, and the exact compost economy, and our 1–99 Farming guide covers the full level path from 1 to 99.

Tree and fruit tree runs — your plank pipeline

Oak (level 15), Teak (level 35), and Mahogany (level 55) trees are the backbone of Construction. A Teak tree yields a stack of planks when cleared; Mahogany yields even more and is what you want for a Sloop, a full POH, and the high-value pieces that actually matter. Plant these on the same timer as herbs — they mature while you train other skills, fight bosses, or sleep. The key is consistency: a tree run every 64 hours (oaks, via the Farming Guild and spirit tree patches) and every ~58 hours (teaks and mahogany) builds a plank reserve that turns Construction from a grind into a non-event. Fruit trees (palm, papaya, dragonfruit) are slower but add Farming XP and useful secondary drops, and the Calquat and Celastrus vines round out a complete run. A full tree run is roughly fifteen minutes of travel for days of passive plank production.

Birdhouses — passive Farming and Hunter, and a seed engine

Once you have started the Bird House Trail (requires a little questing and some Hunter), birdhouses on Fossil Island generate passive Farming XP and, crucially, seeds — including the herb and tree seeds you plant right back into your patches. This closes a mini-loop inside Farming itself: birdhouses give seeds, seeds become herbs and trees, herbs and trees become potions and planks. It is the single highest-leverage daily an Ironman can do, and it stacks with Herbiboar on the same island for a free herb top-up. The birdhouse runs guide walks through the optimal house types, the Fossil Island route, and how to fold the run into your herb and tree timer so the whole island takes one trip.

Hardwood and special trees for the late loop

Once you have a high Farming level, don't sleep on the hardwood patches (teak and mahogany in the Tai Bwo Wannai clearing) and the special trees — the Calquat, the Spirit tree, the Celastrus, and the Redwood. The Redwood in particular is a massive Mahogany-equivalent plank source for a maxed Ironman, and the Spirit tree doubles as fast travel. These are the crops that turn a "good enough" loop into a loop that can fund 99 Construction twice over. The point is not to optimize every patch on day one; it is to keep expanding the loop as your level permits so the inputs never run dry.

3. Herblore Without the GE — From Your Own Herbs

Herblore is the skill mains dread most because of its GP cost — a main buying their way to 99 spends hundreds of millions. For an Ironman the cost is replaced by patience: you cannot buy a stack of Ranarrs, so you grow them. The strategy is to never let your herb stockpile sit idle — every herb you harvest should have a potion destination, and every potion you make should be one you will actually use. Herblore on an Ironman is not a separate grind; it is the consumption end of your Farming pipeline.

Matching herbs to potions by priority

Not all herbs are equal, and on an Ironman you brew by need, not by XP rate. Early on, Ranarrs become Prayer potions (essential for every boss and every Slayer task) and later Super restores (essential for every extended trip and every overloaded inventory). Snapdragons become Saradomin brews, the tanking potion every late-game Ironman leans on for high-level PvM. Toadflax becomes Saradomin brews too at a lower tier and is a fine stepping stone. The priority is simple and ruthless: brew what keeps you alive and what lets you farm and fight longer (Prayer potions, Super restores, brews) before brewing luxury combat potions like Super combats and overloads. Overloads (after the quest line) are the pinnacle, but they consume enormous herb volume, so they belong at the end of the loop, not the start.

Sourcing secondaries without buying

Herblore secondaries (snape grass, red spiders' eggs, white berries, dragon scale dust, potato cactus, desert goat horn, and so on) are where Ironmen get stuck if unprepared. The fix is to fold secondary collection into your regular content rather than treating it as a separate chore. Snape grass comes from the Fog or from farm runs; red spiders' eggs come from the Chaos Tunnels or from Slayer drop tables; dragon scale dust comes from skeletal and other drops; potato cactus and desert goat horn come from the areas you already pass through. Many secondaries also arrive as a byproduct of the Ironman money making loops you run for GP and supplies — a Slayer task that pays GP also pays red spiders' eggs. The discipline is to collect secondaries during other grinds, never to "go farm secondaries" as a standalone session you will procrastinate on.

Herbiboar as a free herb source

At Hunter 80, the Herbiboar on Fossil Island drops banked herbs directly — no farming timer required, no compost, no travel between patches. It is slower per hour than a disciplined herb run, but it is free and it stacks with birdhouses on the same island, so the whole Fossil Island trip becomes a herb-and-seed engine. Treat Herbiboar as the topping on your Farming base, not a replacement for it. Combined with a daily herb run, it smooths out the weeks when a patch dies unexpectedly or you simply forget to compost.

Overloads and end-game Herblore

Once you have the quest requirements and a deep herb bank, overloads (and the various boosted potions) become realistic. They are the single biggest herb sink in the game, which is exactly why you need the loop spinning for months before you attempt them. An Ironman who starts herb runs at 32 and keeps them running will, by the time they are quest-ready for overloads, have a herb stockpile that makes the sink a formality rather than a wall. This is the compounding payoff: the early discipline is what makes the end-game feasible.

💡 Banked Herbs = Banked Progress

On an Ironman, a full herb box is not "stuff you will use later" — it is Herblore levels waiting to happen. Whenever Farming and Herblore both have a free session, convert herbs to potions immediately. Liquid progress (potions) is easier to plan around than raw herbs, and it frees bank space for the next harvest.

4. Construction Planks From Your Own Trees

Construction is the most plank-hungry skill in the game, and on an Ironman those planks are your own trees, sawn at a sawmill or converted with the Plank Make spell. The loop is: grow trees (Farming) → convert logs to planks (sawmill or Plank Make) → build POH (Construction). The only external input is a sawmill fee, which you cover with the GP your other loops and PvM generate. This is the cleanest example of the closed loop: the tree you planted at Farming 35 becomes the altar you pray at for the rest of the account.

Which planks actually matter

You do not need Mahogany for everything, and chasing Mahogany before you need it wastes Farming time. Oak planks carry you through the early POH (study, workshop, costume room, basic storage). Teak planks cover the mid-game (mounted digsite, more storage, the shell of the important rooms). Mahogany is reserved for the high-value pieces: the Gilded Altar (the single most important Construction unlock for Prayer training), the jewelry box, the portal nexus, and the Sloop. Plan your tree runs around the plank you currently need, not around maximum XP. A Teak-heavy early loop feeds 99% of what matters; Mahogany is the luxury tier you add once Teak is comfortably stocked.

The Gilded Altar is the goal

Construction 38 unlocks the Gilded Altar, which grants 350% Prayer XP when offering bones. For an Ironman this is transformative: every bone you earn from Slayer, bossing, and the various drop tables becomes far more Prayer XP, which means more Protect Prayers, more Piety, and more Rigour for harder content. Hitting 38 Construction should be an early milestone, funded by your Teak runs, not something you defer to "later." The altar also makes the Prayer grind dramatically shorter, which in turn makes every other grind safer. It is the highest-value Construction unlock for any account and doubly so for an Ironman funding Prayer without buying training.

Plank Make vs sawmill — pick by the situation

The Plank Make spell (Lunar spellbook, via Dream Mentor, or 86 Magic) converts logs to planks in your bank for runes instead of a sawmill trip. It is slower per action but saves travel and doubles as AFK Magic training — two skills helped by the same click. The sawmill is faster per plank and better when you have a huge log pile and want them gone now. Neither is "better" universally; the smart Ironman uses the sawmill for bulk and Plank Make for the logs that accumulate between sawmill trips. The table below compares them directly so you can decide per situation.

⚠️ Don't Hoard Logs

Raw logs in the bank are useless. Saw them into planks the moment you have a sawmill trip or a Plank Make run. A plank pile you can build from is progress; a log pile is just deferred Construction that occupies bank space you need for herbs and seeds.

5. Closing the Loop — How the Three Interlock

The magic of an Ironman resource loop is that the "output" of one skill is the "input" of another, and the chain has no GE link anywhere in it. Once it is spinning, none of the three skills needs an external purchase — the only thing you "buy" is sawmill service, paid for by the GP your PvM and Sailing loops generate. That is true self-sufficiency, and it is the goal of this entire guide.

The cycle

  • Farming grows herbs → those herbs become Herblore potions (Prayer potions, Super restores, brews).
  • Farming grows trees → those trees become Construction planks (altar, jewelry box, storage).
  • Construction's Gilded Altar converts Slayer/boss bones into Prayer XP → better prayers → longer, safer grinds → more herbs and logs collected.
  • Herblore potions let you survive harder PvM → harder PvM drops the secondaries and seeds that feed Farming and Herblore.
  • Birdhouses (Farming + Hunter) return seeds → replanted → more herbs and trees, feeding the whole cycle again.

Read that list as a circle, not a line. Every arrow points both ways eventually: better prayers let you farm safer, safer farming gives more herbs, more herbs give more potions, more potions let you PvM harder, harder PvM gives more seeds and secondaries, and those seeds and secondaries grow more Farming. The diagram below makes the loop explicit.

🎯 Daily Ironman Loop (≈20 minutes)

1. Herb run (all patches, Ultracompost) → bank herbs.
2. Tree + fruit tree run → bank logs.
3. Birdhouses on Fossil Island → collect seeds + Hunter XP.
4. Convert herbs to the potions you need most; saw logs to the planks you need most.
This 20-minute habit is what keeps Herblore and Construction moving without the GE.

6. Realistic Progression Order for an Ironman

Order matters more than speed. The wrong order strands you: high Herblore ambition with no Farming, or high Construction ambition with no trees. Here is a sequence that avoids dead ends, built so that every phase feeds the next instead of blocking it.

1

Phase 1 — Plant the seed (early game)

Get Farming to 32 for Ranarrs and start daily herb runs immediately, even before you "need" Herblore. Start tree runs at 15 (oak) so planks accumulate in the background. Do the Bird House Trail as soon as you can for passive seeds. By the time you want Herblore, you already have herbs — and by the time you want Construction, you already have a plank pile. The mistake is waiting until you "need" the farm; start it on day one.

2

Phase 2 — First milestones (mid game)

Push Farming to 62 (Snapdragon) and grow Teak for Construction. Get Construction to 38 for the Gilded Altar using your teak pile — this is the single highest-leverage milestone in the whole loop. Begin Herblore in bursts as herbs allow: do not force it, let the farm dictate the pace. Aim for Prayer potions and Super restores first, then brews. At this stage the loop is visibly paying you back in every boss trip.

3

Phase 3 — The closed loop (late game)

With Snapdragon runs, Mahogany trees, and a working altar, Herblore and Construction become nearly free. Use Plank Make at 86 Magic for AFK planks that also train Magic (see the cheap Magic training guide for the Lunar setup). Brew Saradomin brews and overloads for bossing. At this stage the loop is self-sustaining and you can chase 99 in all three without ever opening a (non-existent) GE.

💡 Patience Compounds

An Ironman who does herb and tree runs daily for a month has, without "training," banked weeks of Herblore and Construction progress. The players who complain Ironman is slow are usually the ones skipping the daily loop — not the ones who built it and let it run.

7. Common Ironman Mistakes That Break the Loop

Most stalled Ironmen did not fail at the methods — they failed at the habits. These are the five mistakes that quietly break the loop, and each one is cheaper to avoid than to recover from.

① Skipping herb runs "because I don't need potions yet"

This is the most common and most expensive mistake. The herb run you skip at Farming 40 is the Prayer potion you don't have at Farming 80. Herbs only ever get more valuable, and the bank space they occupy is trivial next to the progress they represent. Run them from 32 and never rationalize a skip — the loop dies one missed run at a time.

② Hoarding logs instead of sawing them

Raw logs do nothing. A plank pile you can build from is progress; a log pile is deferred Construction eating bank space you need for herbs and seeds. Saw on a timer — sawmill trip or Plank Make — and keep the pipeline flowing. The warning box in Section 4 exists because this mistake is that common.

③ Treating secondaries as a separate chore

If you decide to "go collect red spiders' eggs" as a standalone session, you will procrastinate, and your Herblore will stall. Fold secondaries into Slayer, bossing, and the areas you already farm. The secondaries arrive as a byproduct; the discipline is to bank them instead of dropping them.

④ Forcing Herblore ahead of the farm

Brewing potions you don't have herbs for means buying nothing (you can't) and achieving nothing. Let Farming set the Herblore pace. When the herb box is full, brew; when it is empty, farm. The loop is a conversation between the two skills, not a race.

⑤ Forgetting Plank Make as passive Magic

Every Ironman needs Magic eventually, and Plank Make is Magic XP that also produces planks. Ignoring it means you train Magic separately (wasteful) and convert logs separately (wasteful). At 86 Magic it is the most efficient double-dip in the loop. Pair it with the cheap Magic guide so the Lunar setup does not surprise you with costs.

None of these mistakes is a skill issue. They are planning issues, and they are all fixed by the same habit: run the daily loop, convert outputs to inputs immediately, and let Farming lead. 8. Frequently Asked Questions

Can an Ironman really 99 Herblore without the Grand Exchange?

Yes, but it takes discipline, not GP. Grow your own Ranarr and Snapdragon herbs, collect secondaries during Slayer and bossing, and use Herbiboar at 80 Hunter for a free top-up. The XP is "free" in the sense that you pay no GP — the cost is the farming routine that supplies it. Most Ironmen who fail at 99 Herblore simply did not start herb runs early enough.

What Farming level should I reach first for the loop?

Get to 32 for Ranarr as fast as possible, then 62 for Snapdragon when you can. For planks, 35 (Teak) is the key mid-game threshold and 55 (Mahogany) unlocks the best planks. Tree runs should start at 15 and never stop. The earlier Farming leads, the less Herblore and Construction ever stall.

Is the Gilded Altar worth the Construction effort on Ironman?

Absolutely. At Construction 38 it grants 350% Prayer XP, turning every bone from Slayer and bosses into far more Prayer levels. It is the highest-value Construction unlock for any account, and especially for an Ironman funding Prayer without buying training. Hitting 38 should be an early milestone, not something you defer.

How do I get Herblore secondaries without buying?

Fold secondary collection into existing grinds: snape grass from the Fog, red spiders' eggs from drop tables, dragon scale dust from skeleton drops, and ground items from Slayer loot. Birdhouses and Herbiboar also supply seeds and herbs passively. The discipline is banking them during other content rather than running a separate "secondary trip" you will skip.

Plank Make or sawmill — which should I use?

Use both by situation. The sawmill is faster per plank and best for bulk clears and big log piles. Plank Make (Lunar, 86 Magic) is slower but travels nowhere and trains Magic at the same time, making it ideal for the steady trickle from daily tree runs. The comparison table in Section 4 lays out the trade-offs row by row.

Do I need Mahogany, or is Teak enough?

Teak is enough for almost everything that matters — the altar shell, storage, mounted digsite, and most rooms. Mahogany is reserved for the few high-value pieces (full Gilded Altar trimming, jewelry box, portal nexus, Sloop) and for an end-game Ironman with spare Farming time. Run a Teak-heavy loop and add Mahogany once Teak is comfortably stocked.

How long does the daily loop actually take?

A full herb run, tree run, and Fossil Island birdhouse trip is roughly 20 minutes if you know the routes, and it can be split across the day. The XP and resources it produces arrive passively while you do other content. Twenty minutes daily is the difference between an Ironman that cruises to 99 and one that stalls in the 80s.

My loop stalled — Herblore is empty and Construction has no planks. What now?

Stop forcing the consumption skills and go back to production. Run herb and tree runs daily for two weeks, saw every log, and let the farm refill the banks. The loop always recovers from a production burst; it never recovers from forcing consumption on an empty bank. Patience, then brewing.

Where does Sailing or other skills fit this loop?

Sailing and other skills are mostly independent, but they supply the GP for sawmill fees and the occasional secondary buy, and they drop seeds and resources that feed Farming. They are the "external GP" layer that keeps the loop's only cash cost trivial. Our Sailing deep dive covers how to run it Ironman-friendly alongside this loop.

9. Final Tips — Your Self-Sufficient Checklist

→ 1: Start daily herb runs the moment Farming hits 32 — do not wait for "later."
→ 2: Run tree patches on their timers; teak and mahogany fund all your planks.
→ 3: Get Construction 38 for the Gilded Altar before chasing higher Construction.
→ 4: Collect Herblore secondaries during Slayer and bossing, never as a separate chore.
→ 5: Use Plank Make at 86 Magic for AFK planks that also train Magic.
→ 6: Saw logs immediately; raw logs in the bank are deferred Construction.
→ 7: Let Farming set the Herblore pace — never force potions on an empty herb box.
