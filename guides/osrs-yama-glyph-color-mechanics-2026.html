1. 💀 Why Yama's Glyph One-Shot Kills So Many

Adsterra ad slot — paste your Adsterra banner code here

Yama, Lord of the Underworld, is the climax of Desert Treasure II and one of the most punishing duo-capable bosses in the game. Most of his damage is telegraphed and survivable. The Infernal Glyph phase is different: it is an execution check. Get the color/prayer pair wrong and you die in a single tick regardless of your gear. That is why even near-BiS players eat the floor — the boss mechanics system in our Death-Prevention guide treats Yama as a top drill target.

The reason it kills so reliably is that it breaks the usual habit. In most bosses you "pray the style the boss is using." In the glyph phase you must instead pray the style of the ward you are standing in — which is often the opposite of what Yama is attacking with. That inversion is where muscle memory betrays you. This guide rewires it.

💡 The One Rule to Memorize First

Match the floor, not the boss. The glyph's color tells you which Protect prayer to turn on. The beam that follows is unblockable unless you are both (a) on the correct glyph and (b) praying the matching style. Say the pair out loud as you move: "Azure — Magic."

2. 🎨 The Glyph Color → Protection Prayer Table

This is the core reference. Screenshot it, or better, drill it until it is reflex. The colors align with OSRS's standard combat-triangle iconography, which is exactly why the trap works — your brain assumes the obvious mapping and then fumbles under pressure.

Glyph Color Element / Beam Correct Protect Prayer If You're On the Wrong Glyph Safe Tile Rule
Crimson Melee (slash beam) Protect from Melee One-shot — the beam ignores defense Stand inside the ring, Melee prayed
Azure Magic (arcane beam) Protect from Magic One-shot — hits through armour Stand inside the ring, Magic prayed
Verdant Ranged (volley beam) Protect from Missiles One-shot — bypasses your range def Stand inside the ring, Missiles prayed
Infernal Nova (purple flash) Unblockable AoE None — prayer does not help One-shot unless off all glyphs Step to the neutral center tile, off every ring

The inversion to watch: when the Verdant (green, Ranged) glyph is active, Yama is often mid-Melee swing — so your body wants Protect from Melee. Do NOT. Pray Missiles while standing on green. Color wins, boss loses.

Drill the table as a flashcard

Before you fight Yama, test yourself 20 times: "Crimson?" → "Melee." "Verdant?" → "Missiles." "Azure?" → "Magic." "Purple flash?" → "Off, center." Speed matters less than zero hesitation. Build this with the prayer-flick drills in our Prayer Training guide.

3. ⚡ How the Glyph Phase Triggers

Knowing when the glyphs appear is half the battle. The phase has a fixed tell.

① The ground-crack telegraph

Yama raises both arms and the arena floor cracks in three places. This is your ~2-second window. Three colored rings spawn — one Crimson, one Azure, one Verdant — at semi-random tiles around the center. A purple glow at the center signals a Nova is mixed in.

② The channel lock

After the rings spawn, Yama begins a ~3-tick channel. The beam fires from the center outward, striking each ring with its matching element. You must already be standing in a ring and already praying the matching style before the beam lands. There is no reactive fix — only pre-positioning.

③ Frequency scales with phase

Phase 1 spawns glyphs once per ~4 rotations. Phase 2 (enraged, below 50% HP) spawns them every ~2 rotations and adds the Nova more often. If you only learned the Phase 1 rhythm, the Phase 2 cadence will surprise you — plan for it.

4. 🦶 Correct Standing Tiles & Movement

Positioning is a decision you make during the telegraph, not after.

① Pick the ring closest to your current tile

You rarely need a specific color — you need any correct color/prayer pair. Move to the nearest ring and set that prayer. Running across the arena to a "favorite" color wastes ticks and can leave you outside all rings when the beam fires.

② Center tile is only safe during the Nova

The neutral center is a death trap during normal glyph beams (you are on no ring, so no prayer applies — one-shot). It is only correct when the purple Nova flash appears. Train yourself: purple = sprint to center, drop all prayers.

③ Pre-walk, don't post-walk

Start moving the instant the floor cracks

The 2-second telegraph is generous if you commit immediately and fatal if you hesitate. The moment you see the crack, pick your ring and move. Combine this with your gear-switch discipline from the Boss Gear Checklist so movement never conflicts with a pending swap.

5. 🙏 Prayer-Switching Discipline During Glyphs

The glyph phase is fundamentally a prayer-switching test. Get the switch wrong and the color is irrelevant.

① Set the prayer the instant you commit to a ring

Do not walk first and pray second. As you step toward the Crimson ring, your hand should already be flipping to Protect from Melee. The two actions are one motion.

② Hold, do not flick, through the beam

The glyph beam is a single massive hit, not a stream. Hold the correct prayer through the channel — flicking here gains nothing and a missed flick is a death. Our Near-BiS Death Diagnosis explains why holding beats flicking on telegraphed hits.

③ Restore before the phase if you can

Top prayer up during the non-glyph rotations

The glyph phase burns prayer points fast because you are switching styles every few seconds. Sip a Super restore or Prayer flask during the calm rotations so you never drop prayer mid-channel. The brew→restore loop from our death-prevention guide keeps you both alive and accurate.

6. 🔄 Phase-by-Phase Glyph Routine

Here is the exact 6-step loop that turns panic into reflex. Run it every glyph phase until it is automatic.

① See crack → stop attacking

Your DPS pause is free; a death is not. Drop your weapon switch thoughts and focus on the floor.

② Identify colors & Nova

Quick scan: which rings spawned, is there a purple center glow?

③ Move to nearest ring (or center if Nova)

Nearest ring for normal; center tile only for Nova.

④ Flip prayer to the matching style

Crimson→Melee, Azure→Magic, Verdant→Missiles, Nova→none.

⑤ Hold through the beam

No flick, no menu fumble. Just hold.

⑥ Resume DPS, restore if low

Back to damage the instant the beam clears

The glyph phase is a brief interruption, not a reset. The faster you return to DPS, the fewer total phases you survive — and fewer phases means fewer one-shot chances. Tighten this loop with the rotation drills in our Boss Mechanics guide.

7. ❌ Common Mistakes That Get You One-Shot

Every Yama glyph death we reviewed falls into one of these buckets. Audit yourself against them.

① Praying the boss's attack style, not the ring

The #1 killer. You see Yama swing Melee, you pray Melee — but you're standing on the Verdant ring, which demands Missiles. One-shot. Always: color first.

② Standing on center during a normal beam

The center looks "safe" because it is the middle. It is only safe on Nova. On a normal beam, center = no ring = one-shot.

③ Flicking the glyph beam

A missed flick on a one-shot hit is pure death with zero upside. Hold.

④ Late movement after the crack

⚠️ The "I'll make it" Death

Players who wait to finish a hit or a spec before moving usually arrive a tick late and stand between rings when the beam fires. There is no "between" — you are either in a ring or you are dead. Move on the crack, not after your animation.

8. 🎒 Gear & Inventory Prep for the Glyph Phase

The glyph phase is survivable on modest gear — it is a mechanics check, not a DPS check. But a few prep choices remove avoidable deaths.

① Free up a key for instant prayer swaps

Bind Protect from Melee / Magic / Missiles to three adjacent F-keys or one rotation key. The swap must be one press. See the Boss Gear Checklist for a full keybind layout.

② Bring restore potential, not just food

Pack Super restores or a Prayer flask. The style-switching drains prayer; without restores you drop prayer mid-phase. Food alone will not save a prayer-less beam.

③ Inventory layout for the Colosseum-style flow

Keep prayers and restores one click from your hotbar

Mirror the tight inventory discipline used in the Colosseum guide: prayer potions and restores in the same screen region every trip so the glyph-phase swap is never a menu hunt. Pre-pack your Yama inv using our Boss Inventory Prep template.

9. ❓ Yama Glyph FAQs

What does each Yama glyph color correspond to?

Crimson = Melee → Protect from Melee. Azure = Magic → Protect from Magic. Verdant = Ranged → Protect from Missiles. A purple center flash = Infernal Nova → step off all rings to the neutral center, no prayer needed.

Why do I die even when I'm praying the right style?

Because you were on the wrong ring. The prayer only works if you are standing inside the matching colored glyph when the beam lands. Praying Melee while standing on the Verdant ring still one-shots you. Match the floor first.

Should I flick or hold during the glyph beam?

Hold. The beam is a single telegraphed hit, so flicking gains nothing and a missed flick is fatal. Hold the correct prayer through the channel, then return to DPS.

Is the center tile ever safe?

Only during the Infernal Nova (purple flash), when the AoE is unblockable and you must be on no glyph. During every normal beam, the center is a death trap because you are on no ring.

Does the glyph phase get harder in Phase 2?

Yes. Below 50% HP, Yama spawns glyphs roughly twice as often and mixes in the Nova more frequently. Learn the Phase 1 cadence first, then tighten your loop for the faster Phase 2 rhythm.

What gear do I need to survive the glyphs?

Gear barely matters — it is a mechanics check. What matters: a free keybind for instant prayer swaps, Super restores/Prayer flask for the style-switch drain, and an inventory where those are one click away. See the prep section and our Gear Checklist.

How do I practice without dying repeatedly?

Drill the color→prayer flashcard (Section 2) on the ground, then learn the phase on a cheap/alt setup so a death costs almost nothing. Record each death and tag the cause: wrong ring, wrong prayer, or late move. Full loop in our Death-Prevention guide.
