1. 🔐 What the OSRS Bank PIN Actually Does (and Doesn't)

A Bank PIN is a 4-digit code you set on your account that must be entered before certain bank actions can happen. Unlike your login password, it does not stop someone from opening the client — it stops them from taking your stuff once they are inside. For a game where a maxed main's bank can be worth hundreds of millions of GP, that distinction is the difference between "annoying" and "catastrophic."

You choose exactly which actions require the PIN. The smart default is to require it for opening the bank and for withdrawing any item, which covers the realistic theft paths. The Bank & Inventory Management guide explains the bank interface itself; the PIN is the lock you put on top of it.

💡 What the PIN Does NOT Do

It does not stop a hijacker from logging in, from talking in chat, from training your stats, or from getting you banned through rule-breaking. It only guards the bank. That is why the PIN is a complement to — never a replacement for — login security (2SV).

② Which actions can require a PIN

ActionShould require PIN?Why
Opening the bank Yes (recommended) Blocks the very first step of any theft.
Withdrawing items Yes (recommended) Prevents item draining even if the bank opens.
Changing the PIN Always required Stops a thief from silently swapping your code.
Recovering the PIN Delayed by your recovery setting The protection window described in Section 2.

2. ⏳ The Bank PIN Protection Window Explained (3-7 Day Cooldowns)

This is the part almost no guide explains, and it is the heart of the "protection window." The Bank PIN system has built-in delays designed to protect the real owner from a hijacker who briefly gains access. Understanding them turns a confusing wait into a deliberate defense.

③ The change cooldown (the "3-day" window)

How it behaves

  • When you change your PIN, the system keeps a short cooldown (around 3 days) before the change is irreversible.
  • During that window the old PIN often still works, giving the true owner a chance to notice the change and revert it.
  • This is the opposite of a flaw — it is a safety net. A hijacker who changes your PIN cannot instantly and permanently lock you out; you have a few days to fight back.

④ The recovery delay (the "3-7+ day" window)

What you configure

You set a recovery delay when managing your PIN: typically 3, 7, 14, or 28 days. This is how long someone must wait after requesting a PIN recovery before it completes.

Why 3-7 days is the sweet spot

A 3-day delay stops impulsive thieves but is short enough that you can recover quickly if you forget your own PIN. A 7-day delay adds safety with only a little more wait. Most security-minded players pick 7 days; paranoid (wise) players go 14 or 28.

The hidden benefit

If a hijacker tries to "recover" your PIN to drain the bank, they are stuck waiting out your delay — during which you can log in (if you still can) and cancel the recovery. The window is a two-way shield.

⚠️ The Window Works Only If You Set a Delay

The default recovery delay is short. If you never touched the setting, a thief's PIN recovery may complete faster than you expect. Open the PIN settings once and deliberately choose 7 days or more — it is a 10-second change that dramatically raises the bar.

3. 🚫 The Big Myth: Your PIN Is NOT Cleared on Account Recovery

Here is the misconception that costs players the most: "Jagex clears the Bank PIN when they recover my account, so I don't need to worry about it during a hack." This is false, and believing it leads people to skip setting a PIN entirely.

When Jagex restores login access after a compromise, they return control of the account login — they do not wipe the Bank PIN. If the hijacker set a PIN (or changed yours), your bank remains locked behind it after you regain the login. You then have to recover the PIN yourself, waiting out the recovery delay that is configured on the account (commonly 3-7 days, sometimes longer).

The real sequence during a recovery

  1. Jagex verifies ownership and returns your login.
  2. You sign in — but the bank still demands the PIN the hijacker set.
  3. You request a PIN recovery; the clock starts on the account's recovery delay (3-7+ days).
  4. Until that delay elapses, the bank stays locked — which is actually good, because it also locks the hijacker out if they somehow log in again.

So the PIN is not removed by recovery — it becomes a second wait you must survive. The upside: that same wait protects your items from the hijacker during the chaos. The practical lesson is in the Account Recovery After a Hack guide — set your own PIN before anything happens, so a recovery never strands you behind a thief's code.

🔑 Take Back the PIN Immediately

The moment you regain login after a hack, go to the bank PIN interface and start a recovery / re-set using your own recovery delay. Do not wait — the 3-7 day clock only starts once you act, and it is the clock that eventually frees your items.

4. 🛠️ How to Set a Bank PIN (Step-by-Step)

Setting the PIN takes under two minutes. Do it on a device you trust, with no one looking over your shoulder.

⑤ The setup walkthrough

Step 1 — Open your bank

Right-click your bank booth / banker or use the bank interface, then look for the "PIN settings" option (often under the wrench / settings icon in the modern bank UI).

Step 2 — Choose "Set a PIN"

Enter a 4-digit code you have not used for anything else. Do not use 1234, 0000, or your birth year — those are the first combinations a thief tries.

Step 3 — Pick which actions require it

Enable the PIN for opening the bank and withdrawing items. This is the configuration that actually stops theft.

Step 4 — Set your recovery delay

Choose 7 days (or more). This is the protection window from Section 2 — longer is safer.

Step 5 — Confirm and memorize

There is no "email me the PIN" — it is your responsibility to remember it. Write it in your password manager, not on a sticky note by your monitor.

💡 Use Your Password Manager

A 4-digit PIN is easy to forget under stress. Store it in the same password manager that holds your Jagex and email credentials (see the Account Hacked Prevention Guide for manager recommendations). Just never store it inside the email inbox the PIN is meant to protect.

5. 🔄 PIN Recovery Rules: When You Can Change vs. When You Must Wait

Knowing the rules saves panic later. Here is the clean breakdown of when a PIN action is instant and when it is delayed.

SituationWhat HappensWait?
You know the PIN and want to change it Enter old PIN, set new one; cooldown applies before it locks in ~3-day change cooldown
You forgot the PIN (your own) Request recovery; completes after your recovery delay 3-28 days (your setting)
A hijacker changed it, you regained login You recover; waits out the configured delay 3-7+ days typical
Account recovery by Jagex PIN is NOT cleared; you still recover the PIN after Yes (delay remains)
⚠️ You Cannot "Cancel" a Delay You Set

The recovery delay is deliberately hard to shorten — that is the point. If you set 28 days and then forget your own PIN, you wait 28 days. This is why 7 days is the popular balance: long enough to thwart thieves, short enough that your own mistake is not a month-long ordeal.

6. 🧱 Bank PIN vs 2SV — Why You Need Both

Players often ask: "If I have a Bank PIN, do I still need 2-Step Verification?" The answer is an emphatic yes. They protect different layers, and a thief only needs to beat one of them if you skip the other.

⑥ The two layers, side by side

2SV (login security)

  • Stops unauthorized logins with password + second factor.
  • Best defense against the account ever being taken in the first place.
  • Does nothing once a login is legitimate — which is where the PIN matters.

Bank PIN (in-game item security)

  • Stops item withdrawal even if someone is logged in.
  • Your last line of defense if 2SV somehow fails or a session is hijacked.
  • Cannot stop the login itself — which is where 2SV matters.

Together they form a fence-and-vault design: 2SV is the fence keeping intruders off your lawn, the Bank PIN is the vault keeping your items safe even if they get inside. The full Account Security Guide treats 2SV as the foundation; the PIN is the layer you add on top for item-specific protection.

🎯 The 30-Second Security Stack

Unique password + 2SV on the Jagex Account + 2SV on your email + a Bank PIN with a 7-day recovery delay. Four settings, ten minutes, and you are safer than 95% of the player base.

7. ⚙️ Pro Settings: Extra PIN Security & Recovery Delay

Once the basics are set, a few advanced tweaks squeeze maximum value from the system — details the big "beginner" guides skip but that matter for protecting a valuable bank.

⑦ Tweaks worth making

① Lengthen the recovery delay to 14 or 28 days

If your bank is worth a lot and you are confident you will not forget the PIN, the longer delay makes a thief's recovery attempt painfully slow — slow enough for you to notice and act.

② Require the PIN for "open bank," not just withdraw

Some players only lock withdrawals. Locking the open action stops a thief at step zero, before they can even see what to grab.

③ Periodically re-confirm you remember it

Open your bank and enter the PIN monthly. A PIN you forgot is a self-inflicted 7-day lock; a quick monthly check keeps it fresh.

④ Combine with Bank Tags for fast, safe organization

The Bank Tags beginners guide shows how to label tabs — organize your wealth into clearly named tabs so that, if you ever do get drained, you can instantly tell support what was taken for a more accurate item-return review.

🔍 If You Are Drained Despite a PIN

A correct PIN means a thief could not withdraw — so if items vanish, suspect a session you left logged in on another device, a shared account, or a PIN that was never actually set on withdrawals. Review the Bank Tags layout guide to document your setup, then harden the PIN config above.

8. 💡 Bank PIN FAQs

Is the Bank PIN cleared if Jagex recovers my account?

No. Jagex returns login access but does not wipe the PIN. If a hijacker set or changed it, your bank stays locked until you recover the PIN and wait out the configured recovery delay (commonly 3-7 days). That delay also keeps the thief out, so it is a shield as much as an inconvenience.

How long is the Bank PIN protection window really?

Two windows matter: a ~3-day cooldown after you change your PIN (so you can revert a hijacker's change), and a recovery delay you choose — 3, 7, 14, or 28 days — that gates any PIN recovery. The "3-7 day" figure players quote is the short end of that recovery delay.

Can a hijacker just "recover" my PIN and take my items?

They can start a recovery, but they must wait out your recovery delay. During that wait you can log in (if you still have access) and cancel it, or simply let the delay protect your items. A longer delay makes this attack impractical.

Does the Bank PIN protect me from being traded/scammed?

Partly. The PIN stops withdrawals from your bank, but it does not stop you from voluntarily trading items away in a scam, nor does it stop wealth stored outside the bank (cash on hand, items worn, items in noted form being withdrawn before the PIN triggers). The PIN is one layer, not a cure for scams — pair it with scam awareness.

What if I forget my own PIN?

Request a recovery from the bank PIN interface; it completes after your recovery delay. If you set a long delay (28 days), that is the wait you accepted. To avoid this, store the PIN in your password manager and enter it monthly to keep it memorized.

Is a Bank PIN enough on its own?

No. It only guards the bank, not the login. A hijacker who logs in can still get you banned, leak your identity, or drain anything not behind the PIN. Always combine it with 2SV on both your Jagex Account and your email.
