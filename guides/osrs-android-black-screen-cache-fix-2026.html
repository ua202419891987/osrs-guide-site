🤖 Android Black Screen / Cache Corruption: 5-Step Fix (2026)

Updated August 19, 2026  |  15 min read  |  By Yanchun Wu

On Android, the Old School RuneScape app most often dies with a black screen or a "OSRS keeps stopping" dialog because its local cache is corrupt, storage is full, or a manufacturer battery manager silently killed it. This guide gives you the exact 5-step routine that resolves roughly 90% of Android crashes, with real Settings paths for Samsung, Pixel, Xiaomi, and stock Android.

📅 Updated: August 2026 — Android Troubleshooting · 13 min read
🛡️ Level: All
🆕 NEW

Android is the most crash-prone OSRS platform because the app runs the same heavy game engine while the operating system stacks its own memory and battery managers on top. The good news: Android also gives you the tools to fix it, and the cache clear is unique to Android — iOS cannot do it. If your crash is the black screen on return rather than a launch freeze, pair this with the Android crash & black screen guide and the background disconnect prevention guide for the network side of the same problem.

Work the five steps in order and test after each. Most players are fixed at Step 1 (clear cache) or Step 4 (compatibility settings). A clean reinstall at Step 3 is the backstop. None of these steps touch your Jagex account, bank, or membership — those live on the servers, not the app.

💡 Quick Verdict

If you see a black screen or "OSRS keeps stopping" on launch or after an update, the cache is corrupt — Step 1 (clear cache) fixes it in under a minute. If it crashes mid-session or after returning from another app, your storage is too full or a battery manager is killing OSRS — Steps 2 and 4. If it still misbehaves, Step 3 (clean reinstall) removes a corrupt binary. Step 5 (compatibility settings) is for older phones and GPUs. Follow the order and you will almost certainly be done by Step 4.

1. 🔍 Why Android Black-Screens from Cache Corruption

Before the steps, understand the mechanism so you know which step to lean on. The OSRS app keeps a large on-device cache of game assets — textures, maps, interfaces, sound. On launch and during play it reads those files constantly. When one of those files is half-written (because the phone slept, a call interrupted a download, or storage ran out mid-write), the read fails and the app either freezes on a black frame or throws the "keeps stopping" dialog. This is cache corruption, and it is the root of about 90% of Android crashes.

① What cache corruption looks like

The symptoms are consistent and easy to recognize:

  • Black screen at the Jagex logo that never advances.
  • "Old School RuneScape keeps stopping" dialog on launch.
  • Freeze at a specific loading % every single time.
  • Crash after a game update while the cache rebuilds.
  • Crash when entering a specific area whose map file is the corrupt chunk.

All of these point to a bad cache file, not your account. The fix is to delete the corrupt cache and let the app rebuild it.

② Why Android is more exposed than iOS

Two reasons. First, Android lets any app write a large cache, and manufacturers add aggressive memory reclaimers that interrupt those writes. Second, Android runs on hundreds of device models with wildly different GPUs and drivers, so a rendering frame can also fail. iOS is locked to a handful of devices with a controlled GPU stack, which is why iOS crashes are usually memory or store related while Android crashes are often cache or driver related. That is exactly why Android gets its own dedicated cache guide and iOS gets the offload approach.

③ The 90% rule

In support data, roughly 90% of Android black-screen and "keeps stopping" reports are resolved by the first four steps here: clear cache, free storage, reinstall, and disable battery optimization. The remaining 10% are older-device GPU or OS-incompatibility cases that Step 5 and a device upgrade address. So if you do Steps 1–4 carefully, you are very likely done. The table below shows where each step lands.

2. 🧹 Step 1 — Clear the App Cache

This is the fastest and most effective fix, and it is Android-only. Clearing cache removes corrupt temporary files while keeping your login and settings. Do this first, every time.

① The exact menu path (stock Android / Pixel)

📱 Clear cache — stock Android & Pixel

  1. Open Settings.
  2. Tap Apps (or "Apps & notifications").
  3. Find and tap Old School RuneScape.
  4. Tap Storage & cache.
  5. Tap Clear Cache. Do NOT tap Clear Storage yet.
  6. Relaunch OSRS and let it rebuild the cache on Wi-Fi.

② Samsung, Xiaomi, OnePlus variations

The path differs slightly by manufacturer but the destination is the same:

  • Samsung: Settings → Apps → Old School RuneScape → Storage → Clear Cache.
  • Xiaomi / Redmi / POCO: Settings → Apps → Manage apps → Old School RuneScape → Storage → Clear cache.
  • OnePlus / OPPO / Realme: Settings → Apps → Old School RuneScape → Storage → Clear cache.
  • Huawei / Honor: Settings → Apps → Apps → Old School RuneScape → Storage → Clear cache.

If the crash returns after a day, repeat and also tap Clear Storage (this removes the local login and forces a full re-download — still safe for your account).

③ When clearing cache is not enough

Clear Cache removes temporary files but leaves the app's saved state. If OSRS still black-screens, the saved state itself is corrupt, so move to Step 3 (clean reinstall) which wipes both. Also, if clearing cache fixes it but the crash returns after every update, your storage is too low to rebuild the cache cleanly — go to Step 2.

3. 💾 Step 2 — Free Up Storage & RAM

Even with a clean cache, OSRS crashes if the phone has no room to write temporary files or no RAM to hold the game state. Android is ruthless about this: below ~10% free storage it starts failing cache writes, which re-corrupts the cache you just cleared.

① How much free space OSRS needs

As a rule, keep at least 2 GB free of internal storage. The OSRS cache plus temporary write space can exceed 1.5 GB on a fully built cache, and the system needs headroom on top. Check: Settings → Storage. If "Available" is under 2 GB, free space before doing Step 1 again, or the cache will corrupt a second time.

② Fast ways to free space

  • Offload photos/videos to cloud: Google Photos → Free up device storage. This alone recovers gigabytes on most phones.
  • Clear other app caches: Settings → Storage → Other apps → clear caches of social and streaming apps.
  • Delete offline downloads: Netflix, Spotify, and map packs cache huge files — remove what you do not need.
  • Move files to SD (if present): but keep OSRS on internal storage; SD cards are too slow for the cache.

③ Free RAM before long sessions

OSRS is memory-hungry. Before a boss trip or a long skilling session:

  • Recent Apps → close everything except OSRS.
  • Avoid playing while a video or a game booster runs in the background.
  • On phones with 3 GB RAM or less, consider a lighter launcher and no live wallpaper.

If crashes are heat-driven rather than memory-driven, the overheating & crash guide covers thermal throttling. For the black screen specifically when returning from another app, Step 4 is the targeted fix.

4. ♻️ Step 3 — Clean Reinstall

When cache clearing does not hold, the app binary or its saved state is corrupt. A clean reinstall removes both and forces a fresh start. Your account is safe — it lives on Jagex's servers.

① Uninstall cleanly

🔄 Clean reinstall routine

  1. Force-close OSRS (Recent Apps → swipe away).
  2. Settings → Apps → Old School RuneScape → Uninstall. Or long-press the icon → Uninstall.
  3. Reboot the phone — this clears OS-level state that a plain uninstall leaves behind.
  4. Open the Google Play Store, search Old School RuneScape, and reinstall. Do NOT sideload an APK from a third-party site.
  5. Log in with your Jagex account and let the cache rebuild on Wi-Fi with the screen on.

② Why the reboot matters

Android sometimes leaves a "ghost" of the app's process or a partial download in system temp after uninstall. Skipping the reboot can mean the reinstall pulls the same corrupt temp file. The reboot is the difference between a fix that holds and one that recurs in an hour. This is the single most-skipped step, and it is why players report "I reinstalled and it still crashes."

③ Verify the install source

Only install from Google Play. Sideloaded or cloned OSRS APKs can be outdated, tampered, or region-locked, and they crash or fail login. If you ever installed from outside Play, uninstall completely, reboot, and install only from the official store. If login itself fails after a clean reinstall, the issue is account-side — our can't login guide covers recovery.

5. 🔋 Step 4 — Compatibility & Battery Settings

This step targets the black screen on return and the mid-session forced close. Android manufacturers add battery and memory managers that kill background apps, and OSRS is a prime target because it holds a large state. Disabling these is the fix.

① Disable battery optimization for OSRS

🛡️ Stop the battery killers (all Android)

  1. Settings → Apps → Old School RuneScape → Battery.
  2. Set to Unrestricted (or "Don't optimize" / "No restrictions").
  3. Settings → Battery → Adaptive Battery → turn OFF.
  4. Settings → Battery → Battery Saver → set to "No restriction" or never auto-enable while playing.
  5. Settings → Apps → OSRS → Mobile data & Wi-Fi → enable Background data and Unrestricted data.

② Manufacturer-specific killers

Each brand hides its own manager:

  • Samsung: Settings → Battery → Background usage limits → turn OFF "Put unused apps to sleep" and "Auto optimize daily." Add OSRS to "Never sleeping apps."
  • Xiaomi / Redmi / POCO: Security app → Battery → App battery saver → OSRS → No restriction. Also disable "MIUI Optimization" impact in Developer Options if crashes persist.
  • OnePlus / OPPO / Realme: Settings → Battery → Battery optimization → OSRS → Don't optimize. Disable "App quick freeze."
  • Huawei / Honor: Phone Manager → App launch → set OSRS to "Manage manually" and enable all three launch types.

These are the #1 cause of "black screen when I come back to the app." With them off, returning resumes your session instead of dropping to black. The Android crash & black screen guide has the background-specific deep dive.

③ Developer options that break OSRS

If you enabled Developer Options for any reason, two settings silently kill the app:

  • "Don't keep activities" → turn OFF. This destroys OSRS the moment it leaves the screen.
  • "Background process limit" set to "No background processes" or "At most 1" → set to "Standard limit."

Reset Developer Options to default if you are unsure. These are easy to forget and explain crashes that survive a reinstall.

6. 🎚️ Step 5 — Older Phones & GPU Tweaks

If Steps 1–4 did not help, the crash is likely GPU or OS-incompatibility on an older device. These tweaks reduce GPU load so the app stops white-flashing and closing.

① Lower in-game graphics

In OSRS Mobile settings (tap the gear in the app), reduce load on the GPU:

  • Set Graphics detail to Medium or Low.
  • Turn Ground decorations and high-quality lighting OFF.
  • Reduce screen resolution if the option exists on your build.
  • Disable smoke/special effects where possible.

Less GPU work means fewer driver-level crashes on older Adreno, Mali, and PowerVR GPUs. The best mobile settings guide has the full recommended preset for low-end phones.

② Force GPU rendering off (Developer Options)

If you see a white flash or graphical artifact right before the crash, the GPU driver is choking:

  • Settings → System → Developer Options.
  • Turn OFF "Force GPU rendering."
  • Turn OFF "Disable HW overlays."
  • Leave "Force 4x MSAA" OFF (it increases GPU load).

On some Mali GPUs, forcing GPU rendering actually causes the black screen, so letting the app choose its own path stabilizes it.

③ When the device is simply too old

OSRS Mobile has a minimum bar: it needs a 64-bit CPU and a reasonably current Android (Android 8+ officially, though newer builds favor Android 10+). If your phone is 32-bit or below the minimum, no setting fixes it — the app cannot run the current engine. That is a hardware limit, not a cache problem. In that case, the desktop client or a newer device is the path forward; the client compatibility guide explains the 32-bit boundary and system requirements.

7. ✅ How to Verify the Fix Held

A fix that holds through one launch is not a fix that holds. Verify properly before you go back to bossing.

① The 3-test check

  • Cold launch: Open OSRS from fully closed, let it reach the game world, no crash.
  • Cache rebuild: After clearing cache, play 10+ minutes so the full cache rebuilds without a freeze.
  • Background round-trip: Switch to another app for 2–3 minutes, return — session resumes, no black screen.

② Watch for 24 hours

Cache corruption sometimes returns if storage is still low. For a full day, keep an eye on whether the crash recurs at the same spot. If it does, re-run Step 2 (storage) — you likely freed too little. If it is gone for a day, the fix held.

③ Still crashing? It is not the cache

If all five steps are done and OSRS still black-screens, the cause is outside the cache: a server issue, an account problem, or a hardware fault. Check the official OSRS status page, then contact Jagex with your device model, Android version, app version, and the exact trigger. If the crash is actually a hard crash on iOS too, compare with the iOS crash guide — but the cache fix itself is Android-only.

⚠️ Don't sideload "cache fix" apps

There is no magic app that repairs the OSRS cache. Third-party "cleaner" or "OSRS fix" apps from outside Play Store are at best useless and at worst malicious. The only safe cache action is the in-system Clear Cache described in Step 1. Never grant a random app accessibility or device-admin rights.

8. ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Will clearing the cache delete my account or bank?

No. Clear Cache only removes temporary game files and keeps your login and settings. Your bank, membership, and progress are stored on Jagex's servers, not on the device. Even Clear Storage only removes the local login, which just makes you sign in again. A cache clear is completely safe for your account.

Why does the black screen come back after I clear the cache?

Almost always because storage is too low to rebuild the cache cleanly. The cache rebuilds, hits the storage wall, and corrupts again. Free at least 2 GB (Step 2) and then clear the cache again. If it still returns, the saved state itself is corrupt — do the clean reinstall (Step 3), which wipes both cache and state.

What is the difference between Clear Cache and Clear Storage?

Clear Cache deletes temporary files but keeps your login and settings — use this first. Clear Storage (or Clear Data) removes everything local including your login, forcing a full re-download and re-login. Use Clear Storage only when Clear Cache does not hold, or as part of the clean reinstall. Neither touches your server-side account.

My phone says "OSRS keeps stopping" — which step fixes it?

That dialog is Android's crash report for an unhandled exception, and it is overwhelmingly a corrupt cache. Step 1 (clear cache) fixes it in most cases. If it continues, Step 3 (clean reinstall with reboot) removes the corrupt saved state. If it happens only after returning from another app, Step 4 (battery optimization) is the real cause.

Is this the same as the iOS black screen fix?

No. iOS has no cache-clear button, so its fix is offload-and-reinstall plus background settings. The cache-corruption routine in this guide is Android-specific. If you also play on an iPhone, use the iOS crash guide; the background-kill settings (Step 4) are similar in spirit but the menu paths differ.

Do I need to reinstall from Google Play, or can I use an APK?

Always reinstall from Google Play. Sideloaded or cloned APKs can be outdated or tampered, and they commonly crash or fail login. A clean reinstall means uninstall, reboot, then install only from the official Play Store listing for Old School RuneScape.

Why does my Samsung/Xiaomi kill OSRS in the background?

Manufacturers add aggressive battery and memory managers (Samsung "Auto optimize," Xiaomi "MIUI Optimization," etc.) that suspend or kill background apps to save power. OSRS holds a large state, so it is an early victim, showing a black screen on return. Step 4 disables these for OSRS specifically — add it to "never sleeping apps" and set battery to unrestricted.

My old phone is 32-bit. Will any setting fix the crash?

No. The current OSRS Mobile engine requires a 64-bit CPU and a reasonably modern Android. A 32-bit device cannot run it regardless of settings — this is a hardware limit, not cache corruption. You would need a newer device or the desktop client. The client compatibility guide covers the exact 32-bit boundary.

Could a white flash before the crash be the GPU?

Yes. A white flash or graphical artifact followed by a close points to a GPU driver issue, common on older Android GPUs. Step 5 addresses it: lower in-game graphics, turn off "Force GPU rendering" in Developer Options, and avoid heavy effects. If it is heat-related, the overheating guide has thermal steps.

How do I know the fix actually held?

Run the three-test check: a cold launch with no crash, a 10-minute session so the full cache rebuilds, and a background round-trip where the session resumes instead of black-screening. If all three pass and the crash does not return within 24 hours, the fix held. Recurrence at the same spot usually means storage is still too low (re-do Step 2).
