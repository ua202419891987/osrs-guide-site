OSRS RuneLite Mobile Setup Guide 2026 — Can You Run RuneLite on Phone? Safe Routes & Ban Risks

Published September 5, 2026  |  15 min read  |  By Yanchun Wu

Short answer up front: the official RuneLite client is desktop-only — there is no RuneLite app on the iOS App Store or Google Play. If you want plugins on your phone, the only rule-safe route is to run RuneLite on a real computer and stream or remote-play it to your phone. A "RuneLite Mobile" APK you sideload is a separate, unapproved third-party client under Jagex's rules and can get your account banned. This guide gives you the verified 2026 facts, the safe options, and exactly how to set up the official OSRS Mobile app.

1. The Straight Answer — RuneLite Is Desktop-Only

The single most asked question in every mobile thread is "where do I download RuneLite for my phone?" The honest answer, straight from the RuneLite project and Jagex, is that there is no official RuneLite mobile client. RuneLite runs on Windows, macOS and Linux, and on a Jagex Account it launches through the Jagex Launcher. The launcher itself is only built for desktop Windows and macOS — there is no mobile launcher and no mobile RuneLite build that the RuneLite team publishes or supports.

This is not a temporary gap. RuneLite's own FAQ has carried the question "Will there be a RuneLite mobile client?" for years, and the project has never shipped one. The official OSRS Mobile app is Jagex's answer to playing on a phone, and it is a separate, Jagex-made client — not a port of RuneLite. So if a website or video tells you to "install RuneLite from the App Store," that listing does not exist; what you would actually be installing is either the official Jagex app or an unapproved third-party build.

💡 The one-sentence memory hook

RuneLite = your computer. OSRS Mobile = your phone. They are two different clients that log into the same account — RuneLite has never been compiled for iOS or Android.

2. Jagex's Approved-Client Rules — What's Actually Allowed

Before weighing any "run RuneLite on mobile" method, you need the rule that decides whether it is safe. Jagex's published Code of Conduct is explicit about third-party clients:

📜 What Jagex Approves

  • Official Client — Jagex's own desktop client (the "New Official Client")
  • Mobile — the official Old School RuneScape app for iOS and Android
  • RuneLite — the approved third-party desktop client
  • HDOS — the second approved third-party desktop client

The rule continues: "Using other third-party software to play Old School RuneScape violates the Rules of RuneScape, and may result in a temporary or permanent ban." That wording is what makes a mobile RuneLite port a real risk. A sideloaded "RuneLite Mobile" APK is not the approved desktop RuneLite — it is a different, unapproved client, so it falls under "other third-party software." Jagex has stated it does not ban for using approved RuneLite, but it has also been clear that unapproved clients are a bannable offence.

Two more points matter for mobile users. First, a Jagex Account can only log in through the Jagex Launcher or the official Mobile apps — the launcher supports only the Official Client, Old School, and RuneLite. There is no mobile entry point for an unapproved build. Second, what gets accounts banned fastest is not the client alone but what runs inside it: bots and automation. Even on approved RuneLite, any plugin that plays the game for you breaks the macroing rule. Keep that line crystal clear wherever you play.

💡 The safest possible rule

If the client you launch is on Jagex's approved list (Official, Mobile, RuneLite, HDOS) and you are not automating input, you are rule-safe. If it is anything else — including a "RuneLite Mobile" APK — you are taking a ban risk with your account.

3. Why Players Want RuneLite on Mobile

The demand is real, and it is worth naming why before we cover the methods — because the "why" tells you which safe route actually solves your problem.

  • Plugins: Ground Markers, Menu Entry Swapper, Item Identification, Bank Tags, GPU, and the loot tracker are the features people miss most on the bare mobile app.
  • Skilling AFK on the couch: many players want to bankstand, fletch, or train a low-attention skill from a phone without losing their desktop plugin setup.
  • Bigger screen comfort: a tablet feels better than a small phone for long sessions, and players assume "desktop client on a tablet" should just work.
  • One account everywhere: people already bounce between PC and phone and expect the plugin layer to follow them.

The catch is that plugins are a property of the RuneLite desktop client, not of your account. They live in the program on your computer. That is exactly why the only rule-safe way to "have RuneLite on your phone" is to keep RuneLite on a computer and put your phone in front of it — covered next.

4. Safe Ways to Get the RuneLite Feel On the Go

These methods are safe because the actual game client running is the approved desktop RuneLite (or the official app). Your phone is only a screen and input device — it never runs an unapproved client.

① Remote desktop to your home PC (most faithful)

If you have a computer at home with RuneLite installed, a remote desktop app (for example Chrome Remote Desktop, Microsoft Remote Desktop, or Parsec) lets your phone view and control that computer. You launch RuneLite on the PC, then tap and drag on your phone to play it. Every plugin you already use is there, because you are literally using your desktop RuneLite. The downsides are latency and the need for a always-on PC — fine for skilling, less ideal for precise PvM.

② Cloud gaming / cloud PC (no home hardware needed)

A cloud PC service runs a virtual Windows machine in a data centre. Install the Jagex Launcher and RuneLite there once, then open that machine from your phone's browser or app and play. The game renders on the server, your phone just streams it. This is rule-safe (you are running approved RuneLite on a real Windows box) and works on both iOS and Android, but it needs a subscription and a stable connection.

③ Emulator running RuneLite on a PC (not on the phone)

Strictly speaking this is a desktop solution: an Android emulator like BlueStacks or LDPlayer on your Windows/Mac PC can run the official OSRS Mobile app in a window. It does not give you RuneLite plugins — it gives you the mobile app on a big screen with keyboard and mouse. Useful if you want the mobile interface on PC, but it is not "RuneLite on mobile." For true plugins, use route ① or ②.

✅ Rule-safe summary

  • RuneLite on a PC, viewed through remote desktop on your phone — safe
  • RuneLite on a cloud Windows PC, streamed to your phone — safe
  • Official OSRS Mobile app on the phone itself — safe (and the everyday choice)
  • Any app that IS RuneLite compiled for iOS/Android — does not officially exist

5. The Risky Path — Third-Party RuneLite Mobile APKs

It would be dishonest to write this guide without naming the method players actually search for: community-built "RuneLite Mobile" APKs that you sideload onto an Android phone (or, on iOS, sideload through a third-party store). These projects do exist in the community and they can technically run RuneLite-style plugins on a phone. But here is the verified, straight talk on the risk:

  • It is an unapproved client. Jagex's rule lists only RuneLite (desktop) and HDOS among approved third-party clients. A mobile RuneLite build is a different program and is not on that list — so using it "violates the Rules of RuneScape, and may result in a temporary or permanent ban."
  • Account security risk on top of the ban risk. You are installing software from outside the App Store or Play Store and handing it your Jagex login. The RuneLite project itself warns players to "be careful of unofficial clients claiming to be 'RuneLite' that are not from us." A malicious build can steal your credentials regardless of any ban.
  • iOS is even harder and riskier. Apple does not allow sideloading RuneLite-style clients without enterprise certificates or a jailbreak, both of which add their own account and device risk. There is no clean iOS path.
  • Jagex can act on detection. Unapproved clients are a known detection target. Even if many users report "nothing happened yet," that is not a safety guarantee — enforcement can be retrospective.

Our recommendation is blunt: do not install a RuneLite Mobile APK on an account you care about. If you must experiment, use a throwaway with nothing of value, understand it breaks Jagex's rules, and never reuse your main's password there. For any real account, stick to the safe routes in Section 4 or the official app in Section 6.

💡 If a site asks for your Jagex password outside the launcher

Stop. The official launcher and official app are the only places you should ever type your Jagex login. Any "RuneLite Mobile download" page that takes your credentials directly is a phishing setup, not a client.

6. Official OSRS Mobile App — Android & iOS Setup

For daily play on a phone, the official OSRS Mobile app is the right tool: free, Jagex-made, cross-platform with your PC account, and completely rule-safe. Setup takes two minutes.

① Install

  • Android: open the Google Play Store, search "Old School RuneScape," and install the official Jagex app. Make sure your device is 64-bit — Jagex ended 32-bit-only Android support, so a very old 32-bit phone can no longer run it.
  • iOS: open the App Store, search "Old School RuneScape," and install. Requires iOS 13 or later on iPhone or iPad.

② Log in and sync

Log in with your Jagex Account email and password — the same one you use on the desktop launcher. Your stats, bank, quest progress, Grand Exchange offers, and membership all carry over. You can even switch from PC to phone mid-session. If login fails on a fresh account, log in once on desktop to activate it, then try mobile again.

③ Recommended first-settings

🎚️ Settings to change on first launch

  • Graphics Quality: start at Medium — mobile targets roughly 20–30 FPS versus 60+ on PC; Medium is the smooth sweet spot, Low if it stutters.
  • Biometric login: enable FaceID or fingerprint (Settings → Account) so you rarely type your password.
  • Pinch to zoom: Settings → Gameplay — zoom the camera with two fingers, great for farming and clue spots.
  • Tap to drop: Settings → Gameplay — faster inventory clearing than the default long-press.
  • Sound: off or low for public play.
  • 2-step verification (2SV): set up immediately (see Section 7) — this is the single biggest anti-hijack step.

The mobile app has no plugin hub, so you will not get Ground Markers or Menu Entry Swapper there. That is the trade-off for a client that is safe, lightweight, and officially supported. For plugin-heavy sessions, use your PC RuneLite; for playing anywhere, the mobile app is the answer.

7. RuneLite Settings to Mirror & Account Safety

Two finishing topics: how to make your desktop RuneLite run well (so streaming it to a phone feels good), and how to keep the account behind all of this safe.

① RuneLite desktop settings worth turning on

  • GPU plugin: enable it (Configuration → GPU) for smoother frames and lower CPU load — set FPS to 50 and turn anti-aliasing off on weaker hardware. This matters most when you remote-play, since encoding a high-FPS screen is cheaper when the client is already efficient.
  • Fixed vs resizable: resizable mode scales better to a phone screen streamed over remote desktop than the fixed 765×503 window.
  • Ground Items, Menu Entry Swapper, Bank Tags, Item Identification: the classic safe Plugin Hub picks — all free, all approved, none automate input.
  • Wiki plugin: the in-game Wiki lookup, handy when you are away from a second monitor.

② Account safety checklist (non-negotiable)

🔒 Protect the account behind every client

  • Use a Jagex Account, not a character password — it syncs across PC, mobile, and the launcher, and is the modern login Jagex supports everywhere.
  • Turn on 2-step verification (2SV) in Account Settings before you switch devices; it blocks almost all hijacking.
  • Download RuneLite only from runelite.net and the OSRS Mobile app only from the App Store / Google Play — never from a forum link, Discord file, or "download this jar" instruction.
  • Never automate: bots and input macros break the macroing rule even inside approved RuneLite. Plugins that play for you are the fastest route to a ban.
  • One password, one place: only ever enter your Jagex login in the official launcher or official app. A "RuneLite Mobile" page asking for it directly is phishing.

Follow those five and the client you choose barely matters to your safety — the account is locked down regardless of whether you are on PC RuneLite or the phone app.

8. RuneLite Mobile FAQs

Q: Is there an official RuneLite app for iPhone or Android?

No. RuneLite is desktop-only (Windows, macOS, Linux) and launches through the Jagex Launcher. There is no official iOS or Android build, and the RuneLite project has never published one.

Q: Will using a RuneLite Mobile APK get me banned?

It carries real ban risk. Jagex's rules approve only the Official Client, the Mobile app, RuneLite (desktop) and HDOS. A mobile RuneLite port is a separate, unapproved client, and using unapproved third-party software "may result in a temporary or permanent ban." We do not recommend it on any account you value.

Q: What is the safest way to use RuneLite plugins on my phone?

Run RuneLite on a real computer (your PC or a cloud Windows PC) and remote-play or stream it to your phone. The game still runs through approved desktop RuneLite; your phone is only a screen, so it stays rule-safe.

Q: Can I use the official OSRS Mobile app and RuneLite on the same account?

Yes. Both log into the same Jagex Account, and your stats, bank, and membership carry over. Most players use RuneLite on PC and the official mobile app when they are away from their computer.

Q: Does the mobile app have RuneLite's plugins?

No. The official OSRS Mobile app has its own smaller set of built-in settings and no plugin hub. Plugins are a feature of the desktop RuneLite client, not of your account, so they do not appear on mobile.

Q: Is HDOS a safe mobile option?

HDOS is an approved third-party client like RuneLite, but it is also desktop-only. It is not a mobile client, so the same "run it on a PC and stream to your phone" logic applies if you want its look on the go.

Q: Can I play OSRS Mobile on an old 32-bit Android phone?

No — Jagex dropped 32-bit-only Android support, so those devices can no longer launch the app. Any 64-bit Android phone, iPhone, or iPad still works, as does the desktop client and emulator route on a PC.

Q: How do I keep my account safe while switching between PC and phone?

Use a Jagex Account, turn on 2-step verification before you switch devices, download clients only from official sources (runelite.net and the app stores), and never enter your password on a third-party "RuneLite Mobile" page. Those steps protect you no matter which client you open.
