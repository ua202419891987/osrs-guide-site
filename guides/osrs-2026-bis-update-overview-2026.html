OSRS 2026 BiS Update Overview — Sang Staff, Soulreaper Axe & Nex Changes Reshape Best-in-Slot

2026 reshuffled the Old School RuneScape best-in-slot (BiS) ladder harder than any single year since the release of Tumeken's Shadow. Three changes in particular rewrote the math: the Sanguinesti Staff healing rework, the Soulreaper Axe rework, and the Nex defence changes. If your gear plan was built on 2025 assumptions, this overview tells you exactly what moved, what stayed the same, and which setup wins in each scenario. 1. What Changed in 2026 (Summary)

Three balance updates in 2026 touched the core of endgame gear choices. None of them removed a mega-rare from the top spot, but each changed the runner-up tiers and the situational picks enough that older guides are now partly wrong. Before you liquidate a bank tab based on a forum post, read the actual scope of each change below.

Update What Moved Net Effect Who It Helps
Sang Staff ReworkHealing per hit rebalanced; accuracy tweak vs high magic-defenceSang loses some sustain edge, Shadow still kingShadow owners; budget magers unaffected
Soulreaper Axe ReworkStack decay slowed; special attack stack-to-damage raisedSRA rises in sustained melee; new BiS challenger1x1 solo PvMers; slayer mains
Nex Defence ChangesRanged-defence down; crush-defence upTbow edge grows; crush melee gains vs NexTbow owners; crush melee users

The big picture: the four mega-rares (Scythe of Vitur, Twisted Bow, Tumeken's Shadow, and now a stronger Soulreaper Axe) still define the ceiling, but the order you should buy them in, and the budget switches you lean on, have shifted. The changes also made "BiS" even more scenario-dependent than before — a weapon that is mediocre at one boss can be the clear best at another, and 2026 widened those gaps rather than narrowing them.

🔑 Don't panic-sell: None of the 2026 changes made an existing mega-rare obsolete. If you own a Scythe or Tbow, it is still BiS in its role. The changes mostly affect second-best items and specific bosses. The only weapon that genuinely climbed a tier is the Soulreaper Axe, and even then it complements rather than replaces the Scythe.

2. Sanguinesti Staff Change — Mage BiS Impact

The Sanguinesti Staff ("Sang") long held a unique niche: a powered magic weapon that heals you on hit, making it the safest mage option for sustained fights like Zulrah, the Nightmare, and GWD. The 2026 rework adjusted the healing formula and tightened its accuracy on very high-defence targets. Understanding exactly what changed is the difference between keeping a useful tool andmisjudged your mage DPS.

What the rework did

Healing per hit is now slightly lower at low HP and slightly higher at high HP, smoothing the sustain curve so that Sang is no longer a free "never die" button on low-HP lifesteal tricks. Accuracy against top-tier magic-defence bosses was reduced by a small margin. The result: Sang is still the best budget-to-mid maging weapon for sustain, but it no longer threatens Tumeken's Shadow for raw DPS, and on the hardest magic-defence bosses the gap widened. The rework was a rebalance of when the heal matters, not a nerf to its core identity.

Where Sang still wins

  • Zulrah & the Nightmare: Sustain matters more than peak DPS; Sang remains the comfortable pick and still saves you supplies versus a non-healing staff.
  • Slayer & GWD: Long trips where healing saves supplies — Sang pays for itself by cutting food and brew costs.
  • Budget raids: Before you own a Shadow, Sang is the safe mage weapon that lets you learn mechanics without bleeding supplies.
  • Low-Prayer trips: Because the heal is now stronger at high HP, Sang rewards staying topped up rather than relying on the lifesteal as a clutch save.

Once you own a Tumeken's Shadow, Sang becomes a sustain-switch rather than your main weapon. Keep one in the bank for trips where lifesteal beats burst — ToB healer switches, long Zulrah runs, and any fight where you would otherwise burn through brews. The rework did not change that role; it only trimmed the exploit where players sat at 1 HP and relied on the old healing floor.

🧪 Practical takeaway

If your only powered staff is Sang, do not rush to "upgrade" after the rework — it is still a top-three mage weapon for sustained content. The real upgrade path is Sang → Shadow, and that gap is unchanged in direction, only in the exact margin. Spend your GP on the Shadow when you can, not on replacing a still-good Sang.

3. Soulreaper Axe Rework — Melee BiS Impact

The Soulreaper Axe (SRA) was already a strong slash weapon that built damage stacks as you hit. The 2026 rework changed two things: the stacks now decay more slowly when you stop attacking, and the special attack's stack-to-damage conversion was increased. This turned SRA from a "niche solo boss killer" into a genuine BiS challenger in sustained single-target melee — and it is the single biggest gear-story of the year.

Why it matters

Because the stacks persist longer, SRA rewards players who keep attacking through mechanics instead of dropping to pray or move. In fights with frequent safe-attack windows — like Yama or mid-phase Nex — SRA now out-DPSes the Blade of Saeldor and can approach the Scythe on 1x1 targets where the Scythe only lands one splat per swing. The slower decay also means a brief movement phase no longer erases your entire stack investment, which is exactly what held SRA back in the old meta.

Where SRA is now BiS or near-BiS

  • Yama (solo / Duo): Sustained slash windows favour SRA — see our Yama deep dive for the exact rotation.
  • Single-target raid bosses: Where the Scythe's 3-hit AoE is wasted, SRA competes and often wins on DPS per hour.
  • Slayer bosses: Long single-target fights let stacks ramp fully; SRA is now a default pick over Fang for many tasks.
  • Nex (crush): With the Nex defence changes making crush more reliable, an SRA built on crush can rival the Scythe on single targets.

The Scythe of Vitur is still king on large multi-target NPCs (Verzik, Olm, Nightmare), but if you mostly do 1x1 content, the Soulreaper Axe rework makes it a smarter early purchase than a second mega-rare. Our Soulreaper Axe rework breakdown has the exact numbers and the special-attack rotation that maximizes the new conversion. Read it before you commit GP.

⚠️ Stacks need uptime: SRA's power assumes you keep hitting. If a fight forces you to drop attack often (constant movement phases), the Scythe or Fang may still beat it. Match the weapon to the fight, not the meta. On a pure DPS dummy the numbers look amazing; in a real fight with dodges, the Scythe's reliability can still win.

One more nuance: the special attack now converts stacks into a bigger burst, so the optimal SRA rotation is "build to 5 stacks, then spec" rather than speccing early. Players who treated SRA like a constant-DPS weapon under-used it pre-rework; post-rework the spec is the headline, not the passive. Plan your specs around the boss's vulnerable windows for the biggest gain.

4. Nex Defence Changes — Ranged & Melee Shifts

The 2026 Nex update rebalanced her defence rolls and adjusted which styles land most reliably during her vulnerability windows. This is the change that most reshaped per-boss BiS, because Nex is the primary source of several top-tier items (Torva, the rings, the hilt) and because so many players optimize their whole bank around farming her efficiently.

What shifted

Nex's ranged-defence dropped slightly while her crush-defence rose. Practically, the Twisted Bow's accuracy and DPS at Nex improved, and crush-based melee (Scythe, Inquisitor's Mace, SRA) became more reliable than stab or slash alternatives. The Nex defence changes guide goes deep on the numbers, but the table below captures the practical takeaway.

New Nex BiS takeaways

  • Ranged: Twisted Bow is now even more clearly BiS at Nex over the Zaryte Crossbow for DPS, though ZCB remains the bolt-spec pick for burst windows.
  • Melee: Crush styles (Scythe / Inquisitor's Mace) edge out the formerly popular stab setups — bring a Godsword or Scythe, not a Blade of Saeldor, for pure DPS.
  • Magic: Shadow unaffected, but Sang's accuracy nerf makes the Shadow more mandatory for fast Nex clears if you mage the kill.

If your goal is farming Nex for Torva and the rings, the defence changes nudge you toward Tbow + crush melee rather than the old mixed setup. The good news: none of this requires new items, just a smarter style choice. Re-gear your preset to crush and you have already "upgraded" for free.

5. Weapon-by-Weapon BiS Tiers (2026)

To make the shifts concrete, here is how the four headline melee/range/mage weapons now rank, and where the Soulreaper Axe slots in. The chart below is a relative DPS illustration on a 1x1 sustained dummy versus a large multi-target NPC — the two scenarios that define the 2026 meta.

The four mega-rares, ranked by flexibility

  1. Twisted Bow — The most flexible mega-rare. Strong at Nex, ToA, Zulrah, and anywhere with high magic-defence. The Nex changes only strengthened it.
  2. Tumeken's Shadow — The highest raw magic DPS and the clear mage BiS everywhere. Unaffected by the 2026 changes except indirectly (Sang got worse, so Shadow looks better by comparison).
  3. Scythe of Vitur — Still the multi-target melee king. Unaffected in rank; remains the default for ToB, Chambers, and Nightmare.
  4. Soulreaper Axe — The climber. From "strong niche" to "genuine BiS on 1x1." Cheaper than the Scythe and now a smart first melee mega-rare for solo players.

The headline of 2026 is not that the old order fell — it is that the fourth slot got far more competitive. If you were waiting for a reason to buy an SRA instead of saving for a second Scythe, the rework is that reason, provided your content is mostly single-target.

7. Slot-by-Slot Armour & Jewellery Shifts

Weapons grab the headlines, but the 2026 changes also nudged armour and jewellery choices — mostly because the style shifts at Nex and the SRA's new role change which secondary stats you want. None of these are "new BiS" swaps, but they are the optimizations that separate a clean kill from a sloppy one.

Armour

  • Torva: Still the melee BiS body set. The Nex crush change makes Torva's crush bonus marginally more valuable, so wear full Torva for Nex melee rather than mixing in cheaper pieces.
  • Masori (Fighter / Death): The ranged companion to Tbow. With Tbow strengthened at Nex, Masori's ranged accuracy and defence make it the default ranged armour for 2026 bossing.
  • Ancestral: Unchanged as the mage BiS. Pair with the Shadow; keep Sang as a sustain switch in a separate preset.
  • Inquisitor's armour: Rises in relevance because crush is now the Nex melee meta. If you already own the set, wear it for crush tasks; if not, Torva is still fine and more flexible.

Jewellery & caps

  • Ultor / Magus / Bellator rings: Still the style-specific BiS rings. The 2026 changes do not dethrone them; they just make the Magus (magic) ring more clearly mandatory at Nex if you mage.
  • Infernal Cape / Avernic hilt / Elidinis' Ward f: The untradeable upgrades that matter for maxed accounts. None changed in 2026 — they remain the "final" purchases.
  • Venator bow: Not a BiS pick for DPS, but its ricochet is useful on packed trash; unchanged by 2026.

The practical advice: do not go hunting for new armour in 2026. The changes reward wearing the armour you already own in the right style (crush at Nex, ranged accuracy with Tbow) more than they reward buying anything new. 8. Should You Re-Buy or Hold?

With the meta shifted, here is the pragmatic upgrade advice for three budget tiers. The short version: almost nobody needs to sell anything; most players need to re-allocate future GP, not re-buy past purchases.

If you own zero mega-rares

Buy Rigour and Augury first (always), then a Twisted Bow. The Nex and SRA changes did not dethrone Tbow's flexibility. After Tbow, choose between Scythe and Shadow based on your favourite content. The Soulreaper Axe is a cheaper melee option that now performs close to the Scythe on 1x1 content, so it is a smart early melee buy — arguably smarter than saving 1.2B for a Scythe if you mostly solo.

If you own one mega-rare

Hold it — it is still BiS in its role. Spend your next GP on the armour and rings that multiply its output (Masori for Tbow, Torva + Ultor for Scythe, Ancestral + Magus for Shadow). Consider an SRA as a cheap melee alternative if you main ranged or magic, or as your first melee weapon if you main melee and want 1x1 efficiency now.

If you own all four

Your only upgrades are untradeables (Infernal Cape, Avernic hilt, Elidinis' Ward f) and the DT2 rings. The 2026 changes do not add new must-buy items for maxed accounts — they mostly reward using what you have in the right fight. The "upgrade" for you is knowledge: bring crush to Nex, bring SRA to 1x1 content, keep Sang as a sustain switch.

Death and reclaim rules for dropped gear are unchanged; keep the Death Mechanics guide bookmarked so a costly death does not erase your progress. Losing a 1B+ Scythe to a reclaim fee you did not understand is a far bigger setback than any balance change.

9. Funding the Upgrades in 2026

A BiS plan is only as good as the GP behind it. The 2026 changes did not alter money-making rates directly, but they did change which bosses are worth farming and therefore where the GP should come from. Here is how to pay for the new meta without burning out.

Farm what the meta made valuable

  • Nex: With crush + Tbow now the optimal setup, Nex is more efficient than ever for Torva and rings. If you can field that setup, Nex is the single best GP-per-hour for endgame gear funding.
  • Yama: The SRA's rise makes Yama a premier solo moneymaker — the weapon you want is also the weapon that farms its own content well.
  • ToA / ToB: Stable raid income; unchanged by 2026, so still a reliable default when bosses are dead.

Budget-track alternatives

If you are not at the mega-rare stage yet, the Budget Gear guide and Mid-Game Breakthrough guide show the sub-100M setups that bridge you to your first mega-rare. The 2026 changes actually help budget players: an SRA is far cheaper than a Scythe and now performs close to it on 1x1, so your first "big" melee purchase stretches further.

The takeaway: you do not need to out-earn the meta, you need to point your existing money-making at the bosses the meta rewarded. Farm Nex and Yama with the right style, bank the drops, and the SRA or Tbow funds itself faster than it would have in 2025.

10. Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Sanguinesti Staff still worth using after the 2026 rework?

Yes for sustain-focused content. The healing rework smoothed its lifesteal but did not remove it. Sang remains the best budget-to-mid mage weapon for Zulrah, the Nightmare, and long Slayer trips. It just no longer competes with Tumeken's Shadow for raw DPS, and the low-HP healing trick was trimmed, so don't rely on sitting at 1 HP anymore.

Did the Soulreaper Axe rework make it better than the Scythe?

On 1x1 sustained targets, yes — the slower stack decay and stronger special make SRA competitive with or better than the Scythe where the Scythe only lands one hit. On large multi-target NPCs (Verzik, Olm, Nightmare), the Scythe's 3-hit AoE still wins clearly. The honest answer is "it depends on the fight," which is exactly why both belong in a finished bank.

What changed for Nex Best-in-Slot in 2026?

Nex's ranged-defence dropped and crush-defence rose. The Twisted Bow became even more clearly BiS ranged, and crush melee (Scythe / Inquisitor's Mace / SRA) became more reliable than stab or slash. The Nex defence changes guide has the full breakdown, including the exact defence rolls before and after.

Should I sell my Scythe to buy a Soulreaper Axe?

No. The Scythe is still BiS at the majority of multi-target endgame bosses. Buy an SRA as an addition if you do lots of 1x1 content, not as a replacement. The two weapons cover different fights, and selling a Scythe to "rotate" into an SRA loses you group-content DPS for no real gain.

Do the 2026 changes affect ironman account progression?

The weapon and defence changes affect ironmen exactly as they affect mains — SRA is now a stronger 1x1 pick, Tbow is stronger at Nex, and crush is the Nex melee meta. The difference is that ironmen can't buy their way to the new meta, so the changes mostly shift which boss drops to prioritize. The SRA is a scratch-only weapon, so ironmen still need to earn it the long way.

Is Tumeken's Shadow still the mage BiS after these updates?

Yes, unchanged. The Sang rework only made Shadow look better by comparison, and the Nex magic meta is unaffected. If you mage, the Shadow remains the single best damage upgrade in the game and the 2026 changes did nothing to challenge it.

What armour should I wear at Nex now that crush is meta?

Full Torva for melee crush, since the crush-defence change makes Torva's crush bonus more valuable there; pair with an Ultor ring. For ranged, Masori (Fighter) with Tbow. For magic, Ancestral with the Shadow and a Magus ring. None of these armour pieces are "new" — you just wear them in crush rather than mixing stab/slash.

How should a mid-game player adapt without a mega-rare?

Focus on the budget path: a Blowpipe for ranged, a Sang (or Trident) for mage, and an SRA or Fang for melee once you can afford one. The SRA rework is actually good news for you — it performs close to a Scythe on 1x1 for a fraction of the cost. Our Budget Gear guide lays out the exact sub-100M loadout.

Did any armour or jewellery become obsolete in 2026?

No. No armour or jewellery piece was removed from BiS or meaningfully dethroned. The shifts were about which style to wear existing armour in (crush at Nex) and about weapons (SRA climbing). Your Torva, Masori, Ancestral, and DT2 rings are all still correct.

11. Final Tips — Playing the 2026 Meta

  • Match weapon to target size. Scythe for big, SRA/Blade for 1x1, Tbow for high magic-defence, Shadow for magic-vulnerable.
  • Keep Sang as a sustain switch. Even with a Shadow, Sang saves supplies on long trips and the rework didn't remove that role.
  • Farm Nex with crush + Tbow. The defence changes reward that exact combo — free DPS by re-gearing your preset.
  • Don't chase re-buys. Your existing mega-rares are still BiS in their roles; the upgrade is knowledge, not a new item.
  • Read the per-boss table. "BiS" is always scenario-specific, and 2026 made that truer than ever.
  • Point your GP at the new meta. Nex and Yama are the most efficient farms for funding the SRA/Tbow you want.

12. Build Your 2026 Loadout

The 2026 updates did not overturn the gear hierarchy — they sharpened it. Sang stays the sustain king, the Soulreaper Axe rework made it a real melee contender, and the Nex defence changes tilted the boss-farming meta toward crush melee and the Twisted Bow.
