🎭 Fake Discord "Admins" & Support Impersonation: The Full Playbook (2026)

The scam that empties the most OSRS banks in 2026 does not start in-game. It starts with a private message on Discord from someone claiming to be a server admin, a Jagex moderator, or "support" investigating a breach on your account. They sound official, they create panic, and within twenty minutes they have your 2FA code and your bank. This guide reproduces the full playbook so you can recognize every step while it is still happening.

🛡️ Level: All

The single rule that defeats every version of this scam is simple: Jagex will never contact you first, and they will never ask for your password, your 2FA code, or your email login. If a "Discord admin" or "Jagex support" DMs you, it is a scam by definition. Pair that rule with the anti-scam account security checklist, and if you have already been phished, jump straight to the bank-drained recovery guide for the exact first-hour steps.

This guide covers the Discord and chat-based flavor of impersonation specifically. The broader "is this website real" problem is handled separately in our phishing-domain checker guide, and the human "friend" version is covered in the trust-scam guide — but the Discord admin scam is its own animal because it weaponizes authority and fear at the same time.

⚡ Quick Verdict

Any unexpected message from a "Discord admin," "Jagex moderator," or "support agent" is a scam until proven otherwise — and it can never be proven, because those people do not initiate contact with players. They will push you into a voice channel, demand your multi-factor authentication code "to verify you," threaten to ban or lock your account, and use the code you give them to log in as you. Never share a code, never join their "verify" voice call, and report the account the instant the DM arrives.

1. 🎭 What Is Discord / Support Impersonation?

Support impersonation is a scam where the attacker pretends to be an authority figure — a Discord server administrator, a Jagex moderator, or a customer-support agent — to trick you into handing over the keys to your account. Unlike a random phishing link, this scam uses a believable identity and manufactured urgency to bypass your caution. The attacker is not hacking you; they are consensually talking you into giving them access.

① The two faces of the scam

The first face is the fake Discord admin. They join or already sit in a legitimate OSRS clan or community Discord, clone a moderator's name and avatar, and DM members claiming they need to "verify" you or that you have broken a rule. The second face is the fake Jagex support agent — they message you claiming your account is compromised, under investigation, or selected for a security check, and they need your login or MFA code to "confirm ownership." Both faces share one truth: real authorities do not initiate these conversations with players.

② Why Discord is the hunting ground

Most OSRS players live part of their game life on Discord, and Discord DMs feel personal and private in a way the in-game chat does not. Scammers exploit that intimacy. A server full of thousands of players is a ready-made target list, and a cloned-mod profile lets them message dozens of people at once while looking legitimate. Because Discord is outside Jagex's systems, the game's own scam filters never see the conversation — which is exactly why the attack moved there.

③ How they get your Discord and RSN

They rarely guess. They harvest from public clan servers, from "OSRS giveaway" or "trading" Discords they create to attract players, from leaked RSN lists, and from your own posts where you link your Discord tag to your account. Once they have your tag, they send the DM. Sometimes they already sit in your server under a throwaway name, watching who talks the most and who looks inexperienced, then strike when you seem active and distracted.

2. 🪤 The Full Playbook (Step by Step)

Every Discord admin scam follows the same arc. Learning the sequence lets you shut it down at step one instead of step five, which is where the damage happens.

① The unsolicited DM out of nowhere

It starts with a private message you did not ask for. The sender claims a role: "Hey, I'm an admin on the OSRS server," or "Jagex Support here, we're reviewing accounts." The message is polite and ordinary, never aggressive yet. Its only job is to open the conversation and establish a fake identity. If you reply at all, you have confirmed you are a live, responsive target, and the next messages escalate.

② The panic: "your account is compromised"

Within a message or two, they manufacture fear. "We detected a login from Russia on your account." "Your bank has been flagged for RWT." "You violated a rule and your account will be banned in 30 minutes unless you verify." Panic is the engine of the scam — a frightened player stops thinking and starts complying. The details are vague on purpose; they are designed to feel plausible, not to be checkable. Real security systems do not warn you through a Discord DM from a stranger.

③ The fake voice channel / screen-share

Next they move you off text and into a voice channel, usually one they created and named something official like "Jagex Verify" or "Security Check." Once you are in voice, they pressure you to share your screen "so we can walk you through verification." Screen-sharing is the danger zone: they can watch you type, spot your email, and catch autofill passwords. Refusing to join voice is one of the strongest walls you can put up — a real support process never requires a stranger to watch your screen.

④ The MFA / 2FA code demand

This is the kill shot. They tell you a verification code is being sent to your email or phone "from Jagex," and they ask you to read it to them or paste it in a form. What actually happened: they tried to log in as you, Jagex sent YOU the legitimate multi-factor code, and the scammer is tricking you into handing them that code. With it, they clear the only lock between them and your bank. No code from Jagex is ever meant to be shared with another person — by design, the code proves you are you, and giving it away proves you are them.

⑤ The blackmail / ban threat

If you hesitate, they escalate to threats: "If you don't verify now your account is permanently banned," or "We'll report your RSN for RWT and you'll lose everything." Some even pivot to blackmail after access, threatening to "keep your account locked" unless you pay. None of it is real — Jagex bans are handled through the official system, never through a Discord negotiation — but the threat is effective on someone already scared. The moment threats appear, you are deep in a scam; the only correct move is to stop replying and report.

3. 🔍 Real Message Examples (What the DMs Look Like)

Scammers reuse templates. Seeing the actual phrasing helps you recognize it even when the names change. These are representative examples, not screenshots — we never use fake screenshots.

① Fake Discord mod DM

"Hey, I'm a moderator on the OSRS Community server. We're doing a routine security sweep and your account was flagged for suspicious trades. Join the verify voice channel and share your screen so we can confirm you're not a bot. It'll take two minutes." — Note the friendly opener, the fake authority, the rush to voice, and the screen-share ask. None of it is something a real moderator would ever do.

② Fake Jagex support ticket

"This is Jagex Support. We detected an unauthorized login on your account from a new device. To secure it, open the link below and enter the 6-digit code we just sent to your email. Do not share this with anyone except support." — The link goes to a cloned site, and the "code" line is pure misdirection: they want the code Jagex emailed YOU when THEY tried to log in. Jagex support uses the official ticket system, never a Discord DM with a link.

③ Fake "verify your account" link

"Your account will be disabled in 24 hours under the new Jagex Terms. Verify ownership here: jagex-account-verify[.]com." — The domain is the tell. It is not jagex.com, not runescape.com, and not oldschool.runescape.com. Any "verify," "secure," or "login" link sent to you by a stranger is a phishing link, full stop. Validate every domain with our account security guide before clicking anything, and never type your password into a site you reached from a message.

4. 🚩 How to Spot a Fake Instantly

You do not need to read the whole message. Three checks decide it in two seconds. The table below makes the contrast explicit.

Signal Real Jagex / Real Admin Impersonation Scam
Who starts contact You contact them (support ticket) They DM you out of nowhere
Channel Official site / in-game / support email Discord DM, WhatsApp, Telegram, Steam
MFA code Never asked to share it Demands your 2FA / email code
Tone Calm, procedural Urgent, fear-based, threatening
Link domain jagex.com / runescape.com / oldschool.runescape.com Look-alike domains, shortened links
Screen-share Never required Pressures you into voice + screen-share
Payment to "unban" Never Asks for GP, bond, or crypto to fix it

① Official domains are the fastest tell

Jagex operates exactly four legitimate player-facing domains: jagex.com, runescape.com, oldschool.runescape.com, and secure.runescape.com (account login). Anything else — jagex-verify.com, jagex-support-discord.net, runescape-account-secure.info — is a fake. If a message links you somewhere that is not one of those four, it is phishing, regardless of how official the page looks. Bookmark the real login and never click through from a DM.

② Tone and urgency are manipulation, not help

Real support is boring and slow. Scammers are theatrical and fast. "Your account will be banned in 30 minutes," "verify now or lose your bank," "this is your final warning" — these are pressure tactics, not information. A genuine process gives you time and uses official channels. When the message makes your heart race, that feeling is the scam working; step away from the keyboard for five minutes before doing anything.

③ Any request for a code is the red line

No matter who they claim to be, no legitimate party ever needs your multi-factor code, your email login code, or your authenticator number. The code exists to prove you are you; handing it to someone else hands them your identity. If the message asks for a code, it is a scam, and you can stop reading right there. Report and block.

5. 🛡️ How to Protect Your Discord & Jagex Account

Protection means making the scam technically impossible, not just recognizing it. A few settings remove the attacker's entire toolkit.

① Never share codes — and lock DMs down

The bedrock rule: your MFA code is yours alone, forever. Beyond that, tighten Discord itself. Turn off DMs from server members you do not know (Server Settings → Privacy → "Allow DMs from server members"), so cloned-mod scammer accounts cannot even reach you. Use a hardware or app-based authenticator on both Discord and your Jagex account, and never reuse the same password across them. Our anti-scam checklist has the full settings walkthrough.

② Harden Discord security settings

Enable Discord's own two-factor authentication, turn on "Scan QR code login" warnings, and review authorized apps in User Settings → Authorized Apps — revoke anything you do not recognize. Set a strong, unique password and consider linking Discord to an email that is not your Jagex email. The goal is to make your Discord itself un-hijackable, because a hijacked Discord is how scammers impersonate YOU to your clan later.

③ Remember Jagex never DMs and never asks

Internalize this as a reflex: Jagex moderators and support staff do not have Discord, do not DM players, and will never ask for your password or 2FA code. Any "Jagex" presence on Discord is impersonation by definition. If you ever genuinely need support, you open a ticket at support.runescape.com yourself — support does not come to you. The Jagex account migration guide explains why a migrated Jagex account also reduces how much a stolen code can do.

④ Separate emails and monitor recovery

Use a dedicated email for your Jagex account that you use for nothing else, with its own 2FA. That way a leak in some forum or store cannot be used to reset your game login. Periodically review your Jagex account's registered email and linked options, and enable login notifications where available so a stranger's attempt shows up before they succeed. If you play on mobile, also read the mobile fake-app phishing guide so a cloned app cannot capture the same codes.

6. 🚨 What to Do If You Already Gave a Code

If you already pasted a code, joined the voice call, or typed your password into a linked site, assume the account is compromised right now. Speed is everything — the scammer is likely already moving your bank to a mule.

① Change your password immediately

Go to the official Jagex login (type the URL yourself, do not use any link from the scammer) and change your password from a clean device. If you cannot log in because they already changed it, use the official account recovery form right away. Do this from a device you trust, on a network you trust, and after running a malware scan if you ever downloaded anything they sent.

② Revoke sessions and MFA where possible

In your Jagex account security settings, log out all other sessions and re-issue or replace your authenticator so the old code they have becomes useless. On Discord, do the same: change the password, revoke the scammer's access, and review Authorized Apps. Cutting their live session is what stops the drain in progress, so do it before anything else.

③ Report the scammer and your own clan

Report the Discord account (right-click → Report) and notify your server's real moderators so they can warn others and possibly ban the clone. File a Jagex support ticket describing what happened, including the scammer's Discord tag and RSN if known. Then follow the detailed first-hour steps in the bank-drained recovery guide, because the items-moving part of the response is identical to a phishing drain.

④ Secure everything connected

Treat the breach as wider than OSRS. If you reused that password or email anywhere, rotate those too. Check your registered email for takeover attempts, enable 2FA everywhere it was missing, and review your bank for partial losses you might not notice at first. The account-hacked prevention guide is the checklist to work through so one mistake does not become a cascade. Accept that recovered items are unlikely — the win is stopping the bleeding and getting the account actioned.

7. ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Can a real Jagex moderator message me on Discord?

No. Jagex staff do not use Discord to contact players, and they do not have "moderator" accounts in community servers. Any Discord user claiming to be Jagex is impersonating them. The only legitimate support channel is the official ticket system at support.runescape.com, which you initiate yourself.

What if the message shows a Jagex logo and looks completely real?

Logos and branded layouts are trivial to copy — that is the whole point of impersonation. Appearance proves nothing. What proves legitimacy is the channel (official site, not a DM) and the behavior (they never ask for your code). A beautiful fake is still a fake.

I joined the voice channel but gave no code. Am I safe?

Probably, but act anyway. Change your password and revoke sessions as a precaution, because being in that channel may have exposed your screen or typed input. Better to rotate credentials once for free than discover later that something was captured. Report the account regardless.

They sent a "verification link" that looked like jagex.com. How do I check it?

Read the full domain, not the display text. A link can say "jagex.com" while pointing to "jagex-account-verify.xyz." Hover (without clicking) to see the real URL, and only ever log in at jagex.com, runescape.com, or oldschool.runescape.com. Anything else is phishing. Our account security guide lists the exact safe domains.

Can Jagex reverse the trade if a fake admin drained my bank?

Rarely. If the scammer logged in as you and moved items, Jagex may be able to action the account and sometimes restore in clear hack cases, but there is no guarantee and no automatic refund. The realistic priority is stopping the session and getting the account banned. The bank-drained recovery guide covers what support can and cannot do.

Should I pay the "fee" they say will unban my account?

Never. Jagex never charges players to resolve a ban or verify an account, and any "pay to unlock" demand is pure extortion added on top of the scam. Paying confirms you are willing to pay and often triggers more threats. Stop contacting them and follow the recovery steps instead.

How do I warn my clan without causing panic?

Message your real moderators directly with the scammer's Discord tag and a screenshot of the DM (redact nothing they sent). Ask them to post a calm warning. Reporting the account also feeds Discord's systems. A short, factual alert protects everyone without the hysteria scammers try to create.

Is the "admin" scam different from the general phishing guide?

They overlap, but the Discord admin scam is a distinct delivery method: it uses a person pretending to be authority inside a chat app you trust, rather than a cloned website alone. The phishing-domain checker guide covers spotting fake sites; this guide covers spotting fake people. You need both, because scammers combine them — a fake admin who sends a fake link.

My Discord is set to block DMs from server members. Am I fully safe?

Much safer, but not fully. Scammers also use friend requests, group DMs, and cloned servers that you might join willingly. Blocking server DMs removes the most common path, but the rule "never share a code, never click a stranger's link" still applies everywhere. Defense in depth beats any single setting.
