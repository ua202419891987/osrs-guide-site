The difference between a casual NMZ runner and an advanced one is not luck — it is loadout design. The bosses you select, the potions you bring, the gear you wear, and the order in which you spend reward points determine whether you get 30k XP/hour or 90k+, and whether your points bank grows or stalls. NMZ rewards planning more than almost any other combat activity in the game. There is no random drop table, no Prayer-draining wilderness, and no risk of losing your items — every single variable is under your control. That is precisely why the optimizers love it: once you tune the five inputs (boss selection, prayer, potions, gear, and point spending), the output becomes predictable and repeatable. The sections below turn those inputs into a concrete system you can run on autopilot for hundreds of hours without leaving XP or points on the table.

1. Why NMZ Is Still Relevant in 2026

With raids, the Colosseum, and Sailing content dominating the meta, some players assume NMZ is outdated. It is not. Three things keep NMZ in every serious player's rotation, and none of them have changed in a decade because the minigame's design is deliberately isolated from the rest of the economy.

  • Reward points buy permanent upgrades. Imbued rings (Berserker, Archer, Seer, Warrior), the Salve amulet (e/i), and the Slayer helmet imbue are all unlocked here — and none of them decay, degrade, or get nerfed. They are, quite literally, free stat boosts that would cost hundreds of millions on the Grand Exchange.
  • It is the safest high-XP combat training. You cannot die in NMZ. If your Hitpoints and absorption both hit zero, you are ejected from the dream and dropped at the entrance with every item you walked in with. That makes it ideal for training stats on a low-defense account, an early Ironman that cannot afford risky bossing, or any build where you want controlled, predictable XP with zero item risk.
  • Herb boxes & seeds. Reward points convert into herb boxes and other supplies that fund the rest of your account. An Ironman who runs NMZ for imbues and then converts surplus points to herb boxes effectively builds a self-sustaining Herblore pipeline for free.

For mains, NMZ is a pit stop on the way to bigger content — a place to grab a couple of imbues and a Slayer helmet boost before moving on. For Ironmen, NMZ is often the only realistic path to imbued jewelry before raids, which is exactly why the Ironman section below exists. Even for maxed players, a periodic NMZ grind remains the cheapest way to top up ring imbues lost to death or to bank herb boxes during a long skill grind.

💡 NMZ vs Alternative Training

Use NMZ when you want controlled, AFK-friendly combat XP or need imbues. Use Slayer or bosses when you want drops and GP. They are complementary, not competing — many end-game players keep a passive NMZ tab open while doing other things, because the XP-per-attention is unmatched.

It is also worth noting that NMZ scales beautifully with account progression. A brand-new 50 Combat account benefits from the same minigame as a maxed one; only the bosses available and the gear worn change. That continuity is rare in OSRS and is the real reason NMZ never falls out of the meta — it is useful at every stage of an account's life.

2. Best Boss Combinations by Goal

NMZ lets you fight up to 5 quest bosses at once, but only after you buy customisable mode from Dominic Onion. The combination you pick changes three things at once: how much XP you get, how many points you earn, and how much attention the session needs. Before we name specific bosses, separate the goals, because the "best" combo is different for each.

AFK / low-attention combo

For pure AFK, you want bosses that are weak, slow, and attack with a single style (usually melee) so you can camp one Protect prayer and barely glance at the screen. A classic AFK set is King Roald, The Kendal, Tree Spirit, Traitorous Hazeleaf, and The Everlasting Wyrm (substitute Sand Snake if any are unavailable). These low-HP bosses die fast to your DPS, keep your absorption topped up easily, and let you watch a show while training. The trade-off is that low-HP bosses award fewer points, because points scale partly with damage dealt.

Maximum points-per-hour combo

Points are awarded for damage dealt and kills, so higher-HP bosses generally pay more — but they also demand more attention and more food, because they hit harder and you must sip Absorption more often. The well-known "points" combo leans on a few tanky bosses mixed with easier ones. The trade-off: more points, less AFK. If your only goal is banking points to buy imbues, accept that you will be clicking more, and bring slightly more Absorption to survive the heavier hits.

Speed XP combo

If raw XP/hour is the goal, you want the highest-HP bosses you can kill fastest, paired with max DPS gear and an Overload. More HP killed per minute equals more XP per minute. This is where a Toxic Trident, Blowpipe, or Dharok's Greataxe shines, because it shortens each kill and keeps the XP flowing. This combo is the least AFK and the most potion-hungry, but it produces the 90k+ XP/hr numbers people quote.

Ease / new-account combo

Brand-new NMZ accounts should pick the weakest 5 available bosses from their completed quests. You are not optimizing points yet; you are learning the potion rhythm and building a points balance toward customisable mode. Once you can afford customisable mode, graduate to the AFK or points combos above.

🎛️ Combo Decision Matrix

  • Want AFK? Pick 5 low-HP, melee-only bosses. Camp one Protect prayer.
  • Want points? Mix in 1–2 high-HP bosses; accept more active play.
  • Want speed XP? High-DPS gear + Overload + a points-style combo.
  • Want safety? Avoid bosses with dangerous special attacks until you know the fight.

The table below compares the four goal profiles side by side so you can pick the one that matches what your account needs right now. Note that the numbers are directional ranges — your exact result depends on gear, stats, and how cleanly you keep the Absorption pool up.

Goal Boss HP Profile Attention Points/Hr XP/Hr Best When
AFK 5 low-HP, melee-only Very low Low (~30–50k) Moderate (~40–60k) You want XP while doing something else
Points 1–2 high-HP + 3 easy Medium High (~80–120k) Good (~60–80k) Chasing imbues, points are the priority
Speed XP 5 high-HP, fast-killable High Medium (~50–80k) Very high (~80–100k+) Pure XP grind on a geared account
Ease 5 weakest available Low Lowest Low Learning the loop, saving for customisable

Your gear matters as much as your combo. A Toxic Trident or Blowpipe melts low-HP bosses so fast that even an "AFK" combo becomes near-passive. Review the boss gear checklist for the right weapon before optimizing combos, because the gap between a weak weapon and a strong one is the difference between an XP/hour number you are happy with and one you abandon.

3. Absorption Potion & Overload Economy

The potion system is what makes NMZ unique, and mastering it is the heart of "advanced" play. Two potion types do the heavy lifting, and a third exists only as emergency insurance. Understanding how they interact is the single biggest XP multiplier available to you.

Overload: your all-in-one buff

The Overload potion temporarily boosts your Attack, Strength, Defence, Ranged, and Magic well above normal super-potion levels, and it lasts long enough to cover most sessions. Critically, it removes the need to carry separate combat potions — one Overload replaces five super potions. The cost is that Overload drains a chunk of your Hitpoints when it activates, so you must let your Absorption pool (or food) absorb that hit rather than letting it drop you. Advanced players bring exactly one Overload (sometimes two for very long sessions) and never waste a dose, because a second Overload does nothing the first did not already do.

Absorption: the damage sponge

Absorption potions fill a separate "absorption pool" that soaks damage before it touches your Hitpoints. While the pool is active, nearly all boss damage is absorbed instead of lowering your HP. The pool has a high cap (1,000), and a single 4-dose Absorption potion restores a large portion of it — enough that a typical loadout of around ten to twelve Absorption (4) potions covers a full session with Overload handling your stats. The diagram below shows how the pool sits in front of your Hitpoints like a shield: damage hits the pool first, and only when the pool is empty does it reach your red bar.

The standard "dharok-style" NMZ inventory

The most efficient NMZ inventory is famously simple, because the potion system removes every other need:

  • 1 Overload (4) — drink immediately on entry; it handles every combat stat for the whole session.
  • ~10–12 Absorption (4) — sip whenever the pool drops low. This is the only thing you actively manage.
  • 2–3 Super Restore (4) — only if you need to recover from the Overload HP drain or top up prayer after a long session.
  • Your best DPS gear — because you take almost no HP damage, you can wear pure offensive gear with zero defensive concern.

Because Absorption soaks the hits, you can wear maximum offensive gear with zero defensive concern. This is why Dharok's Greataxe (which scales damage with missing HP) is a fan favorite: the Overload's HP drain and any leaked damage push you to low HP, cranking Dharok's bonus while Absorption keeps you alive. The synergy is so strong that "Dharok NMZ" is its own sub-meta, covered in detail in the gear section below.

⚠️ Don't Let the Pool Hit Zero

If Absorption runs out, every hit lands on your Hitpoints. Keep the pool above zero at all times — a single missed sip during a special attack is how advanced players still get ejected. Set a rhythm: sip Absorption on a timer, not when you "feel" low. Most runners sip one dose every 30–45 seconds regardless of how safe things look.

🧪 Potion Rhythm (Advanced)

  1. Enter, drink Overload, let it drain your HP down to the protected low range.
  2. Top Absorption to full before engaging the first boss.
  3. Every ~30–45 seconds, sip one dose of Absorption proactively on a timer.
  4. Only use a Super Restore if prayer or HP is genuinely critical late in the session.
  5. Never drink a second Overload — the first already maxed your stats.

The economy of this is what makes NMZ so cheap to run. A single Overload (4) and a dozen Absorption (4)s cost a fraction of what an equivalent Slayer trip costs in food and teleports, and they produce comparable or better XP with none of the risk. That cost-per-XP ratio is the quiet reason NMZ remains a staple for every account type.

4. Earning & Spending Reward Points

Reward points are the real currency of NMZ. You earn them by dealing damage and landing kills inside the dream, and the rate scales with how much DPS you output and how many bosses you kill. Spend them wisely and they compound into permanent account upgrades that no other activity gives as cheaply.

What to buy (priority order)

  • Slayer Helmet Imbue (400 pts) — turns your Slayer helmet into a damage-boosting imbued version. Top priority for any Slayer-focused account, because it pays for itself in faster Slayer tasks.
  • Ring Imbues (Berserker, Archer, Seer, Warrior — 650 pts each) — free +stats that would otherwise cost hundreds of millions on the GE. These are the single best point-for-GP trade in the entire minigame.
  • Salve Amulet (e/i) Imbue — boosts Melee/Magic/Ranged against undead; huge for bosses like Vorkath and the Kourend undead.
  • Herb Box (15 pts) — converts points into steady herb income; great for funding the rest of your account once the high-value imbues are done.
  • Magic Roots / other supplies — only after the high-value imbues are complete.

The table below ranks the purchases by value so you never waste points on a low-value item while a high-value imbue sits unaffordable. The "GP equivalent" column is illustrative — it estimates what the same boost would cost on the Grand Exchange, which is the whole point of prioritizing imbues.

Berserker Ring (i) 650 ~200M+ 2 BiS melee ring, free
Archer Ring (i) 650 ~100M+ 3 BiS ranged ring, free
Seer Ring (i) 650 ~30M+ 4 BiS magic ring, free

Points per hour reality

Points scale with how much damage you output and how many bosses you kill. A points-optimized combo on a geared account can clear bosses fast enough to bank points quickly; an AFK combo earns fewer points but costs you almost no attention. Decide what your account needs right now: if you still need imbues, run points combos; if you only need XP, AFK. Many players alternate — a week of points combos to afford the next imbue, then a month of AFK grinding while they use it.

💡 Imbues Beat GP

A Berserker ring (i) is worth hundreds of millions on the GE but only 650 NMZ points. No GP you could make in the same time comes close. Prioritize every ring and helmet imbue before spending a single point on consumables like herb boxes. The herb box is a trap for impatient players who have not finished their imbues.

For Ironmen this math is even more lopsided, because the GE "equivalent" is meaningless — they cannot buy the ring at all. That is covered in the next section, but the principle is identical: imbues first, always.

5. Ironman Strategy: What to Buy First

For Ironmen, NMZ is not optional — it is the most reliable source of imbued jewelry and the Salve before you can raid. The constraint is that you must earn every point yourself, so efficiency matters even more than for a main. A main can buy a Berserker ring for GP; an Ironman must grind 650 points, and there is no shortcut.

Phase 1: First imbue

Stack points until you can buy the Slayer Helmet Imbue first. It directly increases your Slayer DPS, which earns you more GP and supplies elsewhere — a snowball that funds the rest of the grind. Because Ironmen cannot buy supplies, every DPS boost compounds harder for them than for a main.

Phase 2: Offensive rings

Next, target the Berserker ring (i) and Archer ring (i) (and Seer ring (i) for magic). These are free BiS-tier bonuses you cannot buy. One NMZ grind replaces hundreds of hours of bossing for the same ring stats, and for an Ironman "hundreds of hours of bossing" is not an exaggeration — the ring drop rates from bosses are brutal.

Phase 3: Salve and sustainability

Once rings are done, imbue the Salve amulet (e/i) for undead bosses, then start converting surplus points into herb boxes to self-supply your Herblore. An Ironman NMZ loop becomes a herb farm that pays for itself, turning what was a one-time grind into a renewable supply line. This is especially strong during long Hunter or Farming grinds where you bank points passively.

Ironman gear note

Ironmen often cannot afford a Toxic Trident or Blowpipe early, so their NMZ DPS is lower and points come slower. That is fine — run the easiest boss combo, use the Absorption/Overload method, and let the points accumulate. Consistency beats speed here. The gear checklist lists budget weapons that still clear low-HP bosses efficiently even on a restricted account.

🛡️ Ironman NMZ Priority List

1) Slayer Helmet Imbue → 2) Berserker (i) → 3) Archer (i) → 4) Seer (i) → 5) Salve (i) → 6) Herb boxes for sustain. Do not skip to herbs early — every point spent on herb boxes before your rings are done is a point stolen from a free BiS upgrade.

6. Dominic Onion, Hard Mode & Customisation

Two features separate casual NMZ from optimized NMZ: customisable mode and Hard Mode, both accessed through Dominic Onion at the NMZ entrance. A third, Practice mode, is the safe way to learn boss patterns before risking points.

Customisable mode

Buying customisable mode (with reward points) lets you pick exactly which bosses appear. This is what makes the "AFK combo" and "points combo" strategies possible — without it you are stuck with a random selection and cannot optimize anything. Buy this the moment you can; it pays for itself in the first few sessions by letting you run the exact bosses that match your goal. Most players consider customisable mode the true "unlock" of NMZ, more important than any single imbue.

Hard Mode

Hard Mode boosts boss stats and rewards but increases difficulty. It is worth it once your gear and potion rhythm are solid, because the extra points accelerate every other goal — a points combo in Hard Mode can roughly double your point rate. New NMZ players should avoid Hard Mode until they can AFK a normal session without the Absorption pool dipping to zero, because Hard Mode multiplies your point loss too if you get ejected. The risk is symmetric: more reward, more punishment.

Practice mode

Practice mode lets you fight bosses without rewards to learn their patterns. Use it to study any boss with a dangerous special attack before adding it to a real combo — knowledge here prevents wasted points and ejected runs later. It costs nothing but time, and a single avoided ejection pays for the practice many times over.

💡 Unlock Order

Customisable first (enables combos) → Normal AFK grinding → Hard Mode once comfortable → Practice mode anytime you meet a new boss. Never jump to Hard Mode blind, because the first time you meet a boss's special attack should not be mid-session with points on the line.

7. Gear Deep-Dive: Dharok's vs the Alternatives

Because Absorption soaks nearly all incoming damage, defence is worthless in NMZ and offensive power is everything. That single fact reshapes the entire gear conversation. Below is how the common setups compare when defense is removed from the equation.

Setup Defence Value Offence / DPS Why Use It Best For
Dharok's (full set + Greataxe) Irrelevant Scales with missing HP Overload drain + leaks push HP low, maxing Dharok's bonus Top XP/hr on a geared account
Blowpipe (ranged) Irrelevant Very high, fast Safe, consistent, no HP gimmick needed Accounts without Dharok's
Toxic Trident (magic) Irrelevant High, AoE on some bosses Strong magic DPS, easy to obtain Magic-oriented builds
Void + best weapon Irrelevant High (set bonus) Cheap accuracy/strength boost Budget max-DPS
Any defensive armour Wasted Low None — it only lowers your DPS Never, in NMZ

The headline takeaway: never wear defensive gear in NMZ. A full Guthan's or Bandos set protects Hitpoints you are not losing anyway, while a Dharok's or pure DPS setup kills bosses faster and therefore earns more XP and points per minute. The only exception is using gear for its offensive bonuses (e.g., Bandos for its strength bonus is fine; Bandos for defence is not the point).

Dharok's deserves a special note. Its Greataxe adds damage the lower your HP is, and NMZ is the one place in the game where you can safely sit at very low HP for an entire session, because Absorption absorbs the hits that would otherwise kill you. Combined with the Overload's intentional HP drain, a Dharok runner spends most of the session at near-minimum HP with maximum damage output. That is why "Dharok NMZ" produces the headline 90k+ XP/hr numbers — it is not a trick, it is the math working as intended.

🗡️ Dharok NMZ Setup (Summary)

Dharok's helm/body/legs/greataxe + best amulet (e.g., Berserker (i) or Amulet of torture) + best offensive ring + 1 Overload (4) + ~10–12 Absorption (4) + 2–3 Super Restore. Drink Overload, let HP drop, keep Absorption topped, and watch the Greataxe delete bosses.

If you do not own Dharok's, do not panic — a Blowpipe or Trident with Void or your best offensive gear still clears low-HP bosses efficiently and produces strong XP. The setup matters less than keeping the Absorption pool up and picking the right combo. 8. XP Rates & Efficiency Math

Understanding the XP curve helps you set realistic expectations and choose between AFK and speed sessions. The chart below shows typical combat XP/hr for the three main play styles on a geared account. These are illustrative ranges — your stats, weapon, and combo choice move the numbers, but the ordering is stable: speed combo beats points combo beats AFK for raw XP.

The efficiency lesson is about attention budget, not just XP. An AFK session at ~50k XP/hr that you run while doing something else is "free" XP, while a speed session at ~90k that consumes your full attention is only worth it when XP is your active priority. The smart play is to run AFK during passive time and speed sessions when you are already at the keyboard. Over a week, that混合 (mixed) approach often beats either pure strategy, because it keeps the NMZ tab open without sacrificing the rest of your game time.

Points tell a slightly different story: the points combo intentionally trades some XP for more points, because points are what unlock imbues. Early on, when you still need imbues, bias toward the points combo even at a small XP cost — the imbue you buy will return far more XP and GP everywhere else than the few thousand XP you "lost" in NMZ.

💡 The Mixed-Week Plan

Need an imbue? Run points combos until you can afford it. Imbues done? Switch to AFK during passive time and speed sessions when active. This keeps NMZ productive in every mode instead of forcing a single play style.

9. Common NMZ Mistakes That Waste Points

  • Wasting Overload doses. One Overload covers a full session. Drinking a second "just in case" burns points for nothing — the stats are already maxed.
  • Reactive Absorption sipping. Waiting until you feel low lets the pool hit zero and ejects you. Sip on a timer, proactively, every 30–45 seconds.
  • Buying herbs before imbues. Herb boxes are tempting but a ring imbue is worth 100x the GP. Prioritize imbues every time.
  • Random boss selection. Not buying customisable mode leaves points and XP on the table. Unlock it early — it is the foundation of every optimization here.
  • Defensive gear in NMZ. Absorption soaks the damage, so wearing defensive gear just lowers your DPS and slows points. Go full offense (Dharok's, Fang, Trident, Blowpipe).
  • Hard Mode too early. It multiplies point loss too if you get ejected. Earn the right to use it by first AFK-ing normal mode cleanly.
  • Wrong combo for the goal. Running an AFK combo while desperately needing points (or vice versa) wastes the session. Match the combo to what your account needs right now.
  • Forgetting Super Restores. Long sessions can drain prayer or HP enough that you eject. Carry 2–3 as insurance even if you rarely use them.

✅ Advanced NMZ Loadout (Summary)

Customisable mode + best offensive gear + 1 Overload (4) + ~10–12 Absorption (4) + 2–3 Super Restore (4) + chosen combo (AFK, points, or speed). That is the entire formula — everything else is execution.

10. Frequently Asked Questions

How many bosses should I pick in NMZ?

In customisable mode you can select up to 5. Five low-HP bosses is ideal for AFK; a mix that includes 1–2 high-HP bosses is better for points. Fewer than 5 simply means less action and slower progress, so fill all five slots unless you are deliberately building an ease combo on a brand-new account.

What is the best boss combo for AFK?

A set of weak, melee-only bosses such as King Roald, The Kendal, Tree Spirit, Traitorous Hazeleaf, and The Everlasting Wyrm (or Sand Snake). They die fast, hit weakly, and let you camp one Protect prayer while barely interacting. The key is that none of them have a dangerous special attack that demands your attention.

How do I get the most points per hour?

Maximize damage output (best weapon + Overload + full offensive gear) and include higher-HP bosses in your combo. Points reward damage and kills, so faster, harder kills = more points. Accept that this is less AFK than the easy combo, and bring slightly more Absorption to survive the heavier hits of tanky bosses.

Is NMZ good XP for Ironmen?

Extremely. NMZ cannot kill you (you get ejected, not dead), making it the safest combat XP in the game. For Ironmen who cannot risk Wilderness or difficult bosses, NMZ is a core training method and the path to free imbues — and the herb boxes it later provides self-fund Herblore.

Can I safe spot in NMZ?

No — NMZ bosses are summoned around you in an enclosed arena and use attacks you cannot block with terrain. Safe spotting does not apply; the Absorption/Overload system is your "safe spot" instead. For terrain-based safe spotting against real bosses outside NMZ, see our safe spots guide.

Why is Dharok's so good in NMZ?

Dharok's Greataxe adds damage the lower your HP is, and NMZ is the only place you can safely sit at very low HP for an entire session because Absorption soaks the hits. The Overload's HP drain plus any leaked damage keep you near minimum HP, maxing Dharok's bonus — hence the top XP/hr numbers. On any other content, that low HP would get you killed.

Should I use Hard Mode as a beginner?

No. Hard Mode multiplies both rewards and losses. Until you can AFK a normal session without the Absorption pool dipping to zero, Hard Mode will just eject you more often and waste points. Unlock it only after the normal-mode rhythm is automatic.

How many Overloads do I need?

One Overload (4) covers a full normal session, because its boost lasts the whole dream. A second is only worth bringing for very long sessions where the first might expire. Drinking a second "for safety" is the classic waste — the stats are already maxed.

What should I spend my first 400 points on?

The Slayer Helmet Imbue. It directly boosts your Slayer DPS, which earns more GP and supplies elsewhere, snowballing into faster progress toward every other imbue. Only after your key imbues are done should points go to herb boxes or other supplies.

Can NMZ make me GP directly?

Not directly — NMZ awards points, not GP. But those points buy herb boxes (which sell for GP or fund Herblore) and imbues (worth hundreds of millions on the GE). So NMZ "makes GP" indirectly, and for Ironmen the imbue value is effectively priceless since they cannot buy the rings.

Do I need customisable mode to use this guide?

Technically you can run random bosses without it, but every combo strategy here assumes customisable mode, because it lets you pick the exact bosses for your goal. Buy it as soon as you can — it is the foundation of efficient NMZ and pays for itself almost immediately.

🏁 Final Tips

  • Buy customisable mode first — it unlocks every efficient combo.
  • One Overload (4) + ~10–12 Absorption (4) is the entire potion plan; never waste a second Overload.
  • Wear maximum offensive gear; Absorption makes defence pointless. Dharok's for top XP.
  • Spend points on imbues before herbs — imbues beat GP every time, often by 100x.
  • Ironmen: Slayer Helmet → Berserker (i) → Archer (i) → Seer (i) → Salve (i) → herbs.
  • Graduate to Hard Mode only after you can AFK normal mode cleanly.
  • Match your combo to your goal: AFK for passive XP, points for imbues, speed for raw XP/hr.
  • Sip Absorption on a timer, not reactively — keeping the pool above zero is what prevents ejection.
