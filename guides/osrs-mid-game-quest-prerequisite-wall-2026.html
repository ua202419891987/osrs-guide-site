1. 🧭 What Actually Stops You at Mid-Game

Every OSRS player hits a strange plateau around base 70s. Early game is a smooth ramp: do Waterfall Quest, rush a few early bosses, level a couple skills, and the next step is obvious. Mid-game is different. The obvious next quests — the ones that unlock the best gear, the best training methods, and the real PvM — are gated behind long chains you never consciously chose to start.

This is why so many accounts "stall" around total level 1,500–1,800. It is rarely a skill issue. It is a prerequisite chaining issue. The fix is not to grind harder; it is to see the chain. Our Quest Cape guide shows the destination, but this page shows the wall in between.

① Why "just do quests" fails at mid-game

In early game, quest requirements are tiny (10 Thieving, 20 Firemaking). At mid-game, they explode: a single "required" quest may itself require 3 other quests, each of which needs 60+ in a skill you ignored. The naive plan "I'll just do Mourning's End Part II tonight" becomes a 3-week detour because you skipped Roving Elves, Regicide, and the Temple of the Eye trail. Seeing the full chain turns a confusing wall into a checklist. Our Best Quests Per Skill lists the XP shortcuts that flatten this exact wall.

② The four notorious blockers

Four quests cause the vast majority of mid-game stalls. Each is famous for quietly demanding far more than its listed requirements suggest:

  • Mourning's End Part II (MEP2) — the gateway to Prifddinas and crystal gear, but its chain drags you through 70+ stats.
  • Desert Treasure II (DT2) — the modern high bar; requires beating four end-game bosses before the quest even starts.
  • Monkey Madness II (MM2) — grants the best non-degradable ranged weapon tier and a huge XP drop, but needs a 70s combat base.
  • Dragon Slayer II (DS2) — the best spec weapon in the game is behind a 200+ quest-point web and multiple boss fights.

③ How the prerequisite chain compounds

💡 The compounding trap

Skip one mid-tier quest and you may be fine for a week — then three later quests all list it as required. The cost of the skip isn't paid once; it's paid every time a future unlock depends on it. That is why a planned order (see Optimal Quest Order) beats reacting to each wall as it appears.

2. 🟣 Mourning's End Part II: The +70 Skill Wall

MEP2 is the single biggest "wait, why do I need 70 Agility?" moment in the game. On paper its direct requirements are modest. In practice, its prerequisite chain — Roving Elves → Regicide → Underground Pass → ... — funnels you toward a cluster of 70+ stats you probably don't have yet.

① The prerequisites that pile up

MEP2 directly requires Mourning's End Part I, which requires Roving Elves, which requires Regicide, which requires Underground Pass, which requires Biohazard → Plague City. Layered on top are the hard stats: the Mourning's End series and its neighbors push you toward 70+ Agility, 70+ Ranged, 70+ Thieving, and high Slayer/Combat to comfortably do the content and survive the areas it opens.

② The realistic grind plan

Do not try to "grind 70 Agility" in a vacuum — that is how players burn out. Instead, batch it: train Agility at the best available course while you knock out the quest chain in parallel, use quests-that-give-that-skill XP to shortcut the early levels, and slot money-making runs (see Mid-Game Money Roadmap) between grinds so your bond or membership stays funded.

③ Why it gates so much end-game content

MEP2 = the Prifddinas key

Completing MEP2 (and its sister quests) is what lets you enter Prifddinas, train Crystal Math Labs, use the Trahaearn mine, and eventually pursue the Elven quest line that culminates in Song of the Elves. Skipping it doesn't just block one quest — it blocks an entire late-game city and its best-in-slot skilling methods.

3. 🏜️ Desert Treasure II: The New High-Bar Gate

If MEP2 is the old-school wall, DT2 is the modern one. Released as a major end-game expansion, it raised the bar so high that many established mid-game accounts suddenly found themselves "too weak to even start the quest."

① The combat & quest requirements

DT2 requires a substantial quest trail (Desert Treasure, The Temple at Senntisten, Secrets of the North, A Night at the Theatre, and more) plus meaningful combat stats. But the headline gate is this: you must defeat four bosses — the Duke Sucellus, the Leviathan, Vardorvis, and the Whisperer — before the quest concludes. These are not safespot-able tutorials; they are real end-game PvM checks.

② The bosses you must beat

Each of the four DT2 bosses teaches a different lesson: the Duke rewards prayer switching and movement, the Leviathan punishes greedy positioning, Vardorvis demands precise tick discipline, and the Whisperer is a mechanically dense puzzle-fight. If you have not done serious boss-mechanic practice, these will humble you. Budget real practice time, not just gear.

③ Why players get stuck here

⚠️ The DT2 trap

Players reach the quest, read "defeat the four bosses," and assume their stats are the only problem. Usually the real blocker is not having a boss-ready setup and the mechanical skill to use it. Fix the skill first (gear switches, prayer discipline, learning rotations), then the bosses become a week-long project instead of a month-long wall.

4. 🐒 Monkey Madness II & Dragon Slayer II: The Mid-Game Confusion

These two are constantly mixed up by returning and mid-game players. Both are "MM/DS" sounding, both need 70s combat, and both reward iconic gear — but they unlock completely different things and should be prioritized differently.

① MM2 requirements and the prayer reward

Monkey Madness II requires Monkey Madness I, a 70+ combat base, and a handful of mid quests. Its payoff is enormous: the best-in-slot non-degradable ranged weapon tiers (through the monkey bow string / ranged upgrades) and a massive XP reward that can leapfrog your account. For most accounts, MM2 is the higher-priority "first big gate" because the gear it unlocks is used everywhere.

② DS2's 200+ quest-point web

Dragon Slayer II is the tougher logistically. It demands 200+ quest points, which means you must have completed the overwhelming majority of the quest list. That single number forces you to do dozens of quests you might have skipped — which is exactly why players who "only did combat quests" get stranded. DS2's reward, the best spec weapon in the game, is worth it, but the entry cost is a near-complete quest log.

③ Which to do first

Priority order for most accounts

Do MM2 first — lower logistical overhead, immediate gear payoff, and it feeds into Slayer and bossing. Then work the long quest-point grind toward DS2 in parallel with your MEP2/SOTE chain. By the time your quest points cross 200, DS2 becomes a victory lap rather than a wall. See our DS2 finish guide and MM2 completion guide for step-by-steps.

5. 🗺️ Mapping Your Prerequisite Chain (by Level)

The single most useful thing you can do is stop thinking "what quest next" and start thinking "what unlocks at my current level, and what does that unlock next." Below is a skeleton of the mid-game chain. The diagram shows how the four blockers sit at the end of converging prerequisite lines.

① The skeleton route (low → mid)

Waterfall → Tree Gnome Village / Grand Tree → Fairy Tale I/II → Lost City → the Western Provinces chain → Roving Elves / Regicide cluster → MEP2. Each step feeds the next; skipping one breaks the line.

② The 70s cluster

Most mid-game walls resolve to "get these stats to 70": Agility, Ranged, Thieving, Slayer, Prayer, and a combat style or two. Batch them. Train Agility passively, Thieving via Ardougne, Ranged via Slayer — never one at a time in isolation.

③ The post-70 cluster (DT2, SOTE-adjacent, DS2)

Once the 70s land, the chain forks into the elite gates: Song of the Elves (needs MEP2 + 70s + Prif access), DT2 (needs the boss trail + four boss kills), and DS2 (needs 200+ QP). These are your "late mid-game to early end-game" milestones.

6. 🔨 Breaking the Wall Without Burning Out

The wall feels infinite only because you are looking at it all at once. Break it into systems and it becomes a few weeks of focused, varied play.

① Batch your skill grinds

Never train one 70 in isolation. Stack Agility (passive, while watching), Thieving (Ardougne Knights / Pyramid Plunder), and Ranged (Slayer) so progress compounds. A 70 in three skills achieved "incidentally" beats a miserable 70-Agility grind that makes you quit.

② Interleave quests with money making

Membership and bonds cost real money. Keep a mid-game money routine running so the account funds itself while you quest. Two hours of bossing or flipping pays for a week of the grind you need.

③ Use achievement diaries as milestones

Turn the wall into a progress bar

Each diary tier (Easy → Medium → Hard → Elite) maps neatly onto mid-game progression and rewards useful teleports and unlocks. Chasing Achievement Diaries gives you natural checkpoints so the "wall" becomes a series of small wins instead of one looming block.

7. ❓ Mid-Game Wall FAQs

Why does Mourning's End Part II need 70+ stats when it doesn't say so?

MEP2's direct stats are modest, but its prerequisite chain (Roving Elves → Regicide → Underground Pass) plus the areas it opens assume 70+ Agility, Ranged, Thieving and combat. Players hit the wall because they skipped the chain, not because MEP2 is secretly 70s — the 70s live in the prerequisites.

Can I skip Desert Treasure II and still be "end-game"?

You can do a lot without DT2, but you'll miss Ancient Magicks upgrades, the quest-point/reward trail, and the bossing experience the four bosses teach. For true end-game PvM, DT2 is effectively required — treat the boss prep as the real gate.

Monkey Madness II or Dragon Slayer II first?

MM2 first for almost every account: lower overhead, immediate best-ranged-tier payoff, and it feeds Slayer/bossing. Work toward DS2 in parallel by grinding quest points; by the time you hit 200+ QP, DS2 is easy.

How many quest points do I need for DS2, and is 200+ realistic?

DS2 requires 200 quest points — essentially a near-complete quest log. It's very realistic if you follow a planned order; it only feels impossible if you've skipped dozens of small quests. Our Quest Cape guide tracks the full list.

I'm burned out grinding 70 Agility — what's the shortcut?

Don't grind it alone. Use quest XP (several quests grant Agility XP), train it passively while doing other content, and consider the best available course for your level. Batching it with Thieving/Ranged removes the "miserable solo grind" feeling entirely.

Is there a single optimal order that avoids all these walls?

Yes — a planned route that does prerequisites before you need them. Our Optimal Quest Order and Best Quests Per Skill are built exactly to prevent the mid-game stall.

Do I need Prifddinas access for DT2?

Not directly, but the broader late-game ecosystem (Song of the Elves, Elven content) overlaps heavily with the same 70s-and-questing investment. Getting MEP2 done early makes every later gate easier, DT2 included.
