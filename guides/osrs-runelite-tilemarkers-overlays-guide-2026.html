1. What Are Tile Markers & Overlays?

If you have ever died to a Vorkath fire bomb because you stood one tile too far left, or lost a prayer swap because you fumbled the right tile, you already understand why RuneLite’s visual aids matter. Old School RuneScape is a grid game. Every step you take, every projectile that lands, and every safe spot you occupy is measured in tiles. The default client shows you almost none of this information clearly, especially during the chaos of a boss fight. RuneLite fixes that with two families of tools: tile markers and overlays.

A tile marker is a persistent colored square painted onto a specific floor tile. In RuneLite the plugin that does this is called Ground Markers, but the community almost always refers to its output as tile markers. You right-click a tile, choose to mark it, pick a color, and from that point forward that square glows until you remove it. Because the marker is tied to the tile’s coordinates rather than your character, it stays put whether you are standing on it or standing across the room. This makes tile markers perfect for teaching your feet where to be: the exact tile you must stand on to safe-spot a monster, the tiles that become dangerous during a special attack, or the single safe tile during a Vorkath acid phase.

An overlay, by contrast, is a live layer of information drawn on top of the game window. Overlays are dynamic — they change from frame to frame as the fight changes. When we talk about prayer overlays, we mean a plugin that highlights which protection prayer you should currently have active, or shows your remaining prayer points as a clean bar instead of the tiny default orb. A boss overlay is a plugin built specifically for one monster that highlights its next attack, its rotation, or the safe tiles you must reach. A timer overlay counts down cooldowns, special attacks, or venom ticks so you know precisely when to eat, when to sip a potion, or when the next big hit lands.

Used together, tile markers and overlays cover both the where and the when of PvM. Markers answer “where do I stand” and “which tiles are deadly.” Overlays answer “what do I do right now” and “how long until the next threat.” For a beginner, learning to set up even a handful of these correctly is the single fastest way to stop dying and start getting kills. You do not need to memorize a boss’s entire rotation from a wiki table. You let RuneLite draw the answers on your screen.

🎯 The Beginner Mindset

  • Mark the danger, not just the safe spot. Painting red on the tiles you must avoid is as useful as painting green on the tiles you must use.
  • Start with one boss. Do not install every overlay at once. Learn the Ground Markers plugin on a single easy boss first.
  • Overlays should reduce thinking. If an overlay makes the fight more confusing, turn it off. Good overlays make the correct action obvious.
💡 Free and Allowed

RuneLite is a free, open-source, community-made client that is fully permitted under Jagex’s third-party client rules. Tile markers and overlays are not bots — they only change what you see, never what your account does. They are safe to use on both regular and Ironman accounts.

2. Creating Tile Markers (Colors & Persistence)

The Ground Markers plugin is the foundation of everything else in this guide, so we will set it up carefully. Open the RuneLite settings panel (the wrench icon, top-right), find the Ground Markers plugin, and make sure it is enabled. You do not strictly need to change any of its settings to start, but two options are worth understanding early.

To create your first marker, stand anywhere in the game world and right-click the ground (not an NPC, not an item — the empty floor tile). In the menu you will see options added by the plugin: Mark, and if you already have markers, Mark Tile variants. Choose Mark and a small colored square appears on that tile. By default the color is a faint blue, which is hard to see during a fight, so the first thing most players do is right-click the tile again and pick Change Color to something bold.

The plugin actually offers several marker styles, and choosing the right one prevents clutter. A filled marker paints the whole tile a translucent color, which is great for “stand here” tiles but can hide ground items. A centered marker draws a small dot in the middle, useful when you want to mark a spot without covering loot. A outline marker draws only the border, which is perfect for danger zones where you still need to see what is on the tile. A highlight marker brightens the tile under your mouse. For most boss work you will use filled for safe tiles and outline for danger tiles.

Color choice is a personal language, but a consistent system pays off. Most players adopt: green for safe / where to stand, red for danger / do not stand, yellow for prayer-swap tiles, and blue for waypoints or loot tiles. Because markers persist across logins and are saved to your RuneLite profile, you only have to set them once. This permanence is exactly why they are so powerful: the lesson you learn on your tenth Vorkath kill is still painted on the floor for your hundredth.

🎯 Step-by-Step: Your First Marker

  • Step 1: Enable Ground Markers in the RuneLite plugin panel.
  • Step 2: Right-click a floor tile and choose Mark.
  • Step 3: Right-click the tile again, pick Change Color, and choose a bold green.
  • Step 4: Right-click once more to switch the style to Filled (or Outline) depending on need.
  • Step 5: To remove it later, right-click and choose Unmark.
💡 Keep Markers Readable

Avoid marking every tile in a room. Too many markers become visual noise and defeat the purpose. As a rule, mark at most three to five tiles per fight: the safe tile(s), the danger tile(s), and your prayer-swap tile. Less is more when the boss is breathing fire at you.

Marker StyleBest ForRisk
FilledSafe tiles, “stand here” spotsCan hide dropped loot
OutlineDanger zones, AoE tilesLess visible at a glance
CenteredWaypoints, subtle cuesEasy to miss in chaos
HighlightHover inspection, building setupsNot persistent as a cue

Persistence deserves a deeper note because beginners often panic when markers seem to vanish. Markers are stored locally in your RuneLite configuration and are tied to the world location, not your character’s position. If you log into a different world, the same tile in the same cave is still marked, because coordinates are global. If you switch computers, your markers travel only if you copy your RuneLite settings folder or use the profile sync feature. We cover backing up and sharing in section six. The key takeaway for now: a marker you place today will greet you tomorrow, so invest the time to place it well.

3. Marking Boss Mechanics (Vorkath & Zulrah Tiles)

This is where tile markers stop being a tidy party trick and start saving your life. Bosses in OSRS telegraph their attacks with tiles: the spot a projectile will land, the line a breath will travel, the pool that will spawn. If you paint those tiles in advance, you are essentially writing the boss’s script onto the floor where your eyes already are. We will use two classic beginner bosses as worked examples — Vorkath and Zulrah — because their mechanics are tile-based and forgiving enough to learn on.

3.1 Vorkath Tile Logic

Vorkath is a single-tile boss fought from a fixed position in the north-east corner of his lair. The fight repeats a predictable rotation, and several of its attacks are solved by standing on (or avoiding) specific tiles. The most famous is the acid phase. Roughly every four to five attacks, Vorkath spits acid that covers most of the arena, leaving exactly one safe tile near the center. If you pre-mark that safe tile green, you remove the panic of “where do I run” — your feet already know. Many players also mark the two tiles adjacent to their standard prayer position so they can step off and back on during the acid without drifting into a death tile.

The second Vorkath attack worth marking is the fire bomb (the exploding ball you must walk away from). When Vorkath launches it, a red target tile appears and expands. Players commonly outline the tiles around their standing spot in red during learning, so the expanding danger reads instantly. The third is the zombie dragon spawn: you can mark the tile where you should stand to safespot the spawned undead so it never attacks you while you finish Vorkath. None of these markers fight for you, but each one deletes a decision from a fight that punishes hesitation.

3.2 Zulrah Tile Logic

Zulrah is the opposite teaching style: instead of one fixed spot, you rotate around a square arena through several phases, and each phase has a correct safe side. The community has standardized a set of “prayer tiles” and “safe tiles” that players mark once and reuse forever. Because Zulrah always begins the same way, you can mark your opening tile, your first prayer-swap tile, and the safe tile for the opening ranged tacos attack. The Zulrah plugin (a boss overlay, covered in section four) will even draw these for you, but learning to place your own markers first makes the overlay make sense — you understand why the green tile is green.

For Zulrah, the practical beginner setup is: mark the tile you stand on during the magic phase in green, mark the tile you run to for the jad phase (where you must be off to the side) in yellow, and outline the tiles that become unsafe during the serpent’s dive attack in red. Because Zulrah’s arena is a perfect grid, these coordinates never change, so your markers are valid for every kill you will ever do there.

🎯 Building a Vorkath Marker Set

  • Green filled: the single central safe tile for the acid phase.
  • Yellow filled: your standard prayer tile (where you camp Protect from Magic).
  • Red outline: tiles immediately around you during the fire bomb, as a “do not be here” reminder while learning.
  • Blue filled: the safespot tile for the spawned zombie dragon.
💡 Markers Teach, Overlays Confirm

For Zulrah especially, set your own markers first, then enable the boss overlay. When the overlay’s green matches the green you painted, you know you understood the phase. If they disagree, the overlay is right — adjust your marker and you just learned something for free.

4. Setting Up Prayer & Timer Overlays

Tile markers tell you where; overlays tell you when and what. The two overlays that matter most for a beginner are the prayer overlay and the timer overlay, with boss-specific overlays (section three context) rounding out the set. Let us configure each.

4.1 Prayer Overlay

RuneLite’s Prayer plugin (separate from the in-game prayer book) adds a clean overlay that shows your current prayer points as a number and a draining bar, often placed right next to the prayer orb so you never look away from the action. More importantly for PvM, several boss plugins include a prayer helper that highlights the protection prayer you should currently have active. For example, during a Vorkath magic attack the overlay tints Protect from Magic, and during his melee phase it hints Protect from Melee. This turns prayer swapping from memory work into a reaction: you see the highlight, you press the key.

To enable it, open the Prayer plugin settings and tick Show Prayer Bar and Strengthen Weakness if available. Then, for each boss you fight, enable that boss’s plugin and look for its Prayer or Display overlay option. Beginners should also turn on Prayer Renewal / Potion reminders if the plugin offers them, so you get a nudge before your prayer hits zero rather than after.

4.2 Timer Overlay

The Timer plugin tracks in-game countdowns and displays them as neat bars or text. Out of the box it can show your anti-fire potion timer, your overhead prayer drain, venomblood ticks, and — crucially — boss attack timers when paired with boss plugins. For Vorkath, the boss plugin draws a countdown to the next special attack so you are never surprised by the acid or fire bomb. For Zulrah, it can count the rotation so you pre-position a half-second early.

Configure the Timer plugin by choosing which timers to display and where on screen they sit. A good beginner layout puts the most urgent timer (the boss special) at the top-center, your prayer bar bottom-center, and passive timers (potions) in a corner. Avoid enabling every timer at once; a screen full of countdowns is just another kind of clutter.

4.3 Boss Overlays

Most popular bosses have a dedicated RuneLite plugin — search the plugin hub for the boss name. These overlays are the “boss” layer mentioned in the title: they highlight the next attack, draw safe tiles dynamically, and sometimes even auto-mark the tiles we discussed in section three. For a beginner, the recommended path is: enable the boss plugin’s overlay after you have practiced with your own markers, so the overlay confirms your understanding rather than replacing it.

🎯 Overlay Layout That Works

  • Top-center: boss special-attack timer (most urgent).
  • Bottom-center: prayer points bar + active prayer highlight.
  • Corner: potion and buff timers (low urgency).
  • Off by default: XP drops, loot beams, and chat spam overlays during learning.
💡 Pair With Prayer Training

Overlays only help if you have the prayer points to actually swap. If your prayer pool drains too fast, step back and build it up first — our Prayer Training Beginner Guide 2026 shows the cheapest ways to a healthy prayer level before you tackle boss overlays.

5. Safe-Spot & Route Markers

Not every PvM moment is a boss. A huge amount of early-game survival — slayer tasks, quest bosses, wilderness escapes — comes down to knowing your safe spot: a tile or arrangement of tiles where a monster literally cannot reach or hit you. Tile markers are the perfect memory aid for these, because a safe spot is a fixed location you will revisit many times.

5.1 Safe-Spot Markers

Classic safe spots rely on obstacles: a tree, a rock, a wall corner that blocks the monster’s path so it stands and attacks from range (or cannot attack at all) while you shoot it from a specific tile. The trick is that the safe tile is often unintuitive — one tile left and the monster hits you, one tile right and it walks around. By marking that exact tile green the first time you find it, you never have to re-learn it. Slayer players build entire libraries of safe-spot markers across the world: one for cave crawlers, one for a specific demon in a dungeon, one for a quest boss in a tower.

When marking a safe spot, use a filled green tile on the spot you stand, and optionally a red outline on the adjacent tile that breaks the safe spot, so you can see the boundary at a glance. Some players also mark the monster’s trapped tile in blue to confirm the creature is where it should be.

5.2 Route Markers

Route markers are a lesser-known but powerful use: painting a path through a dangerous area so you take the fastest, safest line every time. Think of running past aggressive monsters in a dungeon to reach a boss, or crossing a multicombat wilderness strip. Mark each turn tile in yellow so your route reads like a dotted line on the floor. This is especially helpful in areas where a wrong turn means death or a long detour.

A practical example: the path from a bank to a boss lair often passes several aggressive rats or dogs. Mark the entry tile, each corner, and the door tile. On your twentieth trip the yellow breadcrumb trail keeps you honest and fast, and you stop getting picked off by something you forgot was there.

🎯 Safe-Spot Marker Checklist

  • Green filled: the exact tile you stand on.
  • Red outline: the adjacent tile that breaks the safe spot.
  • Blue filled (optional): the monster’s trapped tile, to confirm positioning.
  • Test it: let the monster attack three times; if you take zero damage, the spot is real.
💡 Safe Spots Save More Than HP

6. Sharing & Backing Up Marker Configs

One of the quiet superpowers of RuneLite markers is that they are just data. Your markers, overlays, and plugin settings live in configuration files on your computer, and those files can be copied, shared, and restored. This means you never have to rebuild your carefully painted Vorkath arena if you reinstall RuneLite, switch laptops, or want to give a friend your exact setup.

6.1 Where Markers Live

RuneLite stores settings in its local configuration folder. The exact path differs by operating system, but the principle is the same: there is a settings file that contains your ground markers as a list of coordinates and colors. If you copy that file (or the whole RuneLite settings folder) to a backup drive or cloud storage, you have a safety net. Many players drop their settings folder into a dated zip once a month. It takes ten seconds and has saved countless hours when a client update or a wiped profile threatened to erase months of marker work.

6.2 Sharing With Friends

To share a marker set, you export or copy the relevant configuration and send it to a friend, who places it in the same location on their machine and restarts RuneLite. Because coordinates are global, a Vorkath safe tile you marked on your computer is the exact same safe tile on theirs — no translation needed. This is how boss marker “packs” spread through clans: one person maps a fight, exports the config, and ten clanmates paste it in and instantly fight with expert floor paint.

Some community tools and Discord servers distribute curated marker profiles for popular bosses and raid rooms. Treat shared configs like any download: grab them from trusted clanmates or well-known community sources, and review what you are pasting. A marker file cannot harm your account — it only contains tile colors and positions — but it is good habit to know your files.

6.3 Profile Sync

RuneLite also supports logging into a profile that syncs settings across machines. If you enable this, your markers follow you automatically to any computer where you sign in. For players who grind both at home and on a laptop, this removes the backup step entirely. Just remember that synced profiles are only as safe as your RuneLite login, so use a strong password and, where available, keep your Jagex account secure with two-factor authentication.

🎯 Backup Routine (5 Minutes, Saves Hours)

  • Weekly: copy your RuneLite settings folder to a cloud drive.
  • Before big updates: make a dated zip of your markers.
  • After building a new boss set: export that config immediately while it is fresh.
  • When sharing: send only the marker config, never your whole account file.

7. Tile Markers & Overlays FAQs

Are tile markers and overlays against the Jagex rules?

No. RuneLite is an approved third-party client, and tile markers and overlays only change what you see on screen. They do not automate input, read hidden data, or give you information a normal player could not gather by watching the game. They are safe on regular, Ironman, and Hardcore accounts alike.

Why did my markers disappear after an update?

Markers are stored in your local RuneLite settings. If you switched computers, cleared the settings folder, or logged into a different profile, the markers are not there. Restore them from a backup or re-import the shared config. This is exactly why section six recommends a regular backup habit.

Can I use too many overlays at once?

Yes, and beginners often do. Stacking every boss, prayer, timer, and XP overlay turns your screen into clutter that slows reactions instead of helping. Start with prayer bar, one boss overlay, and one timer. Add more only when you are comfortable and a specific fight clearly needs it.

Do markers work in the Wilderness?

They do. Any tile in the world can be marked, including Wilderness safe spots and escape routes. Just remember that a marker does not protect you from player killers — it only shows you where to stand. Combine markers with real awareness when venturing into PvP areas.

My marker color is hard to see during a fight. What should I do?

Switch to a high-contrast color and a bolder style. Red outline on danger, bright green fill on safe, and yellow for prayer tiles read well even with boss animations flying. Also check the Ground Markers plugin setting for marker opacity and increase it if your tiles look faint.

Can I share my exact boss marker layout with a friend?

Yes. Export or copy your RuneLite settings (or just the ground marker portion) and send the file. Because tile coordinates are global, your friend pastes it in, restarts RuneLite, and sees the same floor paint at the same spots. It is the fastest way to get a whole clan using identical safe tiles.

🎯 What To Do After Setting Up Tile Markers & Overlays

You now have the visual toolkit that separates confident PvMers from repeat deaths. Put it to work by reinforcing the two foundations markers and overlays rely on: a deep enough prayer pool to actually swap, and boss knowledge so the overlays confirm what you already expect.
