1. How to Install RuneLite Plugins Safely

Before we dive into specific plugins, let us get one important fact out of the way: RuneLite is fully approved by Jagex, the company that makes Old School RuneScape. RuneLite is an open-source, community-built client that connects to the official game servers. Millions of players use it every single day, and it is widely considered the standard way to play the game on a desktop computer. Using RuneLite itself will never get your account banned. What matters is how you install and configure it.

The single most important safety rule is this: only ever install plugins from inside the RuneLite client itself. RuneLite has a built-in "Plugin Hub" that downloads code directly from the official plugin repository. There is no reason to visit a random website, download a zip file, or paste a link into a chat box. If someone tells you to download a plugin from anywhere other than the in-game hub, that is a red flag. In this guide we will only ever recommend plugins that appear in the official hub.

Step-by-step: enabling your first plugin

Installing a plugin takes less than a minute. Here is the exact process you will repeat for every plugin we recommend later:

🎯 How to enable any plugin in RuneLite

  • Open the RuneLite client and log in to your account as normal.
  • Look at the top-right corner of the client for the wrench icon — this opens the Configuration panel.
  • Click the "Plugin Hub" button at the bottom of the configuration panel.
  • Type the plugin name into the search box (for example, "GPU").
  • Click the plugin, then click "Install". RuneLite downloads and activates it automatically.
  • Close the Plugin Hub. The plugin now appears in your configuration list where you can toggle its settings.
💡 Keep RuneLite updated

RuneLite updates itself automatically every time you launch it while online. If a plugin suddenly stops working after a game update, simply restart the client — the developers push fixes within hours. Never download a "manual patch" from a forum, as those are almost always scams.

Two accounts, one client

If you play both a normal account and an Ironman, or if you share a computer, do not worry. RuneLite keeps separate settings profiles and stores them locally on your machine. Each plugin's configuration is remembered the next time you log in. This means once you set up the plugins below, you rarely have to touch them again.

2. Combat & Utility Plugins: GPU and Ground Items

If you only install two plugins today, make them these two. The GPU plugin and the Ground Items plugin are the foundation of a modern RuneLite setup. They are the first recommendations in almost every "best RuneLite plugins" list for a reason: they make the game look better and stop you from losing valuable loot.

GPU — smoother graphics and higher frame rates

The GPU plugin moves the game's rendering from your computer's processor (CPU) to its graphics card (GPU). In plain English, this means the game runs smoother, the frame rate goes up, and the world looks noticeably nicer. Old School RuneScape normally uses a flat, 2D-ish style with limited lighting. The GPU plugin adds real 3D shading, smooth zoomed-in views, and a much higher frame rate on most computers.

What you get when GPU is enabled: better lighting and shadows, the ability to zoom in much closer to your character, a higher frame rate cap (up to your monitor's refresh rate), and options for anti-aliasing that make edges look clean instead of jagged. For many players this single plugin is the difference between the game feeling old and feeling modern.

💡 GPU running slow instead of fast?

Sometimes the GPU plugin can actually lower your frame rate if your graphics drivers are out of date or if you turned on too many heavy effects at once. If the game stutters after enabling it, read our dedicated fix guide: RuneLite GPU FPS Drop Fix 2026. It walks through driver updates, the right settings, and the options that matter most for low-end laptops.

Ground Items — never miss loot again

The Ground Items plugin is, for many players, THE reason to use RuneLite. By default, Old School RuneScape shows dropped items as tiny, hard-to-read sprites on the floor with no price information. When you kill a monster or open a drop, you have to hover each item to see what it is, and there is no way to tell at a glance which pile is worth picking up.

Ground Items fixes this completely. It overlays the current Grand Exchange price of every item on the ground, and it color-codes them based on value tiers you choose. For example, you can set it so that anything worth over 100,000 coins shows in bright green, items worth 10,000 to 100,000 show in yellow, and junk under 1,000 shows in a faint gray you can ignore. You can also make valuable items highlighted and outlined so they pop out even in a busy fight.

🎯 Recommended Ground Items setup for beginners

  • Set "Highlight items above value" to a number that matters to you (start with 25,000).
  • Turn on "Color based on price" so high-value drops stand out.
  • Enable "Show ground item text" so names and prices appear directly on screen.
  • Use the "Hide under value" option to hide worthless bones and ashes completely.
  • Right-click any item on the ground and choose "Customize" to always highlight a specific drop you care about.

Why does this matter so much? Because in Old School RuneScape, the floor is where your money lives. A single rare drop can be worth millions, and if you walk away without noticing it, that is hours of progress gone. Ground Items turns the messy floor into a clear, priced shopping list. It is especially powerful when you are making money as a beginner, because it tells you instantly which kills are worth your time.

Together, GPU and Ground Items form the backbone of a good setup. The first makes the game feel good to look at; the second makes sure you never leave gold on the floor. With those running, every other plugin we cover from here is a bonus that saves time and reduces mistakes.

3. Quality-of-Life Plugins: Menu Entry Swapper and Bank Tags

The next pair of plugins does not change how the game looks — it changes how many clicks everything takes. Old School RuneScape was designed in 2004, and some of its menus are slow. Menu Entry Swapper and Bank Tags are the two quality-of-life plugins that almost every experienced player considers mandatory.

Menu Entry Swapper — put the right click on top

In Old School RuneScape, when you right-click an object, the game shows a menu of options. The top option is the one you get if you just left-click. The problem is that the default top option is often not the one you want. For example, when you right-click a bank booth, the top option might be "Bank" (good), but when you right-click an NPC you want to trade, the top option might be "Attack" (very bad if you accidentally left-click).

The Menu Entry Swapper plugin lets you permanently reorder these menus. You can tell RuneLite that whenever you right-click a bank booth, the top option should be "Bank"; whenever you right-click a friendly NPC, the top option should be "Talk-to" or "Trade"; and whenever you right-click your own player, the top option should be "Walk here" so you never accidentally do something embarrassing. You set it once, and it applies forever.

🎯 The swaps most beginners enable first

  • Bank booth → "Bank" on top, so a quick left-click opens your bank.
  • Equipment → "Wear" instead of "Remove" to avoid stripping naked by accident.
  • NPCs → "Trade" or "Talk-to" on top instead of "Attack".
  • Dear familiar / pet → "Pick-up" so you do not dismiss it.
  • Object → "Examine" swapped away so you stop misclicking.
💡 How to make a swap

Hold Shift and right-click the object. You will see a special menu where you can drag one option to the top. Release Shift and the new order is saved. That is the entire process, and you can undo any swap the same way.

Bank Tags — organize a messy bank

As a new player your bank fills up fast. Within a week you have dozens of partial stacks, quest items you forgot about, and gear you are not sure you still need. The default bank is one long, unsorted grid. The Bank Tags plugin lets you create labeled tabs and tag items so they group themselves automatically.

With Bank Tags you can build tabs like "Food", "Herblore", "Gear", "Quest Items", and "Untradeables", then drag items into them. Even better, the plugin can auto-sort items into tabs based on tags you assign. For example, you can tag every combat potion with "potion" and tell the plugin to drop all "potion" items into your Herblore tab. When your bank gets full, you press one key and everything snaps back into place.

Combined with Menu Entry Swapper, these two plugins remove more daily friction than almost anything else in the hub.

4. PvM Plugins: Entity Hider, Prayer and Tile Markers

Once you start fighting tougher monsters — whether that is a Slayer task, a boss, or a Wilderness encounter — a different set of plugins becomes useful. These three help you see clearly, pray correctly, and position safely. They are the plugins that separate a clean kill from a death that costs you your gear.

Entity Hider — clear the clutter

During big fights, the screen can get crowded with other players, pets, familiar graphics, and projectiles. The Entity Hider plugin lets you hide exactly the things you do not need to see. You can hide other players, hide your own player, hide pets, hide familiar overlays, and even hide the second-floor of a building so you can see what is happening on the ground.

For a beginner, the most useful setting is hiding other players during solo content so you are not distracted, and hiding pet and familiar overlays during boss fights so you can read the boss's attacks. Many players also use it to hide the default player model and show only a marker, which keeps the screen clean during crowded events.

Prayer — never lose a prayer flick

Prayer is one of the most important defensive tools in the game, and getting it wrong can kill you. The Prayer plugin (built into RuneLite's core) adds several helpers: a visible prayer drain timer, a tick counter so you can time "flicking" (quickly toggling prayer on and off to save points), and highlights on the prayer book so you can see which prayers are active at a glance.

If you are new to using Protect from Magic, Protect from Missiles, or Protect from Melee against bosses, the prayer overlays are a game-changer. They show you exactly when your prayer points will run out and warn you before they do. This is directly relevant to our Prayer training beginner guide, where we explain how to build up the prayer level and points you need before tackling serious PvM.

🎯 Prayer plugin settings worth enabling

  • Turn on the prayer drain timer so you know when points hit zero.
  • Enable show prayer widget on screen during boss fights.
  • Use the tick counter only once you learn flicking — it is an advanced technique.
  • Set a low-prayer warning color so the bar flashes red before you run dry.

Tile Markers — stand in the right spot

Positioning matters in Old School RuneScape combat. Standing one tile to the left can mean the difference between taking a hit and dodging it. The Tile Markers plugin lets you paint colored squares on the ground to mark safe tiles, prayer-switch tiles, or spots where you should stand during a specific attack pattern.

For beginners, tile markers are most useful in two places: marking the safe spots where a monster cannot reach you (great while learning safe-spotting), and marking the tile you should stand on during a boss's special attack. You can color-code them — red for danger, green for safe, blue for "stand here" — and they stay exactly where you put them.

💡 Start small with tile markers

Do not mark the whole floor. Pick one fight, learn its safe tile, and mark just that. Too many markers become visual noise and defeat the purpose. As you learn more bosses, add markers one at a time.

These three PvM plugins — Entity Hider, Prayer, and Tile Markers — work together. Hide what you do not need to see, watch your prayer like a hawk, and stand where the markers tell you. That combination is how new players survive their first real bosses instead of dying with expensive gear on the ground.

5. Information Plugins: Item Stats, XP Tracker and Wiki

The final group of must-have plugins is all about information. Old School RuneScape hides a surprising amount of useful data behind menus and external websites. These three plugins bring that data to you inside the game, saving you from alt-tabbing to a wiki or a calculator a hundred times per session.

Item Stats — compare gear without leaving the game

The Item Stats plugin shows the attack, defense, and strength bonuses of any item just by hovering over it in your bank, inventory, or the Grand Exchange. More importantly, when you equip something, it shows a comparison with what you were wearing, so you can see at a glance whether the new piece is an upgrade. For a beginner trying to figure out which sword, armor, or amulet is better, this removes all the guesswork.

You can also turn on price tooltips so every item shows its Grand Exchange value when you hover. That single feature tells you, instantly, whether the thing you just picked up is pocket change or a small fortune. It pairs perfectly with Ground Items, because now both the floor and your bank speak in numbers.

XP Tracker — watch your progress in real time

The XP Tracker plugin is one of the most satisfying tools in RuneLite. It tracks your experience gains in any skill and shows you, live, how much XP you are earning per hour, how long until your next level, and how many actions you have done. It also keeps a session log so you can look back at what you accomplished in a playing session.

For motivation alone, XP Tracker is worth installing. Seeing "2 hours to level 50" turn into "1 hour to level 50" as you get more efficient is what keeps many players grinding. It also helps you measure which training method is actually fastest, which connects directly to earning more gold — efficient skilling is a core part of beginner money making.

🎯 XP Tracker settings beginners love

  • Show XP per hour and time to level on screen.
  • Enable the progress ring around your XP drop for a quick visual.
  • Turn on session tracking so you can review total gains later.
  • Use the action counter to see how many things you have killed or crafted.

Wiki — the entire OSRS wiki, in game

The Wiki plugin is perhaps the most powerful learning tool for a new player. It connects your client directly to the official Old School RuneScape Wiki. Hold Control and hover over almost anything — an item, an NPC, a monster, a quest, a skill — and a window pops up with the wiki's article, including drop tables, maps, requirements, and strategies. You never have to leave the game to look something up.

For example, hover over a strange herb you just picked and the Wiki plugin tells you what potion it makes and how much it is worth. Hover over a boss and it shows the kill strategy and loot. Hover over a quest and it shows the start point and requirements. For a beginner drowning in new information, having the wiki one key-press away is enormous.

With these information plugins active, you stop playing half-blind. You know what your gear does, how fast you are progressing, and you can answer almost any question without leaving the client. That is the point of a good RuneLite setup: not more flashy graphics, but fewer moments of confusion.

6. Plugins to Avoid and How to Spot Scams

Knowing what to install is only half the story. Knowing what not to install is what keeps your account alive. Old School RuneScape has strict rules against automation, and Jagex bans accounts that use bots or "macro" plugins. Some plugins in the wider ecosystem cross that line, and some "plugins" advertised outside RuneLite are outright malware. This section is the most important one in the whole guide.

What is safe vs. what gets you banned

RuneLite's official Plugin Hub only contains plugins that are allowed. The hub has a review process, and plugins that automate gameplay (clicking for you, playing the game without you) are not allowed there. If a plugin exists in the hub, it is safe to use from a rules perspective. The danger comes from two places: plugins you are told to install from outside the hub, and "clients" that pretend to be RuneLite but are not.

⚠️ Red flags that mean "do not install"
  • A website or Discord message telling you to download a ".jar" file and place it in your RuneLite folder.
  • Any plugin that claims to auto-play, auto-click, or bot for you — these break the rules and get bans.
  • A "RuneLite Pro", "RuneLite Premium", or "HD RuneLite" site that is not runelite.net.
  • Anyone asking for your password, even to "set up" a plugin for you.
  • Plugins shared as Google Drive or MediaFire links instead of the in-game hub.

Why paid plugin offers are scams

You will sometimes see people advertising "paid RuneLite plugins" with features the free hub does not have. In nearly every case these are either bots (which get you banned) or malware dressed up as a plugin (which steals your account). Remember: every plugin worth using is free and in the official hub. There is no "premium" version you need to pay for. If someone wants your money for a plugin, they are selling you risk, not value.

The phishing trap

The most common way beginners lose accounts is not a bad plugin — it is a fake login page. Scammers create sites that look like RuneLite or like the Old School RuneScape login screen, then trick you into typing your username and password. Always launch RuneLite from the official site or your installed shortcut, and never log in through a link someone sends you. Turn on two-factor authentication (2FA) through your Jagex account settings — it is the single best protection against a stolen password.

💡 The one-minute safety checklist

Before installing anything: (1) Is it in the in-game Plugin Hub? (2) Did it come from runelite.net or the client itself? (3) Does it play the game for you? If the answer to (1) and (2) is yes and (3) is no, you are safe. Anything else, walk away.

To be completely clear: every plugin recommended in this guide — GPU, Ground Items, Menu Entry Swapper, Bank Tags, Entity Hider, Prayer, Tile Markers, Item Stats, XP Tracker, and Wiki — is free and available in the official Plugin Hub. If you stick to those and the hub, you will never run into trouble. The scammers only win when players go looking outside the safe, built-in system.

7. RuneLite Plugins FAQs

Is RuneLite legal and will it get me banned?

RuneLite is fully approved by Jagex and using it will not get you banned. It is an open-source, community-made client that connects to the official game servers. What can get you banned is using bots or automation plugins, which are not part of the official Plugin Hub. Stick to the hub and you are completely safe.

Do I have to pay for any of these must-have plugins?

No. Every plugin we recommend in this guide is free and lives in RuneLite's official Plugin Hub. There is no paid version you need. Anyone selling a "premium" RuneLite plugin is either offering a banned bot or malware. The best plugins cost nothing but a minute of your time to install.

My game runs slower after turning on the GPU plugin. What went wrong?

This usually means your graphics drivers are outdated or you enabled too many heavy visual effects at once. Restart RuneLite, update your GPU drivers, and lower settings like anti-aliasing and shadows. For a full step-by-step fix, see our RuneLite GPU FPS Drop Fix 2026 guide.

Can I use these plugins on the mobile version of OSRS?

The official RuneLite client is desktop-only, so these specific plugins do not run on the Jagex mobile app. Mobile has its own smaller set of built-in settings. If you want the full plugin experience, you need to play on a computer with RuneLite. Many players use RuneLite on desktop and the mobile app when they are away from their PC.

Which plugin should a total beginner install first?

Start with Ground Items and Menu Entry Swapper. Ground Items stops you from missing valuable loot on the floor, and Menu Entry Swapper removes the most common misclicks that embarrass or hurt new players. After those two, add GPU for smoother graphics and Bank Tags to organize your bank. The rest can wait until you start doing real combat.

How do I know if a plugin is safe before installing it?

The simplest rule: if it is in the in-game Plugin Hub, it is safe. The hub only lists plugins that passed RuneLite's review and comply with Jagex rules. Never install a plugin from a forum link, a Discord file, or a "download this jar" instruction. Those outside sources are where scams and bans begin.
