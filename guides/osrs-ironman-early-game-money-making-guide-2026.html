1. The Ironman Money Mindset

Before you collect a single cowhide, you need to rewire how you think about gold. On a normal account, money is something you "buy" solutions with: short on cash? Flip a few items on the Grand Exchange, or buy a stack of something cheap and resell it at a markup. On an Ironman, that entire safety net is gone. You cannot trade with other players, you cannot buy from the Grand Exchange, and you cannot pick up items that another player has dropped. Every single gold piece in your coffers must be earned, gathered, killed for, or crafted. This single rule changes the economics of the entire game, and understanding it early will save you dozens of hours of frustration.

The first trap new Ironmen fall into is treating the early game like a normal account would. They rush to a high-level money method they read about, burn out in ten minutes because their stats are too low, and then wonder why the method "does not work." The truth is that Ironman progression is stage-locked: the best money method at combat level 3 is completely different from the best method at combat level 50, and both are dwarfed by what opens up at combat level 80. Your job is not to chase the biggest number on day one. Your job is to run the best method available to your current stats, bank the gold, and let your account grow into the next tier. Patience compounds on an Ironman faster than on any other account type.

There is a second, subtler mindset shift that separates successful Ironmen from quitting ones: on a Ironman, the value of a material is whatever it would cost you to replace it later, not whatever the Grand Exchange mid-price says today. A clean herb you pick up at level 3 might be worth 15 GP on the GE, but the moment you start training Herblore at level 40 and realize you have none, that same herb is worth an hour of your time. This is why experienced Ironmen hoard. They do not ask "can I sell this for a few coins right now?" They ask "will I need this in two weeks, and how annoying will it be to get more?" Learning to think in opportunity cost rather than spot price is the single most valuable financial habit you can build on this game mode.

🎯 The Three Rules Every Ironman Banker Lives By

  • Rule 1 — Sell on the GE, never buy. Ironmen are allowed to list items on the Grand Exchange to convert them into gold. Use it. That is your primary cash engine.
  • Rule 2 — Hoard until it hurts, then hoard a little more. Most "junk" drops become useful the instant you start a new skill. Bank first, vendor later.
  • Rule 3 — Time is your only currency. Because you cannot buy progress, every hour you spend is an investment. Spend it on methods that scale with the account, not dead-end grinds.

A common worry is whether Ironmen are allowed to use the Grand Exchange at all. The answer is yes, with one hard restriction: you may sell items to the GE to receive gold, but you may never buy from it. This is what makes "money making" on an Ironman both possible and meaningful. You gather raw materials and finished goods, list them, and watch the coins roll in — the same coins a normal player would have simply bought their way to. For a deeper look at how the mode restricts you and what it unlocks, read our Ironman Beginner Guide before you start grinding.

💡 Mindset Tip: Track "Effective GP," Not Just Coins

Because you cannot buy items, a huge chunk of your "wealth" is invisible. When you craft your own battlestaff instead of buying one, you just saved yourself 7,000 GP worth of work. Keep a mental (or notes) tally of the things you make instead of buy. New Ironmen who track effective GP feel far richer and far less stressed than those who only stare at the coin pouch.

Finally, accept that the early Ironman grind is slower but more permanent. A main account can buy a bond, flip a few million, and skip the boring parts. You cannot. But the bank you build at combat level 3 through 30 is the exact bank you will still be drawing from at combat level 100. The cowhides you tan become the ranged gear you craft. The feathers you collect become the fletching supplies you use for 99. The herbs you hoard become the potions that let you kill your first boss. Nothing is wasted on an Ironman — it is only delayed. Hold that thought, because it is the engine behind every method in the rest of this guide.

2. Best Early GP Methods by Account Stage

Not all money methods are created equal, and almost none of them are stage-agnostic. The tables below break the early Ironman journey into three combat brackets and list the methods that actually work inside each one. The GP per hour figures are realistic for an attentive Ironman who is selling on the GE and banking efficiently — they are not the inflated "world record" numbers you see on clickbait videos. Treat them as planning estimates, not promises.

2.1 Combat Level 1–30 (The F2P Starter Bracket)

At this stage you are almost certainly F2P, your stats are low, and your only real assets are your hands and a few thousand starting coins. The good news: F2P Ironman money is honestly some of the most reliable in the game because the methods are simple and the demand is permanent. Chickens drop feathers (used for fly fishing bait and fletching), cows drop cowhides (tanned into leather or sold raw), and the low-level "collect and sell" loop never stops being useful.

The moment you become a member, your money ceiling explodes. Bird house runs appear, better flint-lock gathering opens, and you can finally mine pure essence and craft higher-tier runes. This is also where combat training starts paying you back: monsters you kill for XP also drop valuable seeds, herbs, and alchables. Do not separate "training" from "money making" in this bracket — they are the same activity.

By now you should have a comfortable bank, decent combat, and access to most low-to-mid content. Money methods shift toward repeatable, scalable loops rather than one-off grinds. Your Slayer drops get better, your Runecrafting can sustain law and astral runes, and you can finally do the "real" Ironman money makers like herb runs and farm runs that feed both your bank and your GP. 💡 How to Read These Tables

GP per hour on an Ironman is volatile. A bad RNG streak on bird houses, or a crowded world at the cow pen, can cut your rate in half. The tables are routing guides, not guarantees. Pick the method whose requirements you already meet, and graduate upward as your stats allow. Chasing a 600k/hr method you are not geared for will earn you less than a 150k/hr method you can actually do.

Keep in mind that for you, "money making" is really "resource making," because every resource you gather is one you will never have to buy.

3. Cowhides, Flax, and Essence Running — Step by Step

These three methods are the backbone of the early Ironman economy. They are low-skill, always in demand, and they teach you the core loop of the mode: gather → process → sell on the GE → bank the gold. Master these and you will never be broke again. Below is a practical walkthrough of each, written for someone touching the method for the very first time.

3.1 Cowhides: Your First Real Income

Cows are the kindest monsters in the game. They have almost no defense, they cluster in safe fields, and they drop cowhides that sell reliably on the GE. The classic F2P spots are the Lumbridge cow field (just north of the windmill, west of the river) and the Falador cow field (north of Falador, past the gate). As an Ironman you should ideally tan your hides rather than sell them raw, because tanned leather is worth more and you will need leather later for Crafting.

🎯 Cowhide Loop (Step by Step)

  • Step 1: Kill cows and pick up every cowhide. Wear gear that lets you survive (even a bronze axe works; cows hit very weakly).
  • Step 2: Bank the hides. At the Lumbridge field, run south to the Lumbridge bank. At Falador, run south to the east Falador bank.
  • Step 3: Take hides to the Al Kharid tanner (east of the Al Kharid bank, across the bridge, costs 10 GP to enter the gate). Tan to soft leather for 1 GP each or hard leather for 3 GP each.
  • Step 4: Either craft the leather into leather/hardleather bodies (needs Crafting) for extra value, or sell the tanned leather directly on the GE.
  • Step 5: List on the GE and collect your gold. A full inventory of tanned soft leather sells for a tidy sum even at low levels.

A note on efficiency: tanned hard leather requires 28 Crafting to turn into hardleather bodies, which sell for more than soft leather bodies. If your Crafting is still low, just sell tanned soft leather — the tan cost is tiny and the GE price difference over raw hides is still profit. Either way, do not leave cowhides on the ground. They are free money.

3.2 Flax → Bowstrings: The Membership Workhorse

Once you are a member, flax spinning is arguably the most consistent early Ironman money method in the game. Bowstrings are consumed non-stop by fletchers training ranged, so the GE demand is effectively infinite. The catch: you need a spinning wheel and a bank close together, and you need 10 Crafting to spin flax into a bowstring.

🎯 Flax → Bowstring Loop (Step by Step)

  • Step 1: Teleport to Camelot (unlocked via the Camelot teleport spell or the Ardougne cloak after the quest). The flax field is just north-west of the castle.
  • Step 2: Pick flax until your inventory is full (28 flax). Picking is fast and there are usually plenty of nodes.
  • Step 3: Run south-east to the Seers' Village bank, where a spinning wheel sits right inside the bank building. Spin all 28 flax into bowstrings (15 Crafting XP each).
  • Step 4: Bank the bowstrings and repeat. One full run of 28 bowstrings is worth far more than 28 flax, and the loop takes only a minute or two.
  • Step 5: Sell bowstrings on the GE. They move quickly and the price is stable, making this a dependable daily income.

If you have access to Hosidius in Great Kourend, the Hosidius flax field behind the mess hall plus the nearby bank is another solid option, and it feeds into the Kourend favor grind. For an Ironman, bowstrings are doubly valuable: you will eventually use them yourself for fletching training, so any surplus you sell today is pure profit on top of the stockpile you keep.

3.3 Essence Running & Runecrafting: The Long-Game Cash Engine

Runecrafting has a reputation as the most tedious skill in OSRS, but for an Ironman it is also one of the best money printers once you reach the higher tiers. The loop is: mine pure essence (members) or rune essence (F2P), craft it into valuable runes at the corresponding altars, and sell those runes on the GE. Nature runes, law runes, and astral runes are the big earners.

🎯 Essence Running Loop (Step by Step)

  • Step 1: Mine pure essence at the Essence Mine (teleport there via the wicked hood or the minigame teleport). Fill your inventory and bank it.
  • Step 2: For F2P, mine rune essence at the same location; you can only craft low-tier runes, but it still sells.
  • Step 3: Reach 44 Runecrafting to craft nature runes (via the Ourania Altar for best rates, or the Nature Altar). Nats are used in almost every spell, so demand is massive.
  • Step 4: At 54 Runecrafting, craft law runes; at 72, craft astral runes. Both sell extremely well on the GE.
  • Step 5: Sell your surplus runes on the GE. Keep a stash for your own teleports and enchantments — you will need them constantly.
💡 Don't Waste Your Own Essence Early

Before you have decent Runecrafting, do not burn your pure essence crafting air or mind runes just to make a few coins — those low runes are nearly worthless. Bank the essence, train Runecrafting through the Ourania Altar or Temple of the Eye minigame, and only start selling once you can craft nats or better. Your future self will thank you.

Taken together, these three methods — cowhides, flax, and essence — cover every stage from combat 3 to combat 80. They are the financial floor of your Ironman. Even at combat 80, a quick flax run or a bird-house-adjacent essence trip is a great way to top up your coin pouch between bigger grinds.

4. Bird Runs & Feather Farming for Steady Income

If cowhides and flax are your active income, bird house runs are your passive income — and passive income is the secret to a relaxed Ironman. A single bird house run takes a couple of minutes to set and a couple to collect, but it pays out in feathers, eggs, and bird nests that sell reliably on the GE. More importantly, it runs while you do other things, because the houses fill over time.

4.1 Chicken Feathers (F2P, Level 3+)

Before you ever reach Fossil Island, you can farm feathers the old-fashioned way: kill chickens. The Lumbridge chicken farm (north of the windmill, near Fred the Farmer) and the coop south of the Champions' Guild are packed with chickens that drop feathers 100% of the time. Feathers are used for fly fishing bait and for fletching, so the GE buys them constantly. Bank them noted (chickens drop them in stacks of up to 15 once your inventory has room) and sell in bulk. It is not glamorous, but at combat level 3 it is one of your only real money methods, and it is genuinely profitable.

4.2 Bird House Runs (P2P, 5 Hunter + Bone Voyage)

Bird houses are a Hunter training method on Fossil Island that double as a money printer. You build a bird house (requires 5 Hunter and some logs + hammer), fill it with seeds, place it on one of the four designated spots, and come back later to empty it. Each emptied house yields feathers, eggs, and a bird nest (sometimes with a seed or a ring). The feathers alone are worth a steady trickle of GP, and the nests can be surprisingly valuable.

🎯 A Standard Bird House Run (Step by Step)

  • Step 1: Complete Bone Voyage (talk to the Museum Curator in Varrock, then the barge at the Digsite) to unlock Fossil Island.
  • Step 2: Craft 4 bird houses (e.g., oak bird house at 5 Hunter, willow at 14, maple at 29, yew at 44, magic at 59). Each needs a clockwork + logs.
  • Step 3: Teleport to Fossil Island (Digsite pendant or the rowboat at the Digsite), and place a house at each of the 4 spots: Verdant Valley, Mushroom Forest, Swamp, and the Tar Monster area (use the nearby totems).
  • Step 4: Fill each with cheap seeds (hammerstone, marrentill, or any low herb seed — you will get better seeds back than you put in).
  • Step 5: Wait ~50 minutes (or do other activities), return, empty all four houses, and bank the feathers, eggs, and nests.
  • Step 6: Sell surplus feathers and low-value nests on the GE; keep valuable seeds for Farming and keep any rings you want.

The beautiful part of bird runs is that they stack with everything else. You set your houses, go do a Slayer task or a flax run, and collect on the way back. Over a day of playing, four or five bird runs produce a mountain of feathers that sells for hundreds of thousands of GP with zero additional effort. For an Ironman, this is the closest thing to a "money tree" the game offers.

💡 Feathers Are Never Trash

Even once you are rich, never discard feathers. They are a core fletching supply, and selling them on the GE is a habit you can keep for your entire Ironman career. The same goes for eggs (used in Cooking and Herblore). Bird house runs are a lifelong income stream, not just an early-game one.

When your Hunter climbs higher, you can swap to drift net fishing or chinchompa hunting on Fossil Island for even bigger money, but those are mid-to-late game. In the combat 3–80 window this guide targets, bird houses are the single best "set and forget" GP source you have. Pair them with the Ironman Lategame Supply Guide once you start planning for bossing, because the feathers and seeds you bank now become the supplies you craft later.

5. What to Keep vs Sell

This is the section that separates Ironmen who thrive from Ironmen who constantly restart. Because you can never buy what you need, the single most expensive mistake you can make is vendoring a material you will later want. Below is a practical keep-or-sell framework for the early game. When in doubt, bank it. Bank space is free; regret is not.

5.1 Always Keep (Do Not Sell or Drop)

Item / CategoryWhy Keep It
Herbs (all types)Herblore is mandatory for bossing; cleaned herbs are expensive to replace and you will train it eventually.
Bones & ashesPrayer is required for nearly every end-game activity. Bank them for offering later.
Dragonhide (green/blue/red/black)Crafted into ranged armor you will wear for hundreds of hours. Never sell raw.
Pure / rune essenceYour personal Runecrafting fuel and teleport supply. Sell only what you can replace instantly.
Seeds (tree, fruit, herb, bird nest)Feed Farming runs that give herbs, logs, and food. The backbone of self-sufficiency.
Ores & bars (especially runite, addy)Smithing supplies for armor, bolts, and cannonballs. Bank high-tier ores.
Gems (uncut)Used in Crafting and jewelry (rings of dueling, games necklaces). Cut only what you need.

5.2 Usually Safe to Sell (True Surplus)

These are items where the GE price is real and the "replacement cost" is low. Selling them funds your early coin pouch without hurting your long-term plan — but only sell the surplus, not your entire stock.

Item / CategorySell When…
Feathers (beyond a few thousand)You already have a healthy stack for fletching; the rest sells instantly.
Low-tier alchables (rune scims, addy items)You will not use them and they free up bank space. High alch for GP or sell on GE.
Excess low herbs (tarromin, guam)Only once your Herblore stockpile is comfortably full.
Fish you will not eat (low-level)Below the food you actually use; sell or drop. Keep sharks+ for bossing.
Charms (if overflowing)Summoning is late-game; small surplus can be sold, but bank most.

🎯 The 1-Week Rule

  • Ask yourself: "Will I likely need this within the next week of training?" If yes, bank it.
  • If no, ask: "Is the GE price high enough that selling now is clearly worth it?" If yes, sell the surplus and keep a stack.
  • When truly unsure, the correct Ironman answer is almost always bank it. You can always sell later; you can never buy it back.
💡 Use High Alchemy, Not the General Store

When you do sell alchable drops, use the High Level Alchemy spell (55 Magic) rather than vendoring to a shop. Shops pay a fraction of an item's value, while alching converts it to the full alch price in your coin pouch. For non-alchables, list them on the GE. Never, ever drop useful materials "to save bank space" — make the space by selling actual junk instead.

The discipline of keeping vs selling is what turns a scattered early Ironman into a self-sufficient one. A normal account shrugs off a vendored dragonhide because they can just buy another. You cannot. Every dragonhide, every herb, every bone is a small piece of your future bossing setup. Treat your bank like a warehouse, not a backpack, and the combat 50–80 bracket will feel effortless because you already own the supplies.

6. First Membership & Bond Considerations

Sooner or later every F2P Ironman faces the membership question. The free-to-play world is a fine classroom, but the real Ironman progression — bird runs, pure essence, Slayer, Farming, and the best money methods — lives behind the membership wall. The two ways to pay for it are: spend real money on a membership, or earn a bond in-game by selling enough GP-valued items on the GE. This section does the math so you can decide what fits your account.

6.1 The Bond Math

A bond grants 14 days of membership and trades on the GE for roughly 14 million GP (the exact price fluctuates with the market; check the current GE mid before you commit). For an Ironman, that 14M must be earned entirely through selling your own gathered and crafted goods — you cannot buy GP, so the bond is the ultimate test of your early money methods. Here is how long each stage takes to farm one bond:

MethodGP/hr (realistic)Hours to Farm ~14M
Cowhides (F2P, raw)60k–150k~95–230 hrs
Feathers (F2P chickens)30k–80k~175–460 hrs
Flax → bowstrings (P2P)200k–350k~40–70 hrs
Bird runs + Slayer (P2P)250k–500k~28–55 hrs

The math is blunt: farming a bond in F2P as an Ironman is brutally slow — potentially hundreds of hours of cow-killing. The moment you unlock P2P (via a real-money membership or a bond someone else gifted you), your bond-farming rate improves by 3–5x because flax, bird runs, and Slayer open up. Most serious Ironmen recommend buying your first month with real money, then using P2P methods to fund future bonds. The time you save is worth far more than the few dollars a membership costs.

6.2 Should You Bond or Pay Real Money?

If your goal is to actually progress and enjoy the game, pay for the first membership. If your goal is the "pure Ironman challenge" of bonding entirely from self-made GP, then commit to flax and bird runs the instant you can, because those are the only F2P-adjacent methods fast enough to make bonding realistic. Either way, understand the trade: a bond locks you into 14-day sprints. If you do not accumulate the next 14M before it expires, your account reverts to F2P and your P2P money methods vanish. Plan your bond around a method you can repeat consistently.

🎯 Bonding Checklist (P2P Ironman)

  • Have a stable method: flax + bird runs + early Slayer combined should net 250k–500k GP/hr of sellable surplus.
  • Pre-stock before bonding: bank a few million GP of sellable goods so your first bond does not expire while you "ramp up."
  • Keep what you need: do not sell your last dragonhide or herb just to hit 14M — a bond is worthless if you cannot use the membership.
  • Re-buy before expiry: check your GE sell tab weekly; list surplus early so coins land before the timer runs out.
💡 Membership Unlocks Your Real Economy

The jump from F2P to P2P is not just "more content" — it is the difference between a 100k/hr account and a 400k/hr account. Bird houses alone pay for a large chunk of a bond over two weeks. If you are on the fence, read our F2P to P2P Membership Guide for the full transition plan, and our Ironman Membership Guide for bond-specific strategy.

One last consideration: as an Ironman you also want a real Gear plan once membership hits, because P2P money methods assume you can survive the content. The Beginner Gear Guide and our Combat Training Guide show you how to build that gear from your own drops, which is exactly the self-sufficient loop this entire guide is built around. Money and combat are not separate tracks on an Ironman — they are the same track, and the bond is simply the gate that lets the track accelerate.

To summarize the membership decision: pay real money for your first month if you value your time; bond from self-made GP only if the restriction is part of your personal challenge. Either path is valid. What is not valid is rushing into P2P with no money method, watching your bond expire, and quitting in frustration. Pick a method from Section 2, bank the surplus, and let the membership pay for itself from there.

7. Ironman Money Making FAQs

Can Ironmen use the Grand Exchange at all?

Yes. Ironmen are fully allowed to sell items on the Grand Exchange to receive gold, but they are permanently blocked from buying anything through it. This is the core of Ironman money making: you gather, kill, or craft items, then list them on the GE to convert them into the coins you need. You simply can never use those coins to purchase items from other players.

What is the fastest way to get my first 1M GP as a new Ironman?

In F2P, the fastest honest route is cowhides (tan and sell) plus chicken feathers sold on the GE. That combo can clear 1M in a focused session or two. The moment you are a member, switch to flax → bowstrings and bird house runs, which reach 1M far faster. Do not sleep on feathers — they sell in bulk and the demand never stops.

Should I tan cowhides or sell them raw?

Almost always tan them. Tanned soft leather sells for more than a raw hide, and the Al Kharid tanner charges only 1 GP per hide. If your Crafting is 28+, craft hardleather bodies for even more value. The only time to sell raw is if your bank is overflowing and you just need quick coins — but even then, a 30-second trip to Al Kharid nearly always beats raw prices.

Is Runecrafting worth it for Ironman money?

Yes, but only at the higher tiers. Crafting air or mind runes is basically worthless. The real payday starts at 44 Runecrafting (nature runes), then 54 (law runes) and 72 (astral runes). Those runes are used in countless spells and sell extremely well on the GE. Train Runecrafting through the Ourania Altar or Temple of the Eye minigame, and start selling surplus once you hit nats.

How many bird house runs should I do per day?

As many as you can fit around your other activities. Each house takes roughly 50 minutes to fill, and a full four-house run takes only a couple of minutes to set and collect. Doing 3–5 runs per day produces a steady, mostly passive stream of feathers, eggs, and nests worth hundreds of thousands of GP over a week, with almost no active effort. They stack with everything else you do.

Can I really farm a bond as an Ironman?

You can, but be realistic about the time. In F2P, farming ~14M GP from cowhides and feathers can take well over a hundred hours. In P2P, flax, bird runs, and early Slayer cut that to roughly 30–70 hours. Most Ironmen pay for the first membership with real money and then bond from self-made GP afterward. The bond is achievable — just do not expect it in a weekend.
