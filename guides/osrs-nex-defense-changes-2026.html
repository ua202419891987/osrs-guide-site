OSRS Nex Defense Changes 2026 — Breaker Nerf Impact & Loadout Adjustments

Updated August 19, 2026  |  15 min read  |  By Yanchun Wu

The defence-lowering "breaker" special attacks were nerfed, and that changed how teams strip Nex. Here is exactly what the new cap and diminishing returns do to her, how it shifts every phase, and the precise loadout changes that keep your team fast and safe.

⚔️ Boss & Gear Series · High Level · 18 min read · Updated for 2026
🛡️ Level: 2277+

For years, the fastest way to delete Nex was simple: stack defence-lowering special attacks — the "breakers" — until her defence was on the floor, then unload. The 2026 update changes that. Total defence reduction now has a hard cap and steeper diminishing returns, so you can no longer trivialize her armour with a chain of Dragon Warhammer and Bandos Godsword specs. This guide explains what the breaker nerf actually does to Nex, how it shifts the fight phase by phase, and which loadout adjustments keep your team fast and safe. Whether you run maxed teams or a budget group trying to break into the Ancient Prison, the fix is the same idea: replace debuff-dependence with raw, well-built DPS.

If you run Nex regularly, this is one of the most important 2026 changes to internalize. The supporting gear logic ties directly into our best-in-slot gear guide, the spend-order thinking in our gear upgrade priority guide, and the boss fundamentals in our full Nex boss guide, because the answer to lower defence reduction is almost always "more accuracy and strength everywhere else."   • 📌 Defence reduction now has a hard cap — once you hit it, more breaker specs do literally nothing.
  • 📌 Each successive breaker suffers diminishing returns — the first spec does most of the work, the third or fourth is nearly worthless.
  • 📌 Nex is the boss most affected, because her high base defence and long phases are exactly what the old breaker chain exploited.

1. 🛡️ What Changed: The Breaker Nerf on Nex

Two mechanical changes define the 2026 update, and both matter specifically for high-defence bosses like Nex. If you only remember two sentences from this guide, remember these: there is now a ceiling on how low a target's defence can be pushed, and every breaker after the first one is worth progressively less. Everything else in this article is just the consequence of those two facts applied to the four phases of the fight.

A hard cap on total defence reduction

Before the update, chaining multiple breakers could drag Nex's effective defence extremely low — in well-coordinated teams, sometimes close to zero. That meant a wall of armour basically stopped existing for the window of the chain, and every hit after it landed as if she were naked. The update introduces a ceiling on how much her defence can be reduced, period. Once you hit that floor, additional breaker specs do nothing at all. The days of "one more warhammer just to be safe" are over, because the extra specs are spent on a debuff that is already maxed out and cannot go any lower. The spec energy you used to waste on that fifth breaker is now better spent on your actual damage weapon.

Diminishing returns on stacked specs

Even before you reach the hard cap, the value of each successive breaker now falls off. The first special attack still lowers a meaningful chunk of defence and is worth using. The second is worth noticeably less than the first. The third or fourth special in the same window is worth only a fraction of the first, and in many cases is not worth the special-attack energy you gave up to fire it. This design deliberately discourages the old "spec-stack opener" — the choreographed moment where four players each dumped a Dragon Warhammer in the first three seconds — and rewards controlled, well-timed breakers instead. The skill of the fight shifts from "did everyone bring a breaker" to "did the one breaker land at the perfect moment and did the team follow it with damage."

Why it targets Nex specifically

Nex has always been the poster child for the breaker strategy, and that is not an accident. Her base defence is high, her health pool is enormous, and her phases are long enough that a team could keep her "broken" for a large share of the kill. By capping total reduction, Jagex forces teams to actually out-DPS her through gear and rotation rather than through a debuff loop. It is a difficulty and balance move, not a bug fix — the old strategy was working as intended, it was just too strong and too routine. For comparison, low-defence bosses like Vorkath or Zulrah feel almost nothing from this change, because their defence was never high enough for the breaker chain to matter. Nex, with her armour, is the boss where the nerf bites hardest, which is exactly why this guide exists.

What did NOT change

It is worth being precise about what the update did not touch, because panic-selling gear over a misunderstanding is a real risk. Breakers still reduce defence — the Dragon Warhammer still works, the Bandos Godsword still works, Arclight still works on demons. Nex's base stats, her attacks, her prayers, her enrage, and her loot table are all unchanged. The only thing that changed is the stacking of those effects. If you built a team around one clean breaker and solid DPS, you are largely unaffected. If you built a team around a five-spec collapse, you have real work to do. The rest of this guide is that work.

⚠️ Re-Learn Your Opener

If your team's kill time depended on a long breaker chain, expect slower kills until you adjust. Do not assume the old rotation still works — verify it on a safe early trip or in a practice setting before you commit to an enrage or loot threshold you can no longer hit.

2. ⚔️ How Defence-Lowering Special Attacks Work

Before adjusting anything, understand the toolkit. "Breakers" are weapons whose special attack lowers the target's defence stat for a few seconds. Lower defence means your subsequent hits are more likely to land and, against a target with high defence like Nex, the difference between hitting and missing is the difference between a fast kill and a slow one. The 2026 change does not remove these weapons — it caps how much their effects can stack, which changes how many of them are worth bringing.

The common breakers and their real roles

Not every "breaker" is built the same. The Dragon Warhammer is the king of pure defence reduction: a single big hit that slams defence down by a large flat amount, which is exactly why it was the staple opener. The Bandos Godsword lowers defence by a smaller amount but also heals the user, which gives it a dual role as a sustain tool. Arclight and Darklight lower defence specifically against demons — useless on Nex, who is not a demon, but worth knowing so you do not waste a swap. The Elder Maul is not a breaker at all; it is a high single hit with no defence lower, used purely for damage. Understanding these distinctions is the foundation of the new rotation, because under the nerf you want your one breaker to be the strongest possible, and your "second breaker" to be something that earns its slot through healing or damage rather than through redundant defence reduction.

Weapon Special Effect Post-Nerf Value
Dragon Warhammer (DWH) Lowers target defence (big, single hit) Best single opener breaker — keep one
Bandos Godsword (BGS) Lowers defence + heals user Useful for heal + minor lower in Blood phase
Arclight / Darklight Lowers defence on demons only No value on Nex — skip the swap
Elder Maul High single hit (no defence lower) Damage weapon, not a breaker
Barrelchest anchor / others Minor defence lowers Redundant under the cap — do not stack

The key point after the nerf: these still work, but their combined effect is capped. You get the benefit of one or two well-timed breakers, not a five-spec collapse. Plan your rotation around the first breaker landing hard, then immediate DPS, rather than around a choreographed chain. The deeper math of how defence reduction translates into hit chance is covered in our PvM DPS optimization guide, which is worth reading if you want to quantify exactly how much kill speed the cap costs you on your specific gear.

Dragon Warhammer vs Bandos Godsword: which to lead with

With only one breaker slot truly paying off, the choice of lead breaker matters more than it used to. The Dragon Warhammer wins on raw defence reduction — its flat drop is the largest in the game, so if your goal is to hit the new cap as fast as possible, the warhammer is the opener. The Bandos Godsword's lower is smaller, so leading with it leaves more of Nex's defence on the table during the crucial first damage window. Where the Godsword earns its place is later, in the Blood phase, where its heal offsets Nex's self-healing and its modest defence lower is a bonus on top of survival. So the modern answer is not "warhammer or godsword" — it is "warhammer to open, godsword to sustain," which is a cleaner, more deliberate split than the old spray of both.

💡 One Clean Breaker Beats Five Sloppy Ones

With diminishing returns, the optimal play is usually a single strong breaker (Dragon Warhammer) timed with the team's damage window — not a frantic chain. Save the spec energy for actually hitting. A breaker fired while the team is still repositioning or eating is a breaker wasted.

3. 🔥 Impact on Each Nex Phase

Nex moves through four elemental phases — Smoke, Shadow, Blood, Ice — after the initial pull. The breaker nerf hits some phases harder than others, because the value of stripped defence depends on how much raw DPS you are trying to push in that window. Knowing which phases got slower tells you where to spend your gear upgrades.

Smoke Phase (the pull)

This is where the old breaker chain mattered most. Teams broke her immediately on the pull to accelerate the entire kill, because the smoke phase is the longest single stretch of uninterrupted DPS. With the cap, your opening damage is lower, so the smoke phase drags slightly compared to before. The compensation is stronger opener gear rather than more specs: bring your best accuracy jewellery, make sure Rigour or Augury is active the instant you tag, and ensure everyone's damage weapon is already equipped (not mid-swap) when the warhammer lands. The smoke phase is also where a single perfectly synced breaker pays for itself, because the whole team dumps there.

Shadow Phase

Defence reduction helps accuracy here, but the bigger lever is your own ranged or magic accuracy, because the Shadow phase is where ranged and magic teams do a large share of their work. The nerf barely changes optimal play if your team already brings solid accuracy gear — a Twisted Bow or Tumeken's Shadow was never relying on stripped defence to hit, because those weapons supply their own accuracy. If your shadow-phase DPS was built on "she's broken so even budget gear hits," that is exactly the assumption the nerf punishes, and the Shadow phase is where you will feel it most on an under-geared team.

Blood Phase

Nex heals herself in this phase, so kill speed is everything — every second she spends above your damage threshold, she regens. Lower breaker uptime means you must squeeze more raw DPS to out-pace the heal, and this is the phase where the nerf hurts most on slow teams. It is also the phase where the Bandos Godsword's heal becomes most valuable: a single BGS spec both nudges her defence down and keeps a teammate alive through the drain, which is a more efficient use of a breaker slot than a second warhammer would be. Teams that used to "break and heal later" now do better breaking less and healing on the god sword instead.

Ice Phase & the end

By the end, defence reduction matters less than sustained DPS and not dying to the mechanics. The ice attacks and the final burn are about discipline, not debuffs. The nerf mainly trims your cushion here: a team that used the breaker chain as insurance against a sloppy finish now has less margin, so clean play matters more. Disciplined teams adapt easily; teams that relied on the chain to cover mistakes will find the Ice phase unforgiving. Our boss death-prevention guide covers the mechanical discipline that replaces the lost cushion.

Overall: the nerf lengthens Nex kills by a noticeable but manageable amount, and it is uneven across the fight. The Smoke and Blood phases are where you lose the most time, which is why the gear advice below front-loads accuracy for the opener and sustain for the mid-fight. The fix is gear and discipline, not more breakers.

4. 🪓 Loadout Adjustments — Melee Teams

Melee teams leaned hardest on breakers, because the Scythe of Vitur and other melee weapons love a low-defence target — every extra point of defence stripped meant more of those big AoE hits connecting. Here is how to recover the lost DPS without stacking breakers you no longer need.

Push accuracy and strength higher

With less defence reduction, you need more base accuracy to land hits consistently, and more strength to make the hits that do land count. Full Torva, an Amulet of Torture, Primordial Boots, and an Ultor Ring become more important, not less. The best-in-slot gear guide shows the exact slots and the trade-offs at each budget tier. If you were running budget Bandos because "the breaker chain covered the accuracy gap," that gap is now real again — consider the upgrade sooner. The same logic pushes Inquisitor's armour up the priority list for melee Nex, because its strength bonus directly replaces damage you used to get from a stripped defence.

Keep one breaker, drop the chain

Bring a single Dragon Warhammer for the opener and use it once, timed with the team's damage spike. Drop the second and third breakers from the rotation — their reduced value is not worth the spec energy you could spend on the Scythe or a Zaryte-style switch. This is the single biggest mental shift for melee teams: the warhammer is now a precision tool, not a volley. Call the spec, confirm it lands during the damage window, and then everyone focuses on weapon DPS. Players who keep "just one more for safety" are spending spec energy on a debuff that has already hit its cap.

Weapon choice and switches

The Scythe of Vitur remains the melee BiS, but on Nex its value is now relatively better because raw, uncapped damage matters more than debuff-dependent damage. A Blade of Saeldor or Inquisitor's Mace is a fine budget alternative if you cannot afford a Scythe yet, and the relative gap between them and the Scythe has narrowed slightly in the Scythe's favour post-nerf. Gear switches also matter more now: a Fang or a high-accuracy crush switch for the right phase, a Tormented/Berserker necklace swap for the damage window, and a Synchronised pre-gear loadout all recover time the breaker chain used to buy. Our gear-swap preset guide walks through building these switches so the swap itself costs no ticks.

💡 Bandos Godsword Pulls Double Duty

Since you are keeping breakers minimal, the BGS's heal component gains value — it sustains the team in the Blood phase where Nex heals. One BGS spec can replace a pure DWH chain for survival and modest defence lowering, and it costs you nothing extra if someone was already carrying the godsword for the swap.

Melee teams should also revisit the boss gear checklist before a trip. The checklist forces you to confirm prayers, rings, and switches are maxed before you leave the bank — and after this nerf, a missing Ultor Ring or an un-augmented Torture is no longer "close enough," it is measurable lost DPS that the breaker chain used to hide.

5. 🏹 Loadout Adjustments — Ranged & Magic Teams

Ranged and magic teams feel the nerf less, because both styles have weapons that supply their own accuracy rather than borrowing it from a stripped defence. But they still need tuning, and the teams that ignored accuracy because "the chain covered it" are the ones who will notice.

Ranged teams

  • Twisted Bow or Bow of Faerdhinen: accuracy from the weapon itself matters more now. The Tbow's magic-level scaling keeps you accurate even with less defence reduction, which is why ranged teams are among the least affected by the change.
  • Masori (fortified) + Anguish + Venator Ring: the strength bonuses compensate for the lost defence strip. Upgrade to Masori sooner if you are still on Armadyl, because the raw ranged strength now does work that breakers used to do.
  • One Dragon Warhammer: still worth a single opener spec on the pull, but do not chain it — the ranged DPS window is too valuable to spend on redundant debuffs.
  • Venator bow for clear phases: where the AoE matters, the Venator's pierce recovers some of the multi-target value the breaker chain used to enable.

Magic teams

  • Tumeken's Shadow + Ancestral + Tormented Bracelet: the Shadow's damage multiplier means less reliance on defence reduction, because its power comes from your magic bonus, not from Nex's defence being stripped. Magic teams are the least affected style in the entire fight.
  • Magus Ring: pairs with the Shadow's passive to recover any lost accuracy pressure, and is now a higher-priority pickup than before for magic Nex.
  • Keep a breaker for the opener: a single DWH spec helps the smoke phase, but the Shadow carries the rest of the kill on its own multipliers.
  • Volume / Ancestral switches: as with melee, clean switches during the damage window recover more than a second breaker ever would.

Both styles should review the upgrade priority guide to make sure prayers (Rigour, Augury) and core jewellery are maxed before chasing armour tiers, because the nerf makes those "small" accuracy bonuses count again. 6. 🔄 Replacement Breakers & Strategy Tweaks

Beyond gear, your team's plan needs updating. These are the highest-value strategy changes, and most of them are about discipline rather than new items. A team that executes the new plan on budget gear will out-kill a team that clings to the old chain on BiS.

Time the breaker with the damage window

Because one breaker now does most of the work, coordinate it precisely with when the whole team starts dumping damage. A breaker fired during a mechanic gap, while someone is eating, or before the damage weapon is equipped is wasted. Call it out on voice or in the chat: "breaker on three," then everyone opens on three. The sync is the entire value of the single breaker, and it is why the old "everyone specs whenever" habit has to die.

Shorten dead time

Every second Nex is at high defence and not taking heavy hits is lost DPS, and the nerf punishes lazy uptime more than before. Reduce repositioning, tighten eats, and keep attacking through safe windows. The cushion the breaker chain used to provide — "we have time, she's broken" — is gone, so the time you used to waste is now visible as a longer kill. Our general PvM mechanics guide covers uptime habits that apply to every boss and matter twice as much here.

Use sustain breakers

With the Bandos Godsword's heal, a single BGS spec in the Blood phase both lowers defence a bit and keeps someone alive through the drain. It is a more efficient use of a breaker slot than a second warhammer, because it solves two problems (survival and a small defence nudge) for the price of one spec. Teams that used to carry two warhammers should repurpose one slot to a godsword.

Re-benchmark your team

Run a few measured trips and record kill times. If you are suddenly over the enrage or loot threshold you targeted, adjust gear per the sections above rather than stacking more breakers. "We always hit that enrage" is no longer true after the nerf, and the only way to know your new ceiling is to measure it. Treat the first few post-nerf trips as calibration, not as normal sessions.

📋 New Nex Breaker Rotation

1) One Dragon Warhammer on the pull, called and synced with the team's damage window.
2) Immediate full DPS from all styles — no second breaker yet.
3) Bandos Godsword spec in the Blood phase for heal + minor defence lower.
4) No third or fourth breakers — spend that spec energy on your damage weapon.
5) Tighten uptime and re-benchmark your kill time against your old enrage/loot targets.

8. 💰 Budget Gear Path After the Nerf

Not every Nex team is maxed. If you are a budget or mid-level group, the nerf hits you hardest, but it also gives you a clear upgrade roadmap, because the fix is now "build real accuracy" instead of "hope the chain covers you." Here is the sensible spending order.

Step 1 — max the free accuracy you already own

Before buying anything, confirm Rigour (ranged) and Augury (magic) are active, that your best available prayers are on, and that your rings and necks are the strongest you own. These cost nothing and recover a meaningful slice of the lost hit chance. The upgrade priority guide ranks exactly which of these to secure first, because the order matters more than the total spend.

Step 2 — one accuracy jewellery upgrade

An Ultor Ring (melee), Venator Ring (ranged), or Magus Ring (magic) is the highest-leverage purchase for post-nerf Nex, because each directly replaces defence-reduction value with base accuracy and strength. If you can afford only one upgrade this month, make it the ring for your main style. The budget gear guide lists the cheapest routes to each without jumping to BiS prices.

Step 3 — armour tier bump

For ranged, moving Armadyl to Masori (fortified) is the single biggest armour win after the nerf, because the strength bonus does the work breakers used to. For melee, Bandos to Torva or Inquisitor's pieces closes the gap the strip used to hide. Do not skip straight to Scythe or Shadow if your armour is still budget — the armour tier recovers more lost DPS per GP than the headline weapon does at this stage.

Step 4 — the weapon, last

The Scythe and Tumeken's Shadow are still the best weapons, but their relative value actually improved post-nerf, so they are worth saving for once the accuracy floor is solid. A Blade of Saeldor or a Bow of Faerdhinen carries a budget team fine in the meantime, especially with the accuracy jewellery from Step 2 in place. Our boss gear checklist is the fastest way to confirm you have Steps 1–3 locked before you commit to a trip.

💡 Budget Teams Should Benchmark Twice

Because the nerf widened the gap between clean and sloppy play, a budget team that tightens uptime can often out-kill a richer team that clings to the old chain. Measure your kill time after each upgrade step so you know which purchase actually moved the number.

9. ⚠️ Common Mistakes Teams Make Now

These are the errors we see most often in the weeks after the nerf. None are exotic, but each one quietly costs kills.

① Stacking breakers "just in case"

The most common mistake is bringing three or four breakers because the old habit is hard to break. Past the first spec and the cap, those specs are spent on a debuff that cannot go any lower. That is free damage you threw away. One warhammer, called and synced, is the entire breaker budget that pays off.

② Ignoring the lost accuracy on budget gear

Under-geared teams that "got away with it" because the chain covered their misses now whiff constantly. The fix is not more breakers — it is the accuracy jewellery and armour steps in Section 8. Teams that buy a second warhammer instead of an Ultor Ring are solving the wrong problem.

③ Letting the breaker fire outside the damage window

A breaker that lands while the team is still swapping, eating, or repositioning is wasted, because the reduced defence expires before the hits arrive. The single breaker is only worth it if the whole team dumps during its window. Call it, then hit.

④ Chasing the old enrage target

Many teams keep aiming for the enrage ceiling they used to hit, then wonder why they fall short or die. Your post-nerf baseline is slower — re-benchmark and pick an enrage target you can actually sustain, then climb it as gear improves. Pride-killing for an unreachable enrage just gets people killed.

⑤ Forgetting sustain in the Blood phase

With breakers minimal, the Bandos Godsword's heal matters more, not less. Teams that drop the godsword entirely lose both the heal and the small defence nudge, and the Blood phase drags. Keep one godsword in the rotation specifically for the heal.

⚠️ Do Not Panic-Sell Breakers

The Dragon Warhammer is still the best single opener and is worth keeping. The nerf changed how many to bring, not whether to bring one. Selling your warhammer because "breakers are dead" is the mistake the change was designed to bait — one well-timed DWH is more valuable than ever, not less.

10. ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Dragon Warhammer useless on Nex now?

No. It is still the best single opener breaker, and the first spec lowers a meaningful chunk of defence that helps the whole team's opening damage window. The nerf only kills the old strategy of chaining many breakers for a near-zero defence. One well-timed warhammer is still valuable, and in fact more valuable per spec than it was before, because it is now the only breaker that pays off.

Which combat style is least affected by the nerf?

Magic, especially with Tumeken's Shadow. The Shadow's damage multiplier depends on your gear bonus, not on stripping Nex's defence, so magic teams lose the least kill speed. Ranged is second, because the Twisted Bow supplies its own accuracy through magic-level scaling. Melee teams that leaned on breaker chains lose the most and should upgrade accuracy first.

Should I upgrade from Armadyl to Masori because of this?

If you use ranged and already own a Twisted Bow, yes — sooner than before. The lost defence reduction makes raw ranged strength more valuable, and Masori (fortified) delivers that strength bonus directly. Without a Tbow, finish cheaper accuracy upgrades first; the armour bump helps, but the ring and neck come before it on the priority list.

Do I need a Scythe now where I used budget melee?

Not strictly, but the Scythe's value improved relatively because uncapped raw damage matters more after the nerf. A Blade of Saeldor or Inquisitor's Mace still works; the Scythe just pulls further ahead. Buy the accuracy jewellery and armour tiers before the weapon — those recover more lost DPS per GP at a budget level.

Will Nex kill times go up a lot?

Modestly for disciplined, accuracy-built teams (roughly 15–20%), and more for teams that relied entirely on breaker chains or budget gear (25–30% or more). The cap mostly removes a dominating shortcut, not your whole DPS. Gear, uptime, and a clean single breaker recover most of it. The exact number depends on your starting setup, which is why re-benchmarking matters.

Can I still use Bandos Godsword, or is it dead too?

The godsword is not dead — it is repurposed. Its defence lower is smaller than the warhammer's, so it should not lead the opener, but its heal makes it the ideal Blood-phase breaker, where surviving the drain matters more than another defence point. Keep one in the rotation for the heal and the minor lower, not as a second opener.

How many breakers should a four-man team bring total?

One Dragon Warhammer for the called opener, plus one Bandos Godsword for the Blood-phase heal, is the clean modern loadout. That is two breaker-class weapons across four players, not four warhammers on the pull. The rest of the team should be on pure damage weapons and switches, because spec energy spent on redundant breakers is damage wasted.

Does the nerf affect solo Nex or only teams?

It affects both, but teams feel it more because they could stack more breakers. Solo and duo runners who already used a single warhammer and solid gear are largely unaffected — if anything, their relative efficiency improves versus sloppy teams. The change mostly punishes the choreographed multi-breaker opener that only a coordinated group could pull off.

Should I re-prioritize my whole gear plan because of this?

Not the whole plan, but you should move accuracy and strength upgrades up the list and move "extra breakers" down. The upgrade priority guide still applies; the nerf just makes its accuracy entries more urgent for Nex specifically. Keep working toward BiS, but confirm rings and necks are maxed before chasing armour tiers.

My team keeps missing the enrage we used to hit. What changed?

Slower kills bank less enrage, and that is the direct result of the cap. Either accept a lower enrage target for now, or recover the lost DPS with the gear and uptime steps in this guide. Do not keep attempting an unreachable enrage and dying — re-benchmark your new sustainable ceiling and climb it as upgrades land. Our full Nex guide has the enrage math.

Is there any situation where stacking breakers is still worth it?

Only against very high-defence targets where you have not yet reached the new cap and each spec still adds hit chance you can use immediately inside its window. On Nex, a single warhammer plus a godsword heal covers the useful range for almost every team. If a fifth breaker would still push you under the cap and you can use the window, it is technically useful — but for Nex that situation is rare, which is the whole point of the change.

11. 🎯 Final Tips — Adapt, Don't Panic

  • One breaker, timed well. A single synced Dragon Warhammer beats a frantic chain under the new cap — call it and dump during its window.
  • Keep a godsword for the Blood phase. Its heal matters more now that you are not chaining breakers, and it nudges defence down as a bonus.
  • Upgrade accuracy and strength. Torva, Torture, Primordial, Ultor, Masori, and the Shadow all gain relative value now that the strip is capped.
  • Tighten uptime. Less defence reduction means every dead second costs more — keep attacking through safe windows and cut repositioning.
  • Re-benchmark before chasing enrage. Measure your new kill time and pick a sustainable enrage target instead of attempting the old one.
  • Budget teams: buy the ring before the weapon. Accuracy jewellery recovers more lost DPS per GP than a headline weapon at a low gear level.
  • Don't panic-sell your warhammer. One well-timed DWH is more valuable per spec than ever — the nerf changed how many to bring, not whether.
  • Use the supporting guides. Pair this with the best-in-slot guide, the DPS optimization guide, and the gear checklist to turn the nerf into a concrete plan.
