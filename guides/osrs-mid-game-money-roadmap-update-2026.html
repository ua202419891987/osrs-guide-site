1. 🎯 Where You Are: The Mid-Game Sweet Spot (60–90)

Mid-game is not a single number — it is a profile. Typically you have 60–75 in Attack/Strength/Defence, 70–85 Ranged and Magic, 60–90 in a few moneymaking skills (Slayer, Woodcutting, Fishing, Runecrafting, Herblore), and a bank worth somewhere between 5M and 50M GP. You can already do most content; you just lack the efficiency unlocks that turn "okay" methods into "best in slot" ones. The gap between a 60-stat player and a 90-stat player is large, but the gap between a player who did the quests and one who did not is larger — and that is the part this roadmap attacks first.

Why order matters more than stats

Two players with identical stats can earn 10x differently based on what they unlocked. A player with 75 Ranged but no Dragon Hunter Crossbow (DHCB) struggles at Vorkath; a player with 70 Ranged, Piety, and Barrows Gloves clears Gargoyles and Cannon tasks far faster. The roadmap below prioritizes unlocks that multiply every future hour. Think of each unlock as a lever: pulling it once (a quest, a few days of training) changes the output of hundreds of hours afterward. Skip the lever and you repeat the same weak hour forever.

The one mistake that costs the most

Skipping the quest grind. Players often think "I'll just train Slayer to 85 and then do bosses." But without Piety, without Barrows Gloves, without the DHCB or a decent whip, your Slayer and boss kills are slower and less profitable. Spend the first stretch on quests — it is the highest ROI time you will spend all mid-game. The reason is simple arithmetic: Piety adds roughly 18–23% melee DPS, Barrows Gloves add a clean 12% across the board, and together they can turn a break-even task into a 400K GP/hr task. No amount of raw levelling matches that multiplier in the same time budget.

💡 Use a Quest List, Not a Vague Plan

Work through the "recipe" quests (specifically the ones for Barrows Gloves and Piety) as a checklist. Knock them out in big batches on a quiet weekend. You will feel the difference immediately in every combat method afterward, and you will stop second-guessing whether you are "ready" for the next step.

What "ready" actually means

New mid-game players fixate on hitting a combat level and then rush content they cannot yet profit at. Readiness is a bundle of three things: the stat threshold, the gear threshold, and the knowledge threshold (you know the rotation). This guide gives you the stat and gear order; the knowledge comes from the linked method guides. Do not attempt a boss until all three line up, because an under-geared death costs more than the hour you "saved" by rushing.

2. 🛡️ Step 1: Unlock the Gear That Unlocks GP

Before you chase GP, secure the two unlocks that make every combat hour worth more: Barrows Gloves and Piety. Both come from quest lines, not grinding, which is exactly why impatient players skip them. They are the cheapest permanent DPS you will ever buy.

🧤 Barrows Gloves (via the Recipe for Disaster subquests)

Barrows Gloves are the best all-around gloves in the game and require completing a chain of subquests. They boost your melee DPS noticeably, which speeds up every Slayer task and boss. The subquests also force you through useful content (e.g., Mountain Daughter, Shilo Village, Lost City, Heroes' Quest) that unlock other money methods and teleports. Budget a few days for this — it pays for itself within a week of faster kills. The glove slot is deceptively important: it is one of the few slots where a single upgrade touches melee, ranged, and magic alike, so the value compounds across every combat style you use.

🙏 Piety (via King's Ransom + Knight Waves)

Piety is the best melee prayer in the game and transforms your DPS at Gargoyles, Kurasks, Abyssal Demons, and melee bosses. The prerequisite King's Ransom is a single straightforward quest; the Knight Waves training ground is the only grind, and it is a short one. With Piety, melee Slayer tasks jump from break-even to 300K–700K GP/hr. If you ever plan to do Zulrah or Vorkath on a ranged or magic setup, note that Rigour and Augury are the ranged/magic equivalents — they come later, but the same logic applies: prayers are the highest-ROI DPS you can buy with a quest.

🏹 The Ranged/Magic baseline

You also want a DHCB (from the Chambers of Xeric or the Grand Exchange) for dragon-type bosses, and a decent magic setup (e.g., Trident of the Seas/Swamp or a charged staff) for Zulrah. These are gear unlocks you fund with the Slayer income from Steps 1–2, not before. Do not buy them prematurely — let your income rise to meet them. A useful rule: if buying a 20M GP weapon drops your bank below what you need for supplies (potions, food, runes) for a full session, you bought it too early. Income first, trophy second.

3. ⚔️ Step 2: Slayer as Your Money Engine (70+)

Once Barrows Gloves and Piety are in, Slayer becomes your primary earner — and the beauty is you train combat while you profit. The mid-game Slayer band (70–85) is where the truly profitable tasks open up, and unlike bossing, Slayer does not punish you for "not being efficient yet" — it just rewards consistency.

🗿 Gargoyles (75 Slayer) — 300K–700K GP/hr

Gargoyles drop alchables, coins, and the Granite Maul (occasionally). With a rock hammer and Piety, they die fast and pay consistently. This is your first "real" Slayer money and a gentle intro to task efficiency. Bring a cannon if you can afford the balls — it roughly doubles your kill speed on multi-combat tasks and shortens the time to your next gear purchase. The coin drops alone cover most of your supply cost, so the alchables and rare drops are near-pure bank gain.

🌿 Kurasks (70 Slayer) — 400K–700K GP/hr

Kurasks drop leaf-bladed battleaxes and solid alchables. They are weak to melee and die quickly with your new gear. A reliable mid-tier task that funds your first big gear purchases. Note that Kurasks require a leaf-bladed sword or broad bolts to finish — factor that into your setup before you accept the task. The herb and seed drops also feed your Herblore training, so the task pays you twice.

💀 Nechryaels & Abyssal Demons (80/85) — 700K–1.2M GP/hr

These higher Slayer tasks drop valuable seeds, herbs, and rare items. Nechryaels in particular are a mid-game favorite because of their consistent drop table and the fact that they are often assignable in multi-combat areas where a cannon shines. By 85 Slayer you are firmly in 1M+ GP/hr territory from tasks alone. Abyssal Demons add the Abyssal whip drop, which is both a solid weapon for you and a consistent alchable if you already have one. The Slayer money making guide ranks every profitable task and the exact block/skip setup that maximizes this band.

Use a Slayer master wisely

Get tasks from Nieve (or Konar for the location bonuses) and block or skip the low-value tasks (e.g., caves, pyrefiends, jelly if you dislike them) once you have the block slots. Efficiency trumps raw task count — a skipped junk task is an extra profitable task per hour. Spend your first block slot on the single worst task you get assigned, then build from there. Over a month, smart blocking adds up to days of extra profitable play.

💡 Pair Slayer With Passive Income

While farming Slayer, keep your AFK and farming loops running in the background. The GP from herb runs and birdhouses covers your supplies so your Slayer profit is nearly pure bank gain. Treat passive income as the floor under your active income — it means a bad Slayer day is still a profitable day.

Extended Slayer rotations that print

Once you have the block slots, a strong mid-game rotation is: block the three worst tasks, skip anything below ~250K GP/hr effective, and chain the rest. Use Slayer rings and a skill necklace to teleport between masters and tasks. The goal is to never walk — every minute of travel is a minute not earning. Players who optimize the loop, not just the kills, are the ones who hit the top of the GP/hr band.

4. 🐉 Step 3: Your First Boss — The Front Door

Slayer builds your bank; your first boss multiplies it. The mid-game "front door" bosses are Vorkath and Zulrah — both reachable around 75 Ranged/Magic with reasonable gear, and both pay 1.8M–3M GP/hr. These are the first bosses where your time-per-GP finally beats skilling, and they are the proof that the mid-game roadmap "works."

🐉 Vorkath (75 Ranged, DHCB, Piety/Rigour)

Vorkath is the most consistent mid-game boss. Each kill takes 1–2 minutes and drops superior dragon bones, blue dragonhide, and the occasional valuable. With a DHCB and ~75 Ranged, expect 2M–4M GP/hr. It is the gold standard because the rotation is learnable and the profit is stable. The only real barrier is the upfront cost of supplies and the DHCB, which is exactly why Steps 1–2 exist: by the time you arrive here, your Slayer income should have funded the gear. Learn the acid phase and the zombie phase cold — a missed prayer or a wasted tick is the difference between 2M and 3M GP/hr.

🐍 Zulrah (75 Ranged/Magic)

Zulrah requires learning its phase rotations but pays similarly — 1.8M–3M GP/hr with unique drops worth millions. Slightly more skill-intensive than Vorkath, but a great second boss once you are comfortable. Many mid-game players alternate the two to avoid burnout. Zulrah's learning curve is steepest in the first ten kills; use a rotation guide and accept that the first hour is an education, not an income. After that, the saradomin brews and the unique drops make it one of the best GP/hr in the game for the stat investment.

🗿 Grotesque Guardians (75 Slayer) — stepping stone

Before Vorkath, the Guardians (from the Slayer Tower) are a lighter boss that drops the Granite Maul and decent alchables. They are a good confidence builder and a source of early boss income at 75 Slayer. They also teach you boss mechanics — prayer switching, positioning, DPS windows — in a low-stakes environment, so by the time you stand in front of Vorkath you already think like a bosser.

⚠️ Don't Rush the Boss With Bad Gear

Attempting Vorkath or Zulrah under-geared turns a 2M GP/hr method into a death-toll sink. Hit the stat and gear thresholds first — the Slayer phase exists precisely to fund that gear. A death at Vorkath costs you supplies and a trip; repeated deaths cost you the bank you needed to get better gear, trapping you below the threshold. Patience here is literally GP.

5. 🪓 Step 4: Skilling Pivots for Mid-Game GP

Bossing and Slayer are the top earners, but they demand attention. On days you want a change — or when boss Worlds are busy — these skilling methods fill the gap and still pay mid-game rates. They also double as training, so you are never "wasting" a session.

🪵 Teak & Mahogany (35/50 Woodcutting, 35/50 Construction)

Teak (35) and Mahogany (50) logs are used for Construction training and hold steady value. With a dragon axe, you can clear 100K–250K GP/hr while barely trying. Mahogany especially is in constant demand from players training Construction, and the price is stable because the demand is real and recurring. This is the classic "I want to watch a video while still earning" method, and it belongs in every mid-game rotation as the low-attention filler.

🐊 Mahogany Homes (Construction lite)

The Mahogany Homes minigame pays in planks (which you can sell or use) and Carpentry XP. It is a relaxed mid-game earner that doubles as a Construction trainer — useful if you want the Construction level for the portal nexus or POH upgrades that speed up your boss runs. A higher Construction level also unlocks the Ornate Rejuvenation Pool, which restores stats and prayer free between trips — a quiet but real GP/hr boost for any bosser.

💎 Runecrafting (Nature / Law / Blood)

At 44 Runecrafting, Nature runes pay ~150K–300K GP/hr and are the classic "background" craft. At 77+, Blood runes are far more valuable (400K–700K GP/hr) and pair perfectly with bossing since you use blood spells anyway. Runecrafting is slow to train but a strong mid-game pivot once leveled, because it is one of the few skills where the GP/hr keeps climbing with the level. ZMI and the Ourania Altar are the efficient paths to 77; the best methods guide covers the exact route.

🐟 Fishing Anglerfish (82, after Family Crest)

Anglerfish are the premier healing food for bossing and sell for strong GP. At 82 Fishing (with the Angler outfit from Tempoross), you earn 200K–400K GP/hr while stocking your own boss supplies — a neat self-sustaining loop. Fishing your own anglerfish turns a Vorkath supply cost into a profit centre, because you would have bought those fish anyway. The AFK guide explains how to run Tempoross in the background while you Slayer.

🌿 Herb runs and birdhouses (passive layer)

Two often-overlooked mid-game earners are the passive farming loops. Herb runs on a full plot of ranarrs or snapdragons pay 100K–300K per run with almost no effort, and birdhouse runs on Fossil Island pay 50K–150K per run in nests and seeds. Together they are a few minutes of clicking that return millions per week, and they run on timers you can collect between boss kills. The herb runs profit guide and the birdhouse runs guide show the exact layouts that maximize this layered income.

6. 📈 Step 5: Stacking Methods for 2M+/hr

By the time your core stats approach 90, you should be stacking methods rather than picking one. The wealthiest mid-game accounts run a rotation: Slayer task → Vorkath/Zulrah when tasks are slow → skilling pivot when bored → passive/flipping in the background. Stacking is what turns "I made 2M today" into "my account makes 2M a day whether I try or not."

The rotation model

Instead of "I will do Vorkath for 4 hours," do: a Slayer block (1–1.5 hr), 30 minutes of Vorkath, a herb-run + birdhouse collection, then back to Slayer. This keeps every income stream warm and avoids the burnout that makes players quit. The rotation also smooths your GP/hr: when Vorkath Worlds are dead, Slayer carries you; when Slayer tasks are junk, bossing carries you; when both bore you, skilling carries you. No single bad session tanks your week.

Reinvest, don't hoard

Every 5M you bank should buy either better gear (more boss DPS) or supplies that let you skip a grind (e.g., buy bones to train Prayer to 70 for Piety if you have not). Hoarding GP is the mid-game trap — liquid bank that is not earning is wasted potential. Park idle GP in GE flipping buy limits overnight so even your sleep earns. The discipline is not "spend everything" but "every GP should be either working or on its way to a working tool."

📊 Target Earning Ladder (mid-game → late)

  • 60 stats + Piety/Barrows: Slayer 300K–700K GP/hr
  • 75 Ranged + DHCB: Vorkath 2M–4M GP/hr
  • 80+ Slayer: Nechryaels/Abyssal Demons 700K–1.2M GP/hr
  • 77+ Runecrafting: Blood runes 400K–700K GP/hr
  • 82 Fishing: Anglerfish 200K–400K GP/hr (self-supplies boss)
  • 90 across board + best gear: 2M–4M GP/hr baseline, higher with uniques

The ladder is additive, not exclusive. A player stacking Slayer + Vorkath + blood runes + herb runs is not choosing one line — they are summing all of them, which is how the band's top earners clear 4M+ GP/hr without a single max-level stat.

7. 💰 The Mid-Game Bank: What to Buy and What to Skip

The mid-game bank is where most players leak GP. You suddenly have millions and a hundred "must-have" items in front of you. Spend it wrong and you stall; spend it right and you accelerate. This section is the shopping order that protects your progress.

Buy first: supplies that unlock sessions

Before any trophy, buy the consumables that let you actually run the methods: prayer potions, food, runes, and a cannon (plus cannonballs). These convert directly into GP through every combat method and pay for themselves within a session or two. A player with a 30M bank and no supplies earns less than a player with a 5M bank and a full inventory, because the second one is actually playing.

Buy second: the gear that multiplies DPS

The DHCB, a Tik/rigour setup, an upgraded whip or blade, and the best armor you can afford come next. These are the levers from Step 1 — each one raises your GP/hr permanently. Prioritize the single biggest DPS jump per GP: usually the weapon, then the prayer, then the armor. Do not spread your bank across five small upgrades when one big one would do more.

Skip: trophies and fashion

Expensive cosmetics, over-leveled gear you cannot yet use, and "status" items (a twisted bow at 75 ranged, for example) are traps. They sit in your bank doing nothing while the GP they represent could have funded the supplies for a hundred Vorkath kills. Buy the tool, not the trophy. The trophy comes free once the tool has paid for itself a hundred times over.

The 20% rule for big purchases

A useful discipline: never let a single purchase drop your liquid (ready-to-use) bank below 20% of its pre-purchase value unless it directly enables a method you will run that same day. This keeps you solvent through unlucky dry streaks and price dips, so a bad week never forces you to sell your gear at a loss. Liquidity is what lets you keep playing the roadmap instead of resetting it.

💡 Track Net Worth, Not GP/hr Alone

Mid-game progress is best measured by total bank value week over week, not the GP/hr of a single session. A slow day of questing may show low GP/hr but unlocks a method worth millions per week afterward. Judge the roadmap by the trend, and you will make calmer, richer decisions.

8. ⚠️ Common Mid-Game Money Mistakes

Most mid-game accounts stall for the same handful of reasons. If you recognize yourself in any of these, fix it before reading further — it is almost certainly costing you more than this entire guide's effort is worth.

① Skipping quests to "save time"

The biggest one. Players who skip Barrows Gloves and Piety to train faster end up slower overall, because every hour afterward is weaker. The quest detour is measured in days; the penalty is measured in weeks. Do the quests first, every time.

② Bossing before the thresholds line up

Standing at Vorkath with 70 Ranged and no DHCB is a supply sink, not a money maker. Wait until stats, gear, and rotation knowledge all line up. The two extra days of Slayer you spend getting there pay for themselves in one good boss session.

③ Hoarding instead of reinvesting

A fat liquid bank that is not earning is the silent mid-game tax. Every week you sit on GP instead of buying the next DPS lever, you forfeit the compounded income that lever would have generated. Reinvest aggressively into tools, not trophies.

④ One-method grind and burnout

Doing a single method for four hours straight is how players quit OSRS. Burnout stalls accounts far more than any stat gap. Rotate Slayer, bossing, skilling, and passive collection so no single session feels like a job. The rotation model in Step 5 exists precisely to prevent this.

⑤ Ignoring passive and flipping income

Herb runs, birdhouses, and idle flipping are "free" GP that most mid-game players leave on the table. Over a month they add up to tens of millions — often more than a week of active grinding. The AFK guide shows how to wire them in without extra playtime.

⑥ Bad block/skip Slayer setup

Accepting every task because "a task is a task" wastes hours on cave horrors and pyrefiends. Once you have block slots, use them on your worst tasks. Smart blocking is the highest-ROI Slayer change you can make after Piety.

⚠️ The Mid-Game Trap Is Patience, Not Difficulty

Nothing in this band is hard. The trap is impatience — skipping steps, rushing bosses, hoarding instead of investing. Every mistake above is a shortcut that slows you down. The roadmap is boring on purpose: do the dull high-ROI thing first, and the exciting GP follows.

11. ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

What stats count as "mid-game" for this roadmap?

Roughly 60–75 melee, 70–85 Ranged/Magic, and 60–90 in a few moneymaking skills, with a 5M–50M GP bank. If you are below that, start with our beginner guide. If you are already 90+ across the board with boss gear, you have effectively graduated to late-game and should be looking at the boss profit tier lists instead.

Should I do Barrows Gloves and Piety before any money making?

Yes. They are quest-locked, not grind-locked, and they multiply the GP/hr of every combat method you do afterward. Skipping them to "make GP faster" is slower overall because your kills are weaker. Treat the first few days as an investment — the curve bend at Piety in the chart above is exactly this effect.

Is Slayer really better than just bossing?

For mid-game, Slayer is better because it trains your stats while it pays, and it funds the gear you need for bossing. Pure bossing before you have the stats/gear just loses money. Use Slayer to reach the boss thresholds, then add bosses on top. The two are not competitors — Slayer is the on-ramp to bossing.

Which first boss should I learn — Vorkath or Zulrah?

Vorkath is the more consistent and forgiving first boss; learn it first at 75 Ranged with a DHCB. Zulrah pays similarly but has a steeper learning curve. Many players learn both and rotate to avoid burnout. See our best methods guide for full comparisons and the exact gear setups for each.

How do I avoid mid-game burnout?

Rotate methods instead of grinding one for hours: Slayer, then a boss session, then a skilling pivot, then passive collection. Keep your passive/flipping income running so your bank grows even on off days. Burnout, not stats, is what stalls most mid-game accounts — the rotation model in Step 5 is designed to prevent it.

Do I need a Dragon Hunter Crossbow to start Vorkath?

Effectively yes for efficient kills. Without a DHCB your DPS against Vorkath (a dragon) is dramatically lower, and the method stops being worth the supply cost. Fund the DHCB through Slayer first — that is the entire point of Steps 1–2. A DHCB bought from Slayer income pays for itself within a handful of boss sessions.

What should I do with idle GP between sessions?

Park it in GE flipping buy limits overnight. Even modest flipping on liquid consumables earns while you are offline, and it keeps your bank working instead of sitting. The mid-game rule is: every GP is either a working tool or on its way to becoming one — never just stored.

How important are herb runs and birdhouses at this stage?

More than most players think. They are a few minutes of clicking that return 150K–450K GP per week combined, run on timers you collect between boss kills, and cost almost nothing. The herb runs guide and birdhouse runs guide show the layouts. Over a month this passive layer rivals a week of active grinding.

Should I train Runecrafting during mid-game or wait?

Train it during mid-game, because Blood runes (77+) pay 400K–700K GP/hr and the runes feed your bossing directly. It is slow and tedious, but it is one of the few skills whose GP/hr keeps rising with the level, and it slots into the rotation as a skilling pivot. Start Nature runes at 44 for a background earner, then push to 77 when you can stand the grind.

My bank is stuck around 10M and I can't seem to grow it — what's wrong?

Almost certainly one of the mistakes in Section 8: skipping quests, bossing under-geared, hoarding instead of reinvesting, or one-method burnout. Re-read that section and fix the leak. The roadmap is sequenced so each step funds the next; if you jumped ahead or stalled on one method, the compounding stops and the bank plateaus. Reset to the sequence and the growth resumes.

12. 🎯 Final Tips — Win the Mid-Game

  • Quests before grinds. Barrows Gloves + Piety outperform 20 raw combat levels for GP/hr.
  • Let Slayer fund your boss gear. Don't buy a DHCB prematurely — earn it through tasks first if your bank is thin.
  • Rotate methods to stay sane. Slayer → boss → skilling → passive keeps every stream warm.
  • Reinvest aggressively. Better gear raises DPS, which raises GP/hr — it pays for itself fast.
  • Keep passive income running. Herb runs and flipping earn while you boss; never let the timers lapse.
  • Block junk Slayer tasks. Skipping low-value tasks is free GP/hr once you have block slots.
  • Measure bank value weekly. Progress is the trend, not any single session's GP/hr.
  • Never rush a boss under threshold. An under-geared death costs more than the hour you "saved."
  • Stack, don't pick. The top of the band comes from summing methods, not mastering one.
