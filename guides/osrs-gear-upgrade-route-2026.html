OSRS Gear Upgrade Route 2026 — From Newbie to Best-in-Slot

🛡️ Gear Route 1.0 — Part of the Combat Gear Series · 22 min read · Updated August 2026
🛡️ Level: 110

Every maxed player started with a Bronze scimitar and monk robes. The gap between a fresh account and a best-in-slot (BiS) endgame kit looks impossibly wide — but it is really just a sequence of well-ordered steps. This guide lays out the full gear upgrade route for 2026: a phased path from newbie to BiS, showing exactly what to buy at each stage, in what order, and crucially, when to stop spending and go kill things instead. Follow the route and you will never wonder "what next?" again.

This page ties the whole journey together into one roadmap. If you are still in the early game, our budget gear guide covers the free and near-free unlocks in detail before you begin the route.

⏱️ Quick Verdict — 30-Second Read

  • 📌 Gear is gated by stats, quests, and prayers first — GP last. A maxed, quest-capped account with 50M GP clears more content than a 2B GP account stuck at 75 stats.
  • 📌 Buy weapons and prayers before armor, every single stage. A Fang, Bowfa, or the Rigour/Augury scrolls out-DPS any armor piece by 3–5x per GP spent.
  • 📌 Follow stages, not a shopping list. Stage 0 (free) → Stage 1 (~20–60M) → Stage 2 (~100–200M) → Stage 3 (~200–500M) → Stage 4 (500M–2B+).
  • 📌 Fund each stage by playing it. Slayer pays for Stage 1, Vorkath/Zulrah pay for Stage 2, raids pay for Stage 3 and 4.
  • 📌 You are "done" at one mega-rare + BiS in your main style. More than that buys minutes, not access.

1. 🧭 The Route Philosophy: Stages, Not Items

The biggest mistake players make is treating gear as a shopping list they tick off randomly. The correct mental model is stages. At each stage your account unlocks new content, and that content funds the next stage. You do not "save for Torva" from day one — you earn the stage that makes Torva affordable, then buy it. Progress in Old School RuneScape is almost never limited by the item you are missing; it is limited by whether your account is allowed to use it yet.

There are four forces that gate your progress, and they matter in this exact order: stats (can you wield it?), quests (are the unlocks done?), prayers (are Rigour, Augury, and Piety unlocked?), and finally GP (can you afford it?). Most players over-focus on GP and ignore the other three. A fresh Level 3 cannot wield a Twisted Bow even if you handed them 2 billion coins — the weapon would sit in the bank doing literally nothing. Stats and quests are the real gate; GP is just the final, easiest step once the other three are satisfied.

The route below is written so you always have a "next step" that matches your current stage. Each stage ends when its content comfortably funds the next one. This is also why we strongly recommend building combat fundamentals first through our Slayer beginner guide, because Slayer is the engine that pays for almost every early upgrade on this route.

💡 The North Star

At every stage, ask: "What is the cheapest thing that unlocks new content?" That is your next purchase. Content is what generates GP, and GP is what buys BiS. Never buy an upgrade that does not unlock something new unless your current content is already trivial.

A useful way to visualize the whole journey is as a ladder. Each rung is a stage, and each rung is only reachable once the rung below it is stable. The diagram below shows the five rungs of the 2026 gear route and the GP range where each one "lives."

2. 🐣 Stage 0 — Starter Gear & Free Unlocks (Level 3 to 70)

Goal: reach the stats and quest points that unlock the best free gear in the game. Almost nothing in Stage 0 costs GP, and that is the entire point — you are building the foundation that every later purchase relies on. Skip this stage and no amount of money will help you, because the items you would buy cannot be used yet.

What to wear (all free or near-free)

  • Melee: Steel scimitar → Black scimitar → Dragon scimitar (free reward from Monkey Madness II) once you hit 70 Attack. The Dragon scimitar is the best non-degradable melee weapon below the Whip and costs you nothing but a quest.
  • Ranged: Maple shortbow → Magic shortbow (i) with broad arrows (unlocked via the Fremennik Isles / Western Provinces Diary). Solid ranged damage for hundreds of hours at near-zero cost.
  • Magic: Mystic robes (~200K GP total) and a Staff of the Dead or Trident of the Seas. Mystic is the free best-in-class mage armor until you can afford Ancestral.
  • Defense / Prayer: Initiate (Prayer) or Proselyte (Prayer) armor from The Slug Menace — free and excellent for safe, low-food training thanks to the Prayer bonus.

Quests to rush (the real "gear" of Stage 0)

Four quests hand you more combat power than 100M GP of early purchases: Monkey Madness II (Dragon scimitar + 70+ stats), Dragon Slayer II (Ava's Assembler), Recipe for Disaster (Barrows Gloves — the best all-round gloves in the game until Tormented Bracelet), and The Fremennik Isles (Neitiznot Faceguard). Add Fortess / Fighter Torso from the Barbarian Assault minigame, the Fire Cape from the TzHaar Fight Cave, and the Dragon Defender from the Warriors' Guild, and you own a kit that clears most sub-boss content for free.

Train Attack, Strength, Defence, Ranged, Magic, and Prayer to 70+ through Slayer — it pays you while it trains you. By the end of Stage 0 you should own the free unlocks we document in our budget gear guide: Fighter Torso, Barrows Gloves, Fire Cape, Ava's Assembler, Imbued God Cape, Neitiznot Faceguard, and Dragon Defender. These items carry you deep into Stage 1.

💡 Do not buy gear here

Every GP you "save" at Stage 0 should go toward 70+ stats and the key quests. Gear bought now is replaced within days. Invest in unlocks, not iron. The single best Stage 0 purchase is the quest point and the combat level, not an item.

3. 🌱 Stage 1 — First Real Traded Upgrades (70–90 Stats)

Goal: your first traded weapons and amulets, funded by Slayer and low-level bosses. Budget roughly 20–60M GP for the whole stage. This is where the account stops feeling like a beginner and starts feeling like a PvMer. The key lesson of Stage 1 is that weapons beat armor — almost the entire budget goes to weapons and a couple of rings, not to chestplates.

The purchase order

  1. Abyssal Whip (~1.5M) — the best GP-to-DPS melee weapon in the game. Buy it the moment you hit 70 Attack. It replaces the Dragon scimitar and stays relevant until the Fang in Stage 2.
  2. Toxic Blowpipe (~4M + darts) — your ranged workhorse for hundreds of hours. The Blowpipe is the single most-used weapon on the road to endgame because it is fast, accurate, and works everywhere from Slayer to Zulrah.
  3. Trident of the Swamp (~4M) — a powered-staff magic option for Zulrah, Kraken, and Chambers of Xeric. No runes required; it scales with your Magic level.
  4. Amulet of Fury (~1.5M) → upgrade to Torture / Anguish / Occult (~27M total) as cash allows. The Fury is a fine all-rounder; the three imbued amulets are the best-in-slot neck slot for melee, ranged, and magic respectively.
  5. Berserker Ring (i) (~3M) — best melee ring until the Ultor. An easy, cheap damage bump that you will keep using for a long time.

With this kit you can comfortably do God Wars Dungeon, Zulrah, Vorkath (with a Dragon Hunter Crossbow switch), and most high-value Slayer bosses. Your income now shifts from pure Slayer to actual bossing, which funds Stage 2 far faster. The cost-benefit table below shows why weapons dominate this stage.

When you outgrow it: when Blowpipe DPS starts feeling slow on bigger bosses and you are farming Vorkath and Zulrah like clockwork, you are ready for the power-spike weapons of Stage 2.

4. 🔥 Stage 2 — The Mid-Game Power Spike (100–200M GP)

Goal: the weapons and prayers that redefine your account — Osmumten's Fang, the Bow of Faerdhinen, and the Rigour/Augury scrolls. This is the most exciting stage because the jumps are enormous. A single purchase here (Rigour) can increase your ranged DPS by roughly a quarter forever, on every ranged weapon you ever own.

Priority purchases (in this order)

  1. Rigour + Augury scrolls (~120M total) — buy these before any armor. Rigour gives +25% ranged strength and +20% ranged accuracy; Augury gives +25% magic damage and +20% magic accuracy. These are permanent, account-wide upgrades that multiply the value of every ranged and magic weapon you already own.
  2. Osmumten's Fang (~14M) — replaces the Whip for bossing; its doubled accuracy roll makes it devastating against high-defence targets like Vorkath, Nex, and raid bosses.
  3. Bow of Faerdhinen (charged) (~75M) — the best ranged weapon before the Twisted Bow; it unlocks the Inferno and comfortable solo God Wars. The Bowfa + Rigour combo is a larger DPS jump than any single armor piece you will ever buy.
  4. Boot trio (~46M) — Primordial, Pegasian, and Eternal boots. Permanent all-style upgrades that also save inventory space (no need to swap for diary tasks).
  5. Bandos Chestplate + Tassets (~47M) — your first real melee armor, bought only after the weapons and boots above. Bandos adds strength bonus that matters once you already have the Fang.

With this kit you can attempt the Inferno (for the Infernal Cape), solo God Wars Dungeon, and entry Theatre of Blood and Chambers of Xeric. The Bowfa + Rigour combination alone is a bigger damage increase than any chestplate. This is why the route永远 puts weapons and prayers ahead of armor.

📋 Stage 2 Exit Checklist

You are ready for Stage 3 when: Rigour + Augury are done, Fang + Bowfa are owned, the boot trio is upgraded, and you are consistently clearing Vorkath, Zulrah, and the Chambers of Xeric mage hand. That is roughly 200M GP deployed efficiently — and almost none of it was armor.

5. ⚔️ Stage 3 — Pre-Raid Kit (200–500M GP)

Goal: a kit that reliably clears high-invocation raids and the Theatre of Blood. You now target your first real armor tiers and the cheaper mega-content enablers. Crucially, this is also where the per-boss readiness checkpoints start to matter — different bosses expect different minimum kits, and walking in under-geared wastes supplies and time.

What to buy

  • Ancestral Hat + Bottom (~150M) — your first real mage armor; pairs with the Trident or Sang for Chambers of Xeric and ToB.
  • Armadyl Chestplate + Chainskirt (~66M) — strong ranged armor that works beautifully with the Bowfa if you do not yet own Masori.
  • Masori (f) set (~205M) — only after you own a Twisted Bow (see Stage 4); do not buy Masori to use with the Blowpipe, the accuracy penalty hurts.
  • Zaryte Vambraces (~5M) + Venator Ring (~95M) — a ranged ring upgrade that pays off the moment you are using the Bowfa or Tbow seriously.
  • Infernal Cape — untradeable, earned from the Inferno; the single biggest melee DPS unlock after a weapon (+8 strength over the Fire Cape). See our Inferno guide for the fight.

At the top of this stage you should be attempting ToB and high-invocation Tombs of Amascut. Your DPS is now "good enough" for any group content. The remaining upgrades are about speed and solo capability, not access. Use the checkpoint table below to confirm you are ready before committing to a raid.

The pattern is obvious: bosses unlock when your weapons and prayers reach the threshold, not when your armor does. A Stage 2 account with Rigour and a Bowfa can attempt CoX; a Stage 4 account without Rigour cannot. Always check the weapon/prayer gate first.

6. 👑 Stage 4 — Toward Best-in-Slot (500M–2B+ GP)

Goal: the mega-rare weapons and BiS armor. Buy one at a time, in the order below, based on the content you enjoy most. This is the ceiling documented fully in our best-in-slot guide. Reaching it costs roughly 2.5–3B GP in tradeables per style, plus significant untradeable time investments (Infernal Cape, Ava's Assembler, quest requirements).

The mega-rare order

  1. Twisted Bow (~880M) — the highest-priority mega-rare; it dominates the Inferno, CoX, Zulrah, God Wars, and a huge slice of Slayer. If you only ever own one mega-rare, make it this one.
  2. Scythe of Vitur (~1.15B) — transforms the Theatre of Blood, Phosani's, and size-dependent Slayer. Stronger than the Tbow in its niches but less universal.
  3. Tumeken's Shadow (~980M) — best-in-slot magic; it multiplies your gear's magic damage bonus rather than adding to it, which is why it is so dominant at CoX and ToB mage hands.
  4. Zaryte Crossbow (~135M) — add this after your first mega-rare for Nex and bolt-spec fights where a crossbow switch shines.

BiS armor to finish

  • Masori (Fortified) set (~205M) — paired with the Twisted Bow.
  • Torva set (~375M) — paired with the Scythe of Vitur.
  • Avernic Defender (~75M) — melee off-hand upgrade over the Dragon Defender.
  • Ultor + Venator + Magus rings (~260M total) — the Desert Treasure 2 rings covering all three styles.
  • Elidinis' Ward (f) (~55M) — best magic off-hand.
💡 One Mega-Rare Is Enough

Most endgame players never own all three mega-rares at once. Owning one (usually the Twisted Bow) plus BiS armor in your main style is the realistic "finished" account. The rest is flex and bragging rights, not progression.

7. 🔄 Money-Making-to-Gear Loops & Knowing When to Stop

The route is not a one-way ladder you climb by grinding — it is a loop. You use your current kit to make GP, then spend that GP to reach the next stage, which makes GP faster, which funds the next stage. Understanding the loop is what keeps you from getting stuck. The diagram below shows the cycle; the numbered steps beneath it turn it into a daily routine.

① Pick your money maker for the current stage

In Stage 1 that is high-value Slayer and Vorkath; in Stage 2 it is Vorkath plus Zulrah plus entry CoX; in Stage 3 it is raids and Nex. Match the activity to the kit you already own — do not chase a boss you cannot yet kill. Our bossing money-making guide ranks the best activities per stage.

② Reinvest, do not bank

The trap is letting GP sit. Every unused million is a missed stage. Set a rule: when you cross a purchase threshold, buy the next route item immediately. Liquidity only matters as a buffer for the next planned upgrade.

③ Upgrade, then return to the loop

After each purchase, your DPS rises, so your GP per hour rises, so the next item comes faster. This compounding is why mid-game accounts accelerate so hard — the loop feeds itself once Stage 2 weapons land.

When to stop upgrading

The route has a trap at the end: players with 2B GP keep "optimizing" instead of enjoying the game. Here is how to know you are done:

  • You clear your target content comfortably. If Theatre of Blood deaths are rare and Tombs of Amascut invocations are high, your gear is sufficient. More GP buys minutes, not access.
  • Your bottleneck is skill, not DPS. When missed prayers and bad movement cause more deaths than slow kills, gear is not your problem.
  • You already own one mega-rare + BiS in your style. That is a finished PvM account by any reasonable standard.
  • The next upgrade is under ~3% DPS. An Avernic over a Dragon Defender is ~3–4%. Nice, not necessary.
⚠️ Avoid the Sidegrade Spiral

Do not sell a working item to "try" a sidegrade that the calculator says is +1%. You will lose liquidity and gain nothing measurable. Buy only clear, staged upgrades that unlock new content or a real DPS tier.

At that point, redirect your energy (and GP) into collection logs, boss pets, and the content you actually find fun. The route got you here; the game keeps you here. 8. ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

How long does the full route take?

It depends entirely on how you fund it. A player who learns efficient money makers (Vorkath, Tombs of Amascut, Chambers of Xeric) can reach a pre-raid kit in a few focused months, and a single mega-rare within a year. Casual players take longer, but the route is identical — stages, not speed. The bottleneck is almost always stats and quests, not GP.

Should I follow the route for all three styles at once?

No. Go deep on one style first (usually melee, since it opens the most content), then ranged, then magic. Spreading GP thin leaves you mediocre at everything and slows every later stage. A Fang plus Rigour plus a Torso clears far more than a half-built kit in all three styles.

What if I can't afford Rigour and Augury yet?

Train Prayer to 74 and farm the scrolls — they are the highest-priority purchase in the entire route. Until then, even best-in-slot weapons underperform, because the +25% strength and +20% accuracy are multipliers on everything you wield. Prayers are gear. Budget for the ~120M before any Stage 2 armor.

Is the Infernal Cape part of the route or endgame only?

It belongs in Stage 3. The Infernal Cape is +8 strength over the Fire Cape — a larger melee jump than most armor upgrades — and it unlocks solo content that funds Stage 4. Attempt it as soon as you have the Fang, Rigour, and a Torso. It is untradeable, so treat it as a quest-like milestone, not a purchase.

Twisted Bow or Scythe of Vitur first?

Twisted Bow first unless you mainly do the Theatre of Blood or Phosani's. The Tbow covers more content (Inferno, CoX, Zulrah, God Wars, Slayer) and is the more flexible mega-rare. The Scythe is stronger in its niches but less universal, so it is the better second mega-rare for most accounts.

Why does the route ignore armor until Stage 2?

Because armor mostly adds Defence and a small strength bonus, while weapons and prayers multiply your damage output. In Old School RuneScape, kills-per-hour — and therefore GP per hour — scale with DPS far more than with survivability. You already survive fine with Protect Prayers and good food. Spend Stage 1 money on the Whip, Blowpipe, and Trident, not on a chestplate.

Can an Ironman follow this route?

Yes, but the loop changes shape. Instead of buying with GP, you obtain items through bosses and collectibles. The stage order is the same — free unlocks first, then weapons and prayers, then armor — but the "money maker" step becomes "farming the drop source." The priority of weapons and prayers over armor is even more important for an Ironman, since every item is a time investment.

What is the single best first big purchase after Stage 0?

The Abyssal Whip, followed immediately by the Toxic Blowpipe. Together they cost under 6M GP and triple your kill speed across melee and ranged content, which directly accelerates every later stage. Do not skip them for a "cooler" item — the Whip and Blowpipe are the backbone of Stage 1.

How do I know which boss I am ready for?

Use the per-boss readiness checkpoints in Stage 3 as your gate. If the checkpoint says "Bowfa + Rigour" and you have both, you are ready for CoX. If it says "Fang + Rigour + Torso" and you have them, you are ready for the Inferno. The checkpoints are weapon-and-prayer gates, never pure GP gates, because that is what actually determines success.

Is it worth buying Bandos before the prayer scrolls?

No. Bandos adds strength bonus, but Rigour and Augury add a percentage multiplier to all your ranged and magic damage on every weapon forever. A 120M prayer investment outperforms a 47M armor investment by a wide margin on the content you are doing in Stage 2. Buy the scrolls first, Bandos last in the stage.

✅ Final Tips

  • Unlock before you buy. Stats, quests, and prayers gate more content than GP ever will.
  • Weapons and prayers first, every stage. They out-DPS armor by 3–5x per GP spent.
  • One style deep, then the next. Depth beats breadth for both fun and GP per hour.
  • Run the loop. Reinvest GP the moment you cross a threshold; idle banked GP is a stalled account.
  • Know when you are done. A finished kit is one mega-rare + BiS in your style — not all three.
