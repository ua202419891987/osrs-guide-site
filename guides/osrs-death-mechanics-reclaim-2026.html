OSRS Death Mechanics & Reclaim 2026 — Gravestones, Death's Office & the Keep Rules

Updated August 19, 2026  |  15 min read  |  By Yanchun Wu
🛡️ Level: All Levels

Dying in Old School RuneScape is rarely the end of your account — but it can be the end of your bank if you misunderstand the rules. The death system has three moving parts that trip up even experienced players: the gravestone timer, Death's Office where your dropped items go, and the reclaim fee that scales with how valuable your stuff was. On top of that, the "keep" rules change completely inside the Wilderness and during dangerous PvP. This guide lays out exactly what happens the moment your hitpoints hit zero, how to get your items back for the lowest cost, and the edge cases that turn a recoverable death into a permanent loss. Pair it with our boss death-prevention guide so you die less in the first place.

Most "I lost everything" posts are players who let the gravestone expire or entered a dangerous zone without realising the keep rules changed. Read this once and you will never make that mistake. Whether you are a brand-new Ironman, a maxed bosser, or a skiller who only steps into the Wilderness for a birdhouse, the same three systems decide whether a death costs you nothing, costs you a fee, or costs you your whole bank.

1. 💀 What Happens the Moment You Die

When your hitpoints reach zero outside of a safe minigame, the game resolves your death in one of three ways depending on where you are and what you were doing. Understanding which of the three buckets you fall into is the foundation of everything else in this guide, because the recovery path is completely different for each one.

Safe deaths keep everything

Some content is flagged "safe" — your items are kept and you respawn with nothing lost. This includes most minigames (Pest Control, Castle Wars, Fishing Trawler), the Nightmare Zone, Clan Wars (non-risk), and certain instanced boss entrances where the lobby is safe. If a death is safe, there is no gravestone, nothing drops, and nothing goes to Death's Office. Always confirm whether content is safe before treating a death as free practice — a safe death teaches you the fight with zero downside, which is exactly why safe practice modes exist.

Normal deaths drop a gravestone

In a normal PvM or skilling death (anything in the overworld that is not the Wilderness and not flagged safe), your items drop into a gravestone on your death tile. You are teleported to your respawn point (usually Lumbridge for members, or your last checkpoint), and a countdown begins. Nothing is deleted. You have a real, recoverable window to walk back and collect everything.

Wilderness and PvP deaths are lootable

In the Wilderness, or in any PvP-enabled zone, your gravestone can be opened by other players almost immediately, and the keep rules collapse. This is the bucket where "reclaim" stops being a guarantee and becomes a hope. We cover this in full in Section 6, but the headline is: treat a Wilderness death as a total loss when you plan, because for the valuable items it usually is.

The death tile matters

Your gravestone appears where you died. If you died deep in a Wilderness cave or inside a multi-way PvP zone, returning to it is itself dangerous. Plan your recovery route before you log out or teleport away. A gravestone sitting in a dangerous spot can become a trap: you run back to save your gear and die again on the way, doubling the loss.

💡 First Rule of Dying

Do not panic-teleport. Note your location, then decide whether collecting the gravestone is safe. Sometimes waiting out the timer and reclaiming from Death's Office is cheaper and safer than walking back through danger — especially if the death tile is surrounded by aggro or enemies.

2. ⏳ The Gravestone Timer

The gravestone is your first and cheapest recovery option. As long as it stands, anyone (including you) can click it to retrieve everything that dropped — for free. The moment it vanishes, your items are moved to Death's Office and a fee attaches. So the entire gravestone phase is a clock you are racing, and winning that race is free.

How long does it last?

The base timer is roughly one hour, but it can be shorter in dangerous areas or in certain PvP contexts. The single most important fact: the timer keeps counting even while you are logged out. Logging off does not pause it, sleeping does not pause it, and being offline for "just a quick break" does not pause it. Come back two hours later and the stone is gone — your items are at Death's Office and a fee is now due.

Who can loot your gravestone?

This is the key split between normal and Wilderness deaths. Outside the Wilderness, generally only you (and trusted friends, depending on your gravestone settings) can open it. The gravestone protects you from instant looting in PvM, which is why almost every boss death in the overworld is fully recoverable if you act in time. Inside the Wilderness, other players can loot your gravestone after a short delay — sometimes seconds — which is the core Wilderness risk and the reason reclaim is unreliable there.

When the timer expires

Once the gravestone vanishes, everything in it is transported to Death's Office in Edgeville. It is not destroyed — it is held for you, behind a fee. This safety net is what makes almost every normal PvM death recoverable. The trade-off is the fee, which we cover in Section 4. Think of the gravestone as the free window and Death's Office as the paid backup.

⚠️ The Timer Does Not Pause

Many players lose items because they assumed logging out froze the gravestone. It does not. Treat the timer as real-world time, always running. If you cannot get back to the stone within the hour, plan to pay Death instead — and make sure you will have the coins to do it.

Gravestone vs Death's Office at a glance

The table below sums up the practical difference between the two recovery stages. Internalise it and you will always know which option you are using and what it costs.

Factor Gravestone (before expiry) Death's Office (after expiry)
Cost to recover Free Fee based on total GE value
Who can access You (and trusted friends outside Wildy) You only, by talking to Death
Time limit ~1 hour, counts while logged out Held for an extended period
Wilderness looting Other players can open it fast Killer usually already took the drops

3. 🏛️ Death's Office: Where Items Go

Death's Office sits just north of Edgeville, run by the character Death. When your gravestone expires, your items are delivered here automatically. You do not need to "find" them scattered across the map — they are waiting in one place, priced as a bundle.

What Death holds for you

Death stores the items that dropped on death, minus whatever you kept via the keep rules (Section 5) and minus anything a Wilderness looter already took. Talk to Death and he shows you the list. You then choose to reclaim everything (by paying the fee) or abandon it permanently. There is no partial reclaim of "only the cheap stuff" without paying for the whole value bracket — Death prices the reclaim on the total value of what he is holding, which is why the fee can feel large after a big death.

Reclaiming vs abandoning

You can reclaim items by paying the fee (next section), or you can abandon them permanently to clear the office. Abandoning is sometimes the right call — if the fee is higher than the gear is worth, or if the items are easily replaced — but do it deliberately, not by accident. Many players have abandoned a stack they meant to reclaim because they clicked past the dialogue. Read the prompt.

How long does Death keep items?

Death holds your items for an extended period, effectively until you deal with them. Do not treat it as infinite, though. Reclaim promptly so you do not forget what was there, and so a valuable item is not sitting in limbo while you decide. If you are short on cash for the fee, bank what you can and come back the moment you have coins — the office will still have it.

💡 Edgeville Is the Hub

Because Death is in Edgeville, keep a teleport there ready after any risky trip — an Edgeville teleport tab, a burning amulet, or a lit spellbook option. Fast reclaim means a fast return to farming. Our inventory prep guide covers where to keep that teleport so it is always one click away.

4. 💰 The Reclaim Fee Explained

This is the part players underestimate, and it is why a "free" recovery turns into a real cost. Death does not return your items for free once the gravestone is gone — he charges a fee based on the combined value of what he is holding.

How the fee is calculated

The reclaim fee scales with the total Grand Exchange value of the items in his care. A cheap death — a few thousand GP of skilling gear — costs a few thousand to reclaim. A bank's worth of boss gear can cost millions. The fee is paid in coins directly to Death at the moment of reclaim. If you cannot pay, you cannot reclaim — so always keep a cash stack, or a way to get one fast, before any risky trip.

Why reclaiming from the gravestone is free

If you reach your gravestone before it expires, there is no fee — you simply collect your items. This is why racing back to the stone (when the tile is safe) is almost always better than letting it expire and paying Death. The fee exists only as the penalty for missing the timer, not as a toll on death itself.

Reducing the fee

The only way to reduce the fee is to reclaim fewer valuable items — but Death prices the bundle, not à la carte. Practically, the best "reduction" is to never let the stone expire. For carried cash, remember: coins on death follow the keep rules, so you often keep some GP and can use it toward the fee. If you are skulled in the Wilderness, however, that cash is usually gone too, which is exactly why a Wilderness death is so punishing.

Paying when broke

If you die with your whole bank and cannot afford the fee, you are stuck in a loop. The fix is prevention: keep a small emergency cash stash in a separate bank tab, or on an alt, or in a money pouch / looting bag appropriate to the content. Never carry your entire liquid net worth into a dangerous trip where a fee could strand you. If you do get stranded, the fastest unstick is usually a small money drop from a friend or clanmate so you can pay Death and recover the rest.

Illustrative fee brackets

The exact fee is a percentage of total held value, but the direction is what matters: the more you were carrying, the more it costs to get back. The table below shows the shape of the fee for typical death sizes — treat the numbers as illustrative ranges, not a fixed price list, because Jagex tunes the percentage over time.

Total value held by Death Typical reclaim fee Practical takeaway
Under ~100K GP A few thousand GP Cheap — reclaim almost always worth it
~100K – 1M GP Tens of thousands Recoverable from a normal cash stack
~1M – 10M GP Hundreds of thousands Keep a dedicated reclaim fund
~10M – 100M GP Millions Only worth it if gear is hard to replace
100M+ GP (bis banks) Tens of millions Avoid this death entirely — prevention first
💡 The Fee Is a Timer Tax

Think of the reclaim fee as a tax on missing your gravestone. Every death you recover from the stone directly is a fee you never pay. The single highest-value habit in this entire guide is: when the death tile is safe, run back to the stone first and only fall back to Death's Office when you must.

5. 🎒 The "Keep" Rules: What You Keep on Death

Not everything drops when you die. OSRS uses a "keep" system that decides which items stay on your character and which hit the ground (or the gravestone). Understanding it is the difference between losing three items and losing your whole bank — and it is the system most often misunderstood by returning players.

The baseline: keep 3, protect 1

In a normal (non-Wilderness, non-PvP) death, you keep the three most valuable items automatically, plus a fourth if you have the Protect Item prayer active. The kept items are chosen by value, so your best gear usually survives even a bad death. If you are carrying a massive bank, the keep-3 still only saves your top three by value — the rest drops to the gravestone and follows the reclaim rules above.

Protect Item prayer

Activating the Protect Item prayer (requires 25 Prayer, no quest) adds one extra kept item — taking you from 3 kept to 4 kept. For bossing and any risky PvM, always have it on if there is any death risk. It is the cheapest insurance in the game: a single prayer point drip that can save your best weapon. Our Protect Item & Smite guide explains exactly how the prayer interacts with Smite and with PvP death rules, because the Wilderness version behaves differently.

Skulled deaths

If you are skulled (from attacking another player, entering certain PvP, or some activities), the keep rules collapse: a skulled player keeps nothing on death, even with Protect Item, unless the specific activity overrides it. This is why skulling in the Wilderness is so punishing, and why the skull icon in your buff bar should set off alarm bells the moment you see it.

Forced keeplist

Certain items are always kept regardless of value — for example, some quest items, certain clue scrolls, and specific untradeables that the game protects. The exact list is long and occasionally updated, so the practical rule is: do not assume a "safe-looking" item is auto-kept — verify for high-value one-offs before you risk them. Losing a protected quest item is recoverable through the right NPC, but it is a headache you can avoid by checking first.

Keep rules by scenario

The table below is the cheat sheet you should memorise. Read it before every dangerous trip.

Scenario Items kept Protect Item helps? Risk level
Normal overworld death Top 3 by value Yes → 4 kept Low (recoverable)
Safe minigame / instanced lobby Everything N/A None
Skulled (non-Wildy PvP) Nothing No High
Wilderness death Nothing (skulled) No Extreme
Forced-keeplist item That item always N/A Protected

Keep-Rules Quick Reference

  • Normal death: keep top 3 by value + 1 if Protect Item = 4 kept.
  • Skulled death: keep nothing (Protect Item does not help while skulled).
  • Wilderness: same as skulled, but looters can take your drops fast — see next section.
  • Forced keeplist: specific items always survive — verify before risking them.

6. ⚔️ Wilderness & Dangerous Deaths

The Wilderness and any PvP-enabled zone rewrite the rules. This is where "I'll just reclaim" stops being true, and where the majority of "I lost my whole bank" stories come from. If you take one thing from this guide, let it be this section.

Wilderness death = lootable fast

In the Wilderness, your gravestone can be looted by other players almost immediately after death. There is no long safe window. If you die to a PKer, assume your drops are gone within seconds unless you are deep and alone with no one around. The gravestone protection that makes overworld PvM safe simply does not apply here.

The skull and the risk

Most Wilderness activity skulls you, which removes your keep-3. So a Wilderness death typically costs everything you carried that was not on the forced-keeplist. This is the single biggest source of permanent losses. The deeper you go (higher Wilderness level), the more valuable the PKers hunting there, and the more complete the loss tends to be.

Death's Office still applies — but so does looting

Items not taken by the killer still go to Death's Office, and you can still reclaim them for a fee. But because the killer usually grabs the valuable drops first, what reaches Death is often the leftovers — the low-value junk no one wanted. Do not rely on reclaim in the Wilderness; rely on not dying there in the first place.

Staying safer in dangerous content

Use the world-hop PK-avoidance guide for boss content near the Wilderness (Kril, Callisto, Vet'ion, and similar), and the Wilderness survival guide for the habits that keep you alive. Bring only what you can afford to lose. The best Wilderness death is the one you never have — and the second best is the one where your risk was capped to a sliver of your bank.

⚠️ Wilderness Keep Rule

In the Wilderness, assume a death costs everything you brought. Plan every trip as if reclaim does not exist, because for the valuable items, it usually doesn't. If losing the stack would hurt, you are carrying too much.

7. 🔋 Reclaiming Charged & Broken Items

Some deaths involve items that were charged (God books, rings) or that break on death (Barrows equipment and similar). The reclaim behaviour differs from a plain dropped item, and misunderstanding it leads to two opposite mistakes: panicking that a broken item is gone, or assuming a charged item reclaims for free.

Charged items

Items that consume charges (a charged God book, a ring of wealth (i), a charged blowpipe, etc.) keep their remaining charges when they drop and when reclaimed. You do not lose the charge state — you just pay the standard reclaim on the item's value. The lesson: keep them charged so they are worth reclaiming; a dead, uncharged version is worth far less and may not be worth the fee.

Items that break on death

Several PvM drops break into a "broken" state on death (Barrows armour, the Ferocious gloves in some cases, certain weapons). A broken item is reclaimable but must be repaired — usually by an NPC such as Perdu, or with coins — before you can use it again. The repair cost is separate from Death's fee, so budget for both. Our charged-item-on-death fix guide walks through the exact NPCs and costs for each broken piece, and the Twisted Bow reclaim guide covers the scariest version: losing a top-tier untradeable and getting it back.

Broken = still yours

A broken item is not lost — it is just unusable until fixed. Players sometimes panic thinking a broken drop is gone; it is sitting in Death's Office waiting for the fee plus a repair. For Ironmen this matters even more, because untradeables cannot simply be rebought — the Ironman untradeable recovery guide explains Perdu's role in restoring destroyed and broken untradeables. Our gear checklist notes which boss drops break, so you know the repair cost before you fight.

💡 Budget Repair Into the Trip

If your setup includes break-on-death gear, keep an extra repair fee in your head. A death that breaks your Torag's and then costs a reclaim plus a repair is double the hit of a normal death. Know the Perdu price before you risk it.

8. 🚫 Common Death Mistakes to Avoid

These are the avoidable errors behind nearly every "permanent" loss. None of them are skill issues — they are process issues. Fix the process and you will almost never lose more than the keep-3.

Mistake 1: Logging out to "pause" the gravestone

It does not pause. Come back late and Death has your items — and a fee attached. If you must leave, decide before logging: can you reach the stone in time, or should you just accept the fee and make sure you have coins?

Mistake 2: Walking into the Wilderness with your whole bank

Skulled + lootable = total loss. Only carry what the trip needs. A Wilderness rune-saving trip does not require your Twisted Bow, and a boss kill near the Wildy does not require your full herb box. Strip the risk down to the minimum.

Mistake 3: Forgetting Protect Item

One extra kept item is free insurance. Forgetting it on a boss death can mean losing your best weapon instead of your boots. Make it a reflex: prayer on before the fight starts, not after the first hit lands.

Mistake 4: No cash to pay the reclaim fee

Dying with everything and no liquid coins traps you. Keep a cash buffer in a separate tab or on an alt. A small emergency stack is the difference between a recovered bank and a stranded one.

Mistake 5: Assuming a death was "safe"

Check whether content is safe before treating deaths as free. A wrong assumption costs the keep-3 you thought you had. When in doubt, assume the death is real and plan accordingly.

Mistake 6: Relying on reclaim in the Wilderness

The leftover that reaches Death is rarely your best drop — the PKer took that. Plan Wilderness trips as total-loss events and only bring what you can replace. If you want the boss loot without the risk, use the safe boss PK-recovery guide to understand which content can be done without stepping into danger.

Pre-Death Checklist

  • Protect Item on (for risky PvM)?
  • Cash buffer for the fee if the stone expires?
  • Only carrying what you can afford to lose?
  • Know the keep rules for the zone (skulled or not)?
  • Edgeville / reclaim teleport ready?

9. ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Is my stuff deleted when I die?

No. On a normal death your items drop into a gravestone, and if its timer expires they go to Death's Office in Edgeville. They are held for you — recoverable for a fee — not deleted. Only Wilderness looting or deliberate abandonment makes them truly gone.

How much does Death charge to reclaim?

The reclaim fee scales with the total Grand Exchange value of the items Death is holding. Cheap deaths cost thousands; a valuable bank costs millions. Reclaiming from your gravestone before it expires is free, so the fee is purely a penalty for missing the timer.

How many items do I keep on a normal death?

You keep the three most valuable items automatically, plus a fourth if the Protect Item prayer is active. A skulled player (common in the Wilderness) keeps nothing. The kept items are always chosen by value, so your best gear is what survives.

What happens if I die in the Wilderness?

Wilderness deaths are lootable almost immediately and a skull usually removes your keep-3, so you typically lose everything you carried. Any unlooted drops still go to Death's Office, but rely on reclaim only for the leftovers. Treat Wilderness deaths as total losses when planning.

Do broken items on death stay recoverable?

Yes. Items that break on death (like Barrows armour) drop in broken form and are reclaimable from Death, then repairable for a separate fee via Perdu or coins. A broken item is not lost — just unusable until fixed. Ironmen should read the untradeable recovery guide, because broken untradeables cannot be rebought.

Does logging out pause the gravestone timer?

No. The timer counts real-world time whether you are online or not. If you cannot get back within roughly an hour, assume the stone will expire and make sure you have coins for the Death's Office fee instead.

Can I reclaim only some of my items and skip the rest?

Death prices the reclaim on the total value he is holding, not item by item. You either reclaim the whole bundle by paying the fee, or abandon it. There is no à la carte option, which is why the fee can feel large after a big death.

What is a "safe" death and how do I know?

A safe death keeps all your items and drops nothing — common in minigames like Pest Control and Castle Wars, the Nightmare Zone, and certain instanced lobbies. If you are unsure whether content is safe, assume it is not and plan for a real death. Safe practice modes are the best place to learn a fight risk-free.

I lost an untradeable like the Twisted Bow — can I get it back?

If it dropped on a reclaimable death, yes: it goes to Death's Office like anything else, and our Twisted Bow reclaim guide covers the exact steps. If it was lost in the Wilderness to a looter, recovery depends on the specific item and Jagex's policies — check the relevant reclaim guide rather than assuming it is gone forever.

What should I do immediately after dying?

Note your death tile, check whether it is safe to reach, and decide: run back to the gravestone for a free reclaim, or head to Edgeville with coins for Death's Office. Never log out and assume the timer waits for you. If you were skulled in the Wildy, accept the loss and recover what you can from Death.

✅ Final Tips — Die Informed, Lose Little

  • Recover from the gravestone before it expires — that is the only free reclaim.
  • Keep death prevention habits so you rarely need to reclaim at all.
  • Always run Protect Item on risky trips for a 4th kept item.
  • Keep a cash buffer so you can pay Death's fee if the stone expires.
  • In the Wilderness, assume a death costs everything — only carry what you can lose.
  • Budget a separate repair fee for any break-on-death gear you risk.
