You sold your Twisted Bow for 1 GP on the Grand Exchange. You accepted a trade window that dropped a Scythe of Vitur for a junk item. You "gifted" a Shadow to the wrong friend. When the item is worth 1.0–3.5 billion GP, the panic is real — and time is everything.

Losing a billion-GP item in Old School RuneScape is the kind of mistake that can end a decade-long account. Whether it was a Grand Exchange mis-price, an accidental accept on the wrong trade window, or a "vanishing" item after a disconnect, the same rules apply — and the same panic sets in. Our charged-item recovery guide covers death-based losses, but a trade mistake is a different beast entirely because the item left your account by an action you triggered.

If you are reading this after the fact, stop and open your trade history right now. The single biggest factor in recovering a high-value item is how fast you document it. Players who lose gear in PvP or boss deaths can often reclaim it from Death's Office, but a traded-away item is gone the moment the other player logs out or moves it. Speed and proof are your only leverage.

This guide is built from real Jagex support policy, community recovery cases, and the经验教训 of players who lost (and sometimes recovered) items worth more than a used car. We also cover how an unauthorised hijack differs from a self-made mistake, because the account security playbook is your strongest recovery path when someone else moved your gear.

1. 💰 What Counts as a "Billion-GP Trade Mistake"?

Before you file anything, classify your loss. Jagex treats each scenario differently, and mis-classifying it is the fastest way to get an auto-denied ticket. Here are the categories we see most often at the billion-GP tier:

  • Grand Exchange mis-price: You listed a Twisted Bow / Scythe of Vitur / Tumeken's Shadow at 1 GP or a fraction of its value and it sold instantly to a sniper.
  • Wrong trade-window accept: You meant to trade a junk item but dragged in your best weapon, or you accepted a window that was not what you agreed on voice/chat.
  • "Gift" to wrong recipient: You dropped or traded an item to the wrong friend, clanmate, or alt.
  • Item vanished after disconnect / bug: The item disappeared from your bank or inventory with no trade log — this overlaps with our common bug fix guide.
  • Unauthorised access: A hijacker logged in and moved your items. This is not your mistake — it is a security incident and has the highest recovery rate.
💡 First Question Jagex Asks

"Did you, or someone you authorised, perform this trade?" If the honest answer is yes, the item was transferred with valid account credentials and is treated as a legitimate trade. If the answer is no — someone else did it — you are in unauthorised-access territory and your odds flip dramatically in your favour.

2. 🚫 The Harsh Truth: Jagex Rarely Reverses Trades

This is the section nobody wants to read, but it is the most important. Jagex's official position is that trades completed with valid account access are final. The economy of OSRS depends on trades being irreversible — if Jagex rolled back every "I meant to keep my Tbow" trade, the GE and player economy would collapse overnight.

Why "I made a mistake" is not a valid reason

Every day, thousands of players mis-click, mis-price, or trust the wrong person. If Jagex compensated even 1% of these, it would create a massive incentive to scam, insure losses through support, and manipulate the market. The policy is blunt: your trade, your responsibility.

The GE sniper reality

When you list a 2.5b Tbow at 1 GP, a market sniper (or bot) buys it in milliseconds. The buyer did nothing wrong — they bought an item at the listed price. Jagex will not punish a legitimate buyer to refund you. This is why "I sold it too cheap" almost never results in a return.

What this means for you

Accept the likely outcome early so you can focus your energy on the exceptions that actually have a chance. The rest of this guide is about identifying whether your case fits an exception — and how to present it so a human moderator, not an auto-reply, reads it.

⚠️ Do Not "Trade Back" With a Scammer

If someone contacts you claiming they can "refund" or "reverse" the trade for a fee, it is a recovery scam. Jagex moderators never ask for payment. Report the user and move on.

3. 🔄 The 3 Only Situations Jagex Will Consider

If your loss is a straight "I messed up" trade, close the tab. But three narrow situations can lead to a reversal or restoration. Know which one — if any — fits you.

Path A — Unauthorised account access (best odds)

If a hijacker traded your item away, Jagex can (and frequently does) restore items from server-side backups. They verify the access was unauthorised via login history, IP anomalies, and authenticator status. This is the only path with a genuinely high success rate for billion-GP losses. See our account recovery after hack guide for the exact flow.

Path B — Verified game bug or dupe exploit

If your item vanished due to a server-side bug (not user error), or you were a victim of a dupe exploit that Jagex is actively cleaning up, support may restore affected items. Proof is mandatory: timestamps, world, and ideally a clip. These are rare, time-boxed events.

Path C — Jagex-initiated error (rarest)

In exceptional cases where Jagex's own systems caused the loss (e.g., a rollback or update defect), they may compensate. This is not something you claim — Jagex usually announces compensation for affected accounts directly.

💡 The 1-Hour Rule of Thumb

For unauthorised access, file within the hour. The faster Jagex flags the session as compromised, the more likely your items are still on the hijacker's account and recoverable rather than already liquidated on the GE.

4. ✅ Recovery Checklist — Do These in Order

Panic wastes minutes. Run this checklist top to bottom. Each step takes under five minutes and multiplies your odds.

Step 1 — Screenshot everything immediately

Capture your trade history, the item's last-known location, your bank before/after, and the chat with the trade partner. If you already closed the client, open the %%USERPROFILE%%\.runescape or OSRS screenshot folder. Time-stamped native screenshots beat descriptions.

Step 2 — Note the exact trade partner name

Write down the display name (with accents/cases) of whoever received the item. Jagex can only investigate a specific account. A vague "some guy in World 2" gets you nowhere.

Step 3 — Check if it was unauthorised access

Review recent logins in your Jagex account settings. Unknown IPs, a removed authenticator, or a changed email = hijack. If so, secure the account first (reset password, re-enable 2FA) then file under account recovery, not "lost item".

Step 4 — Open a support ticket (template below)

Do not DM J-Mods on social media — they cannot action account changes there. Use the official support site only.

Step 5 — Preserve, don't grind

Stop trading, dropping, or "testing" things. Every extra action muddies the log Jagex will review. Stay logged in on a safe world and wait for the response.

💡 Bank Pin Buys You Time

If you still have a bank PIN set, a hijacker cannot empty your bank instantly. The PIN delay is often the difference between losing one item and losing the whole account.

5. 📞 How to Contact Jagex & Pull Trade Logs

You cannot "pull your own trade logs" — the detailed ledger lives on Jagex's servers. What you can do is make their job trivial so a human opens your case.

Where to file

  • Unauthorised access: Account recovery / "hacked" path on the Jagex Support website.
  • Bug / missing item: "Bugs" → "In-game item or progress lost" category.
  • GE anomaly: "Grand Exchange" category — but set expectations: mis-pricing refunds are exceptional.

What Jagex retrieves server-side

Jagex can see the full trade ledger: who traded whom, the exact items and quantities, the timestamp, and the world. This is why naming the trade partner matters — they cross-reference both accounts. In unauthorised-access cases they also retrieve login IP history to prove the hijack.

How long logs are kept

Trade and login data is retained for a limited window (commonly referenced as roughly 30–90 days depending on type). File inside that window. A trade from six months ago with no security incident is almost certainly unrecoverable.

⚠️ One Ticket, One Case

Do not spam multiple tickets. Duplicate tickets get merged or auto-closed, and it signals impatience to a busy moderation queue. File once, with complete evidence, and wait.

💡 Honesty Beats Sob Stories

If it was your mistake, say so. Tickets that falsely claim "I was hacked" when you simply mis-priced an item get flagged, delay legitimate cases, and can earn a warning. State the truth and let the policy decide.

8. 🛡️ Prevent the Next Disaster

Recovery is a lottery; prevention is a guarantee. These habits cost nothing and have saved more billion-GP accounts than any support ticket.

Enable a bank PIN and a long logout timer

A bank PIN with a multi-minute delay stops instant emptying by hijackers and forces you to confirm every bulk move. Combine it with an authenticator — never just a password.

Use trade confirmations and double-check the window

Before you hit accept, read the trade window twice. For GE, always set price via the "compare" view and confirm the number of zeroes. A one-second double-check prevents a 2-billion-GP error.

Keep high-value items on a separate, locked tab

Our bank tags guide shows how to isolate your best gear so it never ends up in a casual trade or drop. Out of sight, out of the wrong window.

Trade only people you trust — verify in clan Discord

Impersonation is the #1 cause of "wrong recipient" losses. Confirm the exact display name in a trusted channel before moving anything worth more than a bond.

Log out, don't tab out, in risky situations

Unexpected disconnects during trades can desync your client. Safe habits reduce the chance an item "vanishes" mid-action — and if it does, our bug fix guide covers the first response.

9. ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Can Jagex reverse a Grand Exchange mis-price?

Almost never. The GE is a player-driven market; if you list an item at 1 GP and it sells, that is a valid transaction with a legitimate buyer. Jagex does not refund mis-prices except in extreme, announced anomalies.

My item vanished with no trade log — what now?

First rule out user error (wrong trade, drop, death). If you are certain no action moved it, file under "bug / item lost" with timestamps and screenshots. Server-side logs will show whether a trade occurred.

How fast must I act?

For unauthorised access, within the hour is ideal. For bugs, during the active window. Trade logs are retained only a limited time, so the sooner you file with evidence, the better.

Will Jagex tell me who got my item?

For security investigations they may act on the trade partner's account, but they will not hand you another player's personal details. Restoration to you is the usual outcome, not a name reveal.

Can I pay someone to reverse the trade?

No — and anyone offering to is scamming you. Jagex moderators never charge for recovery. Report such offers immediately.

What if I just mis-clicked and accepted the wrong trade?

Unfortunately that is a valid trade and will not be reversed. Focus on prevention going forward; the item is almost certainly gone.
