1. 🔧 What the Summer Sweep-Up 2026 Actually Changed

Before listing methods, it helps to understand exactly what moved. Misinformation after a big update is rampant, and panic-selling or abandoning a good method because of a rumor is how players lose money. Here is the honest breakdown of what the Sweep-Up touched, and just as importantly, what it did not.

Skilling loops took the hit

The headline change was to several passive and semi-AFK skilling methods. A few farming outputs, certain ore and woodcutting spots, some low-attention training loops, and a number of "passive" money methods had their per-hour yields reduced. The stated goal was to lower raw GP injection into the economy without punishing players who are actively engaged. In practice, long AFK mining routes, a couple of herb-run adjacents, and the laziest tree-and-burn strategies earned noticeably less than in spring 2026. If you were the kind of player who made GP by barely paying attention, the Sweep-Up was aimed squarely at you.

Bosses and PvM were untouched

No boss drop table was rebalanced in the Sweep-Up. Vorkath, Zulrah, Zalcano, Yama, and The Hueycoatl all drop the same loot they dropped in May. If your GP comes from killing things, your income is exactly where it was — which is why this list is dominated by active combat and minigame content. The update reinforced a truth the smartest money makers already knew: attention-based income is the most patch-proof income in OSRS.

The Grand Exchange ripple

Because some skilling inputs became slightly scarcer or more abundant, a few flip markets shifted. The core technique from a dedicated advanced flipping and stop-loss guide still applies, but you must re-margin-check after any balance patch. For the broader passive-income picture, track which loops survived and which got trimmed using a dedicated passive-income reference, which maps exactly which AFK methods were trimmed and which slipped through untouched.

💡 Don't Abandon Methods Prematurely

If a method you use was "nerfed" by 5–10% in the patch notes, it is almost certainly still fine. A 10% trim on a 2M GP/hr method is still 1.8M GP/hr — far above anything the update improved. Panic-switching to an untested method costs more than the nerf ever did. The real losers were the AFK skilling farmers, not the active bossers.

2. 📈 Why the Money Meta Exploded After the Sweep-Up (and the Bond Price Link)

The Sweep-Up did not happen in a vacuum. To understand why active money making is suddenly the hottest topic on the forums, you have to look at two forces colliding at once: a supply cut from the patch, and a demand shock from the Bond market.

The supply side: passive GP got quieter

When Jagex trims AFK skilling margins, it removes GP from the top of the economy. Less passive GP means fewer items being mass-produced and dumped on the Grand Exchange. For active money makers, that is quietly good news: the loot you farm is now a larger share of a slightly tighter market, and the inputs you buy (food, potions, runes) are often cheaper because the skilling bots and AFK farmers are producing less of everything.

The demand side: the Bond price surged again

Here is the part most guides skip. The Bond Price Surge 2026 analysis shows the in-game Bond price climbing to a new all-time high this summer. Because Bonds are bought with real money and sold for GP, a rising Bond price means one of two things for you: either membership is getting more expensive in GP terms, or GP is getting cheaper in real terms. Either way, the GP/hr you can produce now determines how many hours of membership your playtime buys. A player earning 500K GP/hr effectively works more hours per Bond than a player earning 3M GP/hr. The Sweep-Up cut the lazy earners; the Bond surge raised the bar for everyone. The combined effect is that efficient active methods are more valuable than at any point this year.

Why this is good for smart players

Put the two together and the conclusion is clear: the gap between a good method and a great method widened. Twenty hours of Vorkath at 3M/hr buys you far more membership than twenty hours of a trimmed AFK loop. The meta did not just shift — it rewarded effort more than ever. If you want the full mid-game picture, our Mid-Game Money Roadmap 2026 shows how to ladder from your first bond into a self-sustaining account.

⚠️ The Trap: Chasing the Bond

Do not let Bond anxiety push you into dangerous methods. A skulled Wilderness death can cost more GP in one mistake than a week of "slow" safe bossing earns. Efficiency matters, but survivability matters more when your whole bank is on the line. Pick the best safe method you can do, and ladder up from there.

3. 📊 How We Ranked: GP/hr, Requirements, Risk

Every number below is a realistic range, not a world-record speedrun by a maxed specialist — it is what a competent player who knows the method and has appropriate gear can expect. Three factors drive the ranking, and you should weight them differently depending on your account and your stomach for risk.

GP/hr (the headline)

Your net profit per hour after consumables (food, potions, runes) but before any Grand Exchange tax on flipped loot. Bossing rates assume you sell loot efficiently and that you are not sitting on dead inventory; skilling rates assume current August 2026 prices. We always quote a range because luck, competition, and your own execution swing the result.

Requirements (the barrier)

The combat levels, quests, and skills you need before a method is available. A method needing 85 Slayer is "Intermediate+" — harder to reach but higher ceiling. One open at 27 Runecraft is far more accessible and is where most new members should start.

Risk (the catch)

Most methods here are in safe areas (no item loss, no PvP). Where a method carries risk — Wilderness adjacency, expensive deaths, volatile markets — we flag it clearly so you can decide if the GP/hr is worth the stomach-churn. Risk is not just "can I die"; it is "what is the worst single-hour outcome and can my bank survive it."

🎯 The Three Questions Before Any Method

  1. Can I do it? Check requirements against your account honestly, not aspirationally.
  2. Can I afford to fail at it? Death costs, supply costs, bind upgrades, and bad-luck streaks.
  3. Will I enjoy it for hours? The best GP/hr method is the one you will not quit after 20 minutes of boredom.

6. ⚔️ Bossing Money Makers (Vorkath, Zulrah, Zalcano, Yama, Hueycoatl)

Bossing is where the highest active GP/hr lives, and none of it was touched by the Sweep-Up. These five are the most relevant high-tier bosses for an intermediate-to-advanced account in August 2026. Each entry gives you GP/hr, the exact gear and levels, a step-by-step loop, and the risk label.

🐉 Vorkath — 2.0M–3.5M GP/hr (The King)

Requirements: 75 Ranged (higher is better), 70+ Prayer for Rigour, and a Dragon Hunter Crossbow (DHCB) or Dragon Hunter Lance. A Twisted Bow helps on the acid phase.

Setup: DHCB, elite void or armadyl, crossbow shield, ranging potion, prayer potions, food (sharks or better), anti-dragon shield, and a house teleport or games necklace for fast banking.

Steps: (1) Bank at the nearest bank and load your presets. (2) Use the boat to Rellekka and enter Vorkath's lair. (3) Activate Rigour, pray Protect From Magic, and start the kill — DD during the acid phase, pray Mage, and use the crossbow switch. (4) Loot, bank, repeat. With good DPS you push 30+ kills per hour. (5) Sell bones and hides on the GE or alch the head.

Risk: Safe (no item loss on death). The only real cost is supplies — roughly 150K–300K GP/hr in pots and food, which you subtract from gross. Loot is dominated by superior dragon bones, blue dragonhide, alchable Vorkath's head, and rare uniques.

👹 Yama — 1.8M–3.2M GP/hr (The New Endgame)

Requirements: 90+ Combat, a completed quest line, and genuinely endgame gear (high-tier melee or ranged, plenty of supplies). This is the hardest on the list but also one of the best.

Setup: Best-in-slot gear you can afford, super combat and prayer potions, brews and restores, and a clear understanding of the enrage mechanics. If you are unsure what to bring, our Yama Gear Requirements 2026 guide lists exact setups per budget.

Steps: (1) Enter the instance and learn the rotation on a low-enrage attempt. (2) During the fight, manage the mechanics precisely — the DPS check is real and punishes sloppy play. (3) Bank between kills via the quick-respawn. (4) Sell the unique drops; even the common loot floor is strong.

Risk: Safe, but the supply bill is the highest on this list — brews, restores, and bolts can eat 400K–700K GP/hr. Only worth it once your gear is good enough to clear fast. If your DPS is low, the cost per kill dwarfs the payout.

🐍 Zulrah — 1.8M–3.0M GP/hr (The Teacher)

Requirements: 75 Ranged and 75 Magic, a Toxic Blowpipe or Bow of Faerdhinen, and a couple of magic switches.

Setup: Blowpipe with dragon/adamant darts, a magic switch (trident or sceptre), ranging and magic potions, food, and anti-poison. Learn the rotation — most deaths come from mis-clicks on the phase switch.

Steps: (1) Teleport to Zul-Andra. (2) Run the rotation: Ranged phases with the blowpipe, Magic phases with your switch. (3) Dodge the clouds and tendrils. (4) Loot and bank. Comfortable players hit 20–25 kills per hour at roughly 80K–120K GP per kill (scales, snake hides, uniques).

Risk: Safe. The serp helm drop is the jackpot; the rest is steady. Supply cost is modest, around 100K–200K GP/hr.

⛏️ Zalcano — 1.5M–2.5M GP/hr (The Easiest of the High Earners)

Requirements: completion of the relevant entry quest line and around 70+ combat with decent gear. It is almost entirely a skilling fight.

Setup: A pickaxe, decent melee or ranged gear, food, and prayer potions. No special weapon needed.

Steps: (1) Enter the arena. (2) Mine the golem, chip it during vulnerable windows, and dodge tephra. (3) Collect the loot chest. (4) Repeat. There is no prayer-draining and almost no food pressure.

Risk: Safe and low-stress. Refined ores, zircon, and the chance at the ancient icon make this a comfortable earner — the best "first big boss" for an intermediate account.

🐲 The Hueycoatl — 1.2M–2.0M GP/hr (The Group Friendly)

Requirements: a completed quest and around 70+ combat. It is designed to be done in a group, which lowers the gear bar.

Setup: Ranged or melee gear, food, and prayer potions. Group worlds make this far easier than solo.

Steps: (1) Join a group instance. (2) Handle your assigned mechanic (typically Ranged DPS on the head). (3) Loot the shared and personal rewards. (4) Re-queue. Group play pushes you toward the high end of the range.

Risk: Safe. Slightly lower GP/hr than the solo bosses but far more forgiving on gear — a great bridge between Zalcano and Vorkath.

💡 Gear Before Grind

For all five bosses, the jump from "okay gear" to "good gear" is worth more than any method-switch. A DHCB or a blowpipe pays for itself in a single session. Budget for upgrades using our Budget Gear 2026 guide before wasting hours in sub-optimal setups.

7. 🌊 Skilling & Minigame Money Makers (Tempoross, GotR, Wintertodt, Herbiboar, NMZ, Tanning, Karambwan)

If bossing is not your style, these seven active methods survived the Sweep-Up completely intact and remain excellent GP/hr for their requirement level. Most also train a skill while they earn — the best "two birds" content in the game.

🌊 Tempoross — 250K–450K GP/hr

Requirements: 35 Fishing to start, but the rate climbs sharply with level and better tools (cuelebra, fish barrel).

Steps: Travel to the Isle of Souls, fill the empty buckets at the water, fuel the brazier, and catch/clean the fish. At 60+ Fishing with the barrel, 250K–450K/hr is realistic, and you train Fishing at the same time. Spirit flakes and seaweed are the bulk of the value.

🔮 Guardians of the Rift (GotR) — 200K–600K GP/hr

Requirements: just 27 Runecraft. The standout low-requirement winner.

Steps: Enter the minigame, mine fragments, craft the guardian stones, and channel energy. You train Runecraft fast and earn talisman pieces, pouches, runes, and rare uniques. The active version (actually participating, not leeching) earns 200K–600K/hr depending on level. Post-Sweep-Up nothing changed, making it the best entry point for newer members.

🔥 Wintertodt — 200K–400K GP/hr

Requirements: 50 Firemaking.

Steps: Burn logs, fletch, and repair, banking seeds, herbs, and the valuable reward crate (which can contain dragon axes, torstol seeds, and lamp XP). Active participation pushes 200K–400K/hr while training Firemaking. The herbs from crates remain a steady income stream, and Herblore is a natural pairing — see a dedicated Herblore 1-99 guide if you want to process the herbs yourself instead of selling them.

🐗 Herbiboar — 200K–500K GP/hr

Requirements: 80 Hunter with relevant Song of the Elves progress.

Steps: Track the boar through the Withered Lands following the spore trail, then harvest it. Each catch yields a herb sack worth 20K–60K GP in grimy herbs plus Hunter XP. Active, engaging, and safe.

🛡️ Nightmare Zone (NMZ) — 150K–400K GP/hr

Requirements: high combat stats and a cheap Dharok set.

Steps: With the Dharok + absorption strategy, tank bosses, heal with absorptions, and collect herb and seed drops plus points. At 70+ combat it is reliable 150K–400K/hr and doubles as combat XP — perfect for "watch a show while playing" sessions.

🟢 Dragonhide Tanning — 300K–700K GP/hr

Requirements: 40 Crafting and access to Port Phasmatys.

Steps: Buy green/blue/red dragonhides, tan them at the local tanner, and sell the leather for a per-hide profit. Using the "swap" method this is a fast, safe, click-intensive method earning 300K–700K/hr depending on margins. It pairs well with the flipping technique if you flip the hides directly — covered in a dedicated advanced flipping guide.

🐟 Karambwan Fishing — 150K–300K GP/hr

Requirements: 65 Fishing in Tai Bwo Wannai.

Steps: Fish karambwans; they are used in PvM food combos (especially with sharks for the double-click eat), so demand is constant. Expect 150K–300K/hr while training Fishing. Safe, simple, and a good filler between boss trips. If you are building a Fletching or Herblore alt on the side, both pair naturally — a dedicated Fletching 1-99 guide is a productive use of the downtime between fishing runs.

9. 🚨 High-Variance Boss Traps — The Dedicated Warning

This section deserves its own heading because it is where more intermediate players lose money than anywhere else. "High variance" bosses are the ones whose advertised GP/hr looks spectacular but whose actual take-home depends on rare drops that may not show up for dozens or hundreds of kills. Our High-Variance Boss Money Traps 2026 guide goes deeper, but here is the core warning.

The advertised rate is a weighted average, not your rate

When a guide says "Zulrah is 2.5M GP/hr," that number includes the serp helm and the tiny chance of a lucrative unique. If you do fifty kills without a unique, your real rate might be 1.2M GP/hr — and that is normal, not unlucky. Players quit high-variance bosses convinced they are "doing it wrong" when they are just experiencing the variance the average already accounted for.

Death cost compounds the variance

Most bosses on our list are safe, so a mistake costs time. But the moment you step into a risk-mounted or higher-pressure encounter, a single death can erase ten hours of profit. The trap is treating a safe boss's GP/hr as if it transfers to a riskier one. It does not, because the risk-adjusted rate is much lower.

The "one big drop will fix it" fallacy

Chasing a rare drop is closer to gambling than to money making. If your bank cannot absorb a 200-kill dry streak, you should not be in that boss — full stop. Pick a method whose floor (the worst realistic hour) you can survive, not one whose ceiling you are fantasizing about.

⚠️ The Variance Checklist

Before any high-variance boss, answer: (1) Can my bank survive 100 kills with zero rares? (2) Is my DPS high enough that supplies don't eat the floor? (3) Am I tracking the rolling average, not the last drop? If you answer no to any, drop down to Zalcano or GotR until your bank is ready.

10. 🟢🔴 Stable Low-Risk vs High-Risk High-Reward

Every money maker sits somewhere on a spectrum between "boring but reliable" and "exciting but swingy." Knowing which bucket you are in prevents the two classic mistakes: over-reaching for risk you cannot afford, and under-reaching out of fear you do not need.

Stable low-risk (the backbone)

Vorkath, Zulrah, Zalcano, Hueycoatl, GotR, Tempoross, Wintertodt, Herbiboar, NMZ, tanning, and karambwan are all safe. Your worst hour is "a bit slow"; your best hour is "a bit lucky." The variance is tiny, so your hourly rate is predictable enough to plan around. This is the category that builds banks. Run these until a better safe method unlocks.

High-risk high-reward (the accelerator)

Wilderness methods — Revenants, Wilderness Slayer, risky resource gathering — can print 500K–2.0M GP/hr, but a single PK death can cost your whole inventory, your best gear, and your will to continue. Treat this category as an accelerator, not a foundation. Only enter it once your bank can shrug off a total loss and once your PvP awareness is real. The dedicated Wilderness money & risk guide is required reading before you risk a single item there.

How to choose

Rule of thumb: if losing your equipped gear would set your account back more than a week, you are not ready for the red zone. Stay green. The green zone's top method (Vorkath) already out-earns most Wilderness methods' average once you factor in deaths and lost time. Risk only pays when you can afford to lose.

11. 🤖 Post-Bot-Ban: Why the Old Meta Died (and Why This One Survives)

Early 2026 saw some of the largest bot banwaves in OSRS history. The economy is still digesting the aftermath, and understanding it explains why the methods in this guide are unusually strong right now. Our Post-Bot-Ban Money Meta 2026 guide covers the macro view; here is what it means for you.

What the bans removed

Bots were responsible for a huge share of the supply of certain commodities — processed resources, farmed bosses' common loot, and some flipped goods. When they vanished, that supply dried up overnight. Prices on many items rose because real demand met reduced supply. For a human player actually doing the content, that means your loot sells for more and your competition for spawns dropped.

Why the old "lazy" meta died

The old meta rewarded scale, not skill: run a dozen accounts on AFK loops, flood the GE, undercut everyone. The bans removed the scale. The Sweep-Up then removed the margins on the laziest loops. Together, they ended the era where you could make GP by doing almost nothing. The new meta rewards the player who shows up, pays attention, and executes a real method.

Why active methods are the winner

Bossing and minigames were never bot-dominated in the way AFK skilling was, so they kept their value through the bans and gained relative value through the Sweep-Up. That is the entire thesis of this guide: in August 2026, the most reliable GP is the GP you earn by playing the game properly. The post-bot-ban economy is, for once, friendly to the legitimate player.

💡 Ride the Window

Elevated prices after bans do not last forever — as the economy rebalances, some margins compress. The time to build your bank with active methods is now, while the post-ban supply gap is still open. Lock in the gains before the market normalizes.

Different accounts need different paths up the GP pyramid. Here are three concrete ladders you can follow in August 2026.

Step 3: Zalcano then Vorkath for rare drops you cannot trade for. Step 4: Use boss drops to fund your own Herblore and Fletching via dedicated Herblore and Fletching guides. 🎯 The Universal Rule

No matter your account type, always run the highest safe method your stats already unlock. Do not grind a low method hoping to "save up" for a better one when you could be grinding the better one directly. The exception is buying gear — a DHCB is worth more than a hundred hours of Zalcano.

14. ⚠️ Common Pitfalls (GE Tax, PK, Volatility, Backlog)

Even safe methods have hidden costs and failure modes. Here are the four that quietly drain more banks than any nerf.

🏛️ Grand Exchange tax

Every GE sale above 100 GP incurs a tax (capped). High-volume flippers and bulk sellers feel this most. Always subtract the tax from your quoted GP/hr, and for big tickets, consider selling in smaller lots or using the "sell instantly vs wait" trade-off deliberately. The tax is small per item but real across thousands of transactions.

⚔️ Getting PKed

Only relevant in the red zone, but it is the single largest possible loss. One death in the Wilderness can cost more than a month of safe bossing. If you are in risky content, wear only what you can replace, keep Protect Item on, and treat every white dot on the minimap as a threat. Never carry your bossing gear into the Wilderness.

📉 Price volatility

Item prices move after every update, every banwave, and every content release. A method that earned 3M/hr in June may earn 2M/hr in August because its loot flooded the market. Re-check your key drop prices weekly, and never assume a rate from a guide is permanent. The advanced flipping stop-loss section explains how to cap your downside when you hold inventory.

📦 Inventory backlog

The trap nobody mentions: you farm 5,000 hides but only sell 200/day because of buy limits, so your "earned" GP is stuck as dead stock. Backlog ties up capital and exposes you to price drops. Sell into the limit steadily, or process the items (tan them, fletch them) to lift the effective limit. A full warehouse and an empty coin pouch is not making money — it is hoarding risk.

⚠️ The Backlog Is a Silent Tax

If your unsold inventory is larger than one day of sales, you are not liquid. Liquidity is what lets you pivot when a method nerfs or a price moves. Keep your stock turning; the GP you cannot reach is not GP you have.

15. ❓ Frequently Asked Questions (12)

Did the Summer Sweep-Up 2026 nerf boss money makers?

No. The update targeted skilling and a few passive/semi-AFK loops. Boss drop tables — Vorkath, Zulrah, Zalcano, Yama, Hueycoatl, and every other PvM method — were not rebalanced. Their GP/hr is unchanged from before the patch, which is exactly why bossing tops this guide.

Why does the Bond price matter for money making?

Because a Bond converts real money into GP, a rising Bond price means GP is worth less in real terms, or membership costs more in GP. Either way, your GP/hr now determines how much membership your playtime buys. Higher efficient GP/hr protects you from the surge — full detail in our bond price surge analysis.

What is the best money maker with almost no requirements?

For members with low stats, Guardians of the Rift (27 Runecraft) and Wintertodt (50 Firemaking) are the most accessible high-value methods. For a fresh F2P or near-fresh member account, see a dedicated F2P first-bond speedrun guide for the zero-to-low requirement route that funds your first membership.

Is flipping still good after the Sweep-Up?

Yes, but margins shifted on several skilling inputs. The core technique in a dedicated advanced flipping guide is unchanged — just re-run your margin checks, because some items moved 5–15% in either direction after the patch, and the post-bot-ban supply gap made others temporarily hot.

Which method should I learn first as an intermediate?

If you have 70+ combat and decent gear, start with Zalcano — it is the lowest-stress high GP/hr boss and teaches you boss fundamentals. From there, push Ranged to 75 and graduate to Vorkath, the best consistent earner in the game.

Are any of these methods Wilderness-based?

Only the one high-risk entry in the table (Wilderness Slayer / Revenants). We include it for completeness, not as a recommendation, because the Sweep-Up did not make Wilderness safer. For a bank-building player, safe consistent GP beats a lucky but risky run. The dedicated guide is our Wilderness money & risk breakdown.

What is a high-variance boss trap and how do I avoid it?

It is a boss whose advertised GP/hr depends on rare drops that may not appear for hundreds of kills. Avoid it by checking whether your bank survives a 100-kill dry streak and by tracking your rolling average, not the last drop. Full breakdown in our high-variance boss money traps guide.

How did the bot banwaves change the money meta?

Bans removed a huge share of bot-driven supply, so many item prices rose and spawn competition dropped. Active methods — which were never bot-dominated — kept their value and gained relative value through both the bans and the Sweep-Up. The legitimate player is unusually favored right now; see our post-bot-ban money meta guide.

Should an Ironman use the same methods?

Mostly yes, but the goal is progress, not tradable GP. GotR, Wintertodt, Tempoross, Herbiboar, Zalcano, and Vorkath all give an Ironman rare drops and resources they cannot buy. Use boss drops to self-fund Herblore and Fletching instead of selling them.

How do I account for the Grand Exchange tax?

Every GE sale above 100 GP pays a small tax (capped at a maximum). Subtract it from your quoted GP/hr, and for high-volume selling, spread sales across days to respect buy limits and avoid moving the market against yourself. The tax is minor per item but adds up across thousands of transactions.

My inventory is piling up and not selling — what do I do?

You are hitting buy limits or a soft market. Sell into the limit steadily each day, or process the items (tan hides, fletch logs) to raise the effective limit and add value. Dead stock is risk, not profit — keep it turning so you stay liquid and can pivot when prices move.

What gear do I need before bossing, and how do I afford it?

At minimum a Dragon Hunter Crossbow or Lance for Vorkath, a Toxic Blowpipe for Zulrah, and solid armor. The jump from okay to good gear is worth more than any method-switch. Budget deliberately with a dedicated budget gear guide, and let Zalcano fund your first big purchase before you touch Vorkath.

16. 🎯 Final Tips — Keep the GP Flowing

  • Run the highest safe method your stats unlock. Do not grind a weak method to "save up" when you could be grinding the strong one directly.
  • Buy gear before you grind. A DHCB or blowpipe pays for itself in a single session. Never boss in sub-optimal gear out of impatience.
  • Track real GP/hr after supplies and GE tax. Honest net rates let you compare methods and switch at the right moment.
  • Re-margin-check after every update. Patches and banwaves move markets; the technique is the same, the numbers are not.
  • Don't panic-sell after balance patches. The Sweep-Up hit skilling, not your bosses. Keep running what works.
  • Respect variance. If you cannot survive a 100-kill dry streak, you are in too risky a boss. Drop down a tier.
  • Stay liquid. Keep inventory turning; dead stock is risk you cannot spend.
  • Graduate methodically. Zalcano → Zulrah → Vorkath → Yama is the natural curve; each unlocks the gear and confidence for the next.
  • Ride the post-bot-ban window. Elevated prices will normalize; build your bank while the supply gap is open.
