1. 🔒 The Recovery Dead-End: How Email Hijacking Locks You Out

Most account-recovery advice assumes one thing: that you can reach your own inbox. It tells you to "click forgot password" and wait for the reset link. That advice collapses the moment the email itself is compromised. In OSRS the registered email is not just a contact address — it is the recovery channel, the 2SV fallback, and (for older accounts) sometimes the only proof of ownership Jagex can see. When a hijacker owns that inbox, they can intercept every reset attempt you trigger, read your 2SV backup codes, and lock you into a loop where every self-service option routes straight back to them.

This is exactly the trap covered in the broader OSRS Account Hacked Prevention Guide, except here the prevention failed and you are already inside the nightmare. The normal playbook — strong password, authenticator app, bookmarking the real site — stops most attacks, but it cannot save you if the email layer underneath it was always weak. Think of your OSRS login as a vault: 2SV is the steel door, but the email is the key copy sitting under the doormat. Take the doormat and the steel door matters less than you hoped.

There are three distinct "email broken" situations, and the fix is different for each. Before you contact support, identify which one you are in — sending the wrong request wastes days you may not have. The three are: (A) Jagex emails are sent but never reach you, (B) you can still log in but your email is leaked/compromised, and (C) the hijacker already swapped the registered email to one you do not control. We cover each below, then the evidence you need for the manual route described in the Account Recovery After a Hack walkthrough.

💡 The #1 Mistake Players Make Here

They keep clicking "resend reset email" for a week, hoping it eventually appears. If the inbox is hijacked, every resent link lands in the attacker's hands and tells them you are actively trying to fight back — which can prompt them to act faster. Stop self-service resets the moment you suspect the email is compromised and move to the evidence-based recovery path instead.

2. 📭 Scenario A — Jagex Reset Emails Never Arrive (Mailbox Provider Blocking)

This is the most common and the least understood case. You request a password reset, Jagex does send it, but it never shows up — not in inbox, not in spam, not anywhere. Nine times out of ten the message was blocked or silently discarded by your email provider (Gmail, Outlook/Hotmail, Yahoo, or a corporate/ISP mail server), not lost by Jagex.

② Why providers drop Jagex mail

Common causes

  • Aggressive spam filtering — bulk game-mail can trip heuristic filters, especially on free providers.
  • Greylisting — some corporate servers temporarily reject first-time senders and never retry properly.
  • Alias / forwarding breakage — if your OSRS email forwards to another inbox, the forward rule may have been deleted or the destination full.
  • Full mailbox or disabled IMAP — the message bounces invisibly back to Jagex.

③ What to do before contacting support

① Check every folder, not just "Spam"

Look in Promotions, Updates, Social, and any custom filter folders. Search the exact term "Jagex" and "RuneScape" — providers often file legit mail under tabs you rarely open.

② Whitelist the sender

Add no-reply@jagex.com and support@jagex.com to your contacts / safe-senders list, then request one more reset. Allow up to 60 minutes; some providers batch-deliver.

③ Test the mailbox itself

Send yourself an email from another account. If that also fails to arrive, the problem is your mailbox (full, disabled, or forwarding broken), not Jagex.

④ If mail genuinely never arrives after whitelisting

Do not keep resending. Proceed to the manual recovery route (Section 5) and note in your ticket that "reset emails are not being received despite whitelisting" — Jagex can verify send logs on their side.

⚠️ One Reset Is Enough to Diagnose

After you whitelist and wait an hour with no mail, further resets only create duplicate tickets and confuse the timeline. State clearly that self-service email delivery is failing and ask for an alternative verification path.

3. 🔓 Scenario B — You Can Still Log In but the Email Is Leaked

This is the "silent compromise" case: you can still play, but you got a "your password appeared in a breach" warning, or you reused that email/password elsewhere and that other site was hacked. The hijacker does not yet control your OSRS account, but they have the inbox credentials and are one login away. Speed matters more than panic.

④ Immediate containment checklist

Do these in order, within 10 minutes

  1. Change the email password first. Use a unique, 16+ character password stored in a password manager. If the inbox is shared with other accounts, fix all of them.
  2. Enable 2SV on the email with an authenticator app (not SMS if you can avoid it — SIM-swap attacks are real).
  3. Revoke suspicious sessions — Gmail, Outlook and others let you "sign out all other devices" from security settings.
  4. Change your Jagex Account password from a clean device, and confirm your 2SV is still your own authenticator.
  5. Review the OSRS login history in Jagex Account settings for any unknown location or device, and remove anything unfamiliar.

Because you still have access, this is the easiest scenario to fully resolve — you are not fighting the hijacker for the inbox, you are simply evicting them before they finish moving in. The detailed "what to check" steps overlap heavily with the Anti-Scam Guide, which explains how leaked-credential attacks chain together across sites.

🔑 Treat the Email as Already Burned

Even after you change the password, assume the old inbox content (including past Jagex emails, which can contain recovery clues) was read. The only fully safe move is a fresh, dedicated email used only for OSRS, with its own 2SV. Migrating is covered in Section 5.

4. 🚪 Scenario C — The Hijacker Changed Your Registered Email (Hard Lockout)

This is the worst-case and the one most guides skip. The attacker already recovered or social-engineered the account and swapped the registered email to an address you do not own. Now when you try to recover, Jagex's system sees the new email as authoritative and either sends the reset to the hijacker or tells you the account is "not associated" with your address. You are fully locked out through self-service.

⑤ What this means for self-service recovery

The hard truth

  • The "forgot password" link now emails the attacker's address — triggering it just alerts them.
  • Jagex will not silently revert the email just because you claim it's yours; that would let anyone steal any account by asking.
  • You must prove, with historical account evidence, that you created and owned the account long before the hijacker arrived.

This is precisely why the evidence pack in Section 6 exists. Jagex's support model is built around the principle that the person who can recount the account's origins — creation date, first memberships, historical passwords, old recovery questions — is the real owner, because a hijacker who grabbed the account last week almost never has that depth of knowledge. Your job is to out-detail them.

🛑 Do NOT Try to "Hack Back"

Do not pay a "recovery service" that promises to reclaim the account by guessing or brute-forcing. These are scams that take your money and often your remaining credentials. The only legitimate channel is Jagex Support directly, described next.

5. 📨 How to Contact Jagex Support to Change the Bound Email

When self-service fails, the path is the official Jagex Support recovery form (submitted through the Jagex Account / Support site, never via a Discord DM, Twitch "mod," or email reply). The goal is twofold: prove ownership and request that the registered email be updated to one you control.

⑥ Step-by-step support route

Step 1 — Open the recovery form from a clean device

Use a device and network you trust (not the possibly-infected PC the account was lost from). Navigate to the official Jagex Support site yourself; never follow a link from a message.

Step 2 — Select "Account recovery / hijacked account"

Choose the option that matches a compromised or email-changed account. Be specific that the registered email was changed by a third party — this flags it for manual review rather than automated reset.

Step 3 — Provide the evidence pack (Section 6)

Answer every historical question you can. Accuracy and specificity beat volume — one exact creation month is worth more than five vague guesses.

Step 4 — Explicitly request the email change

In the free-text field, state: "The registered email was changed without my authorization. Please update the registered email to [your new clean address] after verifying ownership." Support can action this once ownership is confirmed.

Step 5 — Wait for the review, then secure everything

Reviews can take several days depending on queue depth. When access returns, immediately set the new email, enable 2SV on both the email and the Jagex Account, and rotate the password. The Jagex Account FAQ covers what settings to check the moment you are back in.

⚠️ Jagex Never Asks for Your Password in a Chat

If anyone claiming to be "Jagex Support" DMs you on Discord, Twitch, or in-game and asks for your password or 2SV code to "verify," it is a scam. Real Jagex support works only through the official support ticket system.

6. 🗂️ The Account-Creation Evidence Pack Jagex Accepts

This is the moat of the whole recovery: evidence the hijacker cannot fake. Gather whatever you genuinely remember. You do not need all of it, but the more precise points you supply, the stronger your claim. List everything in your ticket.

Evidence TypeWhy It HelpsExample You Might Recall
Account creation date / year Hard for a recent hijacker to know "Created in late 2007 during the original Wilderness return" or "made the account in 2014"
Original display name & any renames Historical names are sticky in Jagex logs "Was 'XxArcher99xX' before the 2019 name change"
First membership / bond purchase Payment records tie to a real person "Bought my first bond in 2018 via credit card"
Old passwords / email addresses used Only the owner typically recalls these "Used a Hotmail before switching to Gmail in 2020"
Historical recovery questions / answers Legacy accounts had set questions "Mother's birthplace was set to a specific city"
Linked payment / transaction IDs Strongest proof of ownership Any old membership receipt, even from years ago

⑦ Tips to maximize your evidence score

What reviewers look for

  • Specificity over guesses. "Around 2013" with a reason beats "maybe 2012 or 2015."
  • Consistency. Contradicting yourself across the form looks like a hijacker fishing.
  • Receipts win. If you have any old email receipt from Jagex, forward it or reference the date — this alone can settle a claim.
  • Honesty about gaps. Stating "I don't remember" on a few fields reads as genuine, unlike a hijacker who fabricates everything.
💡 Dig Through Your Own Email Archive

Before submitting, search your old inboxes (even deleted/archive folders) for "Jagex," "RuneScape," or "membership." A single 2016 purchase confirmation is often the single piece that converts a rejected claim into an approved one.

7. 🛡️ Prevention: Never Let Email Be Your Only Key

Once you are back in — or if you are reading this to avoid the nightmare — the fix is architectural. The email must stop being a single point of failure.

⑧ Build the layered defense

The resilient setup

  1. Dedicated email used only for OSRS, with a unique password and its own 2SV.
  2. Jagex Account 2SV via authenticator app or passkey, so a stolen password alone cannot log in.
  3. Saved backup / recovery codes stored offline, not in the same inbox.
  4. Recovery questions / historical notes written down somewhere safe, so you can reproduce the evidence pack fast if needed.

The full checklist lives in the OSRS Account Security Guide. The one habit that prevents 90% of email-hijack recoveries: never reuse your OSRS email password anywhere else, and put 2SV on the inbox itself. A secure game login on a throwaway, unprotected email is the exact setup that produces the dead-ends in this article.

🔄 If You Migrated to a Jagex Account

Modern accounts recover through the Jagex Account system, not the old "recovery questions" flow. If you are unsure whether you migrated, check the Jagex Account Migration Guide — the recovery evidence you submit is the same, but the submission portal differs.

8. 💡 Email Hijack FAQs

How long does a manual email-change recovery take?

It varies with support queue depth, but most reviewed claims resolve within several business days. Supply a complete evidence pack up front to avoid back-and-forth that stretches it to weeks. Complicated cases where ownership is contested can take longer, so precision in Section 6 matters.

Can Jagex just tell me what email is registered so I can recover it?

No, and that is intentional. If support revealed the registered email to anyone who asked, hijackers could confirm which accounts they already control and phish the real owner more precisely. Jagex verifies ownership through evidence first, then restores access — they will not hand out the current email address.

My reset emails go to spam but the account is fine — am I at risk?

Not directly, but it signals your email provider is filtering Jagex mail, which becomes a real problem the day you actually need a reset. Whitelist the Jagex sender addresses now so a future recovery email is not silently dropped when you are locked out.

Should I pay a "recovery service" that guarantees results?

Never. Every third-party "we'll get your account back" service is either a scam or violates Jagex rules (and can get the account actioned). The only legitimate route is Jagex Support directly. Anyone demanding payment or your credentials up front is exploiting your panic.

What if I never set recovery questions and have no receipts?

Submit anyway with everything you do recall — creation era, original name, old email domains, approximate first membership. Reviewers weigh the whole picture; many accounts are recovered on historical detail alone. The more specific your memory, the better your odds even without a receipt.

After I get back in, will the hijacker just take it again?

Only if you repeat the weak-email mistake. Once you control a dedicated, 2SV-protected email and enable Jagex Account 2SV, the original entry vector is closed. Also remove any unknown linked devices/sessions during the immediate post-recovery security sweep.
