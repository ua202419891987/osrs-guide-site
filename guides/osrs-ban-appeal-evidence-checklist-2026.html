📂 Ban Appeal Evidence: What to Submit & What to Skip (2026)

Updated August 19, 2026  |  17 min read  |  By Yanchun Wu

An appeal without proof is just an opinion. Jagex reviewers compare your message against server-side logs, so the evidence you attach can confirm your story or quietly bury it. This checklist tells you exactly which records help, which ones hurt, and how to package them so a reviewer can decide in minutes instead of closing your ticket.

📅 Updated: August 2026 — Account & Appeals · 17 min read
🛡️ Level: All
🆕 NEW

A clean evidence package is the difference between a fast unban and a closed ticket. Before you write a word, read our ban appeal template guide so your message and your proof tell the same story, and check the ban types explained guide so you label the right offence — mismatched labels make even good evidence look fake.

💡 Quick Verdict

The single best evidence is anything that ties the account to you and separates your play from the banned activity: stable login IPs, official payment receipts, and a clear timeline. The worst "evidence" is angry screenshots of the ban screen, walls of chat logs, or off-site trade confirmations that actually prove the rule break. Submit proof that confirms your identity and context — never proof that confirms the offence.

⏱️ Quick Summary — 30-Second Read

  • 📌 Helpful: payment receipts, stable IP/login history, recovery email, a dated timeline
  • 📌 Hurts: off-site trade screenshots, ranting chat logs, "proof" of the offence
  • 📌 Format: named files, short captions, one PDF or zipped folder
  • 📌 Golden rule: evidence must confirm YOUR identity, not the ban
  • 📌 If hacked: recovery proof + 2-step enabled beats any denial

1. 🎯 Why Evidence Decides Appeals

Players imagine appeals as a conversation where they explain themselves and a human decides. In reality, the reviewer opens your ticket already holding the server logs: detection flags, trade history, login IPs, and payment records. Your evidence is not there to "prove" the ban wrong from scratch — it is there to confirm or contradict what those logs already show. Win by making the contradiction impossible.

① The reviewer's mental model

They ask three questions: (1) Is this account really yours? (2) Does the claimed story match the logs? (3) Is there new information the detection system could not see? Evidence that answers all three moves you forward. Evidence that only expresses feelings gets skimmed and closed.

② Proof of ownership beats proof of innocence

You rarely need to "prove you are innocent." You need to prove the account is yours and that the banned session is not. A payment receipt with your name, a recovery email sent to your address, and a stable home IP do more than a 40-line essay about how you would never cheat.

③ One clean package, not a flood

Five well-named files with one-line captions beat 60 random screenshots. Reviewers are timed; a tidy package is read, a messy one is deferred or denied. The checklist below ranks every common evidence type by how much it actually helps.

2. 💱 Transaction Records (GP, GE, Bonds)

Trade and transaction records are the heart of any RWT or suspicious-trade appeal. Used correctly they show a normal economy player; used badly they become a confession. The key is to show official, in-game transactions — never off-platform deals.

① What helps: Grand Exchange & Bond history

Official Grand Exchange trades, Bond purchases, and membership payments are logged to your account and confirm legitimate play. If you are appealing a false RWT flag, listing your normal GE flips and noting you only ever used Bonds for membership directly supports "I never traded for real money." The false RWT ban appeal guide shows how to phrase this.

② What hurts: off-site trade confirmations

Do NOT attach Discord screenshots, PayPal receipts, or "I bought GP from site X" messages. Those prove the exact rule break you are denying. If you did trade off-site, a transaction record is not your evidence — your honest admission (see the template guide) is. Attaching proof of the offence is the fastest way to convert a maybe into a permanent.

③ How to present trade evidence

If you reference trades, name them plainly: "All my GP came from GE flips and Boss X loot between [dates]." You do not need to paste 200 trade lines. A short, dated summary that matches the in-game log is enough; the reviewer can pull the full history themselves.

④ What "normal economy play" looks like to a reviewer

Reviewers are not hunting ordinary wealth. A max cash pile, expensive gear, or a high total level is not evidence of RWT by itself — thousands of legitimate players have it. What looks normal is steady GP from GE margins, boss drops, and skilling, with trade partners who are ordinary players. What looks suspicious is a sudden multi-billion injection from one or two accounts you have no other link to, or item movements that mirror known gold-selling patterns. When you describe your sources, match that "normal" picture: name the bosses you farmed, the flips you ran, the skills you trained. The more your story sounds like a real player's week, the less the reviewer needs to dig.

3. 🌐 Login History & IP Evidence

IP and login data is the quiet MVP of appeals, especially hacked-account and false-positive cases. It ties the account to a physical you and exposes sessions that were not you.

① Stable home IP proves "this is me"

If you log in from the same home or work IP most days, that consistency is strong identity proof. Note it in your appeal: "I log in from [city/country] on a fixed connection." A reviewer comparing that to the banned session's location can see whether the flagged activity came from your normal pattern.

② Strange IPs prove a hijack

If the botting or RWT happened from a country you have never visited, say so: "The banned activity came from [region] — I have never been there." That single mismatch is often the strongest hacked-account signal you can provide. Full recovery steps live in our account recovery after hack guide.

③ Shared / VPN / school IP caveats

A shared IP (school, cafe, mobile hotspot) can trigger false flags and also weakens "this is only me" claims. If you were on a shared connection when banned, explain it — but know it cuts both ways: it can explain a false positive or explain why a hijacker looked like you. The account locked / suspicious activity guide covers how Jagex reads these signals.

4. 💳 Payment / Card / Membership Proof

Official payment records are the most dispute-proof ownership evidence you have. They connect the account to a real-world identity Jagex already trusts.

① Membership & Bond receipts

A receipt from the official store (card statement, PayPal record, or Jagex emailed invoice) showing the membership or Bond purchase on this account is gold. It proves the account is yours and that you engaged with the game through approved channels only. Redact the full card number if you like, but keep the date, amount, and last four digits.

② What to avoid: chargeback records

If you ever opened a chargeback on a membership payment, that is itself a bannable action and a separate problem — see the membership refund / chargeback ban guide. Do not attach chargeback disputes as "evidence" in a different ban appeal; it can open a second offence review. Keep payment proof to legitimate, completed purchases.

③ How to submit payment proof

One screenshot or PDF of the relevant statement, cropped to the Jagex line, named "payment-proof.jpg," with a caption like "Official membership bought [date], last4 [1234]." That is all a reviewer needs to confirm ownership.

④ Redacting vs hiding — how much to show

You should hide sensitive digits but never fake or blank the whole record. A reviewer needs three things from a payment proof: the merchant (Jagex / "Old School RuneScape"), the date, and an amount that matches a membership tier or Bond. Cover the full card number and any CVV, but keep the last four digits and the transaction date visible. If you black out everything, the screenshot proves nothing and may be skipped. If you cropped too tightly to show the merchant, add a one-line caption stating it was an official Jagex payment. The goal is "verifiable, not vulnerable" — enough for the reviewer to cross-check against the account's billing record, never enough to expose you to identity theft.

5. 🎥 Video & Screenshot Evidence

Video is powerful for false-positive macro appeals; screenshots are weak unless they show something the logs cannot. Use them with a clear purpose, not as emotional decoration.

① Video for false-positive botting

If you were banned for macroing but play by hand, a short screen recording of you actually clicking — showing manual input, normal reaction times, and a single client — can support your claim. Keep it brief (a few minutes), and narrate what you are doing. Pair it with the false macro ban appeal guide so the message and video agree.

② Screenshots that help vs hurt

Helpful: a settings screen showing "official client only," your 2-step confirmation email, or the recovery success page. Hurtful: the ban message itself (Jagex has it), angry PMs with friends, or "look how legit my stats are" shots that prove nothing. A screenshot must show a fact the reviewer cannot already see in the logs.

③ Format and size

Compress video to a shareable link or small file; do not paste a 2 GB file into a ticket. Name files clearly. If the tool rejects attachments, host on a plain file link and paste the URL in the message with a one-line description.

6. 🚫 What Hurts Your Appeal (Skip These)

Some "evidence" actively damages your case. The table below is the skip list — these either prove the offence, read as spam, or waste the reviewer's time.

Item Verdict Reason
Off-site trade / PayPal screenshots ❌ Skip Proves the RWT rule break you deny
Ban-screen screenshot ❌ Skip Reviewer already has it; adds nothing
Walls of chat logs ⚠️ Trim Emotional, rarely relevant, slows review
"I spent $X so unban me" ❌ Skip Spending does not override a rule break
Threats / public-post screenshots ❌ Skip Reads as coercion, gets ticket closed
Chargeback dispute docs ⚠️ Separate Opens a second offence; handle on its own
Short dated timeline ✅ Send Matches your story to the logs fast
Official payment receipt ✅ Send Strongest ownership proof available
⚠️ The "Proof It Was Me" Trap

Players sometimes attach a screenshot that accidentally shows the offence — abot client window, a trade with a known RWT clan, a gold-selling site tab. If it is in your evidence, the reviewer sees it. Only attach files you have looked at. When in doubt, describe instead of attach.

④ The "less is more" principle

If you remember one rule, make it this: every file you attach should prove either that the account is yours or that the banned session was not. Anything else is noise that lengthens the review and can backfire. A two-file package that says "I own this, and that login wasn't me" is stronger than a ten-file dump of unrelated screenshots. Reviewers are timed and skim; the faster they find the key proof, the faster they decide in your favor. When you are tempted to add "just one more screenshot," ask whether it changes the answer to those two questions. If not, leave it out.

7. 🗂️ How to Organize & Submit the Package

A great set of files in a messy ticket still loses. Package them so a timed reviewer can clear your case in one read.

① Name everything

Use plain filenames: payment-proof.jpg, login-ip.jpg, recovery-email.jpg, timeline.txt. Avoid IMG_4471.png or screenshot2.png. A named file tells the reviewer what it is before they open it.

② One caption per file

Under each attachment write one line: "Official Bond purchase, [date], confirms ownership." No essays. The caption connects the file to your claim so the reviewer does not have to guess.

③ Lead with the strongest, end with context

Order: (1) payment/ownership proof, (2) IP/login history, (3) timeline, (4) video if any. The reviewer forms an impression in the first 30 seconds; put your best proof first. If you were hacked, the hack emergency checklist has the exact recovery artifacts to include.

④ Keep it reviewable

One PDF or a small zip is ideal. If the form only takes a few attachments, pick the top three from the ladder in Section 1. A denied-first-try account can still be recovered through the route in our recovery denied reapply guide if you later find stronger proof.

⑤ Packaging mistakes that sink good evidence

Even strong files fail when packaged badly. The top mistakes: (1) unnamed files — a reviewer will not open "Screenshot_22.png" blind; (2) no captions — a payment shot with no note looks like filler; (3) one giant 50 MB attachment that the form rejects, so nothing arrives; (4) evidence buried in a novel-length message, so the key file is missed; (5) mixing two bans in one ticket, which splits the reviewer's attention. Fix all five by following the simple order in Section 1: ownership first, identity second, timeline third, video last. Name, caption, compress, and stay on one ban. A clean three-file package beats a fifteen-file dump every time, because the reviewer actually reads it.

💡 The 10-Second Self-Test

Before sending, ask: "Does this file prove the account is mine, or does it prove the ban is correct?" Keep only the first kind. If a file makes you nervous it might show the offence, cut it. A smaller, clean package beats a large risky one every time.

8. ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

What is the single most useful piece of evidence?

An official payment receipt (membership or Bond) tied to your account. It proves ownership better than any screenshot and signals you engaged through approved channels only.

Should I send my full trade history?

No. Summarize it in the message and let the reviewer pull the full log. Pasting hundreds of trade lines buries the signal. Name the normal sources (GE, boss loot) and dates instead.

My IP changes often — does that hurt me?

Not by itself. Mobile and dynamic home IPs change and Jagex expects that. What hurts is a sudden login from a country you have never visited — that mismatch is what you should highlight as a hijack sign.

Can a video really overturn a macro ban?

It can support a false-positive claim if it shows manual input and a single official client. It cannot overturn a solid detection where the logs already show a botting client. Use video to confirm, not to argue.

Is it okay to redact my card number?

Yes. Keep the date, amount, and last four digits; hide the rest. Reviewers only need to confirm the purchase happened on your account, not your full financial details.

Will attaching the ban message help?

No — Jagex already has it, and it adds zero new information. Spend that attachment slot on ownership or IP proof instead.

I was hacked — what proves it best?

The recovery email sent to your address, the date you re-enabled 2-step verification, and the strange-region login gap. Together they show a hijack window the botting could not have been yours. See the recovery after hack guide for the full artifact list.

How many files should I attach?

Three to five strong, named files beat dozens of weak ones. Cover ownership, identity/IP, and timeline. If the form limits attachments, send the top three and reference the rest in text.

Can I link a Google Drive / imgur instead of attaching?

Often yes, if the form rejects large files. Use a plain, non-expiring link and write one line describing each file in the message. Avoid links that require login or look like spam.

My appeal was denied — can new evidence reopen it?

Only with genuinely new, previously unseen proof (like a recovery email you just found). Reply to the same ticket or use the recovery route; do not resend the old package expecting a different result.
