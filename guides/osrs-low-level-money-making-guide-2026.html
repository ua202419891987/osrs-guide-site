1. Low Level Money Making Principles

When your account is fresh and your combat level sits anywhere between 3 and 50, the single biggest mistake new players make is assuming they must kill monsters to make money. At low levels your hitpoints, attack, and strength are tiny, your gear is weak, and most monsters that drop valuable loot will simply destroy you. The truth is that the most reliable gold in early Old School RuneScape comes from gathering and processing raw materials, not from fighting. A level 3 account with no weapon can already pick flax, fish shrimp, mine copper, and collect cowhides, and every one of those items has a real buyer on the Grand Exchange.

Before you commit to any method, internalize four principles that will save you hours of wasted effort. First, time is your scarcest resource. A method that earns 20,000 GP per hour but requires you to walk fifteen minutes each trip is worse than a method that earns 15,000 GP per hour next to a bank. Always measure gold in GP per hour of real elapsed time, not GP per inventory. Second, train a skill while you earn. Flax trains Crafting, fishing trains Fishing, mining trains Mining. Money you make while also gaining XP is worth more than money you make standing still. Third, bank space is a hidden tax. If your inventory is full of junk you refuse to drop, you will make fewer trips. Learn what to keep and what to alch or sell, which we cover in section five. Fourth, never underestimate free money. Random events, clue scrolls, and the occasional drop from a low-level creature add up to surprising totals over a week.

The other key mindset shift is understanding the difference between F2P (free to play) and P2P (members) at low levels. Free to play is far more limited, but it is still completely possible to fund your first bond or your first membership with nothing but cowhides, flax, and fishing. Members unlocks far better methods such as flax at Seers Village, flax at Tree Gnome Stronghold, fly fishing at Shilo Village, and early slayer tasks. If you are undecided about whether to become a member, read our beginner money making guide for the broader picture, then come back here for the low-level specifics.

🎯 The Four Rules Every Low-Level Grinder Needs

  • Maximize GP per hour of real time, including travel.
  • Pick methods that double as skill training whenever possible.
  • Keep your inventory clean so you never waste a trip.
  • Collect every free coin: random events, clues, and nests.
💡 Start With a Goal Number

Decide what the gold is FOR before you grind. Is it 10,000 GP for a set of armor, 250,000 GP for your first bond, or 1,000,000 GP for a weapon upgrade? A concrete target keeps the early grind from feeling endless, and most low-level goals are reachable in a single focused evening.

Finally, accept that low-level money making is a stepping stone, not a destination. The methods below will not make you rich by end-game standards, but they will reliably build your first few hundred thousand coins, teach you the Grand Exchange, and give you the confidence to move into stronger methods. Treat every hour as an investment in both your bank and your game knowledge. By the time you finish this guide you should know exactly which method fits your current stats, how much it pays, and when to graduate to the next one.

2. Skill-Based Methods (Flax, Fishing, Mining)

Skill-based gathering is the backbone of low-level wealth because the requirements are essentially zero and the demand is permanent. Millions of players need flax for bow strings, fish for food, and ores for smithing, so the Grand Exchange always has buyers. Below we break down the three best gathering skills for a combat 3-50 account.

2.1 Flax to Bow Strings (Crafting + Cash)

Flax is the closest thing low-level Old School RuneScape has to a printing press. You pick flax for free from fields, spin it into bow strings at a spinning wheel, and sell the strings for a tidy profit. The best free-to-play spot is the flax field north of Seers Village, with the spinning wheel right inside the bank building. Members also have the Tree Gnome Stronghold flax field, which is even closer to a spinning wheel and a bank, making it the most efficient flax run in the game.

The economics are simple. One flax is nearly worthless, but one bow string sells for a steady few hundred coins, and you can process about 1,000 to 1,400 flax per hour depending on your route and how well you know the bank. That translates to roughly 120,000 to 180,000 GP per hour at typical Grand Exchange prices, plus around 1,000 Crafting XP per hour. For a fresh account this is one of the highest GP per hour methods available with literally no requirements beyond being able to click.

🎯 Flax Step-by-Step

  • Teleport or run to Seers Village (or Gnome Stronghold if members).
  • Pick flax until your 28 inventory slots are full.
  • Use the spinning wheel (inside Seers bank; ground floor of Gnome bank).
  • Spin all flax into bow strings, then bank and repeat.
  • Sell bow strings in a single Grand Exchange offer for market price.

2.2 Fishing (Food Demand Is Endless)

Fishing is the most relaxed low-level money maker and it trains a useful skill at the same time. The classic starting loop is shrimp and anchovies at a quiet dock, then fly fishing for trout and salmon once you reach level 20. Fly fishing at places like Shilo Village (members), the Barbarian Village river, or Gunnarsgrunn is nearly as fast XP as any method and the raw fish sells consistently because every PvM player needs food.

Raw shrimp sell for a small amount, but raw trout and salmon are the sweet spot: each raw salmon is worth a few hundred coins and a full inventory of mixed trout and salmon can clear for 20,000 to 40,000 GP depending on the market. Expect roughly 30,000 to 60,000 GP per hour from fly fishing while gaining around 30,000 to 50,000 Fishing XP per hour. It is slower cash than flax, but it is AFK-friendly and builds a skill you will use forever.

💡 Cook or Sell Raw?

At low levels, selling raw fish is usually faster than cooking and banking cooked fish, because cooking adds a banking trip and the price gap is small. However, if you also want Cooking XP, cook the fish on a nearby range and sell the cooked versions, which often fetch a slightly higher price and never burn at high Cooking levels.

2.3 Mining (Ores and Bars)

Mining is the slowest of the three but it has one huge advantage: iron ore. Copper and tin are worth almost nothing, but iron ore sells for a solid price and it is mineable from level 15 Mining with no membership requirement. The Al Kharid mine (three rocks, close to a bank via the toll gate) and the Varrock southeast mine are the standard free-to-play iron spots. Members can use the Motherlode Mine from level 30 for steady pay and gem drops, though that is more of a mid-level method.

A focused iron miner clears roughly 30,000 to 70,000 GP per hour depending on world population and competition, while gaining real Mining XP. Coal becomes relevant once you hit level 30 and pair it with Smithing for cannonballs or steel bars, but that edges into mid-game. For a pure low-level loop, iron ore is your bread and butter, and it doubles as the raw material if you later want to train Smithing.

Most new players rotate between them based on boredom rather than strict optimization, and that is perfectly fine. The important thing is that you are always filling your inventory with something sellable.

3. Combat-Based Methods (Cows & Early Slayer)

If you would rather swing a weapon than gather, combat money at low levels is absolutely possible — you just have to pick fights you can actually win and loot efficiently. Two methods stand out for combat 3-50 accounts: killing cows, and taking early slayer assignments.

3.1 Cowhides (The Classic Beginner Loop)

Killing cows in the field north of Lumbridge or the field west of the Crafting Guild is the most iconic low-level combat money maker, and for good reason. Cows have only 8 hitpoints, they never fight back with any real damage, and each cow drops a cowhide worth a reliable few hundred coins on the Grand Exchange. A fresh level 3 can kill cows with a bronze or iron scimitar and bank the hides within seconds.

The numbers are attractive. A full inventory of 28 cowhides sells for roughly 25,000 to 35,000 GP at standard prices, and a decent cow run clears an inventory every few minutes. Realistic earnings land around 60,000 to 120,000 GP per hour depending on your combat speed and how close your bank is. The Lumbridge cow field is free to play and members alike, and the nearby Lumbridge bank makes it one of the fastest cash loops available with no stats required.

🎯 Efficient Cowhides Route

  • Grab a bronze scimitar from the Lumbridge basement or buy one cheap.
  • Enter the cow field north of Lumbridge, kill cows, pick up hides only.
  • Bank at Lumbridge castle (upper floor) every full inventory.
  • Optional: tan hides at Al Kharid for leather (more GP, costs a fee).
  • Sell cowhides in one Grand Exchange offer; do not merchant individually.
💡 Tan or Sell Raw?

Raw cowhides are the safest bet because tanning costs money per hide. However, if you already have coins to spare, tanning at the Al Kharid tanner turns hides into soft leather worth more, and crafting them into leather items adds Crafting XP. For a pure gold focus, sell the raw hides and skip the middle steps.

3.2 Early Slayer (Members, Combat 1-50)

Once you are a member and have spoken to Turael in Burthorpe to unlock Slayer, you gain access to assignments that gently introduce combat money. Turael gives very easy tasks — typically cows, chickens, rats, or goblins — that you can already kill, but the real value comes later when you unlock Mazchna (requires 20 Combat in members) who assigns slightly tougher monsters with better drops.

Early slayer is less about the GP per kill and more about stacking value: you train Slayer (a skill that unlocks lucrative mid and late game methods), you train combat, and you collect the drops from whatever you are assigned. A task of 40 cows or 50 birds is essentially free cowhides plus Slayer XP. Expect around 40,000 to 90,000 GP per hour from the loot alone during easy tasks, with the long-term payoff being a Slayer level that later unlocks abyssal demons, gargoyles, and superior creatures worth millions per hour.

For combat training itself, our beginner combat training guide explains exactly how to raise Attack, Strength, Defence, and Hitpoints safely so you can take on tougher slayer monsters sooner. The faster your combat stats climb, the faster your combat money methods scale.

The key takeaway is that combat money at low levels is real but always trails the best gathering methods on pure GP per hour. Choose combat methods when you also want to raise your combat level, and choose flax or fishing when you purely want coins. Smart players do both on alternating sessions.

💡 Read the Table With Context

A higher GP per hour is not automatically the best choice. Flax pays the most but trains only Crafting. Cowhides pay nearly as much and raise combat. If your account needs combat levels to unlock better methods, cows or early slayer are the smarter "double dip." Match the method to the stat you want to grow.

Random events deserve a special mention here because they do not appear as a row in the table yet they quietly boost every method above. While skilling or fighting, you will periodically be pulled into a random event such as the Sandwich Lady, Freaky Forester, Drill Demon, or the Genie. Completing them grants a reward token, and the Genie in particular offers 500,000 coins worth of XP lamps or a cash equivalent, while others give useful items like gems, event jewels, or teleport cards. Across a long session these events add an effective several thousand GP per hour on top of your main method, with zero requirements and zero risk.

Clue scrolls are the other hidden earner. Low-level easy clue scrolls drop from cows, chickens, and many low-level monsters, and a completed easy clue can yield valuable rewards such as god pages, trimmed armor, or cash caskets. Even if you ignore clues, the occasional drop still pads your totals. The lesson: never skip a random event, and always pick up clue scrolls dropped by the monsters you kill.

5. What To Keep vs What To Sell

Low-level players lose more money to bad inventory decisions than to bad method choices. Knowing what to hoard and what to liquidate is a skill in itself, and it directly affects your effective GP per hour because a cluttered bag means fewer profitable trips. We cover the fundamentals of organizing your loot in our bank and inventory management guide, but here are the low-level specifics.

5.1 Sell These Immediately

Most low-level monster drops are vendor or alch fodder, but several sell well on the Grand Exchange and should never be dropped. Cowhides, raw fish, flax, iron ore, feathers, and bones all have real buyers. Feathers in particular stack and sell steadily because fletchers buy them by the thousand for arrow shafts. Bones are technically alchable but their real value is Prayer XP; at low levels, selling regular bones is fine if you need cash, but saving them for a later Prayer grind is smarter.

5.2 Keep These for Later

Certain items are nearly worthless now but become gold once you unlock the right content. Unsourced gems (uncut sapphires, emeralds) from mining and fishing can be saved for Crafting. Tree roots and ashes from events may be needed for diaries. Clue reward items such as god pages should be kept until you can complete a set, since complete sets are worth far more than loose pieces. Most importantly, keep any members objects you find as a free player, because you cannot use or sell them efficiently until you subscribe.

🎯 The 10-Second Inventory Rule

  • If it stacks and sells for over 50 GP each, keep it in your loot bag.
  • If it is a one-off drop you cannot use, check GE price; sell if over 100 GP.
  • If it is a clue scroll, bank it immediately — never drop it.
  • If it is junk under 20 GP with no use, drop it to save a trip.

5.3 Use the Grand Exchange, Not Shops

A common beginner error is selling loot to a general store for a fraction of its value. Always use the Grand Exchange in Varrock, Falador, or Edgeville (via the wilderness lever area) to sell at market price. Set your sell offer at the middle or slightly below the current price to ensure a fast sale, then go back to your method while it clears. This single habit can double your effective income versus shop-vending.

💡 Watch the "Instant Sell" Trap

The Grand Exchange shows both a guide price and an instant-sell price. Instant selling is convenient but you leave coins on the table. For common items like cowhides and bow strings, a normal offer at guide price usually sells within minutes and nets you more. Only instant-sell if you urgently need the cash.

Finally, resist the urge to alch everything. Low-level items have low alchemy values, and the nature runes cost more than the difference for cheap items. Save High Alchemy for mid and high level drops where the margin is worth the rune cost. At combat 3-50, plain Grand Exchange selling is almost always the better math.

6. Transitioning to Mid-Level Money Making

The methods in this guide will comfortably carry you from a blank account to your first few hundred thousand coins and a respectable combat level. The moment your combat level crosses 50, your Mining or Fishing breaks 40, or your Slayer reaches the high teens, a whole new tier of money opens up and you should begin to rotate out of pure low-level loops. This section maps that graduation so you never outgrow a method without a replacement ready.

6.1 Stat Milestones That Unlock Better Gold

At combat 50 you can tackle monsters like hill giants (big bones for Prayer and limpwurt roots) and begin profitable Slayer from Mazchna and beyond. At Mining 30 the Motherlode Mine becomes available for steady ores and gems. At Fishing 40 you unlock anglerfish and karambwan later on, though those are mid-game. At Woodcutting 35 teak and mahogany open tropical wood money. Each of these eclipses the low-level table above, so track your stats and shift the instant you qualify.

6.2 Build a Buffer Before You Specialize

The danger zone is between 50 and 70 combat, where low methods feel slow but mid methods feel risky. Use your low-level savings to buy better gear: a rune scimitar, an amulet of strength, and a ring of wealth dramatically raise your combat GP per hour and reduce deaths. Spend a portion of every grind on upgrades rather than hoarding coins, because upgraded gear compounds your future income far faster than the coins alone.

🎯 Your First 1,000,000 GP Roadmap

  • Phase 1 (0–100k): flax and cowhides, learn the Grand Exchange.
  • Phase 2 (100k–400k): keep flax, add iron mining or fly fishing.
  • Phase 3 (400k–700k): start Turael/Mazchna slayer for Slayer XP.
  • Phase 4 (700k–1M): buy rune scimitar + amulet, push combat to 50.
  • Phase 5 (1M+): graduate to mid-level Slayer and Motherlode Mine.

When you are ready to leave the low-level bracket entirely, our beginner money making guide picks up exactly where this one ends, covering the 50-70 combat methods, the best early members money makers, and how to chain them into your first bond or membership. Pair that with the combat training guide so your stats keep pace with your ambitions.

💡 Don't Burn Out on One Method

Variety protects your motivation. Alternate flax, cows, and fishing across sessions. You will make nearly the same total gold, learn more of the game map, and avoid the mental fatigue that makes players quit the grind before they reach their goal. A player who enjoys the loop will always out-earn a player who forces one method for hours.

Remember that low-level money making is not a test of patience but a foundation. Every bow string spun, every cowhide banked, and every clue completed is a brick in the account you are building. The players with the biggest banks almost all started exactly where you are now, clicking flax in a field north of Seers Village. Follow the tables, keep your bag clean, and let the coins compound.

7. Low Level Money Making FAQs

What is the absolute fastest way to make GP at combat level 3?

The fastest zero-requirement method is picking flax and spinning it into bow strings at Seers Village or the Tree Gnome Stronghold. It pays roughly 120,000 to 180,000 GP per hour with no stats, no gear, and no membership needed. Cowhides are a close second and also raise your combat level, so many players alternate the two.

Can I make good money as a free-to-play low-level player?

Yes. Free to play limits your options but cowhides, flax at Seers Village, shrimp fishing, and iron mining at Al Kharid all work without membership. A disciplined F2P player can fund their first bond entirely through these methods, which then unlocks the stronger members money makers described in our beginner guide.

Are random events actually worth doing for money?

Absolutely. Random events pay nothing directly most of the time, but the Genie reward alone can be worth 500,000 coins of XP or cash, and other events hand out gems, teleport cards, and items that sell. Across a long skilling or combat session they add an effective several thousand GP per hour on top of your main method, with zero risk, so never skip them.

Should I sell raw fish or cook it first?

For pure speed, sell raw fish. Cooking adds a banking trip and the price difference is usually small at low levels. The exception is if you also want Cooking XP, in which case cook on a nearby range and sell the cooked fish, which often fetches slightly more and never burns once your Cooking level is high enough.

When should I stop using low-level methods?

Begin rotating out as your combat level passes 50 or your gathering skills pass 30 to 40. At those thresholds mid-level methods like Motherlode Mine, hill giants, and higher Slayer assignments pay noticeably more per hour. Use your low-level savings to buy better gear first so the transition is smooth rather than risky.

Is early Slayer worth it if I only care about gold right now?

If you only care about immediate GP, pure flax or cowhides pay more per hour. But early Slayer builds a skill that later unlocks the most profitable methods in the entire game, so the small GP gap now is an investment. Most players do easy assignments alongside their main method so they get both cash and long-term progress.
