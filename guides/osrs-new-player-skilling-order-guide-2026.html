New Player Skilling Order 2026: What to Train First (and Skip)

New accounts fail the same way: they train skills in the order they appear in the stats panel. That wastes hundreds of hours. The right order depends entirely on what you want — GP, bossing, or quest completion. This guide gives you the universal early skills every account wants, then three goal-specific paths, plus the skills you should flat-out skip for now.

📅 Stage 0.3 — Part of the Complete Beginner Series · 18 min read · Updated August 2026
🛡️ Level: 3+ (New Account)
🆕 NEW

There is no single "correct" skilling order — only the order that fits your goal. Start with the new player guide for the big picture, then use the skill priority list as a quick reference. This article is the reasoning behind both: why some skills come first, why others wait, and how to branch by what you actually want from the game.

💡 Quick Verdict

Every new account should first push combat to ~40–50 via quests, get Thieving to ~43 (Pyramid Plunder money), and Agility to 50+ (run energy). After that, split: money focus → Thieving/Fishing/Runecraft/Hunter; PvM focus → Slayer + Prayer + combat styles; quest focus → the quest-required skills in optimal order. Construction, Farming, and most "slow" skills can wait. Idle the AFK-able ones; skip the rest until they serve a goal.

1. 🧠 The Core Principle: Train by Goal, Not by Alphabet

The stats panel lists skills in a fixed order — Attack, Defence, Strength, Hitpoints, Ranged, Prayer, Magic, Cooking, Woodcutting… New players read that as a to-do list. It isn't. The order that matters is the one that serves your objective.

① Why Order Beats Raw Hours

Some skills unlock money or power immediately; others sit dead until high levels. Training a dead skill early is wasted time you could have spent on a skill that pays you back the same hour. Efficient new accounts front-load "leverage" skills — the ones that make every later hour more productive — and defer the rest.

A quick way to spot a leverage skill: ask what it unlocks. If training it opens a money method, a boss, or a shortcut you'll reuse for hundreds of hours, it's leverage. If its only reward is "more XP in this one skill," it's probably deferrable. That single question eliminates most early-game wasted hours and is the habit that separates fast accounts from stuck ones.

② The Three Goals (Pick Yours)

Almost every new player falls into one of three lanes, and the order branches from there:

  • Money: You want GP to fund gear, bonds, or a comfortable bank. Prioritize income skills.
  • PvM / Bossing: You want to fight bosses and raid. Prioritize combat, Prayer, and Slayer.
  • Quests / Completion: You want the quest cape or diary completions. Prioritize exactly the skills quests demand, in optimal order.

③ What "Skip for Now" Means

"Skip" doesn't mean "never." It means the skill isn't worth active hours yet. You'll still gain it passively (quest rewards, training other skills, or AFK sessions). Skipping actively protects your early-game time for leverage skills. The 7-day roadmap shows how this looks day to day.

💡 The 80/20 of Skilling

Roughly 20% of skills deliver 80% of your early account power and income. Find that 20% for your goal and train it first. The other 80% can be idled, quest-fed, or ignored until a goal actually needs them.

2. 🌱 The Universal First Skills (Every Account)

Regardless of goal, a handful of skills help every account early. Do these before branching.

① Combat to ~40–50 via Quests

Don't grind combat. Quests like Waterfall Quest, Fight Arena, and Tree Gnome Village hand you tens of thousands of Attack/Strength/Defence/Hitpoints XP for free. You reach a useful combat level with almost no training time, which unlocks Slayer, bosses, and protection from tougher monsters. The free quest guide lists the XP-rich ones.

Waterfall Quest alone gives 13,750 XP split across Attack and Strength, enough to jump a fresh account from level 1 combat to the 30s in a single quest. Stack two or three of these and you are at boss-ready combat before you have trained a single hour manually. This is why "combat via quests" is step one for literally every account type — it is the highest XP-per-hour activity in the entire game, and it is free.

② Thieving to ~43 (Pyramid Plunder)

Thieving is the best early moneymaker most new players ignore. At 43 Thieving you enter Pyramid Plunder, a solo minigame that pays 100K–300K GP/hr at low levels with almost no requirements beyond a Pharaoh's Sceptre (or just walking in). Even to 43, blackjacking or just pickpocketing gets you started. This is your first real income engine.

You do not even need the sceptre to start — walk into the Pyramid Plunder rooms from the entrance — but a Pharaoh's Sceptre (obtained from the quest Contact! or as a drop) teleports you straight to the entrance and saves minutes per trip. At 100K–300K GP/hr, those minutes compound into millions over your first weeks. Thieving is the single skill I tell every new player to prioritize above almost everything else.

③ Agility to 50+ (Rooftops)

Agility doesn't make GP, but it gives run energy recovery — and run energy is time, every single trip, forever. Get to 50 for the decent rooftop courses, then keep it ticking up when idle. Low Agility quietly taxes every hour of your account; fixing it early pays back constantly. The comparison below ranks these universal skills by early leverage.

Even a few Agility levels help, but the big jump is at 50 (better courses) and again at 60+ where rooftop courses become efficient. You don't need 99 — most players stop around 70–80 and reap nearly all the benefit. Treat Agility as a background skill you chip away at, not a grind you finish in one session, and it will quietly save you time on every trip for the rest of the account.

3. 💰 Branch A — Money-Making Focus

If your goal is GP, the order is ruthless: train the skills that have a profitable activity as early as possible, and skip the ones that only pay at 90+.

① Skills That Make GP Early

These have a money activity reachable at low-to-mid levels:

  • Thieving (43): Pyramid Plunder — best low-level GP.
  • Fishing (76): Anglerfish — steady, AFK-able income.
  • Runecrafting (77+): Blood runes — high GP but slow to train.
  • Hunter (80): Herbiboar / bird house runs — strong GP + herbs.
  • Woodcutting (75+): Redwood / magic logs for consistent sales.

Rough GP/hr by milestone: Pyramid Plunder ~150K–300K at low Thieving; Anglerfish ~200K–400K at 76+ Fishing (and it's almost fully AFK); Blood runes ~400K–700K at 77+ Runecrafting but the grind there is punishing; Herbiboar ~300K–500K plus herbs at 80 Hunter. The pattern is clear: one skill pays early (Thieving), the rest pay only after a long climb. So the money order is "get Thieving working now, then slowly climb the others while you bank from Thieving."

② The Money Order

③ What to Skip (Money Focus)

Skip Construction (expensive, no income until high levels), Farming (slow early, herbs only pay with scale), and Hunter below 80 (weak GP until then). Train these only when a goal demands them. The bank you build from Thieving/Fishing/Hunter funds everything else.

⚠️ Don't Train for "Future" GP

Runecrafting to 77 or Hunter to 80 is a long grind with no income along the way. Bank GP from Thieving/Fishing first so you can buy your way through the boring parts later. Active hours belong on skills that pay now.

4. ⚔️ Branch B — PvM / Bossing Focus

If you want bosses and raids, combat-adjacent skills come first and almost everything else waits.

① Combat Styles First

Pick your main style (Melee/Ranged/Magic) and push its attack/strength or ranged/magic level alongside Hitpoints. Use the Combat Achievements guide as a milestone tracker — early CAs double as combat training. Aim for 70+ in your chosen style before serious bossing; 80+ for most bosses comfortably.

Gear thresholds matter as much as levels. At 70 Attack/Strength with a Dragon Scimitar you can handle Scurrius and the Giant Mole; at 75+ with a whip or better you open Barrows and mid bosses; at 80+ with Barrows/God gear you are ready for Vorkath, Zulrah, and most GWD bosses. Train the style, then fund the weapon via the money branch — the two paths feed each other.

② Prayer & Slayer Are Core

Get 43 Prayer immediately (Protect prayers make bossing survivable). Then train Slayer — it's both combat XP and a money/gear source, and it unlocks boss tasks. Slayer is the backbone of a PvM account: it levels combat, drops gear, and feeds CAs. See the skill priority guide for the Slayer-vs-combat balance.

③ What to Skip (PvM Focus)

Skip pure money skills (Fishing/Hunter/Runecrafting) unless you need GP for gear — then dip into Thieving only. Skip Construction beyond a basic POH (gilded altar at 50 Construction is worth it mid-game). Everything non-combat waits until your PvM goals are funded. The mid-game guide covers the gear you're training toward.

5. 📜 Branch C — Quest / Completionist Focus

If you're chasing the quest cape or diaries, the order is dictated entirely by quest requirements. Train the skill a quest needs, right before you do that quest — never earlier.

① Quest-Required Skills Only

Quests gate on specific levels (e.g., 50 Mining for one, 65 Firemaking for another). Train each skill to exactly the required level, do the quest, move on. Spreading XP across many skills "just in case" is the trap — you waste hours on levels no quest needs yet. The optimal quest order sequences this perfectly.

② Tie Into Optimal Quest Order

The optimal order front-loads quests that grant huge XP rewards, which levels your combat and other skills for free. Following it means you barely train anything manually — quest rewards do the work. Skills you train are only the ones a specific upcoming quest demands. This is the most efficient path to a high quest point total with minimal grinding.

For example, a tightly ordered quest list can take a brand-new account to a quest point total in the hundreds and combat in the 60s–70s with almost no manual training, simply by chaining XP-rich quests in the right sequence and only stopping to train the exact level a next quest requires. The optimal quest order is the script; your job is just to execute it and train the minimum between steps.

③ What to Skip (Quest Focus)

Skip any skill not on a quest requirement path: Construction, Farming, Hunter, Runecrafting (unless a quest needs it), and most money skills. Your time goes into quest prep only. Once the quest cape is done, you can re-branch into money or PvM.

💡 Quest Order = Free Levels

Many quests give 10K–50K XP in a skill. Stacking them in the right order can take a skill from 1 to 50+ with zero training. That's why "train by quest requirement" beats "train by panel order" by a mile for completionists.

6. 🛋️ Skills to Idle / AFK

Some skills are perfect for background play — low attention, steady XP. Use them while watching something else or during downtime.

① The Best AFK Skills

  • Fishing (sharks/anglerfish): Near-zero attention, decent XP and GP.
  • Woodcutting (regular trees → redwoods): Classic idle skill.
  • Mining (motherlode mine): AFK-friendly, feeds Smithing.
  • Cooking (on a range): Fast, passive, supports everything.
  • Agility (rooftops): Semi-AFK, permanent run-energy payoff.

② How to Set Up AFK Training

Use the most click-light method available (a range for cooking, a full inventory of one fish type, the motherlode mine for mining). Turn on XP trackers and check periodically. The point of AFK skills is they don't compete with your active goal time — they fill the gaps.

Good AFK setups also double as background GP. Cooking raw fish you already caught, or mining in the motherlode mine while watching a video, produces both XP and sellable resources with near-zero attention. Reserve your focused, alert sessions for the leverage skills (Thieving, your main combat style, quest-required levels) and let the idle-friendly ones run in the background. That split is what makes an efficient account feel low-effort.

③ When to AFK vs When to Focus

AFK skills are for your "leverage" skills that also happen to be idle (Fishing for money, Agility for energy) and for the slow skills you must eventually raise (Cooking, Woodcutting). Never AFK a skill that's actively blocking your goal — do those in focused sessions. The chart contrasts active vs idle value.

7. 🚫 Skills to Skip Early (The "Not Yet" List)

These skills are useful eventually but punish early active training. Skip them until a goal forces them.

① Why These Wait

Construction is brutally expensive for little early return. Farming is slow and only pays at scale. Hunter, Runecrafting, and Herblore are slow grinds that mostly pay at high levels. Training them at account level 3–70 is a time sink with weak payoff. They'll come — just not first.

The opportunity cost is the real reason to skip. Every hour on Construction at level 20 is an hour not spent on Thieving (which pays immediately) or combat (which unlocks bosses). Because early hours are your scarcest resource, spending them on slow, late-paying skills delays everything you actually want. Bank first, then buy your way through the slow skills later when GP is abundant and time is less critical.

③ Revisit Later, On Purpose

None of these are "never." When your goal shifts — you start bossing (Herblore), want a gilded altar (Construction 50), or chase GP (Hunter 80, RC 77) — pull them off the skip list deliberately. The mistake is training them before a goal exists, not training them at all. Let quests and AFK sessions handle the passive gains in the meantime.

🎯 The New-Player Skilling Loop

  1. Combat ~40–50 via quests (free XP).
  2. Thieving → 43 for Pyramid Plunder income.
  3. Agility → 50+ for run energy (AFK it).
  4. Pick your goal branch (money / PvM / quests).
  5. Train only the skills your branch demands.
  6. AFK the idle-friendly ones in downtime.
  7. Skip Construction/Farming/Hunter/RC/Herblore until a goal forces them.

8. ❓ New Player Skilling FAQs

What is the very first skill I should train on a new account?

Combat, but via quests rather than grinding. Quests like Waterfall Quest and Fight Arena give huge Attack/Strength/Defence/Hitpoints XP for almost no effort, getting you to ~40–50 combat fast. That unlocks Slayer and basic bossing, which everything else builds on.

Should I train skills in the stats panel order?

No. The panel order is cosmetic. Train by your goal: money, PvM, or quests. A new player who trains Cooking before Thieving, or Construction before Agility, wastes dozens of hours. Order by leverage, not by the list Jagex shows you.

What's the best early moneymaking skill?

Thieving to 43 for Pyramid Plunder. It needs almost no gear, is solo, and pays 100K–300K GP/hr at low levels — far better than fishing or woodcutting at the same account stage. It's the universal first income skill for nearly every account type.

Is Agility worth training early if it makes no money?

Yes. Agility raises your run energy recovery, which saves time on every single trip for the rest of your account's life. It's a "leverage" skill: no direct GP, but it makes all your other hours more efficient. Get to 50+ early, then keep it ticking up while AFK.

Should I do Slayer as a new player?

If you have any PvM ambition, yes — start Slayer once you have ~43 Prayer and decent combat. Slayer is combat XP, money, gear drops, and boss unlocks all at once. If you're purely a money or quest player, Slayer can wait, but most accounts benefit from it eventually.

When should I train Construction?

Not early. Construction is expensive and offers little until you build a Player-Owned House with a gilded altar (needs 50 Construction + a few hundred K GP) for fast Prayer training. Unless you want that altar, skip Construction until late game when GP is abundant.

Can I just AFK everything?

Some skills, yes — Fishing, Woodcutting, Mining (motherlode), Cooking, and Agility are AFK-friendly and great for downtime. But goal-blocking skills (your main combat style, Thieving for money, quest-required levels) deserve focused sessions. AFK fills gaps; it doesn't replace purposeful training.

Do I need to get every skill to 99?

No — and most players don't. A focused account might have a few 99s (the ones its goal demands) and leave others at quest-required or utility levels (Agility 50–80, Thieving 43–99, and so on). Chasing all 23 skills to 99 is a completionist goal, not a requirement. Train the skills your goal needs; let the rest sit wherever they naturally land from quests and AFK sessions.

What if my goal changes halfway through?

Re-branch. The universal first skills (combat, Thieving, Agility) help every account, so early time is rarely wasted. Then follow the new branch's order from where you are. The skip-list skills you avoided will still be there when your new goal needs them.

How is this different from the skill priority list?

The skill priority list is the quick ranked checklist; this guide is the reasoning and branching behind it. Read this to understand why skills are ordered, then use the list as a fast reference while playing. They're two views of the same plan.
