OSRS Ironman Early Game Guide 2026 — First 50 Levels, Quest Order & Gear

A brand-new Ironman is the most demanding start in OSRS: you cannot trade, cannot buy, and every piece of gear has to be earned. The good news is that the first 50 levels are almost entirely skippable. By completing the right quests in the right order you can go from a level 3 nobody to roughly 45 Attack, 30+ Strength and 31 Defence without a single hour of grinding. This guide gives you that order, the verified XP each quest pays out, the gear tiers you should be wearing at each stage, how to fund yourself when you cannot sell to players, and the unlocks — fairy rings, spirit trees, Explorer's Ring — that quietly save you dozens of hours later.

  • 📌 Quest XP beats grinding. The early melee quest chain alone gives about 65,000 Attack XP before you fight anything on purpose.
  • 📌 Level 43 Magic is the real gate. Fire Strike + safespotting lets you beat bosses you have no business beating.
  • 📌 You need 32 Quest Points to start Dragon Slayer I. Filler quests for QP are mandatory, not optional.
  • 📌 Fund yourself with skilling, not trading. Cowhides, flax, ores and early Farming carry you to your first 300K.
  • 📌 Do not skip the utility unlocks: Fairy Tale I (fairy rings), Tree Gnome Village (spirit trees), Lumbridge Diary (Explorer's Ring).
  • 📌 Bank everything. An Ironman cannot re-buy a drop they threw away — this is the #1 mistake.

1. 🧭 What "Early Game" Actually Means on an Ironman

On a main account the early game is a shopping trip. You kill a few cows, sell the hides, buy a scimitar and move on. On an Ironman the same stretch of the game is a logistics problem: you cannot trade with players, you cannot use the Grand Exchange, and you cannot pick up anything another player dropped. Every rune, every log, every piece of food has to come from your own hands.

That changes what "efficient" means. On a main you optimise XP per hour. On an Ironman you optimise XP per unit of self-supplied input — which is exactly why quests dominate the early game. A quest reward is XP you get without spending a single arrow, rune or shark that you had to gather yourself.

So the first 50 levels of an Ironman have one job: use quests to buy yourself combat stats, then use those stats to unlock the gear and travel networks that make everything afterwards cheaper. The rest of this guide is the order that does it fastest.

💡 The Core Principle

Quests first, gear second, grinding third. If you find yourself killing Hill Giants at level 12 for Attack XP, you have skipped about 40 free levels of quest rewards.

2. 🏝️ Step 0: Tutorial Island and the Lumbridge Setup

Nothing here is optional, but it is also only about 30 minutes of work.

📜 The Starter Checklist

  1. Finish Tutorial Island and pick Ironman at the final dialogue. This is a one-way door — Ironman status cannot be freely toggled on afterwards, and downgrading later has its own rules.
  2. Do the free starter quests immediately: Cook's Assistant, Sheep Shearer, Romeo & Juliet, Ernest the Chicken and Goblin Diplomacy. Together they are worth 16 Quest Points for maybe 40 minutes of walking.
  3. Start every free quest you can find just to hold them open. Quest logs are a checklist; half the early game is not forgetting what you already unlocked.
  4. Buy your first tools from Lumbridge and Varrock general stores: pickaxe, axe, tinderbox, fishing rod, small fishing net, hammer, chisel. You will use all of them within an hour.
  5. Set up a bank tab system from day one. Ironmen live and die by their bank organisation because you can never re-buy what you sold.

💡 Do Not Sell Anything Yet

On a normal account you would dump every junk drop into the Grand Exchange. As an Ironman the only thing you can sell is to shops, and shop prices are terrible. Hold your low-tier junk until you know which quests need it — a lot of early quests want "any axe", "a bucket of milk", "a pot of flour" and so on.

🔑 How to Actually Beat These Bosses at Level 3

  • Train Magic to 13 for Fire Strike. It is cheap, and it is the tool that makes every early quest boss trivial.
  • Safespot wherever the geometry allows. The Khazard Warlord in Tree Gnome Village, the Fight Arena bosses and the Black Demon in The Grand Tree can all be attacked from outside their reach.
  • Flinch when you cannot safespot. Step into melee range, hit once, step out. The Black Knight Titan in Holy Grail is the classic flinch fight.
  • Bring food you made yourself. Trout and salmon from your own fishing trips are free; do not waste your starter cash on shop food.
  • Do Witch's House early on Hardcore. That 6,325 Hitpoints XP is what stops a lucky hit from ending your account outright.

4. 🔥 Level 43 Magic — Your Boss-Killing Licence

Ask any experienced Ironman what single level matters most in the early game and the answer is usually 43 Magic. That is when Superheat Item comes online, and it does three jobs at once on an account that cannot buy anything.

📜 Why 43 Magic Changes Everything

  • Superheat Item = free Smithing. You turn your own mined ore straight into bars without a furnace, which is the backbone of early Ironman gear progression.
  • It doubles as Magic training. Every cast is both a Smithing action and Magic XP.
  • Fire Strike carries you to 43. Runes are cheap from shops and rewards, and strike spells are the standard early Ironman combat route.
  • It unlocks Ironman questing. Superheating is required for several mid-game quest item requirements you would otherwise buy.

Before 43, your Magic training is strike spells on low-defence monsters — anything you can hit reliably without wasting runes. After 43, you pivot to superheating gold ore, which is the classic Ironman Magic-plus-Smithing combo: mine your own gold, then superheat it into bars for Crafting and later jewellery.

💡 The Trusted-Rune Rule

On an Ironman your rune supply is finite, so never train Magic on high-defence monsters early. Splashing on something you cannot hit is the fastest way to burn a week of shop-stock runes in an afternoon.

5. 🎯 Getting to 32 Quest Points

Dragon Slayer I is the wall that divides "early Ironman" from "actual Ironman", and it will not open until you hold 32 Quest Points. Most players finish the melee quest chain sitting somewhere in the low twenties — which means you need a deliberate QP top-up.

The good news: QP filler quests are almost all free, short and requirement-free. The classic efficient set is Cook's Assistant, Sheep Shearer, Romeo & Juliet, Ernest the Chicken and Goblin Diplomacy, but the underlying principle matters more than any specific list: sort your quest log by Quest Points and work down it, prioritising anything with zero skill requirements.

🗺️ QP Routing Tips

  • Bundle quests by region. Do everything in and around Varrock in one trip, then everything around Ardougne, and so on. Walking is the real time cost.
  • Unlock teleports before you need them. Spirit trees from Tree Gnome Village and fairy rings from Fairy Tale I turn long walks into two clicks — do them before grinding QP, not after.
  • Read the reward, not the length. Some 30-minute quests give 5 Quest Points; some 90-minute quests give 1. Quest Points per hour is the metric.
  • Note the combat requirements. Dragon Slayer I itself needs to be finished against Elvarg, so arrive with food, an anti-dragon shield and a plan.

💡 Scimitars, Not Longswords

Until you reach the Dragon longsword, scimitars win almost everywhere: faster attack speed means more hits, and more hits means more XP per hour on the low-defence monsters you will be fighting. The only early exception is a mace or warhammer against something with unusually high Defence.

7. 🧰 Which Skills to Train, and in What Order

An Ironman's early skill order is driven by what the next quest requires, not by what makes the prettiest XP rate. In practice the priority looks like this.

📊 Recommended Early Skill Priority

  1. Magic to 43. As covered above, this unlocks Superheat Item and your boss-killing toolkit.
  2. Agility to 25, then higher. 25 is a hard gate on The Grand Tree. Beyond that, every Agility level is more run energy, and run energy is the currency you spend all day.
  3. Fishing and Cooking in parallel. You need food, you cannot buy food efficiently, and both skills feed each other. Train them together on the same trip — catch it, cook it, bank the surplus.
  4. Mining and Smithing. Your entire melee gear progression comes out of this pair. Add Crafting later for jewellery and d'hide.
  5. Prayer. Free Prayer XP from Holy Grail gets you started; after that, big bones from your own Slayer tasks are the realistic source.
  6. Slayer, as soon as combat allows. Slayer is where Ironman supplies come from long term — it is the skill that eventually replaces the Grand Exchange.

Two skills are deliberately not on that list yet: Herblore and Runecrafting. Both are slow to start and neither blocks anything you need in the first 50 levels beyond a couple of quest requirements. Put them off until you have a stable supply chain and a comfortable bank.

8. 💰 How to Fund an Ironman Without Trading

New Ironmen consistently underestimate this problem. You cannot sell to players, so your income is limited to shop sales and quest rewards — and shops pay a fraction of real value. The practical target for the early game is simple: get to roughly 300,000 GP in liquid cash, which is enough to buy runes, tools, shop stock and quest items without constantly detouring to make money.

SourceWhy It Works for Ironmen

💡 Never Buy What You Can Make

The single biggest Ironman money leak is buying finished goods from shops — cooked food, bars, potions. Every one of those has a self-supplied equivalent that also trains a skill. Spend cash on things you genuinely cannot produce yet: runes, quest items, and the occasional tool.

9. 🚪 The Utility Unlocks That Save You 50+ Hours

These are not combat content and they give almost no XP, which is exactly why new Ironmen postpone them. Do not. Each one permanently reduces the cost of everything else you do.

📜 Do These Before You Grind

  • Tree Gnome Village → spirit trees. A free fast-travel network that shortens a dozen later quests.
  • Fairy Tale I → fairy rings. Arguably the single most valuable early unlock on any account. Fairy rings reach places no teleport spell does.
  • Lumbridge & Draynor Diary (easy) → Explorer's Ring. Free run energy restoration and a free low-level alch — both are quality-of-life multipliers early.
  • Ardougne cloak (easy diary). An unlimited teleport to a central hub with a bank, altar and shops nearby.
  • Dramen staff / staff of air combo. The standard fairy-ring kit; sort it out at the same time as Fairy Tale I.
  • An Amulet of glory, eventually. Charging it via the Fountain of Heroes (Heroes' Quest) matters when you cannot buy a new one every week.

One more unlock deserves a mention: the Nightmare Zone. Once you have a stock of your own absorption potions, NMZ becomes the classic Ironman AFK combat-training venue, and it is often where players finish their first 99s. It is not an early-game activity, but knowing it is coming should shape how you value Prayer and Herblore.

10. 🚫 Common Early Ironman Mistakes

  • Throwing drops away. The #1 Ironman regret. If your bank is full, make a second tab — do not drop something "you will never need".
  • Doing quests in arbitrary order. Waterfall Quest first is not a suggestion; it is the difference between reaching 30 Attack in an afternoon and reaching it in a week.
  • Ignoring Agility. No run energy means every trip takes twice as long, forever. It is the most invisible and most important early investment.
  • Training on high-Defence monsters. Both melee and Magic waste enormous amounts of time on monsters your level cannot reliably hit. Pick low-Defence targets.
  • Buying food and potions. You are supposed to be making those. Shop prices on an Ironman budget are ruinous.
  • Skipping the utility unlocks. Fairy rings and spirit trees are not side content; they are infrastructure.
  • Rushing Slayer too early. Slayer is excellent, but doing it before your combat stats can handle the tasks wastes far more time than it earns.
  • Forgetting Hardcore risk. On a Hardcore Ironman, do Witch's House early and always carry a teleport. Dying is permanent.

💡 What Comes Next

After 50 the Ironman game changes shape. Desert Treasure opens Ancient Magicks, Recipe for Disaster opens Barrows gloves, and Slayer turns into your permanent supply line. The quest-heavy approach does not stop working — it just gets slower and more specific.

12. ❓ Ironman Early Game FAQs

Q: What is the very first quest an Ironman should do?

Waterfall Quest. It has no skill requirements and hands out 13,750 Attack and 13,750 Strength experience, which takes a fresh account from level 1 to around 30 in both stats instantly. Doing it before anything else is the single biggest time saving available to a new Ironman.

Q: Can I do these quests at combat level 3?

Most of them, yes. Waterfall Quest, Vampyre Slayer, Tree Gnome Village, Fight Arena and Witch's House are all commonly completed on brand-new accounts. The trick is safespotting with low-level Magic such as Fire Strike, and bringing your own food. A few (The Grand Tree, Haunted Mine) have Agility or Crafting gates rather than combat gates.

Q: How do I beat Elvarg on an early Ironman?

Arrive with the best food you can produce yourself, an anti-dragon shield (non-negotiable, or the fire breath will delete you), and a melee weapon you are comfortable with. Many players finish it right after Holy Grail so they have the Defence XP cushion. On a Hardcore Ironman, bring a teleport and do not gamble.

Q: Is it worth training Magic before melee on an Ironman?

Yes — for the quests, not for the combat XP. Getting to 13 Magic for Fire Strike and eventually 43 for Superheat Item unlocks the ability to safely defeat early quest bosses and to smelt your own bars. The melee stats come from quest rewards anyway.

Q: How much money does an early Ironman actually need?

Around 300,000 GP in the bank covers runes, tools, quest items and shop stock comfortably through the first 50 levels. You get there through cowhides, flax into bowstrings, surplus ore and bars, and surplus fish — never through player trading, because you cannot trade.

Q: Should I do Slayer early on an Ironman?

Start it as soon as your combat stats can handle the tasks you get, but do not make it your focus before then. Slayer is the long-term replacement for the Grand Exchange because it supplies herbs, seeds, bones and gear, so it becomes essential in the mid game — just not at level 20.

Q: Do I need 32 Quest Points for Dragon Slayer I?

Yes. Dragon Slayer I requires 32 Quest Points before you can start it, which is why experienced Ironmen deliberately route cheap requirement-free quests in parallel with the melee quest chain instead of leaving the QP grind until the end.

Q: What Agility level should I aim for early?

25 is the hard requirement for The Grand Tree, so treat that as your first milestone. Beyond it, keep going when you can — Agility levels convert directly into run energy, and run energy is the limiting factor on nearly every early Ironman task.

Q: Is Group Ironman early game different?

Somewhat. Group Ironmen can share resources within their group, which removes a lot of the self-sufficiency pressure in this guide. But the quest order, the XP table and the utility unlocks are identical — the group just changes how you fund yourselves, not what you should do.

Q: What is the biggest mistake new Ironmen make?

Dropping items. On a normal account a junk drop is nothing; on an Ironman, the item you threw away at level 15 can be a quest requirement at level 60, and you have no way to buy it back. Bank everything, organise by tab, and never sell to a shop unless you are certain you can re-obtain it.

Q: When should I start bossing on an Ironman?

After Dragon Slayer I and Lost City, with a Rune scimitar or Dragon longsword and a full set of dragonhide. The realistic first targets are low-mechanic bosses and Slayer bosses that you can reach, and the entry boss progression route matters far more than raw combat level.
