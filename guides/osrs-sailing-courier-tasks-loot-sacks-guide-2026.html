OSRS Sailing Courier Tasks & Loot Sacks Guide 2026 — GP, Tiers & Best Ports

Published September 12, 2026  |  12 min read  |  By Yanchun Wu

Courier Tasks used to be the least rewarding way to train Sailing — you delivered crates, you got XP, you moved on. The August 26, 2026 update changed that by adding Loot Sacks to every completed task, with five tiers that scale on both the task level and how far you actually sailed. This guide covers how the sack tiers work, what the published GP ranges look like, every port's thematic supply drop, and an honest read on which ports are worth deliberately routing toward.

1. 📦 Why Courier Tasks Finally Pay in 2026

When Sailing launched, Courier Tasks were the honest-but-boring option: pick up crates from a ledger table, sail to the destination port, deposit the crates, collect XP. The reward was a coin bag and a rare shot at Shark Paint. Functional, but nobody ran them on purpose.

The August 26, 2026 update fixed the incentive problem by attaching a Loot Sack to every completed Courier Task. The sack is not a flat bonus — it scales on two axes: the level of the task you completed and the distance you actually sailed to finish it. That means the tasks that used to feel like the biggest time sink are now the ones that pay the most, because long-haul deliveries push you toward the higher sack tiers.

💡 Why This Matters More in 2026

Sailing is a relatively young skill, and Jagex has been steadily rebalancing its training methods. Courier Tasks now sit in a useful middle ground: less click-intensive than the Barracuda Trials, more rewarding than pure AFK salvaging, and — critically — they now generate real sellable output instead of just XP.

Be honest with yourself about the ceiling, though. Jagex has not published GP-per-hour figures, and the sacks alone will not out-earn established money makers. What they do is convert a skill you are probably training anyway into something that pays a little on the side. Treat it as a value-add on your Sailing grind, not a replacement for a dedicated money maker.

2. 🧭 How Courier Tasks Work: Cargo, Ledger Tables & Task Slots

Courier Tasks are one of the two port task types, alongside Bounty Tasks. The loop is short but has a few rules that catch new players out:

📋 The Courier Task Loop

  • Collect the cargo from the ledger table. Both hands must be free to pick up a crate.
  • Some tasks need more than one crate, so you may need several trips between the ledger table and your boat's cargo hold.
  • Sail to the destination port.
  • Withdraw every crate from the cargo hold and deposit them at the destination ledger table.
  • Collect your reward — XP plus, now, a Loot Sack.

Task Slots Unlock With Level

At level 1 Sailing you can only hold one port task of either type at a time. More slots unlock as you level, which matters because it lets you queue several deliveries instead of running them one at a time:

🎰 Port Task Slots by Level

  • Level 1: 1 task slot
  • Level 7 — 2 task slots
  • Level 28 — 3 task slots
  • Level 56 — 4 task slots
  • Level 84 — 5 task slots

Two rules are worth memorising. First, the level requirement to accept a task is not boostable — a Sailing boost will not let you take a task above your real level. Second, you are restricted to using your own boat for Courier Tasks, so you cannot borrow a friend's hull to farm high-tier deliveries.

💡 Do Not Teleport With Cargo

Cargo is confiscated immediately if you teleport while carrying it, and relocating a boat that is holding cargo — by summoning it or sampling sealed crates — empties the holds. Finish the delivery, then teleport. Trying to shortcut the route costs you the whole task.

3. 🎁 Loot Sack Tiers: Tiny to Huge and What They Pay

Loot Sacks come in five tiers — Tiny, Small, Medium, Large and Huge — and which one you receive depends on the level of the task completed and the distance travelled to finish it. Higher-level tasks and longer routes push you up the ladder.

Inside the sack, the roll is split roughly four-to-one:

🎲 What Is Actually Inside

  • 4 in 5 chance — straight GP. Jagex has published the extremes: a Tiny sack rolls 800–1,200 GP, while a Huge sack rolls 6,400–9,600 GP.
  • 1 in 5 chance — a supply bundle. Delivered all at once when you open it, and themed to whichever port issued the task.

💡 Read Published Numbers Carefully

Jagex has only confirmed the Tiny and Huge GP ranges. The Small, Medium and Large figures have not been officially published, so any exact GP-per-sack table you see online for the middle tiers is an estimate, not a confirmed value. Treat those numbers with suspicion until Jagex posts them.

The supply bundles can include a wide spread of boat-building and skilling materials: dragon sheets, nails from bronze up to dragon, cannonballs from iron up to dragon, planks from normal up to ironwood, bars from bronze up to rune, and ores from copper up to runite. Because the roll is randomised, a single sack can feel underwhelming while the next one pays for several tasks — judge them on the average, not on one opening.

Shark Paint deserves a special note. Jagex originally said it would move into Loot Sacks, then reconsidered: it remains a separate drop on completing any Courier Task so that it does not replace loot you would otherwise have earned. That means your Shark Paint chances are unchanged by the update — they are simply no longer the only reason to run a task.

4. 🗺️ Port Thematic Drops Ranked: Which Ports Are Worth It

This is the part most players will miss. The supply roll is not generic — each port has its own thematic drop, and the difference in value between the best and worst ports is enormous. Taking a task from a food port can hand you bananas; taking one from the right port hands you crystal shards or Zulrah's scales.

🟢 High Value — Prioritise These Ports

  • Prifddinas — Crystal shards
  • Port Tyras — Zulrah's scales
  • Cairn Isle — Uncut gems, from opals up to diamonds depending on sack tier
  • Lunar Isle — Astral runes

🟡 Medium Value — Solid, Situational

  • Jatizso — Ores from copper up to rune
  • Void Knights' Outpost — Bronze to rune full helms, platebodies and platelegs
  • Corsair Cove — Bronze to rune scimitars
  • Port Roberts — Cannonballs from bronze up to dragon
  • Hosidius — Compost, supercompost or ultracompost buckets
  • Port Sarim — Caskets
  • Red Rock / Summer Shore — Corals and coral fragments, elkhorn up to umbral

🔴 Low Value — Skip If You Are Optimising GP

  • Musa Point — Bananas
  • Ardougne — Cake or chocolate cake
  • Brimhaven — Pineapples
  • Catherby — Raw cod, tuna, lobster, bass or sharks
  • Sunset Coast — Buckets of sand
  • The Pandemonium — Cocktails

There is a neat design detail here worth appreciating: Jagex added port-specific drops after player feedback, specifically so that running the same routes repeatedly would feel less monotonous. If you are optimising purely for GP, that means the port matters more than the task — a medium-tier task from Prifddinas is worth more than a large-tier task from a banana port.

💡 Practical Routing Advice

You cannot always choose which port issues a task, but when the board gives you a choice, weight it toward the gem, scale, shard and rune ports. When your route allows it, also prefer longer travel distances — distance is the second scaling factor for the sack tier, so a longer delivery from a high-value port is the best combination available.

5. 📈 The XP Side: Distance, Round Trips and Crewmates

Courier Task XP is driven by distance travelled. The important consequence: a round-trip delivery grants double the XP of a one-way trip covering the same route. If you accept a task in Catherby, pick up cargo in Port Sarim and deliver back to Catherby, you earn twice what you would for accepting in Port Sarim, picking up there and delivering to Catherby.

That creates a genuine efficiency trick: because you can teleport yourself and relocate your boat before loading cargo, tasks where the noticeboard and the cargo location are in different places offer more XP per hour. You are effectively paid for the distance without having to sail it yourself.

👥 Crewmates Can Carry Your Crates

You can order crewmates to carry crates between the ledger table and the cargo hold, which removes a lot of the manual back-and-forth on multi-crate tasks. Crewmates with a deckhandiness stat of 3 or 4 can carry two crates at a time. Relevant hires and their Sailing levels include:

  • Jobless Jim (40) and Ex-Captain Siad (50) — two crates each
  • Adventurer Ada (55), Cabin Boy Jenkins (60) — one and two crates respectively
  • Oarswoman Olga (65), Jittery Jim (70), Bosun Zarah (80)
  • Jolly Jim (85) — two crates; Spotter Virginia (90), Sailor Jakob (95)

Also worth noting: Courier Tasks received a 10% Sailing XP increase earlier in 2026, on top of an earlier ~15% buff to tasks from level 40 and above. Combined with the Loot Sack change, Courier Tasks are in the best state they have ever been for players who want steady XP with a side of GP.

💡 Boat Handling Got Better Too

The same update smoothed boat alignment on offshore fishing spots — they no longer have a south-western bias, so approaching from any direction behaves consistently, and your boat is no longer considered overlapping a spot if it only clips the outer tiles. Small change, real quality of life if you combine fishing with your sailing routes.

6. 🗓️ Slotting Courier Tasks Into Your Sailing Grind

Courier Tasks work best as a layer on top of training you are already doing, not as a standalone grind. Here is how to fold them in without wasting trips:

⏰ A Practical Routine

  • Load your task slots before you set off. Once you unlock multiple slots, queue several deliveries so you are never sailing empty.
  • Prefer long-distance, high-value-port tasks when the board gives you the choice — they hit both sack scaling factors at once.
  • Bank your sacks instead of opening them. They are stackable, so batch-open them at the end of a session to see your real average return.
  • Use the noticeboard-versus-cargo mismatch to bank extra XP by teleporting ahead and relocating your boat before loading.
  • Delegate crate hauling to a deckhandiness 3–4 crewmate so multi-crate tasks do not eat your time.

The key habit is to never finish a session with empty task slots. Courier rewards scale with task level and distance, so the players who see the best returns are simply the ones who keep the pipeline full rather than running one task at a time.

💡 Pair It With Bounty Tasks

Courier and Bounty Tasks share the same slot pool. If you are chasing sea creature uniques, Bounty Tasks are the better use of a slot; if you want passive GP and supplies alongside your XP, Courier Tasks are now a legitimate choice rather than a filler option.

7. 🧠 9 Tips, Tricks & Common Mistakes

✅ Do This

  • Prioritise high-value ports — Prifddinas, Port Tyras, Cairn Isle and Lunar Isle beat every food port comfortably.
  • Take the longest route available when you have a choice, since distance is a sack-tier scaling factor.
  • Bank your sacks and open them in batches so you can judge the real average rather than one bad roll.
  • Use a deckhandiness 3–4 crewmate to carry two crates at a time on multi-crate tasks.
  • Keep your task slots full at all times — idle slots are wasted sack potential.
  • Accept the round-trip version of a task when it is offered, because it grants double XP.
  • Teleport and relocate your boat before loading cargo on noticeboard-versus-cargo mismatched tasks to bank extra XP.

❌ Avoid This

  • Teleporting while carrying cargo — it is confiscated immediately and the task is wasted.
  • Relocating a boat that is holding cargo — summoning it or sampling sealed crates empties the holds.
  • Grinding Courier Tasks purely for GP. The sacks are a value-add, not a top-tier money maker — set your expectations accordingly.
  • Trusting exact GP figures for the middle tiers. Only the Tiny and Huge ranges are officially published.
  • Taking tasks from food ports when a gem, scale or shard port is on the board.

💡 The Single Best Habit

Before you accept a task, ask one question: which port issued it? If the answer is a food or material port and a better one is available, reroll. The port choice affects your supply roll more than the sack tier does, and it costs you nothing but a click.

8. ❓ Courier Tasks & Loot Sacks FAQs

Q: What are Loot Sacks in OSRS Sailing?

Loot Sacks are rewards added to every completed Courier Task in the August 26, 2026 update. They come in five tiers and contain either GP or a bundle of supplies themed to the port that issued the task.

Q: How many Loot Sack tiers are there?

Five: Tiny, Small, Medium, Large and Huge. Which tier you receive depends on the level of the task you completed and the distance you travelled to finish it.

Q: How much GP does a Loot Sack give?

Jagex has confirmed a Tiny sack rolls 800–1,200 GP and a Huge sack rolls 6,400–9,600 GP. The middle tiers have not had official numbers published.

Q: What are the odds of getting GP versus supplies?

Roughly a 4 in 5 chance of GP and a 1 in 5 chance of a supply bundle, based on Jagex's published breakdown.

Q: Which port gives the best Loot Sack supplies?

Prifddinas (crystal shards), Port Tyras (Zulrah's scales), Cairn Isle (uncut gems up to diamonds) and Lunar Isle (astral runes) are the standouts. Food and material ports such as Musa Point and Ardougne are the weakest.

Q: Can I stack Loot Sacks?

Yes. Loot Sacks are stackable and work similarly to Hunter Rumour loot sacks, so you can bank them and open them in batches rather than mid-task.

Q: Did Shark Paint move into Loot Sacks?

No. Jagex reconsidered and kept Shark Paint as a separate drop on completing any Courier Task, so it does not replace any loot you would otherwise have earned.

Q: How many Courier Tasks can I hold at once?

One at level 1, two at level 7, three at level 28, four at level 56 and five at level 84. The level requirement to accept a task is not boostable.

Q: Do round trips give more XP?

Yes. XP is based on distance travelled, so a round-trip delivery grants double the XP of a one-way trip over the same route.

Q: Is training Sailing for Loot Sacks worth it for money?

Not on its own. The GP per hour from Loot Sacks alone does not compete with dedicated money makers, and Jagex has not published full GP-per-hour figures. The sacks are best treated as a valuable side reward on training you are doing anyway.
