Hacked, Then Banned for Botting: How to Prove It Wasn't You (2026)

Updated August 19, 2026  |  15 min read  |  By Yanchun Wu

Someone broke into your OSRS account, ran a bot on it, and Jagex banned it for macroing — with you none the wiser until you tried to log in. This is the worst-case account scenario, but it is recoverable if you do things in the right order: secure the account first, then prove the botting wasn't you, then recover, then lock it down so it never happens again. Start with our account recovery after hack guide, then use this page for the botting-ban specific appeal.

📅 Updated: August 2026 — Account Recovery & Appeals · 19 min read
🛡️ Level: All
🆕 NEW

When an account is hijacked and used for botting, Jagex sees the same automation signature as a willing rule-breaker — the system does not know the keyboard changed hands. Your appeal therefore has two jobs the ordinary macro appeal does not: first, prove the account was compromised (unusual logins, password reset you did not do, items you did not move), and second, prove the botting happened during the hijack window, not under your control. Our false macro ban guide covers the botting side in general; this page is the hacked-specific version, where the timeline of the hack is your strongest evidence.

If the attacker also traded your gold away or bought/sold GP, you may additionally face an RWT flag — handle that with our RWT ban appeal guide in parallel. And if any clean account shares your login or IP, read our chain ban guide before you lose more than one account.

⚡ Quick Verdict

  • 📌 Secure before you appeal. Changing the password and stripping tokens stops the attacker from botting further while you wait.
  • 📌 The hack timeline is your evidence. Unusual logins + a reset you didn't do = proof it wasn't you.
  • 📌 Two claims in one letter: "I was hacked" AND "the botting was during the hack."
  • 📌 Recovery is real when the compromise evidence is clear — Jagex does reverse bot bans caused by hijack.
  • 📌 Prevent the sequel. 2FA + clean device + unique email, per our hack prevention guide.

1. 💀 The Nightmare Scenario: Hacked, Then Bot-Banned

Understanding how you got here tells you what evidence to gather. The attack usually follows a predictable chain.

① How the hijack happens

A phishing link, a reused password from a breached site, a malware keylogger, or a "free membership" scam steals your credentials. The attacker logs in, often from another country, disables or ignores your authenticator if they already had a session, and changes the registered email so you cannot get back in. By the time you notice, they may have already bonded the account or moved items.

② The botting window

Instead of just stripping the bank, many modern hijackers run a bot on the account to farm GP they can extract or sell. That is when Jagex's macro detection fires — on automation the attacker initiated, not you. The ban lands on the account, and the attacker is long gone. You discover it when your login fails or you see the "Macroing" disable screen.

③ Why the ban looks deserved to Jagex

From Jagex's side, the account exhibited textbook bot behavior: inhuman click cadence, 20-hour sessions, impossible XP gains. The system has no field for "but it was stolen." Your entire appeal is about inserting that missing context with evidence the reviewer can verify: login locations that are not you, a password reset timestamp you did not trigger, and a gap in your own play history.

④ Why speed matters after discovery

The longer a hijacked, bot-banned account sits with the attacker's session still live, the more damage accrues — more botting, more RWT, more links to bad clusters — and the harder your "it wasn't me" story becomes. Acting within hours of discovering the breach (secure, then appeal) keeps the compromise window clean and short, which is exactly what a reviewer wants to see. A four-week delay before you even change the password looks like you were fine with the activity, even if you were not. Move the moment you notice something wrong.

⚠️ Do Not Log In and "Fix It" First

If you still have access, do secure the account (Section 2) — but do not delete the attacker's bot progress or pretend the weird activity never happened. The odd session data is your evidence. Changing the password and removing unknown devices is fine; scrubbing the history is not needed and can look like covering up.

2. 🔒 Step 1 — Secure the Account Before Anything Else

The single most common mistake is appealing while the attacker still has access. They keep botting, the ban hardens, and your appeal looks like a botter covering tracks. Secure first.

① Change the password and recover the email

If you can reach the registered email, reset the OSRS password immediately from the official recovery page. If the attacker changed the email, use the account recovery form to reclaim it with your original details (creation date, past passwords, old transaction IDs). Our recovery after hack guide walks the exact recovery steps. Lock the email account itself with its own 2FA too.

② Remove unknown devices and the authenticator

Strip every device and linked authenticator you do not recognize. Jagex lets you remove the authenticator via the recovery process if the attacker added their own. The goal is to ensure that when you win the appeal, the attacker cannot immediately hop back in and re-ban the account. A recovered account with the hijacker's session still live is a account that gets banned again within a week.

③ Scan and clean your own devices

Run a full antivirus / anti-malware scan on every device you used to log in. A keylogger on your PC is how the attacker got in; if you recover the account but the keylogger remains, they will simply steal the new password. Change the password only after the device is clean, or you are handing them the new one too.

3. 🧬 Step 2 — Build Your "It Wasn't Me" Evidence Chain

A hacked-then-bot-banned appeal wins on a chain of facts, not a single denial. Gather these so the reviewer can follow the story from breach to ban.

Gap in your normal play historyYour recall of when you last playedThe botting window was a period you weren't on

Clean linked accounts' behaviorYour other accounts' historyThe botting was isolated to the compromised one

① Pin the hack window

Identify the dates you last played normally and the first sign of strange activity (a login alert, a missing item, the ban email). That window — when the bot ran — is the heart of your evidence. State it plainly: "Between [date] and [date] I did not log in; all activity then was the hijacker."

② Prove ownership, not just innocence

Reviewers also need to know you are the real owner reclaiming the account, not the hijacker appealing their own ban. Include original creation details or a past transaction ID you can recall. Ownership + hack timeline together are very persuasive.

③ Keep it consistent with recovery claims

If you also filed an account-recovery request, use the same dates and story. Contradictions between your recovery form and your appeal get both rejected. One consistent narrative across every Jagex interaction is the rule — see our recovery-denied reapply guide for the same principle.

💡 The Hack Timeline Is Gold

Reviewers can verify login locations and reset timestamps on their side. When your letter says "I was in [country] and saw a login from [other country] I didn't make," they can confirm it. That confirmation is what separates a hacked account from a lying botter — lean on it heavily.

4. ✉️ Step 3 — Write the Hacked + Botting Appeal

This letter does double duty. Copy, fill the brackets, send through the official appeal form once you have secured the account.

① The category line

Reason: "Macroing." Statement: Appeal — account was hacked and botting occurred during the hijack; I am the rightful owner and have now secured it.

② The body template

Account display name: [your name]
Account created: [year]
Ban category shown: Macroing
Date of ban: [DD/MM/YYYY]

I am the rightful owner appealing a macro ban. The botting was
done by a hacker, not me. My account was compromised:

- I last played normally on [DATE]. Between [DATE] and [DATE]
  I did not log in; all activity then was the hijacker.
- I received a login alert / password reset I did not trigger
  from [location/IP] on [DATE].
- The hijacker moved items/GP I did not move: [describe].
- I have since reset the password, removed unknown devices,
  and cleaned my PC of malware.

I am the original owner (created [year], details on file). The
macro behavior was during the hijack window only. I have secured
the account so it cannot happen again. Please review the login
and reset records, which show the compromise.

Thank you for restoring a long-owned, hijacked account.

6. 🛡️ After Recovery — Prevent a Second Attack

Winning the appeal is half the battle. Most "hacked again" cases happen because the player re-secured weakly. Lock it down for good.

ActionWhy it mattersPriority
Enable the authenticator (2FA)Blocks 99% of credential-stuffing loginsCritical
Unique password, not reused anywhereStops breaches on other sites unlocking OSRSCritical
Dedicated email for the accountIsolates the recovery path from your main inboxHigh
Keep the device malware-freeKills the keylogger that caused the first hackCritical
Never enter details on fake "Jagex" sitesPhishing is the #1 entry pointHigh
Watch login alertsEarly warning if someone tries againMedium

① Authenticator first, always

The moment the account is restored, enable the official authenticator and back up the code offline (not in the same email a hacker could reach). This single step prevents the vast majority of re-hijacks. Our hack prevention guide has the full lockdown checklist.

② Break the password-reuse chain

If the password you lost was used on other sites, change those too — a single breached forum password is how most OSRS accounts fall. Use a password manager so every account gets a unique, strong password without memorizing them all.

③ Treat the device as guilty until scanned

Do not log the recovered account in from a PC you have not scanned. A surviving keylogger will simply capture the new credentials and the cycle repeats. Full scan, then change password, then log in — in that order.

④ Watch, don't just set and forget

Hardening is not a one-time chore. Turn on login alerts and actually read them; if you see a login from a country you have never visited, you can act the same day instead of discovering it via a second ban. Periodically re-scan your device and rotate the password. The accounts that stay safe long-term are the ones whose owners treat security as a habit, not a checkbox they ticked once after a scare. A recovered account left unmonitored is a recovered account waiting to be lost again.

💡 Recovery Without Hardening = Temporary

Jagex can restore the account, but they cannot stop the next hijack if your setup is unchanged. The players who get hacked twice are the ones who skipped 2FA. Harden immediately on recovery — it is the difference between a story and a habit.

7. 🚫 If the Appeal Is Denied

A denied hacked-account appeal is often a first-pass close, especially if the hack evidence was vague. Strategy below.

① Check whether you proved the hack clearly

If your first letter just said "I was hacked" without dates, login locations, or a reset you didn't do, the reviewer had nothing to verify. Your move: resubmit with the specific hack window and the verifiable signals (login alerts, reset emails) named explicitly.

② Add your recovery reference

If you also filed an account-recovery request, reference its ticket number in the appeal so the two are linked. Consistency between recovery and appeal is what flips a denial — see our recovery-denied reapply guide. Use the same dates and story in both.

③ Protect the rest and accept if needed

If two clean appeals fail despite real hack evidence, pause and consider a longer wait before a third try. Meanwhile, secure your other accounts so the hijacker's network does not take them — our chain ban guide and Ironman appeal guide cover linked-account protection. If the attacker also committed RWT on the account, run the RWT appeal in parallel rather than hoping one letter covers both.

④ Don't let frustration drive a bad move

A denied appeal is emotionally brutal, especially when you are innocent. But the worst reaction is to buy a replacement maxed main from an RWT site out of anger — that instantly creates a second account with a real, admitted ban, and often one already linked to the hijacker's cluster. Channel the frustration into a tighter second appeal instead. Patience protects more of your portfolio than a rushed, rule-breaking purchase ever will, and it keeps you from explaining a fresh ban you caused yourself.

⚠️ One Story, Every Channel

If your recovery form says the hack was in March and your appeal says April, both get rejected as inconsistent. Write the dates once, memorize them, and reuse them verbatim across recovery, appeal, and any follow-up. Consistency is itself evidence of truth.

8. ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Can Jagex really tell my account was hacked and bot-banned?

Yes. They can see login locations, password/email resets, and session patterns that contradict your history. When your letter's hack window matches those records, reviewers routinely reverse bot bans caused by hijack. The evidence is on their side — you just have to point them at it.

Should I secure the account before or after appealing?

Before. Appealing while the attacker still has a live session lets them keep botting, which hardens the ban and can make your appeal look like a cover-up. Reset password, remove devices, scan PC, then appeal.

What if the hacker also did RWT on my account?

Then you may face both a macro and an RWT flag. Handle each with its own appeal: the macro one argues hijack, the RWT one argues the trades were the attacker's, not cash you received. Use our RWT ban appeal guide alongside this one, with consistent dates.

My first appeal was denied — is the account gone?

Usually not, if the hack was real. A vague first denial is often automated. Wait about 30 days and send one improved appeal with specific hack evidence (login alerts, reset timestamps, the window). Consistency with any recovery request is key.

Will enabling 2FA after recovery stop another hack?

Almost entirely. The authenticator blocks credential-stuffing and phishing-based logins even if your password leaks. Pair it with a unique password and a malware-free device, and re-hijack becomes very unlikely. Our hack prevention guide has the full list.

The attacker changed my registered email — can I still recover?

Yes, via the account recovery form using original details (creation date, past passwords, old transaction IDs). Jagex weighs those heavily. Our account recovery after hack guide walks the exact steps. Reclaim the email first, then reset the game password.

How long does a hacked-account bot ban take to resolve?

Typically 10–30 days for a first decision, same as any macro appeal, sometimes longer if recovery and appeal must be cross-checked. Secure the account immediately so nothing worsens while you wait.

Could my other accounts be banned too?

If they share your IP, email, or payment method with the compromised account, yes — and if the hijacker also used them, definitely. Secure and unlink the clean ones promptly. Our chain ban guide explains the full graph so you protect everything you still have.

Do I need to delete the bot's XP/progress before appealing?

No, and you usually cannot while banned. The odd session data is your evidence of the hijack; leave it. Focus on proving the window was the attacker's, not on erasing it. Jagex restores the account as-is and trusts your security steps to prevent recurrence.
