1. 🔄 The 2026 Summer Sweep-Up: What Changed for Idle Earners

The Summer Sweep-Up was Jagex's largest cleanup of skilling numbers in years. The headline goal was to close the gap between "intended" GP/hr and what players actually earned, and that naturally touched a few methods people ran on autopilot. Understanding exactly what moved — and what did not — stops you from abandoning a method that is still perfectly good, and stops you from pouring time into a method that quietly lost half its value overnight. The panic after a sweep-up is usually worse than the actual change, because prices on the Grand Exchange wobble for a few days as nervous players dump stock regardless of the underlying math.

What actually got rebalanced

The methods that saw meaningful change were the "high GP for near-zero input" outliers: a couple of over-tuned farming side-products, certain ore/bankstanding AFK ticks, and a few Sailing provision nodes briefly over-rewarding at launch. None touched the core farming loops most players rely on. If your loop got hit, it was a fringe outlier, not herb runs or birdhouses. The key tell is this: Jagex targets methods where the reward-per-attention ratio is so high that no sane player would ever do anything else. Your herb run, by contrast, requires travel, teleports, compost, and a return trip 80 minutes later — it has built-in attention cost, so it is structurally safe from "too efficient" nerfs.

What stayed exactly the same

Herb runs, birdhouse runs, giant seaweed, tree and fruit tree runs, and farming contracts were left intact. They remain the most efficient passive GP in the game, and the 2026 update did not touch the Grand Exchange — so the GE flipping strategies you pair with passive income are just as strong. This matters because passive income and flipping share a philosophy: set something in motion, walk away, and let time do the work. A player who runs herb patches in the morning and places buy limits at night has two compounding income streams that barely overlap in attention.

Why sweep-ups create buying opportunities

When any balance change hits, the market overreacts before it understands the change. Players read a patch note, assume their method is dead, and liquidate the inputs — seeds, compost, saplings — at a discount. That is frequently the best moment to buy those inputs for your own runs, not to quit the method. The underlying demand for herbs, nests, and fruit never went away; only the short-term sentiment moved. Treat the first 72 hours after any sweep-up as noise, then re-evaluate the real per-run math once prices settle.

💡 Don't Over-React to Patch Notes

After any sweep-up, prices on the GE wobble for a few days as panicked players dump stock. That is often a buying opportunity for the inputs (seeds, compost) you need for passive runs — not a reason to quit the method. Recalculate your per-run profit after the dust clears, not during the panic.

The bigger lesson is that passive income is resilient by design. Because it is spread across many independent timers — herbs, birds, seaweed, trees, contracts, a Sailing voyage — no single nerf removes your income. You lose one stream, you keep five. That diversification is exactly why passive players weathered the 2026 changes with a shrug while single-method grinders scrambled. Build for redundancy and sweep-ups become background noise.

2. 🌿 Passive Methods That Still Work (and How to Run Them)

These are the methods to build your passive income around in 2026. Each takes minutes to set up and pays hours later. Run all of them and you have a near-constant passive drip of GP that requires almost no active concentration. The beauty of farming-based passive income is that the grow timers are long enough that you can do an entire day's worth of setup in one short session and then ignore the game for hours while the patches do the work.

🌿 Herb Runs — 100K–400K GP per Run (80-minute cycle)

Plant Ranarr, Snapdragon, or Toadflax in 5–9 herb patches, cast the patch with supercompost, and come back 80 minutes later. A full 9-patch Ranarr run can return 200K–450K GP depending on yield and GE prices. The active work is ~3 minutes per run. Do 6 runs across a day and you are already at 1M+ GP with under 20 minutes of effort. The magic number is the Trollheim patch from My Arm's Big Adventure — it can never become diseased, which removes your single biggest yield risk. Pair it with the Harmony Island and Hosidius patches reachable via fairy rings and you have a tight, low-risk loop. Our herb runs profit guide has the full teleport order and the exact compost math that doubles your average yield.

🐦 Birdhouse Runs — 10K–30K GP per Run (50-minute cycle)

Set up birdhouses on Fossil Island between your herb runs. Each run is ~2 minutes of work and returns bird nests (seeds, rings, and the occasional clue). The GP is modest, but the Hunter XP and the fact that it fits perfectly into the 50-minute gap between herb runs makes it free money. This loop was untouched by the sweep-up. The real value is not the 20K GP — it is the seeds. A birdhouse run quietly funds the next herb run's seeds, turning your passive system into a self-sustaining loop where one method's byproduct is another method's input. Stack it with a Feb 2025+ Fossil Island bank and the run drops to under 90 seconds.

🌾 Giant Seaweed — 50K–100K GP per Run (70-minute cycle)

Once you have 23 Farming, plant giant seaweed spores underwater at Fossil Island. Each run yields 30–50 seaweed worth ~1,000 GP each after a 70-minute grow. It is the highest-volume farming side-product and stacks naturally with your herb and birdhouse timers. With a T4 compost and the underwater seaweed speed buffs, the yield climbs further. Seaweed feeds the super-compost economy indirectly too: dry it, turn it into compost, and you reduce your herb-run input cost. Again, the loop feeds itself.

🌳 Tree & Fruit Tree Runs — 100K–500K GP per Harvest

Calquat, teak, mahogany, palm, and dragonfruit trees take hours to grow but cost almost nothing to manage. A single fruit tree run across 6 patches can return 150K–500K GP when the fruit is sold or used. Because the grow timer is long, these are the most "set and forget" of all farming income — check them once or twice a day. The strategic play is to plant trees before logging off for the night: by the time you return, they have grown and you collect on login. Palm and dragonfruit in particular hold strong GE value, and the wood has uses if you ever train Construction.

📜 Farming Contracts (Hespori)

The Farming Guild contracts give seed pack rewards and the occasional valuable drop, plus they funnel you free seeds to fuel the runs above. Completing contracts also progresses Hespori, which drops the bottomless compost bucket and useful seeds. This ties your passive loops together into one system. At 45 Farming the guild unlocks, and the contracts become a daily habit that pays you in the exact inputs your other runs consume. Treat the contract as the "management layer" of your farm — it is the one daily action that makes every other action cheaper.

Lower-level accounts with fewer patches will see 300K–800K GP/day, which still covers a bond within a couple of weeks, making passive income the fastest route from F2P to self-funded membership.

3. ⚙️ AFK Replacements If Your Old Loop Got Nerfed

If the specific AFK method you leaned on was one of the outliers adjusted in 2026, here are the safest replacements. These are stable, low-risk, and unlikely to be touched because they were already within intended GP/hr bands. The defining property of a good AFK replacement is a long gap between required clicks — if you must interact every few seconds, it is not really idle, and Jagex eventually targets the most extreme versions of those loops.

🐟 Fishing Monkfish — 80K–150K GP/hr (very AFK)

At 62 Fishing (after Swan Song), Monkfish is the gold-standard AFK fisher. Drop or bank the fish, watch a show, and collect 80K–150K GP/hr. It also trains Fishing, which feeds into future money makers like Anglerfish and Tempoross. If your old AFK loop was a banking ore tick that got trimmed, Monkfish is the most direct swap because the click pattern is similarly relaxed: you fish, you bank, you repeat, with long stretches where nothing is required of you. The catch rate at Monkfish is fast enough that the XP justifies the slightly lower GP/hr versus dedicated money methods.

🪓 Woodcutting Magic/Yew Logs — 80K–200K GP/hr (AFK)

Yew (61 Woodcutting) and Magic logs (75+) are classic AFK earners. Magic logs in particular sell for strong GP and also fuel Fletching, so the byproduct supports a second income stream. The attention needed is just occasional banking, making this perfect for background play. Redwood (90+) is the late-game pivot and is genuinely AFK because the tree is huge and the inventory fills slowly. The trade-off versus fishing is that you need higher Woodcutting, but the GP/hr ceiling is higher and the logs are always in demand for fletching training.

⛏️ Motherlode Mine — 60K–120K GP/hr (semi-AFK)

At 30 Mining, the Motherlode Mine in the Falador Dwarven Mine lets you bank without leaving the area and pays out in gold ore plus gems. It is slower than dedicated power-mining but requires almost no attention, which is exactly the AFK profile most idle earners want. The payout is "free" because you are also training Mining toward the 72+ needed for Amethyst and the 85+ needed for rune essence-rich content. For a brand-new member, Motherlode is the safest first AFK loop because the entry level is low and the area is safe.

💎 Amethyst Mining — 100K–180K GP/hr (semi-AFK)

At 92 Mining, Amethyst near the Mining Guild is a high-value, low-attention option. The ore is used for broad arrows, bolts, and javelins, keeping demand steady. It is the late-game AFK pivot once your stats climb. Because Amethyst requires a high Mining level, it is structurally safe from nerfs — Jagex rarely touches content that only a small, high-level minority can even access. The GP/hr is respectable and the Mining XP is among the best in the game, making it a dual-purpose loop for accounts building toward maxing.

💡 Match the Method to Your Attention

The best AFK replacement is the one you can actually leave running. If interrupted often, pick Monkfish (long gaps between clicks); if you can check every few minutes, Motherlode or Amethyst edge ahead. A method you abandon because it demands too much attention earns zero — pick the one you will actually finish.

The throughline is this: after a nerf, do not chase the next "most efficient" AFK loop, because that is exactly the one Jagex will revisit. Pick a method that is comfortably within intended bands, accept the slightly lower GP/hr, and keep it for the long haul. Consistency over a year beats a method that prints for a month and then gets cut. 4. 🚢 New 2026 Passive Angles (Sailing & Contracts)

The 2026 content slate added Sailing as a trainable skill, and with it a slower, steadier passive loop worth folding into your routine. While Sailing is more active than farming, certain provision and contract nodes reward you for logging in, submitting a voyage, and collecting later — a passive-shaped rhythm. Think of it as a tree run with a nautical skin: low input, delayed payoff, collect on your own schedule.

Sailing Provision Contracts

Some Sailing contracts pay out in tradeable provisions and resources with steady GE value. Setting a voyage and collecting the crate hours later mirrors a tree run: low input, delayed payoff. Early numbers suggest 50K–150K GP per day once your Sailing level supports the better contracts. The contracts that suit passive players are the long-duration voyages — you submit before bed and collect the next day, exactly like a palm tree. Short, attention-heavy voyages are for active players; ignore them for passive income.

Why this matters for passive players

The takeaway is diversification. A passive portfolio that leans only on Farming is fine, but adding a Sailing voyage and a couple of AFK skills means your account always generates something even when you cannot sit at the keyboard. Treat the 2026 additions as a supplement, not a replacement, for the farming core. The farming methods above are proven across years of balance changes; Sailing is new and will be tuned. Anchor your income on the proven core, then skim the new content for upside once its numbers stabilize.

⚠️ New Content Always Spikes Then Settles

Freshly released methods often show inflated GP/hr for the first week as prices peak. Don't pour all your time into a brand-new loop until the market settles — the passive farming core is proven and stable. The first-week numbers on any new skill are marketing, not reality.

There is also a meta angle: because Sailing is new, the early provision markets are thin and volatile, which means the items you collect can swing hard in price. If you are collecting tradeable provisions, watch the GE trend and sell into strength rather than hoarding. The discipline you use for farming outputs applies equally here — realize the GP, don't gamble on a price you cannot predict. As the skill matures, the passive voyage loop will settle into a dependable 50K–150K GP/day supplement that asks almost nothing of you.

5. 🗓️ Building a Realistic Daily Passive Routine

The power of passive income is consistency, not heroics. Here is a routine that fits around a normal day and requires roughly 25 minutes of active input for 1M–2.5M GP. The design principle is to cluster all your "setup" clicks into three short bursts and let the timers run in between. You are not playing the game for 25 minutes — you are touching it three times, briefly, and the game plays itself the other 23.5 hours.

Morning (5 minutes)

On login: plant herb patches, set 2–3 birdhouses on Fossil Island, plant giant seaweed, and submit a Sailing voyage. Then go about your day. This single five-minute burst sets every timer you will collect later. With fairy rings and the Trollheim patch unlocked, this is a clean teleport loop with almost no running.

Midday (5 minutes)

Collect the first herb run and birdhouses, replant immediately, and do a fruit tree check if a harvest is ready. This keeps the timers rolling so nothing sits at "ready" wasting grow time. The midday check is the one most people skip, and it is the most expensive skip — a patch sitting harvested for hours is GP not growing. Set a phone alarm if you must; the five minutes pays for itself many times over.

Evening (15 minutes)

Collect the final herb runs, seaweed, and any tree harvests. Bank everything, sell on the GE in bulk, and place your flipping buy orders so your GP keeps working overnight. The evening session is where you convert the day's harvest into liquid GP and set the overnight flip — it is the only "work" part of the day, and even it is mostly clicking "sell" and "buy limit."

📊 Sample Daily Payout

  • 6 herb runs @ ~250K avg = 1.5M GP
  • 8 birdhouse runs @ ~20K = 160K GP
  • 4 seaweed runs @ ~75K = 300K GP
  • 2 fruit tree harvests @ ~250K = 500K GP
  • Sailing + AFK overflow = ~200K GP
  • Total ≈ 2.6M GP for ~25 min of work

6. 💡 Turning Passive GP Into Active Wealth

Passive income is the floor, not the ceiling. The smartest players use their passive drip to fund active methods that earn 10x faster, then reinvest those gains back into scaling passive runs (more patches via quests, better seeds, more Sailing contracts). The loop is: passive pays the subscription, active builds the bank, the bank buys gear that unlocks even better active methods, and the better active methods fund more passive infrastructure.

Fund your bond and quests first

Use the first week of passive GP to secure membership if you ever lapse, and to knock out Farming quests like My Arm's Big Adventure (Trollheim patch — never diseased) and Fairytale I (fairy rings). Every quest unlock raises your passive ceiling permanently. The Trollheim patch alone removes your biggest herb-run risk; fairy rings cut travel time in half; neither ever expires. These are one-time investments of quest effort that pay passive dividends forever. The F2P to P2P bond guide shows exactly how the first bond pays for itself from passive runs.

Then graduate to active money

Once passive income covers your bond, point your active playtime at the higher-GP methods in our best money making methods guide and the Slayer money making guide. The goal is a loop: passive pays the subscription, active builds the bank, and the bank buys gear that unlocks even better active methods. A player who runs herbs in the morning and farms Kree'arra in the evening has both a safety net and a growth engine. The passive layer means a bad RNG night on a boss costs you nothing — your herbs still sold.

💡 Never Let GP Sit Idle

Idle GP in your bank earns nothing. Either reinvest it into seeds/supplies for tomorrow's runs or park it in GE buy limits. Even 1M sitting in Nature Runes overnight is working for you. Liquid GP is a tool; parked GP is a missed turn.

The reinvestment mindset is what separates a passive player who stays poor from one who gets rich slowly. Every GP you earn should either (a) buy tomorrow's inputs, (b) buy a bond, (c) buy gear for active methods, or (d) sit in a flipping buy limit. GP that does none of those four things is dead weight. Audit your bank weekly: anything that has been "sitting" for more than a few days should be redirected into one of those four channels. The compounding effect over months is enormous — a player recycling 1M/day through flipping buy limits at a 5% margin is quietly earning 50K/day of pure sleep income on top of the farming.

7. ⚖️ Passive vs Active: Which Should You Run?

A common question is whether passive income is "worth it" versus just grinding an active method. The honest answer is they are not competitors — they are layers of the same portfolio. But it helps to see them side by side so you allocate your attention correctly. The trap is thinking you must pick one. The players who build the biggest banks run passive as the non-negotiable floor — it pays the bond and covers bad weeks — and layer active methods on top when they have the time and attention. If you only have 25 minutes a day, passive wins decisively. If you have a free Saturday, an active bossing session on top of your passive floor doubles your weekly intake. This is the core of passive portfolio management: identify the dominant stream, harden it, and let the rest run on autopilot.

8. ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Do passive methods still beat active methods after the 2026 update?

No single method beats dedicated active bossing for raw GP/hr, but passive income wins on effort. After the sweep-up, herb runs, birdhouses, and seaweed are untouched and still return 1M–2.5M GP/day for ~25 minutes of work. Use passive as your baseline and layer active methods on top. The comparison in Section 7 shows exactly how the two fit together rather than compete.

What is the minimum Farming level for good passive income?

You can start birdhouses at level 5 and giant seaweed at 23. Herb runs become strong at 32+ (Ranarr). Fruit trees need 27+. Realistically, aim for 45+ to unlock the Farming Guild contracts, which tie the whole system together and supply free seeds. Even at level 5 you are already earning from birdhouses, so there is no reason to wait.

Can F2P players earn passive income?

Not really — farming patches, birdhouses, and Fossil Island are members-only. F2P players should focus on the active methods in our beginner guide (such as Wine of Zamorak) and treat passive income as a reason to grab membership. Even one bond's worth of passive runs pays for itself quickly, which is why the F2P to P2P guide recommends passive farming as the payback loop.

How much GP per day is realistic passively in 2026?

For a mid-level member with decent Farming, 1M–2.5M GP/day is realistic with roughly 25 minutes of active input. Lower-level accounts with fewer patches will see 300K–800K GP/day, which still covers a bond within a couple of weeks. The number scales with how many patches and timers you have running, so every quest unlock lifts it.

My AFK method got nerfed — what should I switch to?

If your old loop was a banking AFK tick, move to Fishing Monkfish (62 Fishing) or Motherlode Mine (30 Mining). If it was a skilling outlier, the farming core above is the most stable replacement and also trains a skill while it earns. Avoid chasing the next "most efficient" AFK loop, because that is the one Jagex will revisit — pick a method comfortably within intended bands.

Is supercompost really worth the cost on herb runs?

Yes, decisively. Supercompost roughly doubles your average herb yield and prevents disease, which is the single biggest passive GP multiplier on the farm. The extra seed cost is tiny next to the yield gain, and the bottomless compost bucket from Hespori makes it nearly free once you are farming contracts. Skip compost only if you enjoy leaving GP on the table.

Should I sell my harvest immediately or wait for a price spike?

Sell on a normal schedule rather than hoarding for spikes. Passive income works because it is steady; trying to time the GE turns a reliable stream into a gamble. The one exception is after a sweep-up, when panicked dumps create genuine buying opportunities on inputs — but that is buying seeds cheap, not holding your harvest hoping it rises. Realize the GP and redeploy it.

How do I fit passive income around a busy schedule?

Cluster setup clicks into three short bursts (morning, midday, evening) as described in Section 5. Set phone alarms if needed. The entire active commitment is ~25 minutes, and the midday check is the only one easy to skip — protect it, because a harvested patch sitting idle wastes grow time. Passive income is designed for busy players; that is the entire point.

Does the new Sailing skill replace farming for passive income?

No. Sailing voyages add a useful 50K–150K GP/day supplement, but the farming core is proven across years of balance changes and should remain your anchor. Treat Sailing as upside once its numbers stabilize, not as a replacement. New skills spike then settle, so anchor on what has survived every previous sweep-up.

9. 🎯 Final Tips — Passive Wealth, Minimal Effort

  • Run timers, not sessions. Passive income rewards logging in 3–4 times a day for 5 minutes far more than one 2-hour grind.
  • Always supercompost your herb patches. It doubles average yield and prevents disease — the single biggest passive GP multiplier.
  • Unlock fairy rings and the Trollheim patch early. Travel time is the only real "cost" of passive runs; cut it wherever you can.
  • Pair passive GP with overnight flipping. Park end-of-day profits in GE buy limits so your bank grows while you sleep.
  • Don't chase new content spikes. Stick to the proven farming core; add Sailing voyages only after prices settle.
  • Reinvest into quest unlocks. Every Farming quest you complete permanently raises your passive ceiling.
  • Protect the midday check. A harvested patch sitting idle is GP not growing — set an alarm if you must.
  • Never let GP sit idle. Idle GP earns nothing; route it into seeds, a bond, gear, or a flipping buy limit.
