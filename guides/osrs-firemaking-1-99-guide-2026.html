1. 🎯 Why Train Firemaking in 2026

Firemaking is often dismissed as a "waste" skill, but it punches above its weight in 2026 for three reasons:

🔥 Three Big Payoffs

  • Wintertodt money: The minigame is the single best low-requirement money maker for mid-level accounts, paying 100K–300K GP/hr indirectly through supply crates.
  • Firemaking cape: A free glow when you hit 99, and the skill itself is a quest requirement for a handful of later content.
  • AFK-friendly: Unlike most skills, you can train it almost passively, making it ideal for filling dead time.

If your account is mid-game and you have not done Wintertodt yet, Firemaking should be high on your to-do list. It pairs perfectly with Woodcutting — the logs you chop can become the logs you burn.

💡 The Golden Rule of Firemaking

Never buy logs to burn unless you are in a hurry. Chop them yourself, or — better — run Wintertodt, where the "burning" is automatic and you get paid for it. Buying logs just to light a fire is the most expensive way to train.

2. 🪵 Firemaking Basics & What You Need

Firemaking could not be simpler. Equip a taverly / regular tinderbox (free from the tutorial or a general store), click a log, then click the ground. Each log gives a fixed amount of XP based on its tier.

  • Tinderbox: Required for every method. Keep one in your tool belt or inventory.
  • Logs: Dropped by trees (via Woodcutting) or bought on the Grand Exchange.
  • Bank space: For the early method you want to be near a bank/ring of fire spots.
  • Wintertodt requirements (recommended): 50 Firemaking to enter, but 1 HP risk is real — bring food.

3. 🌱 Early Game (1–50): Banking & Burning Logs

From level 1 to 50, the classic method is to stand near a bank and light logs one by one. It is cheap (or free with your own wood) and mostly AFK.

① Levels 1–15: Regular & Oak Logs

Burn normal logs from trees around Lumbridge, or buy a few hundred oak logs (~20 GP each). You will hit 15 in minutes. Burn them at the Grand Exchange for instant banking.

② Levels 15–30: Oak & Willow

Willows become available at 30, but oaks fill the gap nicely. A fast route: chop willows at Draynor or bank and burn them. Willows are the F2P favorite because they are cheap on the GE (~10–15 GP) and burn quickly.

③ Levels 30–50: Willow Logs

This is the sweet spot for free-to-play and early members. Buy ~10,000 willow logs (~120K GP total) or chop them yourself in an hour. Light them at a bank; you are at 50 in roughly 2–3 hours of light clicking.

🔥 Light in a Ring for Efficiency

Drop logs in a 3x3 ring and light the center, then move outward. This "fire ring" trick lets you keep clicking without waiting for old fires to clear, noticeably speeding up the early grind.

4. ❄️ Mid Game (50–75): Wintertodt Entry

At level 50 you unlock Wintertodt, the Winter RPG minigame in the Northern Tundra. This is where Firemaking stops being a chore and becomes a money printer.

How it works: You feed bruma roots into the brazier to keep the boss suppressed, fletch roots into kindling for bonus points, and fight off attacks. Your Firemaking level determines your XP and rewards — you do not manually light logs, the game does it for you.

🧊 Wintertodt Setup (level 50+)

  • Bank preset: 4–6 lobsters or better food, a hammer, a knife, and a tinderbox.
  • Strategy: Chop roots → fletch into kindling → feed brazier. Repair and heal the pyromancer when needed.
  • Reward: Each kill gives a supply crate with seeds, herbs, food, and sometimes the Pyromancer outfit pieces.
👕 Grab the Pyromancer Outfit First

Every Wintertodt crate can roll a piece of the Pyromancer outfit. Wearing the full set gives +2.5% Firemaking XP. Prioritize completing the set before pushing to 99 — it speeds up every hour after.

5. 🔥 Late Game (75–99): Wintertodt Grind

From 75 to 99, Wintertodt is unbeatable. With the Pyromancer outfit and good food, you can sustain near-constant kills.

① Expect 80K–110K XP/hr

Experienced runners average around 100K XP/hr with the outfit. A full 99 from 75 takes roughly 25–35 hours of Wintertodt depending on your consistency.

② Profit While You Train

Crates yield herbs (ranarr, snapdragon, torstol), seeds (snapdragon, torstol), and fish. Liquidated, a Wintertodt session often nets 150K–300K GP in supplies — so 99 Firemaking can effectively pay for itself.

③ Alternative: Redwood Logs (90+)

At 90 you can burn redwood logs (350 XP each) for a calm, AFK finish if you dislike Wintertodt. It is slower and costs more, but perfect for a second screen.

💡 Use the "Fletch Everything" Method

Always fletch roots into kindling before feeding — it roughly doubles your points per root and pushes your XP/hr higher. Skipping this is the #1 reason beginners get low XP at Wintertodt.

7. 🗺️ Recommended Path to 99

🏆 The Optimal Route

  1. 1–50: Burn willow logs at a bank (~2–3 hours, ~120K GP or free with your own wood).
  2. 50–60: Start Wintertodt. Build a bankroll and grab Pyromancer pieces.
  3. 60–75: Keep running Wintertodt; outfit should be complete by now.
  4. 75–90: Peak Wintertodt efficiency, ~100K XP/hr while profiting.
  5. 90–99: Wintertodt to 99, or switch to AFK redwood logs if you prefer.

Total time: roughly 35–50 hours depending on how much you AFK. Total cost: near zero if you Wintertodt, or ~8–12M GP if you buy every log. For most players Wintertodt makes 99 Firemaking one of the cheapest 99s in the game.

Want a faster combat-oriented account instead? Pair this with Melee Combat Training so you can survive Wintertodt with less food.

⚠️ Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Burning logs past level 50 instead of Wintertodt — That trades 80K–110K XP/hr and crate profit for a slow, costly grind. Switch to the minigame as soon as you hit 50.
  • Skipping the Pyromancer outfit — Each piece from Wintertodt crates adds XP, and the full set is +2.5% you're leaving on the table.
  • Not fletching roots into kindling — Feeding raw roots halves your points per root and drags your XP/hr well below average.
  • Entering Wintertodt with no food — The cold and attacks chip HP, and at 1 HP you can actually die. Bring a few lobsters minimum.
  • Buying every log instead of chopping — Purchasing logs is the most expensive route; use Woodcutting to supply your own nearly free.
  • Training Firemaking in the Wilderness — Lighting fires there invites player attackers and risks losing your progress and any loot you're carrying.

8. ❓ Firemaking FAQs

What's the fastest way to get 50 Firemaking as a new account?

Burn willow logs. Buy ~10,000 on the Grand Exchange (about 120K GP) or chop them yourself at Draynor. Light them in a ring at the Grand Exchange and you will be level 50 in 2–3 hours of light effort. Free-to-play players can do exactly this without membership.

Is Wintertodt really better than burning logs?

Yes, dramatically. From level 50 Wintertodt gives 80K–110K XP/hr versus 55K–75K for burning banked logs, and it pays you in supply crates worth 150K–300K GP/hr. Burning logs past 50 only makes sense if you hate the minigame or want passive training.

How much food do I need for Wintertodt?

Bring 4–6 lobsters (or better) at level 50. As your HP and Firemaking rise, you take less damage and need less food. Most experienced runners bring a few sharks or brews for safety but rarely eat more than 2–3 per kill.

Can I train Firemaking profitably as F2P?

Partially. F2P caps at willow logs (no Wintertodt access), so your profit is limited to the small value of leftover logs. The real money begins at member Wintertodt. As F2P, treat Firemaking as a cheap, AFK skill rather than a money maker.

Do I need the Pyromancer outfit?

Not strictly, but it adds 2.5% XP and is free from Wintertodt crates. Skipping it costs you hours over a full grind. Always fletch roots and open crates hoping for outfit pieces before pushing to 99.
