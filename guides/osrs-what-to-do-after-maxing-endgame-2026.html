1. 🕳️ The Post-99 Void: Why Maxing Feels Empty

You are not alone, and you are not "over" the game. The emptiness after maxing is a well-known psychological dip: for years your account had a built-in compass — "get the next 99." Remove the compass and the same 20 million XP of content feels like a fog. The fix is not to quit; it is to install a new compass made of endgame goals.

The mistake most maxed players make is logging in and asking "what should I do?" with no filter. Endgame OSRS has more content than the road to 99 — infinitely more, in PvM's case — but it is opt-in. Our Endgame Skill Training guide covers the efficient side; this page covers the direction side.

① The "no goals" trap

Without a chosen goal, every activity feels optional, so nothing gets done. Players drift into low-effort endgame skilling, get bored, and quit within a month of maxing. The players who stay for years are the ones who adopted a visible, measurable endgame target.

② Why the 99s hid the real game

The grind to 99 is largely solo and mechanical. Endgame OSRS is social, mechanical, and strategic: raids require teams, Combat Achievements require mastery of bosses you once feared, and Collection Logs reward obsessive consistency. Maxing was the tutorial for all of it.

③ You already have the toolkit

💡 Good news

A maxed account is the most capable thing in the game. Every endgame goal below is now available to you — you are not gated by stats anymore. The only gate is deciding which mountain to climb first. That is a luxury, not a problem.

2. 🧭 Endgame Goal Framework: Pick a North Star

Before you do anything, choose one "North Star" — a single prestigious goal that will take months and give you an identity. Everything else becomes side-content. This single decision ends the void.

① The two prestige standards

If you want respect from the community, the two gold-standard endgame goals are Grandmaster Combat Achievements and all Elite Diaries completed. Both are long, skill-testing, and visibly impressive on a profile. Pick one as your North Star and let the other be a natural byproduct.

② Match the goal to your fun

Not everyone likes PvM. If you hate bossing, your North Star might be the Collection Log or 200M XP in a favorite skill. If you love teams, it's raids and group content. The right goal is the one you'll still enjoy on a bad day.

③ Write it down and date it

Make the goal real

Put your North Star in your bank notes or a Discord bio: "Grandmaster CA by Q4" or "All Elite Diaries by next league." A written, dated goal converts vague intention into a project — and a project is exactly what replaces the 99 compass. Our Combat Achievements guide is the natural first stop for the PvM-minded.

3. 🏅 Elite Diaries & Combat Achievements

These two systems are the backbone of respectable endgame progression and they overlap beautifully: doing one tends to complete the other.

① Achievement Diary tiers

Every region has Easy → Medium → Hard → Elite tiers. The Elite Diaries are the prestigious capstone and reward excellent teleports, resource unlocks, and skilling bonuses. Our Achievement Diary guide walks each tier; treat "all Elites" as a 1–3 month project that also forces you to touch content you skipped.

② Combat Achievements tiers (Easy → Grandmaster)

Combat Achievements (CAs) are bossing challenges: kill speeds, no-damage runs, and brutal "GM" tasks like speed-killing the hardest content. The Grandmaster tier is one of the most respected flexes in the game. It requires you to actually master every major boss — which, conveniently, also makes you rich and raid-ready.

③ Why these are the "real" endgame

They convert stats into skill

A Max Cape proves you grinded. A Grandmaster CA or full Elite Diaries proves you play. That distinction is why these are the default North Stars — they are the clearest signal that a maxed account is also a good account.

4. 📦 Collection Log & Completionist Goals

If PvM isn't your thing, the Collection Log (CL) is the ultimate "completionist" endgame. It has no finish line in practice — new drops keep it alive for years.

① What the Collection Log tracks

The CL records every unique drop, boss pet, and rare item you've obtained. Completion percentage becomes a quiet, lifelong scoreboard. Chasing 100% (or just "all boss pets") gives structure to otherwise random bossing.

② Pet hunting as a sub-goal

Many endgame players re-run bosses specifically for pets. A pet is a permanent, tradable-or-bond-worthy flex and a natural CL milestone. It turns "I'm bossing for GP" into "I'm bossing for the journal."

③ The completionist identity

💡 A different kind of respect

High CL% players are a recognizable archetype in the community — the "completionist." If you like checklists more than speed-kills, lean fully into this. It pairs perfectly with endgame skilling for a low-stress, years-long journey.

5. ⚔️ PvM Progression: Bosses, Raids & Invocations

PvM is the only "infinite ceiling" content in OSRS. There is always a harder invocation, a faster kill, a better score. For many maxed players this is where the game truly begins.

① The boss ladder

Start where your gear allows and climb: entry bosses → mid bosses (Vorkath, Zulrah) → high bosses (Muspah, Hueycoatl) → elite bosses (Yama, Leviathan, Vardorvis, Whisperer) → the "big four" raid bosses. Each step teaches mechanics that the next step assumes.

② Raids and invocations

Tombs of Amascut (ToA) uses an invocation system that scales difficulty and reward almost without limit — 300+ invocation runs are a genuine end-game pinnacle. Chambers of Xeric and Theatre of Blood scale via mode and challenge runs. Group or solo, raids are the deepest PvM loop in the game.

③ The Colosseum & Inferno

The solo pinnacles

The Fortis Colosseum (and its Quiver reward) and the Inferno (Infernal Cape) are the two iconic solo endurance fights. Both are brutal, both are prestigious, and both are natural "I maxed, now prove it" projects. Our Colosseum guide and Inferno guide are your on-ramps.

6. 📈 Skill Mastery: 200M XP & Uniques

Maxing is 99 — the middle, not the top. True skill mastery is 200M XP, the real cap, and it is a multi-year commitment per skill.

① 200M in one skill vs all skills

Most players pick one favorite skill for 200M (a "200m skiller" identity) before attempting all 23. A single 200M is a monumental, highly respected grind; all 23 is among the rarest account states in the game.

② Skill uniques & accomplishments

Beyond XP, skills have prestige unlocks: the Music Box, Skill Capes (incl. the maxed/completionist variants), and record-times. Chasing these gives skillers a goal framework identical in structure to a PvMer's CA grind.

③ Efficient endgame training

💡 Don't waste the XP

Post-99 training should be deliberate. Use the most GP-efficient or time-efficient methods from our Endgame Skill Training guide so your 200M push doesn't become a gold sink that competes with your PvM gear fund.

7. 🏠 Construction, Wealth & Community Content

Two under-rated endgame pillars: your house and your people.

① Construction as an endgame project

A maxed Construction level lets you build the best POH (Portal Nexus, rejuvenation pool, spellbook altars, combat dummy). Many players treat "perfect POH" as a real goal — and it pays back in every future activity via teleports and restores. Our Construction guide covers the build paths.

② Wealth as a scoreboard

For some, the endgame goal is simply net worth — a bank worth billions, all skill capes displayed, a full wardrobe of rare cosmetics. Money-making at this stage shifts from "fund my membership" to "build a legacy bank." It's a valid, measurable North Star.

③ Clans, Leagues & seasonal content

Endgame is social

Join a clan for raids, group content, and shared goals. Leagues (periodic game-modes) and seasonal events reset the freshness entirely — many maxed mains treat League season as their favorite "new game" each year. Community turns a solo grind into a shared one.

8. 🔁 The 6-Step Endgame Loop

Here is the system that replaces the 99 compass. Run it weekly and the void never returns.

① Choose → ② Train → ③ Achieve

Each week, pick one sub-goal (a CA task, a diary step, a boss pet attempt), train specifically for it, and achieve it. Small, frequent wins keep momentum higher than rare big ones.

④ Collect → ⑤ Socialize → ⑥ Repeat

Bank the reward (CL entry, diary flag, GP), do it with or alongside people (clan raid, pet hunt stream), then reset the loop with a slightly harder sub-goal. This is how a Max Cape becomes a Grandmaster CA, a full CL, or a 200M.

9. ❓ After-Maxing FAQs

I just maxed and feel lost — is that normal?

Completely normal. The post-99 dip is well documented; it's a loss of direction, not a loss of interest. Install a North Star goal (Grandmaster CA, Elite Diaries, Collection Log) and the feeling evaporates within a week of having a project again.

What's the most respected endgame goal?

Grandmaster Combat Achievements and a full set of Elite Diaries are the two community gold standards. Beyond those, maxing Construction, high Collection Log %, and 200M XP are the long-term "legendary" pursuits.

I hate bossing — is endgame still for me?

Yes. The Collection Log, 200M XP skilling, Achievement Diaries (most of which are skilling/exploration), Construction, and money/wealth goals are all non-PvM endgame paths. Pick the one matching your fun.

How long does Grandmaster Combat Achievements take?

For a fresh maxed account, typically 3–9 months of focused PvM, depending on prior boss experience. It requires genuinely mastering every major boss — which is also why it's so respected and why it makes you rich along the way.

Should I do the Inferno or Colosseum first?

The Colosseum is generally considered the more approachable solo pinnacle and its reward (the Quiver) is broadly useful; the Inferno is the harder, more iconic Infernal Cape fight. Many players do Colosseum first as a skill-builder, then Inferno.

Is 200M XP realistic or only for elites?

A single 200M in one skill is achievable for any dedicated player over months-to-a-year. All 23 at 200M is among the rarest account achievements in the game — a true "end of the road" goal for completionists.

How do I stay motivated after the novelty wears off?

Use the 6-step loop (Choose → Train → Achieve → Collect → Socialize → Repeat) and do it with people. A clan, a pet-hunting group, or a League season converts a solo grind into a shared, accountable project — that's what keeps maxed players logging in for years.
