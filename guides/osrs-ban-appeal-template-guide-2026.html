📝 Ban Appeal Templates: Copy-Paste Responses That Work (2026)

Updated August 19, 2026  |  19 min read  |  By Yanchun Wu

A banned account is fixable — but only if the appeal says the right things. Below are ready-to-send templates for the four ban types players ask about most: RWT, macro/botting, hacked-account botting, and false positives. Each comes in a short polite version and a long detailed version, plus a line-by-line note on what each part of the message actually does for the reviewer.

Getting banned in Old School RuneScape is stressful, but the appeal form is your one real chance to be heard. This guide gives you copy-paste appeal text you can drop straight into the Jagex support ticket — no guessing, no filler. 💡 Quick Verdict

The appeals team reads thousands of tickets a week. The messages that win are short, factual, own up to real mistakes, and give the exact account name plus the offence shown in the ban message. The messages that lose are angry, vague, copy-pasted from a "guaranteed unban" website, or deny an offence the system is certain about. Use the templates below as a base, then tailor the bold parts to your real situation.

1. 🔍 How the Jagex Appeal System Works

Before you copy a template, understand what happens after you hit send. An appeal is not a live chat and it is not a debate — it is a written statement a human (or, for clear cases, an automated check) reviews against the logged evidence on your account. Knowing the flow stops you from making the classic mistakes: sending five tickets, arguing in the ticket, or promising to "never do it again" when the system has video-proof you did.

① Where you actually submit the appeal

Appeals go through the official Jagex Support site, not in-game, not on Twitter/X, and not through a Discord "unban bot." Log in to the blocked account (or the Jagex account that owns it), open the ban message, and click the appeal link. If you cannot log in, use the account recovery form — covered in our account recovery after hack guide. Submitting through the wrong channel is the fastest way to have your request ignored.

② What the reviewer sees

The reviewer sees your message plus the backend flags: the detected offence, the date, the rule broken, and often a snippet of the botting client, trade log, or chat that triggered the ban. This is why honesty matters — if the log shows a botting client and you claim "I was hacked," the contradiction alone can sink the appeal. Match your story to the evidence the system already has.

③ One account, one appeal, one decision

Most bans allow a single appeal. If it is denied, opening a new ticket about the same ban is usually auto-closed. Save your best, most complete message for the first try. The flowchart below shows the realistic path from "submit" to "outcome."

2. 💰 RWT Ban Appeal Templates

Real-World Trading (RWT) means buying or selling GP, items, or accounts for real money outside the official Bonds system. Jagex treats RWT as a serious, usually permanent offence. If you did it, the polite version shows accountability; the detailed version explains context that might reduce the penalty. If you were chain-banned for trading with a known RWT account, see our chain ban guide — your appeal wording is different.

① RWT — Polite Version (own the mistake)

Display name: [YOUR_DISPLAY_NAME]
Offence shown: Real-World Trading

Hi Jagex,

I am writing about the permanent ban on my account [NAME]. I accept that
I broke Rule 12 by trading GP for real money with another player. That
was a mistake and I understand why it is against the rules.

I have played this account for [X years] and care about it. I am not
asking to ignore what happened — I am asking for one chance to return to
the game and follow the rules going forward.

Thank you for your time,
[YOUR_DISPLAY_NAME]

What each part does: The display name lets the reviewer pull the right account instantly. Naming the exact offence shows you read the ban reason. "I accept" removes the denial fight the reviewer expects. The ask is clear and modest — you are not demanding, just requesting a chance.

② RWT — Detailed Version (context + first offence)

Display name: [YOUR_DISPLAY_NAME]
Offence shown: Real-World Trading
Ban date: [DATE]

Hi Jagex Support,

My account [NAME] was permanently banned for RWT. I want to give full
context so you can judge this fairly.

What happened: On [DATE] I sold roughly [AMOUNT] GP to another player for
real money through a Discord trade. I now understand this is Rule 12 and
it puts the economy and other players at risk.

Why I am appealing: This is the first offence on an account I have owned
since [YEAR]. I have [X] total level and have never been banned before.
I was short on time and made a poor choice — I regret it and will use
Bonds only from now on.

I am not disputing the rule. I am asking whether, given a clean history,
a final warning or a temporary restriction is possible instead of a
permanent loss. Either way, thank you for keeping the game fair.

Regards,
[YOUR_DISPLAY_NAME]

What each part does: Adding the date and amount shows specifics, not a generic plea. "First offence" and account age are the two facts that most influence whether a penalty is reduced. Framing the ask around "clean history" gives the reviewer a reason to pick a lighter outcome without you arguing the ban is wrong. For a deeper strategy, read the false RWT ban appeal guide if you believe the trade was mislabeled.

3. 🤖 Macro / Botting Ban Appeal Templates

Macroing (using third-party clients, scripts, or input automation) is the most common ban and one of the hardest to reverse when the detection is solid. The templates below work best if you have a real explanation — a legitimate client setting, a misunderstood plugin, or a genuine false positive. If you did bot, lead with honesty; if you did not, lead with the facts that prove it.

① Macro — Polite Version (honest, short)

Display name: [YOUR_DISPLAY_NAME]
Offence shown: Macroing / Third-party client

Hi Jagex,

My account [NAME] was banned for macroing. I used a third-party client
with plugins I now realise crossed the line, and I take responsibility
for that.

I have put many hours into this account and would value a second chance
to play fairly. I will stick to the official client only from here on.

Thanks for reviewing,
[YOUR_DISPLAY_NAME]

What each part does: "Third-party client with plugins" names the real cause without over-explaining. "Take responsibility" ends the denial phase immediately. "Official client only" is the single promise reviewers want to hear from a macro appeal.

② Macro — Detailed Version (false positive focus)

Display name: [YOUR_DISPLAY_NAME]
Offence shown: Macroing
Ban date: [DATE]

Hi Jagex Support,

I believe the macroing flag on [NAME] is a false positive and I would
like to explain why.

1. Client: I use the official client only. I do not run any bot, script,
   or automation software.
2. Play pattern: I was training [SKILL] by hand at [LOCATION]. My session
   times were [EXAMPLE], which are normal human hours, not 24/7.
3. Inputs: I click and move manually. There is no auto-clicker or mouse
   recorder on this PC.
4. Hardware: I play on [DEVICE], one machine, no shared or virtual setup.

I have never been banned before and have [X] total level. Please review
the input and session logs — I am confident they show manual play.

Thank you,
[YOUR_DISPLAY_NAME]

What each part does: The numbered list turns a vague "I didn't bot" into checkable claims. Session times and a single device are exactly the signals that separate a human from a bot. This version pairs directly with the false macro ban appeal guide, which explains which logs actually help your case.

4. 🔓 Hacked Account (Botting From Hack) Templates

This is the case players most often get wrong. If someone hijacked your account and used it for botting or RWT, the ban is real but the blame is not yours — and the wording must say that clearly. The key phrase reviewers look for is "unauthorised access": you are not appealing the offence, you are reporting that a stranger committed it on your account. Full recovery steps are in our account recovery after hack guide.

① Hacked — Polite Version

Display name: [YOUR_DISPLAY_NAME]
Offence shown: Macroing (after account hijack)

Hi Jagex,

My account [NAME] was banned for macroing, but I did not do it. The
account was accessed without my permission around [DATE] — I noticed
logins from places I was not, and my password no longer worked.

Whoever got in used the account for botting. I am the original owner and
ask that the ban be reviewed as unauthorised use, not my own action.

Thank you,
[YOUR_DISPLAY_NAME]

What each part does: "I did not do it" plus "accessed without my permission" establishes the hijack frame. Naming the date you noticed strange logins gives the reviewer a window to compare against the botting timestamps — if they don't match your history, that is strong evidence.

② Hacked — Detailed Version (with recovery proof)

Display name: [YOUR_DISPLAY_NAME]
Offence shown: Macroing / RWT (unauthorised)
Ban date: [DATE]

Hi Jagex Support,

I am the original owner of [NAME] and I am reporting unauthorised use.

Timeline:
- [DATE]: I could not log in; password had been changed.
- [DATE]: I recovered the account and enabled 2-step verification.
- [DATE]: I saw the macroing/RWT ban from activity while I was locked out.

Evidence I can provide:
- The email I used to recover the account.
- The new device/IP I now log in from (my own, stable one).
- The fact that the botting happened while I had no access.

I have owned this account since [YEAR] and never broke a rule myself.
Please treat this as a hijack, not my play. I have already secured the
account and will keep it that way.

Regards,
[YOUR_DISPLAY_NAME]

What each part does: A timeline beats a paragraph — reviewers can line it up against server logs. "Recovered + 2-step on" shows you fixed the hole, which matters because they will not lift a ban just to have it hacked again. This version dovetails with the banned botting account recovery guide.

5. ❌ False Positive / Wrongful Ban Templates

A false positive is when the system bans you for an offence you did not commit and did not result from a hack. Common causes: a laggy input pattern that looked like a bot, a trade wrongly tagged as RWT, or a shared IP at a cafe/school. Stay calm and factual — anger reads as guilt. The templates below are for when you are 100% certain you did nothing wrong.

① False Positive — Polite Version

Display name: [YOUR_DISPLAY_NAME]
Offence shown: [OFFENCE]
Ban date: [DATE]

Hi Jagex,

I believe my ban for [OFFENCE] is a mistake. I have never done this and
have always played by the rules on [NAME].

Please re-check the logs for my account. I am happy to answer any
questions about my recent activity to confirm it was normal play.

Thank you,
[YOUR_DISPLAY_NAME]

What each part does: Short and non-accusatory. "Please re-check the logs" is the right ask — it invites review instead of demanding reversal. Offering to answer questions signals confidence, not defensiveness.

② False Positive — Detailed Version

Display name: [YOUR_DISPLAY_NAME]
Offence shown: [OFFENCE]
Ban date: [DATE]

Hi Jagex Support,

I am appealing a wrongful [OFFENCE] ban on [NAME]. I did not commit this
offence and would like to provide context.

Recent activity before the ban:
- Training [SKILL] at [LOCATION] by hand, [DATES/TIMES].
- Trades: only GE and friends; no off-site money or item deals.
- Network: I was on [HOME/WORK/SCHOOL] Wi-Fi, a shared IP at [PLACE].

I suspect the shared IP or my play rhythm triggered a false flag. I have
never used bots, bought GP, or broken a rule. My account history is clean
since [YEAR].

Please compare the flagged session to my normal pattern — they do not
match. I appreciate you correcting this.

Regards,
[YOUR_DISPLAY_NAME]

What each part does: Listing trades, location, and network removes the usual suspects one by one. "Shared IP" is a known false-positive cause, so naming it helps. The ask — "compare flagged session to normal pattern" — is exactly what a reviewer can do. If your ban was an Ironman wrongly flagged, the Ironman ban appeal guide has extra wording for that mode.

6. ✍️ What to Write vs What NOT to Write

The templates get you 80% there. The last 20% is tone. Reviewers are people who read abusive messages all day; a calm, specific ticket stands out in a good way. The table below is the cheat sheet; the diagram after it shows the tone split visually.

Write This Avoid This Why
Your exact display name "my main account" with no name They cannot find you without the name
The offence from the ban screen "unban me idk why" Shows you read and understand the reason
"I made a mistake" if you did "this is a scam, I want a refund" Honesty is the only lever you have
Specific dates and places Vague "a while ago" stories Details are checkable against logs
One clear, polite ask Threats, demands, or bribes Threats get the ticket closed fast
Promises to follow the rules "I'll sue / post this everywhere" Public threats hurt, never help

7. ⏳ After You Submit — Timeline & Follow-up

You sent the appeal. Now the hard part: waiting without hurting your case. Here is what to expect and what to do (or not do) while it sits in the queue.

① Realistic wait times

Simple, clear false positives can resolve in a few days. Complicated RWT or macro cases with heavy evidence can take one to three weeks, especially during seasonal spikes. There is no paid fast-lane — anyone selling "instant unban" is scamming you, often the same people who got you banned in the first place.

② Do not spam the queue

Opening a second ticket about the same ban usually merges into or closes the first. If you have genuinely new evidence (for example, you found the recovery email proving a hijack), reply to the same ticket or reference the original ticket number. One clean message beats ten angry ones every time.

③ If it is denied

A denial is usually final for that ban. Your options are: accept it, or — only if you have brand-new proof the reviewer could not have seen — try the account recovery route in our recovery denied reapply guide. Do not recycle the same template expecting a different result; the system remembers the decision.

💡 One Last Check Before You Send

Read your message out loud. If it sounds like a real person explaining a real situation, send it. If it sounds like a script, a rant, or a bribe, rewrite it using the templates above. The first appeal is your best appeal — make it count.

8. ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Can I copy these templates word-for-word?

Yes — that is what they are for. But always fill in the bracketed parts ([YOUR_DISPLAY_NAME], dates, offence) with your real details. A template sent with blank brackets looks like spam and hurts you.

Should I admit to RWT if I actually did it?

If the system has the trade log, denying it just contradicts the evidence and weakens your case. Honest admission plus a clean history is your best shot at a reduced penalty. If you truly did not do it, say so with facts instead.

How many appeals do I get?

Most bans allow a single appeal. Use it well. Spamming new tickets about the same ban typically gets them closed without review, so put your strongest, most complete message in the first try.

My account was hacked and then banned for botting — what do I say?

Frame it as "unauthorised use," not as your own play. Give the dates you lost and recovered access, and note you enabled 2-step verification after. This is the hacked-account template in Section 4, and it pairs with the account recovery after hack guide.

Will promising to never do it again get me unbanned?

A promise alone does little if the evidence is solid. It helps most when combined with a first-offence history and a clear, honest explanation. Reviewers weigh account history and the strength of the detection far more than a vow.

Can I appeal a permanent ban at all?

Yes, permanent bans usually still have an appeal option in the ban message. "Permanent" describes the intended penalty, not a closed door. Whether it is lifted depends on the offence, your history, and how your message matches the logs.

Is it worth paying a "professional unban service"?

No. These services cannot do anything you cannot do yourself through the official form, and many are run by the same RWT or hacking groups that cause bans. They take your money and sometimes your account. Always use the official Jagex appeal channel only.

My Ironman was banned — does the wording change?

Slightly. Ironman accounts cannot trade, so an RWT or trade-based ban is unusual and worth highlighting. Mention the mode explicitly and see the Ironman ban appeal guide for wording that fits a locked, untradeable account.

What if my appeal is denied but I have new proof?

Reply to the same ticket or use the recovery route with the new evidence — do not just resend the old template. New, previously unseen proof (like a recovery email) is the only thing that tends to reopen a closed decision.

How long does a decision take?

Clear false positives can be a few days; RWT or macro cases with heavy evidence often take one to three weeks. There is no paid express option, so ignore anyone selling one.
