1. Ironman Boss Tiers Explained (Early / Mid / Late)

On a normal account you can buy your way through any gear wall the moment you have gold. On an Ironman you have no Grand Exchange, no trading, and no one to carry your consumable costs. That single constraint reshapes the entire boss progression. You do not climb the boss ladder by raw combat stats alone; you climb it by accumulating the gear and supplies that each later boss demands. A boss you could technically survive at combat level 90 is often a waste of time until you have the food, potions, and ammunition to kill it efficiently and repeatedly. The roadmap below is built around that reality.

Before we get to the order, you need a mental model of the three tiers. These tiers are not official game categories. They are practical bands that group bosses by the gear you need, the supplies they eat, and the account milestones they assume. Understanding the bands stops you from skipping steps and losing hours to under-geared deaths.

1.1 Early game bosses — the bootstrap tier

The early tier is everything you can do with barrows-tier or slightly better gear, modest combat stats (roughly 70s to low 80s), and supplies you farm yourself. This tier includes the Corrupted Gauntlet (CG), the God Wars Dungeon 1 (GWD1) bosses (Kree'arra, General Graardor, Commander Zilyana, K'ril Tsutsaroth), and the King Black Dragon (KBD). The defining trait of this tier is that the bosses are repeatable, low-commitment, and self-funding. They drop the supplies (bones, seeds, herbs, food, runes) that you would otherwise have to grind by hand, and they drop the upgrade pieces (armor seeds, godsword shards, dragon items) that bridge you into the mid tier.

🎯 What "early game" actually means

  • Combat stats: most melee and ranged stats in the 70s, with a couple pushed toward 80+ for specific content.
  • Gear: barrows, blessed d'hide, or crystal (from CG) — no raids gear yet.
  • Goal: bank a buffer of brews, restores, food, and ammo, and pick up your first major weapon upgrades.
  • Death cost: low. Losing a barrows setup hurts far less than losing a full raids loadout.

1.2 Mid game bosses — the efficiency tier

The mid tier is where your early-game farming pays off. Here you fight Vorkath, Zulrah, and the Nightmare (NM). These bosses demand real DPS checks and punish sloppy play, but they reward you with the gear that defines a mid-level Ironman: the dragon hunter lance or crossbow, the toxic blowpipe, the trident of the swamp, ancestral robes, and the nightmare staff. The mid tier is also where profit-per-hour and supply throughput peak. A well-geared Vorkath or Zulrah run refills your bank faster than almost anything in the early tier.

The transition into mid game is gated by two things: you must own a crossbow (rune or better, ideally dragon or the accursed one from the Gauntlet) for Vorkath, and you must have a reliable ranged or magic DPS option for Zulrah. If you skip the early tier, you will not have these, and Vorkath/Zulrah become supply sinks instead of supply fountains.

💡 Why the mid tier matters most

The mid-game bosses are the engine of your account. Vorkath alone can supply you with bones for prayer, alchables for cash, and resources for training. Zulrah funds your blowpipe and seeds. The Nightmare feeds your magic offense and drops the jar for a big construction/project boost. Treat this tier as a long-term farm, not a one-time unlock.

1.3 Late game bosses — the pinnacle tier

The late tier is the raids and the raid-adjacent bosses: Chambers of Xeric (CoX), Theatre of Blood (ToB), Nex, and the Chambers of Xeric on Challenge Mode (CoC), plus the harder solo content like the Corrupted Gauntlet at scale and the Tombs of Amascut (ToA) if you extend the roadmap. These bosses assume you already own mid-tier best-in-slot or near-best-in-slot gear. They are where you convert your accumulated power into the absolute top-tier items: the twisted bow, the scythe of Vitur, the tumeken's shadow, the ancestral set, and the harmony/elder maul brackets.

The defining trait of the late tier is team dependence and supply intensity. ToB and CoX are almost always done in teams, which means you need to be a competent, geared raider who does not bleed the team's supplies. Nex is one of the most supply-hungry bosses in the game and will bankrupt an under-prepared Ironman. This is why the roadmap forces you through early and mid first: by the time you reach the late tier you should be self-sufficient enough to fund the runs.

1.4 How to read the roadmap

The sections below give you (a) the gear checkpoints per tier, (b) the best supply bosses to start with, (c) a step-by-step order, (d) the account-feedback loop, and (e) the mistakes that waste the most Ironman time. Read it as a chain: each step removes a gear or supply blocker for the next. If you are tempted to jump ahead, re-read the gear checkpoint table and ask whether you can actually sustain the boss you want to skip to.

For each boss or boss group, the required gear is the minimum that makes the kill reliable and self-funding, and the "how to get it as Ironman" column tells you exactly where the upgrade comes from on a fresh account. Treat this table as your gatekeeper — do not move to a row until the row above it is done.

💡 The checkpoint rule

If a row's "required gear" depends on the boss itself (for example, the twisted bow comes from CoX), that is intentional. It means you first clear CoX with your mid-tier bridge gear, then upgrade. The bridge gear is always one tier below the boss. Never try to enter a late boss with only early-tier gear — the supply drain will stop your account.

Notice the pattern: almost every late-tier weapon is dropped by the content that uses it. That is the whole point of the Ironman ladder. You earn the bridge gear in the tier below, use it to unlock the next boss, and that boss hands you the real upgrade. The gear checkpoint table is therefore also your dependency map: read it top to bottom and you can see exactly which earlier boss pays for which later one.

3. Best First Bosses for Supplies

New Ironmen make one fatal error more than any other: they rush toward a "cool" boss (usually Vorkath or a raid) before they have a supply base. On a main that is fine — you buy brews. On an Ironman, your first bosses must be the ones that give you brews, food, potions, and ammo. These are the bootstrap bosses, and they are Corrupted Gauntlet, GWD1, and KBD.

3.1 Corrupted Gauntlet — the single best Ironman starter

The Corrupted Gauntlet deserves to be your first real PvM goal for three reasons. First, it is self-contained: you gather your own resources inside the instance, so it costs you nothing in outside supplies while you learn. Second, it teaches the exact skills every later boss needs — prayer flicking, inventory management, DPS timing, and movement — in a repeatable, low-stakes environment. Third, and most important for an Ironman, its drops are foundational: armor seeds become crystal armor (some of the best mid-tier defense in the game), weapon seeds become the blade of saeldor or the bow of faerdhinen, and the enhanced weapon seed is a long-term money and power spike.

CG is the boss that converts your early combat stats into real, permanent account power with zero tradeable cost. A few hundred CG kills give you crystal armor that you will wear into Vorkath, Zulrah, and even early raids. No other early boss does this much for so little input. If you only take one thing from this roadmap, let it be this: spend your first 100–200 hours getting comfortable in the Gauntlet.

🎯 Why CG bootstraps your account

  • Free practice: the instance supplies its own resources, so failed attempts cost almost nothing.
  • Crystal armor: best-in-slot-ish defense for several mid-tier bosses, farmed entirely by you.
  • Weapon seeds: blade of saeldor and bow of faerdhinen replace your barrows/Dharok setup permanently.
  • Skill transfer: the flicking and movement you learn here carry straight into every raid.

3.2 GWD1 — the four-boss supply engine

Once you can survive GWD1, the God Wars Dungeon becomes your consumable factory. Each of the four generals drops what you need to keep bossing: Kree'arra drops Armadyl ranged gear and feathers plus alchables; General Graardor drops Bandos melee gear, adamant and rune salvage, and charms; Commander Zilyana drops the Saradomin sword and a steady stream of seeds and herbs; K'ril Tsutsaroth drops the Zamorakian spear/hasta and the herb and seed supplies that feed your brews and restores.

The key insight for an Ironman is that GWD1 is not just about the gear — it is about the continuous herb, seed, and alchable income. A GWD1 trip ends with a full inventory of bones, seeds, herbs, and salvage you process into potions and gold. That processing pipeline is what keeps your bank stocked for Vorkath and Zulrah later. Do not treat GWD1 as a one-time Bandos grind; treat it as a recurring supply run.

💡 GWD1 kill-order tip

Most Ironmen start with Kree'arra (ranged, easier to safe) then move to Graardor once they have a godsword or whip. Build your Bandos through Graardor while you farm Armadyl on Kree. The two sets cover your melee and ranged mid-game needs completely.

3.3 KBD — the quiet workhorse

The King Black Dragon is easy to overlook because it is old content, but for an Ironman it is a quiet workhorse. KBD drops dragon bones (your prayer training fuel), the dragonbone necklace upgrade path, the draconic visage (which becomes the dragonfire ward or shield), and a steady trickle of dragon bolts and runes. Those dragon bolts are what you fire at Vorkath, and the visage is what protects you there. In other words, KBD directly pays for your first mid-tier boss.

KBD is also one of the safest bossing experiences in the game — with an anti-dragon shield and antifire potions you take almost no dragonfire damage, so your supply cost per kill is tiny. That makes it the ideal low-risk, high-reward supply farm while you build toward Vorkath. Pair KBD with your GWD1 and CG routine and you have a complete early-game supply triangle.

3.4 The bootstrap triangle

Put together, CG + GWD1 + KBD form a triangle: CG gives you gear and skills, GWD1 gives you bulk supplies and gear, and KBD gives you the anti-dragon tools and prayer fuel for Vorkath. No other three bosses prepare you for the mid tier as completely or as cheaply. Everything else in the early tier is optional; these three are mandatory.

4. Recommended Boss Order Walkthrough

This is the step-by-step path. It assumes a fresh Ironman that has finished the important quest bosses (e.g., Dragon Slayer II prerequisites, the quest that unlocks the Gauntlet, and the God Wars entry requirements). If you have not done those quests, do them first — quest bosses are free gear and unlock the whole ladder. Then follow these steps in order.

Step 1 — Master the regular Gauntlet, then Corrupted Gauntlet

Start in the regular Gauntlet to learn the room, the resource flow, and the Hunllef fight without the extra pressure. Once you can clear it comfortably, move to the Corrupted Gauntlet. Goal: a full crystal armor set and a weapon seed. This is your gear foundation and your combat-school graduation. Expect to die a lot at first — that is the point, and it costs you nothing.

Step 2 — Open GWD1 with Kree'arra, then Graardor

With crystal or barrows gear and 70+ Ranged, enter GWD1. Start on Kree'arra for Armadyl, then push Graardor for Bandos once your melee stats and food allow. Bank your first Armadyl and Bandos pieces. These two sets are your workhorse gear for the rest of the mid game.

Step 3 — Add KBD to the loop for dragon supplies

Slot KBD into your routine. Farm dragon bones for prayer, grab the visage, and stockpile dragon bolts. Keep your Gauntlet and GWD1 runs going — the goal now is to build a comfortable buffer: a few hundred brews' worth of herbs, a few thousand dragon bolts, and your first real prayer level investment.

Step 4 — Unlock Vorkath with your dragon crossbow

Once you own a rune or dragon crossbow and a stock of dragon bolts (from KBD and Zulrah prep), take on Vorkath. This is your first mid-tier boss. Vorkath will test your antifire management and your DPS, but it rewards you with bones, alchables, and the dragonbone and supply income that dwarfs the early tier. Vorkath also drops the dragon hunter lance and crossbow, which become your best melee/ranged options against dragons and several later bosses.

🎯 Mid-tier unlock checklist

  • Vorkath: DHCB or rune crossbow + dragon bolts, 78+ Ranged.
  • Zulrah: trident of the swamp or blowpipe, 75+ Magic/Ranged, antifire + antipoison.
  • Nightmare: trident/blowpipe, 75+ offense, 70+ Defence, brews.
  • Do not attempt any of these without the early-tier supply buffer behind you.

Step 5 — Add Zulrah for the blowpipe and trident

With Vorkath income stabilizing your bank, learn Zulrah. Zulrah drops the toxic blowpipe (mutagen), the trident of the swamp (scale), and a flood of valuable seeds and herbs. The blowpipe becomes your best ranged DPS for most content, and the trident becomes your default magic weapon. Zulrah also prints money through tanzanite scales and uniques, which funds your potion and ammo crafting.

Step 6 — Farm the Nightmare for magic power

Once you have a trident and decent magic stats, take on the Nightmare. The NM drops the nightmare staff, the jar of dreams, and a strong supply of herbs and seeds. The staff is a top-tier magic weapon that rivals the trident for many bosses, and the jar is a construction/account boost. NM is also where many Ironmen first earn their overload ingredients in bulk.

Step 7 — Enter Chambers of Xeric with bridge gear

Now you are ready for your first raid. Enter CoX with your DHCB/blowpipe/trident bridge setup, 77+ Prayer (rigour/augury), and a full supply kit. Your first CoX runs are about learning the rooms and banking points, not maxing loot. The reward is the twisted bow and the ancestral set, which are the gateway to the true late game.

Step 8 — Move to Theatre of Blood once scythe-ready

After CoX gear (especially a twisted bow and ancestral), step into ToB. ToB demands tighter mechanics and a bigger supply pool, but it drops the scythe of Vitur and the ghrazi rapier, the best melee weapons in the game. ToB is almost always teamed, so be a reliable, geared raider — your early-game practice in CG and GWD1 is what makes you that.

Step 9 — Tackle Nex for Torva

With raids gear and a deep supply bank, attempt Nex. Nex is brutally supply-hungry, so only go in when you can afford to lose a full overload + brew loadout repeatedly. The reward is Torva armor, the best melee defense in the game, and the completion of your best-in-slot melee setup.

Step 10 — Push CoX Challenge Mode as the flex

Finally, once you are maxed or near-maxed with all the above, attempt CoX Challenge Mode for the best-in-slot rewards and the prestige. This is the end of the core roadmap; from here you extend into ToA, the Gauntlet at scale, or whatever 2026 content drops.

💡 The order is a chain, not a menu

You can deviate (many Ironmen do Zulrah before Vorkath, or NM later), but the dependency direction never changes: early supply bosses fund mid bosses, and mid bosses fund raids. Skip a link and you will feel it as empty brews and wasted deaths.

5. How Bossing Feeds Your Account

The reason this roadmap works is that bossing on an Ironman is a closed-loop economy. On a main, you sell loot for gold and buy what you need. On an Ironman, your loot is what you need — you convert it directly into the next upgrade or the next supply stack. Understanding this loop is the difference between an Ironman that stalls at 1,500 total and one that reaches the late game smoothly.

5.1 Loot becomes gear

The most obvious link: boss drops become equipment. CG seeds become crystal; GWD1 drops become Armadyl and Bandos; Vorkath drops the lance and crossbow; Zulrah drops the blowpipe and trident; CoX drops the twisted bow and ancestral; ToB drops the scythe. Each piece is a permanent, account-wide power increase that makes the next boss faster and safer. This is compounding: every upgrade shortens the time to the next upgrade.

5.2 Loot becomes supplies

The less obvious but more important link: boss drops become the consumables that let you boss at all. Herbs and seeds from GWD1 and Zulrah become brews and restores. Bones from KBD and Vorkath become prayer levels. Alchables from every boss become the gold you spend on runes and quest requirements. Dragon bolts from KBD become Vorkath ammo. The supply link is what keeps the gear link running — without it, you kill one boss, run out of brews, and stop.

🎯 The Ironman feedback loop

  • Farm supply boss → bank herbs, seeds, bones, bolts.
  • Process loot → brew potions, train prayer, craft ammo.
  • Kill upgrade boss → earn better gear with your new supplies.
  • Better gear → kill the next boss faster → more supplies → repeat.

5.3 Loot becomes unlocks

Some boss loot is not gear or supplies but unlocks that open entire systems. The CG enhanced weapon seed funds construction and ultimately the max-house pool. The NM jar boosts construction. ToB and CoX drops fund the gear that qualifies you for high-level clans and group content. Even the gold from alching boss drops pays for construction PLANK MAKING, farm runs, and quest requirements that would otherwise stall your account. Bossing is never "just bossing" on an Ironman — it is the engine of everything.

5.4 Why you should over-farm the mid tier

A common mistake is to rush from "I can kill Vorkath" straight to "I should try ToB." The smarter play is to over-farm the mid tier. Spend an extra few hundred Vorkath and Zulrah kills banking supplies, maxing your blowpipe, and building a huge brews buffer. That buffer is what lets you attempt CoX and ToB without fear of going broke on deaths. The mid tier is where you print the safety net for the late tier. The Ironmen who struggle at raids are almost always the ones who under-farmed Vorkath and Zulrah.

💡 Measure progress in supply weeks, not boss kills

Ask "how many weeks of bossing can I fund from my bank right now?" not "how many bosses have I unlocked?" If the answer is less than a few weeks of supplies, keep farming the tier you are in before advancing.

6. Common Ironman Bossing Mistakes to Avoid

These are the mistakes that waste the most Ironman hours. Most are versions of the same root error: skipping a step in the supply-and-gear chain. Avoid them and your progression will be dramatically faster.

6.1 Rushing raids without a supply buffer

The number one mistake. Ironmen see a maxed friend do ToB and think they are ready. They are not, because the friend buys brews and the Ironman must farm them. Entering CoX or ToB with a thin brews bank means every death is a net loss and progress stalls. Fix: over-farm Vorkath and Zulrah until your supply bank is deep, then raid.

6.2 Skipping the Corrupted Gauntlet

Some players hate the Gauntlet and skip to GWD1, then wonder why they struggle at Vorkath and die in raids. CG is not optional practice — it is your gear and skill foundation. Fix: commit to at least 100 CG kills before mid-tier bosses. The crystal armor alone is worth it.

6.3 Not banking resources and alchables

Ironmen who drop or neglect their alchables and herbs waste the very supplies that fund their account. That "junk" from GWD1 is your potion pipeline. Fix: process every herb into potions, alch every unused drop, and keep a running supply count. Treat drops as inventory for future bosses, not clutter.

6.4 Under-investing in prayer and combat stats

Bossing is far easier at 80+ stats and 70+ Prayer than at 70/43. Ironmen who rush with low prayer die more and use more brews. Fix: use KBD and Vorkath bones to push Prayer, and train Attack/Strength/Ranged/Defence through Slayer and Chinning before each tier gate.

6.5 Spreading too thin across bosses

Jumping between CG, GWD1, KBD, Vorkath, and Zulrah in one week means you master none of them and your loot is unfocused. Fix: pick one tier target at a time. Clear CG, then settle GWD1, then Vorkath, then Zulrah. Depth beats breadth on an Ironman.

6.6 Ignoring inventory prep and death costs

Even safe bosses have death costs, and raids can delete a full loadout. Ironmen who do not prep teleports, brews, and restores properly wipe and lose progress. Fix: always run a full, correct inventory (see our boss inventory prep guide) and never enter a late boss without a plan to recover from a death.

💡 The 10-death rule

If you die more than ~10 times to a boss without a kill, you are under-geared or under-supplied. Step back, farm the tier below, and return later. Pride is the most expensive resource on an Ironman.

7. Ironman Bossing FAQs

What is the very first boss an Ironman should learn in 2026?

The Corrupted Gauntlet. It costs nothing to attempt, teaches prayer flicking and inventory management, and drops crystal armor and weapon seeds that you will use all the way into the mid and late tiers. It is the most efficient first PvM goal for a fresh Ironman and the foundation of the entire roadmap above.

Can I skip GWD1 and go straight to Vorkath?

You can attempt Vorkath with barrows and a crossbow, but you will be underpowered and you will miss the bulk herb, seed, and gear income that GWD1 provides. GWD1 is what stocks your brews and gives you Armadyl/Bandos. Skipping it makes Vorkath and everything after it slower and more supply-hungry. Do GWD1 first.

How much should I farm the mid tier before raids?

Enough to bank several weeks of bossing supplies: a deep brews and restores stack, a topped-up blowpipe, a solid alchable and herb buffer, and comfortable magic/ranged levels. Most Ironmen need a few hundred Vorkath and Zulrah kills to feel safe entering CoX and ToB. Over-farming the mid tier is almost never a mistake.

Do I need a twisted bow to start CoX?

No. The twisted bow drops from CoX itself. You bridge CoX with a dragon hunter crossbow, a blowpipe, and a trident, plus 77+ Prayer and full supplies. The Tbow is the reward for learning CoX, not the requirement to enter it. The same logic applies to the scythe at ToB and Torva at Nex.

Why is the Nightmare worth doing on an Ironman?

The Nightmare drops the nightmare staff (a top-tier magic weapon), the jar of dreams (a construction/account boost), and a large volume of herbs and seeds that feed your overload and potion production. It is one of the best mid-tier magic upgrades and a key supply source for the late game.

What kills most Ironman boss progression?

Running out of supplies after rushing ahead. Ironmen who enter raids or Vorkath without a supply buffer stall hard, because every death is a net loss they must re-farm. The fix is always the same: slow down, over-farm the current tier, and treat bossing as a supply chain rather than a series of one-off kills.
