🛡️ OSRS Avoid Scams & Phishing 2026: Why Banks Get Drained & How to Stop It

Beginner

Every week on our account security guide we see the same story: a player logs in, opens their bank, and finds it empty. The items they ground for months — a Toxic Blowpipe, Ancestral robes, 50M GP — are gone. The scary part is that almost none of these losses come from "hacking." They come from the player willingly handing over access or items because a scam looked legitimate.

The mechanics scammers use are surprisingly small in number, but each one is refined to exploit a specific human instinct: greed, trust, urgency, or helpfulness. If you understand the Grand Exchange system and how trades actually resolve, you already remove 90% of the risk. This guide adds the missing 10%: recognizing the exact moments scammers strike.

Whether you are a fresh level-3 with 5K GP or a maxed main with a 2B bank, the patterns below apply to you. We will dissect real methods — not vague warnings — so you can spot them in the wild within three seconds. Start with our account hack prevention checklist if you have not locked down your login yet.

⏱️ Quick Summary — 30-Second Read

  • Phishing links are the #1 cause of drained banks — they steal your login, not your items.
  • "Double your GP" and "trust trade first" are 100% scams with no game mechanic behind them.
  • Item swaps happen on the second trade screen — always re-read what you receive.
  • Fake GE items use look-alikes; buy only from the official Grand Exchange.
  • Scammers now social-engineer you before 2FA — never share codes or backup emails.

💀 1. Why OSRS Banks Get Drained

Before we name the scams, understand the economy of scamming. Unlike a credit card hack, draining an OSRS bank is almost always a consent-based theft: the victim authorizes the trade or types their password into a fake page themselves. That is why no amount of Jagex server security stops it — the "hacker" is just a convincing actor.

The motivation is pure profit. A stolen maxed main can be stripped of 1–3 billion GP worth of items and resold on black markets for a few hundred real dollars. Even a modest mid-game bank of 20–50M GP is worth the scammer's time. This is why scammers are patient, polite, and persistent.

📊 The 3 Layers Scammers Target

  • Login layer: Phishing + social engineering steal your email/password (bypasses everything).
  • Trade layer: Trust trades, item swaps, and fake items take items directly from your bank.
  • Trust layer: Long cons where a "friend" builds rapport for weeks before striking.

The rest of this guide walks through each layer with the exact scripts scammers use, so you recognize them instantly. Pair this with our Jagex Account migration guide to shut the login layer completely.

🎣 2. Phishing Links: The #1 Bank-Killer

Phishing is responsible for the majority of drained banks in 2026. The scammer never touches your items — they steal your credentials and log in as you. Here is the anatomy.

① The Hook Message

You receive a DM in-game, a Discord private message, or an email that looks semi-official:

Common hooks you will see

  • "🎁 You won a free bond! Claim here: [link]"
  • "⚠️ Your account will be banned in 24h unless you verify: [link]"
  • "🔥 50% GP bonus event, log in to qualify: [link]"
  • "I accidentally reported you — appeal here or get muted: [link]"

② The Fake Login Page

The link leads to a pixel-perfect clone of the OSRS login. You type your email and password. Sometimes they even show a "wrong password" error to make it feel real, then harvest what you retry. Within minutes your real account is compromised.

🔍 How to Verify Any Link in 3 Seconds
  • Official login is only oldschool.runescape.com or account.jagex.com.
  • Watch for tricks: oldscho0l-runescape.com (zero), oldschool-runescape.com (missing .jagex), jagex-verify-login.com.
  • Never click links from DMs. Type the URL manually or use a bookmarked login.

③ The Drain

Once in, the scammer moves your valuable items to mules, empties your GP, and often changes the registered email so you cannot recover. This is why account recovery is slow and uncertain — prevention is the only reliable defense.

🤝 3. Trust Trade & "Double GP" Scams

These scams prey on greed and politeness. They are pure social tricks — no fake website required.

① The "Double Your GP" Lie

A player tells you they run a "dupe" or "GP doubler" and will return double what you send. They may show edited screenshots of "happy customers." There is no game mechanic that duplicates GP. Send it, and they vanish. Our flipping guide shows the only legitimate way to grow GP.

② The Trust Trade

A high-level player offers to lend you an expensive item (a Scythe of Vitur, a Tumeken's Shadow) if you "trust trade" them first by giving them something of equal value. The moment you hand over your item, they log out. "Trust trading" is just a polite name for theft.

🎯 The Iron Rule of Trading

Never give an item or GP to receive an item or GP later. OSRS has no escrow for player-to-player "promises." If the value does not change hands simultaneously in the trade window, it is a scam.

③ The "Split the Profit" Con

"I'll buy this rare and we split the flip — send me your half first." Same mechanic, dressed as a partnership. Real profit-sharing happens through the Grand Exchange, not promises.

🔄 4. Item Swap & Fake GE Items

These scams happen during a real, visible trade — which is what makes them so convincing.

① The Classic Item Swap

You agree to buy a Dragon Scimitar (≈100K) or a specific armor piece. On the first trade screen they show the real item. Just before you both click Accept, they swap it for a near-identical untradeable or worthless version — e.g., a Dragon Longsword, or a cosmetic/charged-vs-uncharged variant.

How to defeat the swap

After both players click Accept, a second trade screen appears listing exactly what you receive. Read it word-for-word. If the item name, charge, or note differs, click Decline. Never rush the second screen.

② Fake GE / "Off-Exchange" Items

Scammers advertise items "cheaper than GE" and insist on a direct trade. The item they show may be a look-alike: a (g) gold-trimmed duplicate, a broken/charged mismatch, or an entirely different item with a similar icon. Always cross-check the live price on the Grand Exchange and prefer buying there.

🕵️ 5. Social Engineering Before 2FA

Many players think "I have 2FA, I'm safe." Scammers know this and attack around 2FA through social engineering.

① The "Support Agent" Impersonation

Someone claims to be Jagex Support (in Discord, in-game, or email) and says they need your authenticator code or backup codes to "verify" you. Jagex Support will never ask for these. Hand them over and the scammer breezes past 2FA.

② The Recovery Email Pivot

Scammers phish your email first, not your game login. With email access they trigger a password reset and read the Jagex verification code from your inbox — 2FA on the game does not protect the email. Enable 2FA on your email too.

③ The "Friendly Mentor" Long Con

A "helpful" higher-level player befriends you over weeks, offers to help with bosses, then one day asks for a "loan" or a "trust trade" "just this once." By then, rapport has replaced caution. This is why our 2FA management guide stresses treating every unsolicited trade request as hostile, regardless of history.

🚩 Social-Engineering Red Flags

  • Anyone asking for your authenticator or backup codes → scam.
  • "Jagex Support" contacting you first → impossible, scam.
  • Pressure to act "before your account is locked" → scam.
  • A long-time "friend" suddenly requesting items/GP → pause and verify.

✅ 6. 12-Point Anti-Scam Checklist

Print this mentally. Run it before every trade, every DM, and every login.

  1. Enable Jagex Account + 2FA via authenticator app (not SMS).
  2. Turn on 2FA for your email — it is the master key.
  3. Save backup codes offline (paper, not your PC).
  4. Bookmark oldschool.runescape.com; never click login links.
  5. Never send GP "to be doubled." No such mechanic exists.
  6. Never trust-trade — value must move simultaneously.
  7. Re-read the second trade screen before confirming.
  8. Buy tradeables on the GE, not direct "cheaper" trades.
  9. Ignore DMs from "admins/mods/support." Real staff don't DM.
  10. Never share authenticator or backup codes with anyone.
  11. Report scammers in-game (right-click → Report → Scamming).
  12. Treat urgency as a symptom — real OSRS never expires in minutes.

Run through our full security guide once to confirm every layer is closed. Most victims had 9 of these covered — scammers only need one gap.

🆘 7. If Your Bank Was Drained

If you already lost items, act fast — but know recovery is not guaranteed.

① Secure What Remains

If you can still log in, change your password and email immediately, enable 2FA, and log out all sessions. Move any remaining valuables to a safe alt.

② Submit Account Recovery

Through Jagex account recovery, prove original ownership (creation date, first membership, original ISP). Expect 1–14 days of waiting.

③ Report the Scammer

Even after the loss, reporting helps Jagex ban the account and may aid recovery of uniquely identifiable items.

⚠️ Prevention Beats Recovery

Recovery rarely returns all lost GP. The 12-point checklist above is faster, free, and 99% effective. Do it today, not after the drain.

❓ 8. FAQ

Can 2FA alone stop all these scams?

2FA stops login-based phishing dramatically, but it does not stop you from voluntarily trust-trading, item-swapping, or handing over codes via social engineering. You need both technical protection and the awareness in this guide.

Is buying GP from third-party sites safe?

No. Real-World Trading violates Jagex rules and risks a permanent ban. Many "GP sellers" also phish your credentials or chargeback after delivery, getting your account chain-banned. Earn GP through our money-making guides instead.

What if a "friend" asks to borrow an item?

In OSRS there is no secure loan system. A genuine friend won't mind using the GE or a trusted middleman service. If they pressure you or get defensive, it is a scam signal.

How do I know a GE item price is real?

Use the in-game GE "Price" lookup and compare to our flipping guide norms. If an off-GE "deal" is 20–50% below market, assume it is a fake-item or bait trade.

Someone sent me a "Jagex" email — how do I verify it?

Check the sender domain: it must end in @jagex.com or @runescape.com. Even then, Jagex never asks for passwords or codes by email. When in doubt, log in via your bookmark and check notifications there.
