1. 🧭 Why Run a Security Audit (and How Scoring Works)

Security is not a feeling — it is a list. An audit turns "I'm probably fine" into a number you can act on.

① 🎯 The cost of guessing

Players rarely get "partially hacked." They get drained. A single missing control — an unsecured recovery email, a reused password, no bank PIN — is often the only gap an attacker needs. Auditing surfaces those gaps while they are still theoretical, not after your rares are gone. The mindset shift is the hardest part: most players equate "I haven't been hacked" with "I am secure." Those are different statements. Not-yet-hacked simply means no attacker has aimed at your particular open door yet. The audit checks whether the door is even there.

② 🧮 The scoring model

Twenty checks, five points each, for a clean 0–100. We deliberately weight the fundamentals (migration, MFA, password) as pass/fail because a missing fundamental is not "a small gap" — it is an open door. The checklist is grouped into nine categories so you can see where you are weak, not just that you are. We resisted making every point equal weight in spirit while keeping the math simple: the fundamentals are pass/fail precisely because a missing one is not "mostly safe," it is "open." If you find that harsh, remember the alternative is discovering the gap at the worst possible moment, with a drained bank as the teacher.

③ 🔁 Audit is a habit, not a one-time task

Threats change, devices change, and you change. A score of 95 today means little if you authorized a sketchy app tomorrow. Treat the audit like a monthly bank statement: quick, routine, and worth the peace of mind. Players who skip audits are not careless — they are simply optimistic. The uncomfortable data point is that the vast majority of recovered "hacks" trace back to a single missing control the owner assumed was fine. An audit converts that assumption into evidence. Fifteen minutes once a month is cheaper than the hours, emails, and emotional cost of losing a maxed account with years of progress.

2. ✅ The 20-Point Checklist

The order of the points is deliberate. Points 1–3 (foundation) and 4–6 (MFA) are the heaviest hitters: without them, every later control is built on sand. Points 7–11 (PIN and email) limit the damage and protect the reset channel. Points 12–17 (recovery, devices, password) harden the edges. Points 18–20 (third-party and monitoring) are the ongoing hygiene that keeps the score from sliding. Read it as a pyramid: fix the base first, then the middle, then the top.

Notice how the points stack: no single control is a silver bullet, but each removes a whole class of attack. Migration and MFA stop password-based takeover; the bank PIN limits damage even after a breach; email MFA protects the reset channel; a unique password contains the blast radius of other site breaches. Score yourself honestly, because the number only helps if it is true. A 100 you earned by checking is worth more than a 100 you assumed.

3. 📊 Scoring Your Account (Tiers)

Add your passed points. Your tier tells you how urgent the fixes are.

① 🟢 Strong (90–100)

You have the fundamentals and the details. Stay vigilant: re-audit monthly and after any suspicious email or login alert. A 95 today can drop if you authorize a bad app tomorrow. Strong does not mean invincible; it means the obvious doors are closed and only sophisticated, targeted effort could succeed — and that effort is rare against a well-secured account. The tier is a snapshot, so treat a "Strong" result as a license to keep the habit, not to relax it.

② 🟡 Fair (70–89)

Solid base, but gaps remain. A determined attacker only needs one. Prioritize the heaviest fundamentals first (migration, MFA, password), then close the rest within the week. If you can only do a few things this week, those three close the widest gaps; the remaining points are about depth and durability, not survival. A "Fair" score is not a failure — it is a prioritized to-do list. Work it top-down and you will usually reach "Strong" within a weekend, because the fundamentals are also the quickest fixes: migration, MFA, and a unique password can each be done in minutes. The longer items are habit changes, not technical ones.

③ 🔴 At Risk (below 70)

You are one bad link away from a drained bank. Treat this as urgent: fix points 1, 4, 7, 9, and 16 today, then work down the list. Our emergency checklist is your backup plan if something already feels wrong.

④ 📝 Worked example: scoring a sample account

Imagine an account that passes migration, MFA, bank PIN, and email MFA (points 1, 4, 7, 9 = 20) but fails backup codes, PIN strength, recovery email, and monitoring (points 5, 8, 10, 20 = −20), and passes everything else. Score: 100 − 20 = 80 — a "Fair" tier with clear, fixable gaps. The audit did its job: instead of a vague "I'm probably fine," you now have four specific actions. That is the entire point — turn anxiety into a checklist.

4. 🔧 Fix-It Guide: Every Failing Point

Missed a point? Here is the exact fix. Each step uses official Jagex settings — never a third-party tool. If a fix feels technical, slow down and do it from account.jagex.com directly; the official interface is designed to be self-serve, and the linked guides cover every step. The worst mistake is rushing a fix with a "helper" tool that is itself malware.

① 🏗️ Fix foundation (1–3)

  • Point 1 (migration): If you still use a legacy RuneScape login, migrate to a Jagex account via the official migration flow. Our migration guide has the step-by-step.
  • Point 2 (your email): Confirm the registered email is an inbox you own. If it is an old or shared address, update it from account settings.
  • Point 3 (no sharing): Shared and bought accounts are against the rules and far easier to lose. Keep one owner, one login.

② 🔐 Fix MFA (4–6)

  • Point 4 (enable MFA): Turn on the Jagex authenticator or a passkey in account settings. See our multi-account 2FA guide if you run several characters. If you manage multiple accounts, resist reusing one authenticator seed across them; a single leaked seed then compromises everything. Set up each account's MFA independently and store its backup codes separately, so one lost device is a contained incident rather than a total breach.
  • Point 5 (backup codes): Save recovery codes to a password manager or printed paper kept offline — not in the same email that can be hijacked.
  • Point 6 (resilience): If you lose your phone, you still need a route back in. Store backup codes and know the recovery path (our lost authenticator guide).

③ 💳 Fix PIN & email (7–11)

  • Point 7 (bank PIN): Set a bank PIN in-game; it blocks item movement even if someone gets in. Our bank PIN setup guide covers it.
  • Point 8 (strong PIN): Avoid 1234, birthdays, or anything written near your desk.
  • Point 9 (email MFA): Enable 2FA on the inbox itself — your OSRS MFA is only as safe as the email that resets it.
  • Point 10 (recovery email): Use a separate secure address for account recovery, not your daily spam inbox.
  • Point 11 (no breach): Check the inbox against a breach-checker; if it appeared in a breach, change that password everywhere and move recovery to a clean address.

④ 🛠️ Fix recovery, devices, password (12–17)

  • Point 12 (accurate recovery): Make sure your recovery details are things you will actually remember (original creation info, old passwords).
  • Point 13 (no stranger recovery): Review attached recovery methods and remove anything unfamiliar.
  • Point 14 (trusted devices): Log out of shared/library PCs and revoke unknown sessions.
  • Point 15 (patched): Run antivirus and update OS/browser; an unpatched machine leaks tokens (see our session hijack guide).
  • Point 16 (unique password): Generate a new, unique password for OSRS only.
  • Point 17 (manager): Adopt a password manager so uniqueness is automatic.

⑤ 🔗 Fix third-party & monitoring (18–20)

  • Point 18 (no shady apps): Revoke any third-party integration you did not intentionally set up.
  • Point 19 (official launcher): Uninstall anything pretending to be the client; download only the official Jagex Launcher.
  • Point 20 (monitoring): Set a monthly reminder to review active sessions and read login alerts.

Third-party authorizations are the quietest risk on this list — they sit in your settings unused until an attacker or a breached service activates them. Review the authorized-apps list every audit, not just when something goes wrong.

⚠️ Fix the email before the rest

If point 9 or 10 fails, fix it first. An attacker who controls your inbox can reset your OSRS password and MFA regardless of how strong everything else is. Email security is the keystone of the whole audit.

Do not try to fix everything in one sitting if you are short on time — but fix the keystone first. Email security (points 9–11) and MFA (4–6) are the two clusters that, once solid, neutralize the majority of real-world attacks. A player who migrates, enables MFA, secures their inbox, and sets a bank PIN has already eliminated perhaps 90% of the risk surface, even before the finer points.

5. 🔄 How Often to Re-Audit

An audit is a snapshot. The value comes from repeating it.

① 📅 Monthly for most players

A 15-minute monthly pass catches drift: a new authorized app, an old device still logged in, a password you reused somewhere. Put it on the same calendar reminder as your membership renewal. Consistency beats intensity — a quick monthly pass done religiously beats a heroic four-hour review you never repeat. The attackers rely on inertia; a standing reminder breaks it.

② 🚨 Immediately after any incident

If you clicked a suspicious link, got a login alert you did not expect, or saw a weird trade, re-audit that day — and jump to the emergency checklist if anything moved.

③ 🔄 After big life changes

New phone, new computer, new email, or a shared device at a LAN event? Each is a reason to re-run the audit and revoke stale sessions. Think of re-auditing like changing your car's oil: skipping it does not break anything today, but the slow accumulation of stale devices, reused passwords, and forgotten app approvals is exactly what a hijacker exploits months later. The calendar reminder is the cheapest insurance in this entire guide.

6. 🧰 Tools & Official Settings You Need

You do not need paid software. These free, official, or built-in tools cover every point.

① 🏠 Official Jagex surfaces

account.jagex.com for migration, MFA, recovery, and session management; the official Jagex Launcher for play. Everything else claiming to be "Jagex support" by email is covered in our Jagex account FAQ. Keep these surfaces bookmarked and use them as your only entry points. Bookmarking account.jagex.com and the launcher removes the phishing link from your routine entirely — you never need to click a login link, because you already know exactly where to go. This single habit satisfies the spirit of several checklist points at once.

② 🔑 A password manager

Built-in browser managers or a standalone app generate unique passwords and store recovery codes. This single tool clears points 16 and 17 and helps point 5. None of these tools cost money, and none require granting a stranger access to your account. If a "security tool" asks for your Jagex password, it is the threat, not the cure. Stick to official surfaces and reputable, offline-first password managers, and the audit becomes a five-minute monthly habit rather than a project.

③ 📧 Inbox 2FA and breach checks

Enable MFA on your email provider and periodically check whether the address has appeared in a public breach. This protects points 9, 10, and 11.

④ 🛡️ Antivirus and updates

Free, reputable antivirus plus automatic OS updates close point 15 and stop the malware path that steals session tokens.

7. ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

How long does the 20-point audit take?

About 10–15 minutes the first time, once you know where each setting lives. Subsequent monthly audits take five minutes because you are only confirming nothing changed.

What score do I need to be "safe"?

Aim for 90 or above. Below 70 is genuinely at risk. But even a 95 has weak spots — the point of scoring is to find and fix them, not to hit a number and forget it. Anecdotally, accounts that score below 80 are dramatically over-represented in recovery requests, which is exactly why we draw the "At Risk" line where we do — it marks the point where luck, not security, is doing the protecting.

I failed point 1 — I'm still on a legacy login. Is that urgent?

Yes. The legacy login lacks the modern Jagex account protections, including stronger MFA and recovery controls. Migrate as soon as possible using the official flow; our migration guide walks through it.

My bank PIN is set but easy to guess. Does that pass point 8?

No. A guessable PIN (1234, birthday) fails the "strong" criteria. Set a non-obvious PIN and do not store it where someone at your desk could read it. Our bank PIN guide explains the settings.

Does MFA on OSRS protect me if my email has no MFA?

Weakly. Your email often resets your OSRS password and MFA, so an unsecured inbox is the master key. Enable 2FA on the email itself (point 9) — it is the single highest-leverage fix after migration.

What if I lost my authenticator and have no backup codes?

You will need the Jagex account recovery process, which is easier if your recovery details are accurate (points 12–13). Our lost authenticator guide covers the exact steps. This is why point 5 (stored backup codes) matters.

Can I audit several characters at once?

Run the checklist per account, but manage MFA efficiently with our multi-account 2FA guide so you do not lose access to one while securing others.

My email appeared in a breach. What now?

Change that password everywhere it was reused (point 16), move OSRS recovery to a clean separate address (point 10), and enable inbox MFA (point 9). If the breached password was also your OSRS password, change it and revoke all sessions immediately.

Is a password manager really necessary, or is it overkill?

It is the most reliable way to satisfy unique passwords (16) and stored backup codes (5/17). Without one, reuse creeps in and a single forum breach compromises your account. It is the opposite of overkill — it is the baseline.

What if I already think I was hacked?

Stop the audit and go straight to our emergency checklist, then the recovery after hack guide. Audit after you have regained control, not during the breach.

Does a perfect score guarantee I will never be hacked?

No control is absolute. A perfect audit means you have removed the common, automated paths — the ones responsible for the overwhelming majority of losses. It does not defend against a government-grade targeted attack or a zero-day in software you trust. But those are rare for normal players; the audit puts you far outside the easy-victim pool, which is exactly where you want to be.
