OSRS 90+ Skill Planning Guide 2026 — What to Train After Level 90 & Priority Order

🛡️ Level: 90+
📅 Published: August 9, 2026 · Updated August 2026 · 18 min read

You finally did it: several of your skills sit at level 90 or above. The 90s are a strange place in Old School RuneScape. You are no longer a mid-game account, but you are not "maxed" either, and the gap between 90 and 99 is famously the slowest, most expensive stretch in the entire game. This guide helps you plan that stretch intelligently — weighing the real returns each high-level skill gives you, understanding long-term account value, and building a priority order that matches your goals instead of blindly grinding the nearest bar to full. Whether you are a bossing main, an Ironman, a skiller, or a completionist, the framework below lets you reverse-engineer the cheapest, fastest route to "done."

⏱️ Quick Summary — 30-Second Read

  • 📌 The 90s are only ~8% of total XP but feel like the longest grind because XP rates do not improve with level.
  • 📌 Combat 90→99 is nearly free when trained through Slayer — you earn GP while boosting DPS and unlocking prayers.
  • 📌 Bankroll with profit first: Runecrafting, Hunter, Farming, and Sailing 90+ can be profitable and fund the costly Herblore/Construction/Prayer 99s.
  • 📌 Keystones pay off forever: Construction 83→90 (POH) and Agility 90 speed up every future session.
  • 📌 Wait for Double XP before paying full price for Herblore, Construction, or Prayer 99.

1. Why Hitting 90+ Changes Everything

The jump from level 1 to 90 is roughly 92% of the total XP required for 99. The final ten levels — from 90 to 99 — demand the remaining 8% of XP, but that 8% takes a disproportionate amount of time because XP rates per method do not scale with your level. A Mithril Dragon gives the same XP at 90 Slayer as at 99 Slayer; the only thing that changes is how close the end feels. Level 99 requires 13,034,431 XP in total, while level 90 sits at roughly 11,950,000 XP, so the last nine levels are about 1,084,000 XP — a sliver of the journey that somehow feels like a second job.

This is why planning matters more at 90+ than at any earlier stage. Early on, almost any training is "good enough" because you are unlocking content constantly — a new quest, a new teleport, a new crop. Past 90, most unlocks are already in your pocket, so the marginal value of the next level is smaller and must be justified by a concrete goal: a diary, a boss, a max cape, or a money-making method. Grinding 90 to 99 with no destination is how players burn out and quit accounts that were weeks away from being amazing.

💡 The 90 Line Is a Psychological Milestone

Level 90 is the threshold for several high-value activities (90 Slayer, 90 Prayer via bones, 90+ Herblore overloads, 90 Agility shortcuts, 90 Crafting dragonhide, 90 Runecrafting). Crossing it often unlocks the single most efficient method in a skill, which can suddenly make the last nine levels cheaper than the prior 20 felt. Treat 90 as the "on ramp" to the finish, not the finish itself.

Before you commit to a grind, decide what "done" looks like for your account. A main chasing the max cape plans very differently from a bossing pure, an Ironman, or a skiller. The rest of this guide gives you the framework to make that call, plus concrete numbers so you are never guessing how much a 99 will cost you.

A useful way to think about the 90s is that they reward patience, not effort. Early levels reward effort because each new method is faster than the last. Past 90, you have usually already found the best method for the skill, so the only lever left is time and GP. This is exactly why a plan matters: with no new unlock to look forward to, motivation comes from a goal you chose, not from the game handing you one. Players who treat the 90s as a mechanical continuation of earlier training are the ones who quit — those who attach each 99 to a diary, a boss, or a cape finish them.

2. The Real Value of High-Level Skills (Returns by Category)

Not all 99s are equal. Some skills pay for themselves many times over; others are pure trophy XP. Before you spend a bank on a 99, classify it into one of three return categories so you know what you are actually buying.

② Combat Skills — The Highest-Reward 90s

Pushing Attack, Strength, Defence, Ranged, Magic, and Prayer from 90 to 99 delivers the clearest return because it directly increases your DPS, survivability, and access to end-game content. At 90+ you can wield the best weapons (Inquisitor's mace, Scythe of Vitur, Tumeken's shadow), use the strongest prayers (Piety at 70, Rigour and Augury at 74), and attempt the hardest bosses. Our combat training guide covers the route; past 90 the priority is simply "finish what you started" because the per-level damage gain is real and permanent. Crucially, combat 90→99 trained through Slayer is often net-profitable — you make GP while training, which is why it sits at the top of every priority list.

③ Artisan Skills — Convenience & Profit, Not Power

Herblore 90+ (overloads at 96, ancestral flasks), Crafting 90+ (dragonhide, light orbs, midnight runes), and Construction (the real quality-of-life king) improve your experience rather than your raw stats. A 99 Herblore does not hit harder, but it lets you make overloads that effectively raise every combat stat for minutes at a time. Construction 90 gives a fully kitted Player-Owned House with portal nexus, fairy ring, and a mounted jewellery box. These are "force multipliers" best finished once you can afford them — never first, because they are the most expensive skills in the game.

④ Gathering & Support Skills — The Long Game

Mining, Fishing, Woodcutting, Hunter, Farming, Agility, Thieving, Slayer, and the new Sailing skill mostly pay off through unlocks and money rather than stats. Agility 90 unlocks the best rooftop shortcuts and grace period; Slayer 90 reaches the best tasks; Farming 90 lets you grow the most valuable crops. Several of these (Runecrafting, Hunter, Farming, Sailing at 90+) are themselves profitable, which makes them the ideal funding source for the costly artisan 99s. These 99s are valuable but rarely "urgent" unless a specific unlock is blocking you.

3. Long-Term Account Value — Combat, Bossing & Wealth

A maxed account is more than a number on a hi-score. It is a key that opens every door in the game. Think about 90+ planning in terms of three long-term payoffs, because each one compounds over your entire OSRS career.

⑤ Combat Readiness

The single biggest long-term value of finishing your combat 90s is boss access. Virtually every high-GP boss in OSRS has a combat stat or quest requirement that sits in the 70–90 range. Completing 90+ Attack/Strength/Defence/Ranged/Magic/Prayer means you can attempt content like the Theatre of Blood, Tombs of Amascut, Nex, and the newest 2026 bosses without stat gates. That translates directly into the best money makers in the game, which in turn funds the rest of your 99s. Combat readiness is the engine; everything else is fuel it buys.

⑥ Wealth Generation

Several 90+ skills are themselves money printers. Runecrafting 91+ (death / blood runes), Hunter 90+ (red chinchompas, then Herbiboar), Farming 90+ (ranarr, snapdragon, toadflax runs), and Sailing 90+ (top-tier salvaging and long voyages) generate millions per hour. If your bank is thin, pushing the cheapest profitable 90→99 first funds the expensive ones later. Our money-making guides rank these methods in detail, but the planning principle is constant: let profitable skills pay for trophy skills.

⑦ The Max Cape & Completionist Goals

For many players the 90s are a stepping stone to the max cape and eventually the Completionist Cape. These are not just bragging rights: the max cape gives a free daily teleport, a small total-level boost, and acts as a skill cape for any skill. Planning which 99s to finish first based on cost lets you reach that cape months earlier than a random order would. The max cape route guide breaks the full 23-skill (plus Sailing) journey into a costed sequence.

The Completionist Cape goes further, requiring not just all 99s but every quest, all elite diaries, and a long list of minigame and boss milestones. For most players the 90+ planning phase is really the opening act of a completionist run, even if they only realize it later. Knowing that early lets you stack requirements (Section 6) so a single 90→99 also ticks a diary or quest box, turning one grind into two or three checked goals. It is the highest-leverage habit you can build in the 90s.

💡 Ironman Note

On an Ironman, 90+ planning is even more critical because you cannot buy your way past bottlenecks. Prioritize self-sufficient skills (Farming for herbs, Hunter for bones, Slayer for supplies) so the expensive artisan 99s become feasible from your own drops. An Ironman who rushes Construction 90 before building a Farming and Herblore base will stall for months gathering supplies by hand.

4. How to Set Your 90→99 Priority Order

There is no single "correct" order, but there is a reliable decision process. Rank every remaining 90+ skill on three axes: cost to 99, time to 99, and impact on your goals. Then apply these four rules, in order, until every skill has a slot.

⑧ Rule 1 — Finish Cheap, High-Impact Combat First

If you are one or two levels from a combat 99 that unlocks a weapon or prayer, finish it. The per-hour cost of the last few combat levels via Slayer is near zero because you make money while training. A free 99 that boosts your DPS is always priority one, and it makes every subsequent money-making method faster.

⑨ Rule 2 — Do Profitable 90s Before Expensive Ones

Use the GP you earn from Runecrafting, Hunter, Farming, or Sailing 90+ to bankroll Herblore, Construction, and Prayer, which can each cost 50–200M to 99. Training expensive skills first, before you have a money engine, is the most common way players stall out in the 90s for months. Profitable skills are the only ones that improve your bank while they level.

⑩ Rule 3 — Chase Unlocks That Unblock Other Goals

Some 90s are "keystones." Construction 90 makes every future session faster (teleports, altar, fairy ring). Agility 90 reduces run-energy downtime everywhere. Slayer 90 unlocks the most profitable tasks. Knock these out when they are the bottleneck, even if they are not the cheapest, because they shorten every grind that follows.

⑪ Rule 4 — Leave Pure Trophies for Last

Skills like Firemaking, Woodcutting, or Fishing 99 are trophies with little account impact. Train them in downtime (Wintertodt, birdhouses, Tempoross) rather than committing focused hours. They will fill in naturally while you wait for Double XP weekends or run other grinds between them.

⚠️ Double XP Weekends

Save expensive supplies for Double XP weekends. Buying 200M worth of Herblore ingredients and training during DXP effectively halves the GP cost per XP because the bonus XP doubles your progress per supply. Plan your 90→99 of costly skills around the calendar, not around impatience. Combat and profitable skills do not need DXP — train those any time.

6. Stacking Goals — Diaries, Quests & Boss Unlocks

The smartest 90+ planning never trains a skill "just for the 99." It trains toward a requirement that also advances a diary, a quest, or a boss unlock, so each level does double duty. This is how efficient players finish multiple goals from a single grind.

⑫ Quest Requirements

Many late-game quests gate on 90s. If a quest you want sits behind 90+ Herblore, Construction, or Slayer, that skill jumps the queue because the quest also unlocks content, XP lamps, or a diary tier. Our complete skill training guide maps which quests feed which skills, so you can sequence a 90→99 to also clear a quest log entry.

⑬ Achievement Diaries

Elite diaries often require 90+ in several skills and reward teleports, XP boosts, and unique items (like the Wilderness sword or the kits that skip skill processes). If three of your target 99s are also elite diary requirements, finishing them clears both goals at once. The achievement diary guide lists every 90+ diary gate so you can plan overlaps.

⑭ Combat Achievements

High-tier Combat Achievements frequently need specific 90+ stats or 99s to attempt. Pairing a combat 99 with a CA task list means you are progressing your stats and your achievement tiles simultaneously. The combat achievements guide shows which tasks open up at each combat milestone, turning a grind into a checklist.

💡 The "One Grind, Three Rewards" Test

Before starting any 90→99, ask: does this level also complete a diary, a quest, or a CA? If yes, it is high priority. If no, it is a pure trophy — schedule it for downtime, not focused hours. This single question eliminates most wasted grinds in the 90s.

7. Common 90+ Planning Mistakes

Players waste the most time in the 90s by repeating a few predictable errors. Recognizing them now saves you weeks of misdirected effort.

Mistake 1 — Training Expensive Skills Before Banking Wealth

Dumping a 30M bank into one Herblore 99 then being too poor to do anything else. Build the money engine first (Runecrafting, Hunter, Farming, Sailing 90+), then spend the surplus on artisan 99s.

Mistake 2 — Ignoring Double XP Windows

Paying full price for a 200M-XP skill when DXP would halve it. Time your costly 99s to the calendar; the supplies cost the same but the XP per supply doubles, so you buy half as many.

Mistake 3 — Grinding Trophies While Bottlenecks Remain

Getting Firemaking 99 while Construction 83 blocks your POH. Fix keystones first — a finished POH saves more time across your account than any trophy 99 ever will.

Mistake 4 — No Clear "Done" Definition

Drifting from skill to skill. Pick a target (max cape, a boss, a diary set) and reverse-engineer the order. Accounts without a definition stall in the 90s for years.

Mistake 5 — Over-Investing in One Combat Style

Maxing Melee while Ranged and Magic sit at 90, then failing boss DPS checks that need balanced stats. Keep all six combat skills in the same tier so you are never gated by a weak style.

8. Sample Roadmaps for Different Account Types

The "right" order depends entirely on what your account is for. Below are four starter roadmaps you can adapt. Each assumes you already have several skills at 90+.

⑮ Bossing Main

Finish all six combat 99s via Slayer first (free DPS + loot), then Herblore 96 for overloads, then Construction 83→90 for the POH, then a profitable money engine (Runecrafting or Hunter) to fund the rest, and finally downtime trophies. See the combat training guide for the Slayer route that carries you through the combat 90s.

Slayer is the bridge because it trains all three melee stats, Ranged, and Magic simultaneously through diverse tasks, and it drops the bones, herbs, and supplies you need for the artisan 99s. A bossing main that treats Slayer as the default combat activity effectively funds its own Herblore and Prayer while finishing combat 99s for free. The only 90+ spend a bossing main cannot avoid is Construction 83→90 and the herb supplies for 96 — everything else is earned back through content.

⑯ Ironman

Self-sufficiency first: Farming 90+ for herbs, Hunter for bones and nests, Slayer for supplies. Only then attempt Herblore and Construction 99 from your own drops. An Ironman who rushes Construction 90 before a Farming and Herblore base will spend months gathering supplies by hand. Patience on the money engine pays off for every artisan 99.

⑰ Skiller / Completionist

Treat every 99 as required eventually, so order by cost: profitable and cheap skills first (Runecrafting, Farming, Sailing, Agility), then mid-cost combat, then the three artisan 99s during DXP, and trophies last. The max cape route is the natural end state for this account type.

⑱ Bossing Pure / Specialist

If you only care about one content type, skip unrelated 99s entirely. A raid specialist may stop at 90+ combat + Herblore 96 + Construction 90 and never touch Farming or Runecrafting to 99. "Done" for a specialist is a boss unlock, not a max cape — and that is fine.

🎯 Universal First Three Moves (Any Account)

  1. Finish any combat 99 that is within ~2 levels — it is free via Slayer and boosts everything.
  2. Start one profitable 90+ (Runecrafting / Hunter / Farming / Sailing) to build a money engine.
  3. Queue Construction 83→90 as your first big spend — the POH pays back on every future session.

9. Frequently Asked Questions

Should I finish all combat 99s before any artisan 99?

Usually yes for a bossing main — combat 99s are nearly free via Slayer and boost every other activity. But if you are broke, a profitable Runecrafting or Hunter 99 first funds the rest. The combat 90s also unlock the strongest prayers (Piety, Rigour, Augury), which improve every money-making method you try afterward.

Which 90+ skill gives the best return on time?

Construction 83→90, because the POH teleports, altar, and fairy ring save time in every future session — the payoff is permanent and account-wide. For raw power, any combat 90→99 trained through Slayer is the best "free" return since you profit while training.

Is it worth getting 99 in a skill I will never use?

Only if you are chasing the max cape or completionist goals. Otherwise train such skills passively during downtime (Wintertodt for Firemaking, birdhouses for Hunter, Tempoross for Fishing) rather than committing focused hours that could go to a goal-unlocking 99.

How much bank should I have before Herblore / Construction 99?

A combined 200–300M GP is comfortable. If you are below that, build it through profitable 90+ skills first, and use Double XP weekends to cut the bill roughly in half. Never liquidate your whole bank for one 99 — you will stall on everything else.

Does training during Double XP really halve the cost?

For supply-based skills, yes in practical terms: the bonus XP doubles progress per supply, so you buy about half as many bones, herbs, or planks. The GP cost of the supplies themselves does not change, but the amount you need does. Combat and profitable skills gain little from DXP, so save it for Herblore, Construction, Prayer, and Smithing.

What is the single best first 99 for a 90+ account?

Whichever combat skill is closest to 99 — it is free via Slayer, boosts your DPS, and often completes a Combat Achievement or diary at the same time. If no combat skill is close, the best "first" big spend is Construction 83→90 for the POH, because its quality-of-life return compounds across your whole account.

How do I plan around the new Sailing skill?

Sailing 90+ unlocks the best voyages and salvaging, which are profitable and feed other skills with supplies. Treat it like Runecrafting or Hunter in your priority list: a money engine to fund artisan 99s. The Sailing 1–99 guide covers the most efficient 90+ routes.

Should an Ironman prioritize Construction 90?

Yes, but later than a main — only after you have a Farming and Herblore base to supply it. An Ironman POH still needs herbs for the altar and planks for the rooms, so build the supply skills first or the Construction grind becomes a massive gathering chore. The Construction guide details the cheapest room layouts.

How long does the full 90→99 stretch take across all skills?

It varies hugely by account, but a focused main can go from "several 90s" to maxed in roughly 400–800 hours of efficient play, heavily weighted toward Herblore, Construction, and Prayer (the slow, expensive ones). Profitable and combat 99s add time but not cost. Using DXP and a money engine is the difference between 800 hours and 400.

What if I only want one boss, not a max cape?

Then plan backward from that boss's requirements only. Identify its combat stat gates and any skill prerequisites (e.g., Herblore 96 for overloads, Construction 90 for a teleport hub), finish those, and ignore the rest. Specialists should not chase 99s that do not serve their goal — that is the whole point of 90+ planning.

  • Sort by impact. Combat & keystone unlocks first; pure trophies last.

  • Wait for DXP. Never pay full price for Herblore, Construction, or Prayer 99.
  • Define "done." Max cape, a boss, or a diary set — then reverse-engineer the order.
