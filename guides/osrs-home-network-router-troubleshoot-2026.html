🔍 1. Why Your Home Network Breaks OSRS (Not Your PC)

Before touching anything, confirm the problem is actually your network. OSRS is a low-bandwidth game — it needs only a few dozen kilobits per second — but it is extremely sensitive to latency and packet loss. A 200 Mbps connection with 2% packet loss will feel worse than a stable 20 Mbps line. The general OSRS bug-fix hub covers client-side errors; this guide is purely about the plumbing between your device and Jagex's servers.

The tell-tale signs of a home-network problem (versus a Jagex server issue) are: the issue follows you across worlds and devices, it gets worse when other people stream or download at home, and a reboot of your router temporarily fixes it. If that matches, the culprit is almost always one of six things: router firewall/NAT, DNS, NIC drivers, ISP throttling, QoS, or antivirus. Mobile players on Wi-Fi should also read our mobile settings guide since a weak Wi-Fi signal produces identical symptoms.

Most competitor articles stop at "change your DNS to Google." That solves maybe one of the six failure modes. Below, each cause gets its own fix — which is exactly the technical depth that separates this guide from the thin "just use 8.8.8.8" advice ranking on page two.

🧩 The Six Network Failure Modes

  • Router firewall / NAT — silently dropping or misrouting game packets.
  • DNS timeout — name resolution hangs, causing login timeouts.
  • NIC driver bug — the network card drops the link for seconds at a time.
  • ISP throttling / routing — your provider deprioritizes or poorly routes game traffic.
  • QoS absence — other home traffic spikes your ping mid-boss.
  • Antivirus / firewall — the security suite blocks the client's connections.
💡 Rule of Thumb

If a router reboot fixes it for an hour, you have a state problem (NAT table, DNS cache, or driver) — not a hardware failure. Work the checklist top to bottom; don't buy a new router first.

🧱 2. Router Firewall, NAT & UPnP Settings

Your router's firewall is the first gate every OSRS packet passes through. Misconfigured NAT or an overzealous SPI firewall can drop the long-lived TCP connection the client keeps open, producing random "Connection lost" pop-ups.

🔓 Put Your PC in the DMZ (Temporarily, for Testing)

To confirm the router firewall is the cause, place your gaming PC in the router's DMZ for a test session:

How to Test With DMZ

  • Log into your router (usually 192.168.0.1 or 192.168.1.1 in a browser).
  • Find DMZ under Firewall / Advanced and enter your PC's local IP.
  • Play OSRS for 30 minutes. If disconnects stop, the firewall was the cause — then use a proper allow-list instead of leaving DMZ on.

🔁 Enable UPnP & Check NAT Type

OSRS uses standard HTTPS (port 443) and does not strictly require port forwarding, but a strict NAT can still interfere with certain world connections. Enable UPnP (Universal Plug and Play) so the client can negotiate cleanly:

Router Tweaks

  • Enable UPnP in the router's advanced settings.
  • Set SPI Firewall to a moderate level rather than "Maximum security."
  • If your router has "Game Mode" or "Adaptive QoS", turn it on (see Section 6).
  • Reboot the router after changes to clear the NAT table.

📦 MTU & IPv6

An MTU mismatch causes large packets to fragment and drop — a classic cause of mid-game stutters. Try setting WAN MTU to 1472–1492 if your ISP uses PPPoE, and test with IPv6 both on and off (some ISPs have flaky IPv6 routes that OSRS falls foul of).

⚠️ Don't Leave DMZ On

DMZ exposes your PC directly to the internet. Use it only as a diagnostic. Once confirmed, remove DMZ and instead add a firewall allow-rule for the Jagex client (see Section 7).

🌐 3. DNS Timeouts & How to Fix Them

DNS translates oldschool.runescape.com into an IP address. If your ISP's DNS is slow or intermittently failing, you get the "Login timeout" or "Error connecting to server" screen even though your internet "works" for everything else.

🔧 Switch to a Fast Public DNS

Set DNS on Windows

  • Settings → Network & Internet → your adapter → Edit DNS.
  • Set Preferred: 1.1.1.1 (Cloudflare) and Alternate: 8.8.8.8 (Google).
  • On macOS: System Settings → Network → Details → DNS → add 1.1.1.1.
  • On the router (best for all devices): set WAN DNS to 1.1.1.1 / 8.8.4.4.

🧹 Flush the DNS Cache

Changing DNS alone is not enough if the old cached bad entry lingers:

Flush (Windows)

  • Open Command Prompt as admin and run ipconfig /flushdns.
  • Then ipconfig /release and ipconfig /renew.
  • Restart the OSRS client and try logging in.

💡 Does DNS Affect In-Game Ping?

DNS only affects the initial connection (login). It will not lower your in-game ping — that is routing/latency, covered in Sections 4–6. If login timeouts are your only symptom, DNS was your fix.

💻 4. Network Card (NIC) Drivers & Intermittent Drops

This is the most overlooked cause and the one competitors never mention. An outdated or buggy NIC driver can drop the physical link for 5–30 seconds, during which OSRS shows "Connection lost" — even though Wi-Fi bars stay full and your browser pages eventually reload.

⬆️ Update the NIC Driver (Do Not Rely on Windows Update)

Windows — Proper Driver Update

  • Device Manager → Network adapters → right-click your Wi-Fi/Ethernet → Update driver.
  • Choose "Browse my computer" → "Let me pick" and pick the newest dated driver, OR download the latest from your motherboard/laptop maker's site (Intel, Realtek, Killer, Broadcom).
  • For Killer / Rivet NICs, install the vendor's Control Center and disable "Killer Prioritization Engine" — it is notorious for dropping game packets.

🔋 Disable Power-Saving on the NIC

Windows loves to power down the NIC to save battery, which severs the connection:

Stop the NIC From Sleeping

  • Device Manager → NIC → Properties → Power Management → uncheck "Allow the computer to turn off this device."
  • Advanced tab → set "Green Ethernet" / "Energy Efficient Ethernet" to Disabled.
  • Set "Roaming Aggressiveness" (Wi-Fi) to lowest so it does not hop access points mid-session.

🔌 Ethernet Beats Wi-Fi (Always)

For any serious bossing or PvP, run a CAT6 Ethernet cable. Wi-Fi adds 5–20 ms of jitter and is vulnerable to microwave/neighbor interference. If you must use Wi-Fi, connect to 5 GHz (not 2.4 GHz) and sit close to the router. Mobile players on Wi-Fi should also see the OSRS Mobile guide for signal tips.

⚠️ The 30-Second Drop Pattern

If you disconnect for exactly ~20–30 seconds on a repeating cycle, it is almost always a NIC driver or power-management issue, not Jagex. Updating the driver fixes it in 5 minutes.

📡 5. ISP Throttling & How to Detect It

Your ISP may not block OSRS, but it can deprioritize or poorly route its traffic, especially during peak hours. The symptom: great speed tests, but OSRS ping climbs to 200+ ms between 7–11 PM.

🧪 The VPN Test

Detect Throttling in 5 Minutes

  • Note your normal OSRS world ping (in-game, top-right).
  • Connect a reputable VPN and re-check the same world's ping.
  • If the VPN ping is lower and more stable, your ISP is throttling or misrouting the direct path.

🗺️ Routing, Not Speed

OSRS servers sit in specific regions. A bad route can send your packets on a 3,000-mile detour. Use a traceroute (tracert oldschool.runescape.com) — if one hop shows a huge latency jump, that is your ISP's backbone, not Jagex. A VPN or a different ISP often resolves it.

📞 Talk to Your ISP (With Evidence)

Before calling, gather: your traceroute, ping logs, and the times of day it worsens. Ask specifically about "bufferbloat" and "priority for real-time traffic." Some ISPs will enable a low-latency queue on request. If they will not, consider a business-tier plan or a fixed-wireless / fiber alternative.

💡 Bufferbloat Is the Silent Killer

Run a bufferbloat test (e.g., waveform.com/bufferbloat). If your latency spikes when you upload/download, that is bufferbloat — and QoS (next section) is your fix, not a new ISP.

⚙️ 6. QoS, Port Forwarding & Traffic Priority

QoS (Quality of Service) tells your router to prioritize OSRS packets over a roommate's Netflix or a cloud backup. This is the single best fix for "ping spikes only when someone else uses the internet."

🚦 Enable QoS / Adaptive QoS

Router QoS Setup

  • Router admin → QoS / Traffic Control → enable.
  • Set mode to "Gaming" or "Adaptive" if available.
  • Add your PC's IP (or MAC) to the high-priority device list.
  • Save and reboot the router.

🔢 Port Forwarding (Optional but Helpful)

OSRS does not require forwarding, but adding a static lease + forward removes edge-case NAT issues on strict routers:

Port Forward for OSRS

  • Reserve a static LAN IP for your PC (DHCP reservation).
  • Forward TCP 443 (and optionally UDP 43594/43595 used by the client) to that IP.
  • Protocol: Both / TCP+UDP; external and internal ports the same.

📶 Bandwidth Caps & Scheduled Downloads

Cap background devices. Set game consoles, phones, and cloud-sync apps to low priority, and schedule large downloads (Steam, backups) for off-peak hours. Even 5 Mbps of unscheduled upload (a cloud backup) can wreck your OSRS ping via bufferbloat.

🎯 The "Clean Pipeline" Checklist

  • QoS on, PC set to highest priority.
  • Static IP + port 443 forwarded.
  • Background uploads capped or scheduled.
  • 5 GHz Wi-Fi or Ethernet only during bossing.

🛡️ 7. Antivirus & Security Suite Blocking the Client

Modern security suites (third-party AV, "internet security," and even Windows Defender's controlled folder access) can flag the RuneScape client's network behavior and silently drop its packets — producing disconnects with no error message.

➕ Add OSRS / Jagex to Allow-Lists

Whitelist the Client

  • Find the Jagex Launcher and Old School RuneScape executables (typically in C:\Program Files\Jagex\).
  • In your AV → Exceptions / Exclusions, add both the .exe files and the install folder.
  • In Windows Defender → Firewall & network protection → Allow an app → ensure both Jagex entries are checked for Private and Public.
  • Disable "Controlled Folder Access" temporarily to test (re-enable after confirming).

🧰 Third-Party Suites to Check

Particularly aggressive ones: Norton 360, McAfee, Avast/AVG (their "Wi-Fi Inspector" and "Real Site" features intercept DNS and can break login), and Malwarebytes (web protection). Add exclusions or pause the web-shield feature while playing. If you cannot log in at all, the can't-login guide covers account-side causes too.

💡 One-Minute Isolation Test

Temporarily disable your AV's real-time/web protection and launch OSRS. If disconnects vanish, you found it — re-enable protection and add the proper exclusions rather than leaving it off.

If you have done all eight and still disconnect, the problem is likely outside your home — your ISP's backbone or a Jagex-side issue. ❓ 9. Home Network FAQs

Will changing DNS lower my in-game ping?

No. DNS only resolves the server address at login. It fixes login timeouts but not mid-game latency. For ping, you need QoS, a better route (VPN test), or Ethernet — covered in Sections 5 and 6.

My disconnects last exactly ~20–30 seconds on a loop — what is that?

That is the classic NIC driver or power-management symptom, not Jagex. Update your network card driver and disable "Allow the computer to turn off this device" in Device Manager. It usually resolves within minutes.

Do I really need to forward ports for OSRS?

Not strictly — OSRS uses standard HTTPS (443) and works behind most NATs. Port forwarding is optional and only helps on strict/corporate routers. The bigger win is QoS and a static IP so QoS can target your PC reliably.

Could my antivirus actually be disconnecting me with no warning?

Yes, very commonly. Web-shield and "real site"/DNS-protection features in Norton, McAfee, Avast, and Malwarebytes intercept the client's traffic and drop packets silently. Add Jagex exclusions and the disconnects typically stop.

How do I prove my ISP is throttling OSRS and not just bad Wi-Fi?

Run a VPN and compare the same world's ping. If the VPN is lower and steadier, your ISP is deprioritizing the direct route. A traceroute showing one hop with a huge latency jump is further proof. Bufferbloat tests show whether your own connection is the bottleneck instead.

Is Wi-Fi ever good enough for bossing on OSRS?

On a clean 5 GHz signal close to the router, yes for casual play. But for high-stakes PvM or PvP, Ethernet removes the jitter and interference that cause deaths. If you crash only at home and not on mobile data, your Wi-Fi/router is the issue — see our Android crash guide for the device side.
