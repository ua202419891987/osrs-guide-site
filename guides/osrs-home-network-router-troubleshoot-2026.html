🛡️ Level: All Levels

When Old School RuneScape stutters, drops you mid-boss, or refuses to log in even though every other website loads fine, the problem is almost never Jagex. It is the plumbing between your device and their servers: your home router, your network card, your DNS resolver, your internet provider, and the security software sitting on top of all of it. OSRS is a tiny game in bandwidth terms — it needs only a few dozen kilobits per second — but it is brutally sensitive to latency and packet loss. A 500 Mbps fiber line with 2% packet loss will feel far worse than a rock-steady 20 Mbps DSL connection. This guide is built to walk you through every layer of that plumbing, in the order you should test it, so you can fix the real cause instead of guessing. If your issue is on the client or device side rather than the network, the OSRS common bug-fix hub covers those errors directly.

The single biggest mistake players make is treating "lag" as one problem. It is not. A login timeout, a random disconnect, a high ping, and a stutter are four different symptoms with four different root causes, and they respond to completely different fixes. Switching your DNS will never lower your in-game ping. Buying a gaming router will never fix a buggy network-card driver. Disabling your antivirus will never fix ISP throttling. This guide separates those causes cleanly so you stop wasting time on fixes that cannot possibly work for your specific symptom. ⚡ Quick Verdict — The 30-Second Read

  • 📌 Switch DNS to 1.1.1.1 / 8.8.8.8 and flush it — DNS timeouts cause the "login timeout" screen, not in-game lag.
  • 📌 Update your NIC (network card) driver — outdated drivers cause intermittent 10–30s disconnects that look exactly like server lag.
  • 📌 Add OSRS/Jagex to your antivirus & firewall allow-list — security suites silently drop the game's packets with no error.
  • 📌 Set QoS to prioritize your PC so a Netflix or cloud-backup stream does not spike your RuneScape ping.
  • 📌 Test for ISP throttling with a VPN before blaming Jagex — routing, not raw speed, is usually the issue.
  • 📌 Run Ethernet, not Wi-Fi for any serious PvM — Wi-Fi adds jitter and interference that kills you at boss.

1. 🔍 Why Your Home Network Breaks OSRS (Not Your PC)

Before you touch a single setting, confirm the problem is actually your network. The fastest way is to answer three questions: Does the issue follow you across different worlds? Does it get worse when someone else in your home streams video or runs a big download? And does a simple reboot of your router temporarily fix it for an hour or two? If you answered yes to all three, you are looking at a home-network fault, not a Jagex server outage. Server-side problems do not care which world you pick, do not improve when your roommate stops streaming, and are not fixed by unplugging your router. The random disconnect & "world unavailable" guide explains how to tell a genuine Jagex-side incident from a local fault using the official status page.

The reason OSRS is so sensitive comes down to how the client talks to the server. It keeps one long-lived connection open and sends small, frequent updates about your position, inventory, and actions. If even a tiny fraction of those packets arrive late or out of order, the client has to wait, which you experience as a stutter, a "Connection lost" pop-up, or a teleport-back when the server rejects your input. Video streaming, by contrast, just buffers — a dropped packet merely skips a frame you never notice. That is why your Netflix can look perfect while OSRS is unplayable: the two workloads punish network problems in completely different ways. Speed-test numbers measure throughput, the thing OSRS cares about least; they say almost nothing about the jitter and packet loss that actually ruin your game.

Most competitor articles stop at "change your DNS to Google" and call it a day. That advice solves at most one of the six failure modes below, and it does nothing for the other five. The rest of this guide gives each cause its own dedicated fix, in the order you should test them, which is exactly the depth that separates a guide that actually fixes your disconnects from one that just ranks on page two of search results.

🧩 The Six Network Failure Modes

  • Router firewall / NAT — silently dropping or misrouting the game's long-lived connection.
  • DNS timeout — name resolution hangs, causing login timeouts and "error connecting" screens.
  • NIC driver bug — the network card drops the physical link for seconds at a time.
  • ISP throttling / bad routing — your provider deprioritizes or poorly routes game traffic.
  • QoS absence — other home traffic spikes your ping mid-boss or mid-PvP.
  • Antivirus / firewall — the security suite blocks or inspects the client's connections.
💡 Rule of Thumb

If a router reboot fixes it for an hour, you have a state problem (a stale NAT table, a poisoned DNS cache, or a driver glitch) — not a hardware failure. Work the checklist top to bottom and do not buy a new router first. New hardware hides the symptom for a while but the underlying misconfiguration comes back.

2. 🎯 Match Your Symptom to the Cause

Before diving into settings, pin down your exact symptom. The table below maps each common OSRS complaint to its most likely root cause, so you can jump straight to the right section instead of applying fixes at random. This single step saves most players an hour of wasted effort.

Symptom Most Likely Cause Jump To
"Login timeout" / "Error connecting to server" at launch DNS resolution failure or blocked port DNS (§4)
Disconnects for ~20–30s on a repeating cycle NIC driver bug or NIC power-saving NIC (§5)
Ping climbs to 200+ ms only 7–11 PM ISP throttling or peak-hour congestion ISP (§6)
Ping spikes only when someone else uses the net No QoS / bufferbloat on the router QoS (§7)
Disconnects with no error message at all Antivirus / firewall silently dropping packets Antivirus (§8)
Random DC that a router reboot briefly fixes Stale NAT table / SPI firewall Router (§3)

Notice that none of these symptoms point to Jagex first. When you see a pattern that follows your own home activity, trust the pattern. The exception is a world-specific outage, which the status page will confirm — if the status page is green and your disconnects follow your router reboot cycle, the fault is local.

3. 🧱 Router Firewall, NAT & UPnP Settings

Your router's firewall is the first gate every OSRS packet passes through. A misconfigured NAT table or an overzealous SPI (Stateful Packet Inspection) firewall can drop the long-lived TCP connection the client keeps open, producing random "Connection lost" pop-ups that look identical to server lag. The good news is these are the easiest faults to test and fix.

🔓 Put Your PC in the DMZ (Temporarily, for Testing)

To confirm the router firewall is the cause, place your gaming PC in the router's DMZ (Demilitarized Zone) for a single test session. DMZ exposes your device directly to the internet, bypassing the firewall entirely:

How to Test With DMZ

  • Log into your router (usually 192.168.0.1 or 192.168.1.1 in a browser — check the sticker on the device).
  • Find DMZ under Firewall / Advanced and enter your PC's local IP address.
  • Play OSRS for 30 minutes. If the disconnects stop, the firewall was the cause.
  • Then remove DMZ and use a proper allow-list rule instead of leaving it exposed.

🔁 Enable UPnP & Check NAT Type

OSRS uses standard HTTPS (port 443) and does not strictly require port forwarding, but a strict or "moderate" NAT can still interfere with certain world connections and with the Jagex Launcher's background update checks. Enable UPnP (Universal Plug and Play) so the client can negotiate a clean path:

Router Tweaks

  • Enable UPnP in the router's advanced settings.
  • Set SPI Firewall to a moderate level rather than "Maximum security."
  • If your router has "Game Mode" or "Adaptive QoS", turn it on (covered in §7).
  • Reboot the router after changes to clear the stale NAT table.

📦 MTU & IPv6

An MTU (Maximum Transmission Unit) mismatch causes large packets to fragment and drop — a classic but overlooked cause of mid-game stutters. If your ISP uses PPPoE (common on DSL and some fiber), try setting WAN MTU to 1472–1492 instead of the default 1500. Also test IPv6 both on and off: some ISPs have flaky IPv6 routes that OSRS falls foul of, and forcing IPv4-only can instantly stabilize a connection that was dropping every few minutes.

⚠️ Never Leave DMZ On

DMZ exposes your PC directly to the public internet with no firewall. Use it only as a diagnostic for one session. Once you have confirmed the firewall was the cause, remove DMZ and instead add a precise firewall allow-rule for the Jagex client (see §8). Leaving DMZ on is a genuine security risk.

4. 🌐 DNS Timeouts & How to Fix Them

DNS (Domain Name System) translates a name like oldschool.runescape.com into the numeric IP address the client actually connects to. If your ISP's DNS server is slow or intermittently failing, you get the "Login timeout" or "Error connecting to server" screen even though your internet "works" for everything else — because every other site already resolved its address and cached it.

🔧 Switch to a Fast Public DNS

Set DNS on Windows

  • Settings → Network & Internet → your adapter → Edit DNS.
  • Set Preferred: 1.1.1.1 (Cloudflare) and Alternate: 8.8.8.8 (Google).
  • On macOS: System Settings → Network → Details → DNS → add 1.1.1.1.
  • On the router (best for every device at once): set WAN DNS to 1.1.1.1 / 8.8.4.4.

🧹 Flush the DNS Cache

Changing the DNS server alone is not enough if the old, broken entry is still cached on your machine:

Flush (Windows)

  • Open Command Prompt as administrator and run ipconfig /flushdns.
  • Then ipconfig /release followed by ipconfig /renew.
  • Restart the OSRS client and try logging in again.

💡 Does DNS Affect In-Game Ping?

No. DNS only affects the initial connection at login. It will not lower your in-game ping — that is routing and latency, covered in §6 and §7. If login timeouts are your only symptom, DNS was your fix and you can stop here. If you still lag after logging in, DNS was never the problem.

5. 💻 Network Card (NIC) Drivers & Intermittent Drops

This is the most overlooked cause and the one almost no competitor mentions. An outdated or buggy network-card driver can drop the physical link for 5–30 seconds, during which OSRS shows "Connection lost" — even though your Wi-Fi bars stay full and your browser pages eventually reload. Because the drop is brief and silent, players blame Jagex for "server lag" when the fault is sitting in Device Manager.

⬆️ Update the NIC Driver (Do Not Rely on Windows Update)

Windows — Proper Driver Update

  • Device Manager → Network adapters → right-click your Wi-Fi/Ethernet → Update driver.
  • Choose "Browse my computer" → "Let me pick" and pick the newest dated driver, OR download the latest directly from your motherboard or laptop maker's site (Intel, Realtek, Killer, Broadcom).
  • For Killer / Rivet NICs, install the vendor's Control Center and disable "Killer Prioritization Engine" — it is notorious for dropping game packets in favor of its own priorities.

🔋 Disable Power-Saving on the NIC

Windows loves to power down the network card to save battery, which severs the connection exactly when you are standing still afk at a bank:

Stop the NIC From Sleeping

  • Device Manager → NIC → Properties → Power Management → uncheck "Allow the computer to turn off this device."
  • Advanced tab → set "Green Ethernet" / "Energy Efficient Ethernet" to Disabled.
  • Set "Roaming Aggressiveness" (Wi-Fi) to the lowest value so it does not hop access points mid-session.

🔌 Ethernet Beats Wi-Fi (Always)

For any serious bossing or PvP, run a CAT6 Ethernet cable. Wi-Fi adds 5–20 ms of jitter and is vulnerable to microwave ovens, Bluetooth, and neighbor interference. If you must use Wi-Fi, connect to 5 GHz (not 2.4 GHz) and sit close to the router. Mac users with recurring drops should also see the macOS disconnect & failed-login fix, which covers the platform-specific network-stack quirks. The public Wi-Fi captive-portal guide covers the extra layer of pain you hit when playing from a hotel, café, or airport network.

⚠️ The 30-Second Drop Pattern

If you disconnect for roughly 20–30 seconds on a repeating cycle, it is almost always a NIC driver or power-management issue, not Jagex. Updating the driver and disabling power-saving fixes it in five minutes and saves you from falsely reporting a "server lag" bug.

6. 📡 ISP Throttling, Routing & Bufferbloat

Your internet provider may not block OSRS, but it can deprioritize or poorly route its traffic, especially during peak evening hours. The classic symptom: perfect speed-test numbers, but your OSRS world ping climbs past 200 ms between 7 and 11 PM while your roommate's YouTube stays smooth.

🧪 The VPN Test

Detect Throttling in 5 Minutes

  • Note your normal OSRS world ping (shown top-right in game).
  • Connect a reputable VPN and re-check the same world's ping.
  • If the VPN ping is lower and more stable, your ISP is throttling or misrouting the direct path.

🗺️ Routing, Not Speed

OSRS servers sit in specific regions. A bad route can send your packets on a needless continental detour. Use a traceroute (tracert oldschool.runescape.com on Windows, traceroute on macOS/Linux) — if a single hop shows a huge latency jump, that is your ISP's backbone, not Jagex. A VPN that picks a better path, or in worst cases a different ISP, often resolves it. The accelerator stuck guide covers the same routing problem for players behind cross-border routing restrictions.

📞 Talk to Your ISP (With Evidence)

Before calling support, gather your traceroute, a few ping logs, and the times of day it worsens. Ask specifically about "bufferbloat" and "priority for real-time traffic." Some ISPs will enable a low-latency queue on request. If they will not, a business-tier plan or a fixed-wireless / fiber alternative often solves it. The chart below shows how much bufferbloat alone can add to your effective latency.

💡 Bufferbloat Is the Silent Killer

Run a bufferbloat test (e.g., the waveform bufferbloat test site). If your latency spikes when you upload or download at the same time, that is bufferbloat — and QoS (next section) is your fix, not a new ISP. This single test explains more "random lag" than any other home-network factor.

7. ⚙️ QoS, Port Forwarding & Traffic Priority

QoS (Quality of Service) tells your router to prioritize OSRS packets over a roommate's Netflix or a cloud backup. This is the single best fix for the complaint "my ping only spikes when someone else uses the internet." Without QoS, all traffic is equal and a big upload can stall your game packets behind it.

🚦 Enable QoS / Adaptive QoS

Router QoS Setup

  • Router admin → QoS / Traffic Control → enable.
  • Set mode to "Gaming" or "Adaptive" if available.
  • Add your PC's IP (or MAC address) to the high-priority device list.
  • Save and reboot the router.

🔢 Port Forwarding (Optional but Helpful)

OSRS does not require forwarding, but adding a static lease plus a forward removes edge-case NAT issues on strict or corporate routers:

Port Forward for OSRS

  • Reserve a static LAN IP for your PC (DHCP reservation).
  • Forward TCP 443 (and optionally UDP 43594/43595 used by the client) to that IP.
  • Protocol: Both / TCP+UDP; external and internal ports set the same.

📶 Bandwidth Caps & Scheduled Downloads

Cap background devices. Set game consoles, phones, and cloud-sync apps to low priority, and schedule large downloads (Steam, backups) for off-peak hours. Even 5 Mbps of unscheduled upload — a silent cloud backup — can wreck your OSRS ping through bufferbloat. The "Clean Pipeline" checklist below is the end goal.

🎯 The "Clean Pipeline" Checklist

  • QoS on, with your PC set to highest priority.
  • Static IP reserved + port 443 forwarded.
  • Background uploads capped or scheduled for off-peak.
  • 5 GHz Wi-Fi or Ethernet only during bossing.

8. 🛡️ Antivirus & Security Suite Blocking the Client

Modern security suites — third-party antivirus, "internet security" bundles, and even Windows Defender's Controlled Folder Access — can flag the RuneScape client's network behavior and silently drop its packets. The result is disconnects with no error message and no warning, which players naturally blame on the server. This is the most misdiagnosed cause in the entire guide.

➕ Add OSRS / Jagex to Allow-Lists

Whitelist the Client

  • Find the Jagex Launcher and Old School RuneScape executables (typically in C:\Program Files\Jagex\).
  • In your antivirus → Exceptions / Exclusions, add both the .exe files and the install folder.
  • In Windows Defender → Firewall & network protection → Allow an app → ensure both Jagex entries are checked for Private and Public networks.
  • Disable "Controlled Folder Access" temporarily to test, then re-enable after confirming.

🧰 Third-Party Suites to Check

Particularly aggressive ones: Norton 360, McAfee, Avast/AVG (their "Wi-Fi Inspector" and "Real Site" features intercept DNS and can break login), and Malwarebytes (web protection). Add exclusions or pause the web-shield feature while playing. If you cannot log in at all, the can't-login fix guide covers account-side causes too, so you can tell a network block from a lockout.

💡 One-Minute Isolation Test

Temporarily disable your antivirus's real-time and web protection and launch OSRS. If the disconnects vanish, you found it — re-enable protection and add the proper exclusions rather than leaving it off. Running with AV disabled is not a fix; a clean exclusion is.

9. 📶 Wi-Fi Deep-Dive: 2.4 GHz vs 5 GHz vs Ethernet

If you play on Wi-Fi — on desktop, laptop, or the mobile app — the radio layer adds its own failure modes that have nothing to do with your ISP. Understanding the difference lets you stop blaming your provider for a problem that is three feet away from your router.

2.4 GHz: range, but crowded

The 2.4 GHz band travels farther and through walls better, but it is shared with microwaves, Bluetooth, baby monitors, and every neighbor's router. In a dense apartment, 2.4 GHz is a noise floor, and OSRS stutters become constant. Use it only if you have no choice and sit far from the router.

5 GHz: fast and clean, short range

The 5 GHz band is far less crowded and gives much lower jitter, but it loses range through walls quickly. For stable OSRS, connect to 5 GHz and stay within one room of the router. This alone fixes a large share of "mobile disconnects at home" complaints.

Ethernet: the only real answer for PvM

For high-stakes bossing or PvP, nothing beats a cable. A CAT6 run removes radio jitter entirely and is immune to neighbor interference. If running a cable is impossible, a powerline adapter or a dedicated 5 GHz mesh backhaul is the next best thing. The mobile best-settings guide and the full mobile guide cover the device-side signal tips that pair with this network advice, and the Android crash guide handles the device crashes that mimic network drops.

⚠️ Don't Blame "Lag" for a Crash

A network drop and an app crash look similar — both kick you out. But a crash freezes the screen and shows an error, while a network drop shows "Connection lost" and the world keeps running for a moment. If you see a freeze, the cause is the device, not your router — go to the client-side guides above instead of reconfiguring your network.

11. ❓ Home Network FAQs

Will changing DNS lower my in-game ping?

No. DNS only resolves the server address at login. It fixes login timeouts but not mid-game latency. For ping you need QoS, a better route (the VPN test), or Ethernet — covered in §6, §7, and §9.

My disconnects last exactly ~20–30 seconds on a loop — what is that?

That is the classic NIC driver or power-management symptom, not Jagex. Update your network-card driver and disable "Allow the computer to turn off this device" in Device Manager. It usually resolves within minutes and saves you from a false "server lag" report.

Do I really need to forward ports for OSRS?

Not strictly — OSRS uses standard HTTPS (port 443) and works behind most NATs. Port forwarding is optional and only helps on strict or corporate routers. The bigger win is QoS plus a static IP so QoS can reliably target your PC.

Could my antivirus actually disconnect me with no warning?

Yes, very commonly. Web-shield and "real site" / DNS-protection features in Norton, McAfee, Avast, and Malwarebytes intercept the client's traffic and drop packets silently. Add Jagex exclusions and the disconnects typically stop within the same session.

How do I prove my ISP is throttling OSRS and not just bad Wi-Fi?

Run a VPN and compare the same world's ping. If the VPN is lower and steadier, your ISP is deprioritizing the direct route. A traceroute showing one hop with a huge latency jump is further proof. A bufferbloat test shows whether your own router is the bottleneck instead.

Is Wi-Fi ever good enough for bossing on OSRS?

On a clean 5 GHz signal close to the router, yes for casual play. But for high-stakes PvM or PvP, Ethernet removes the jitter and interference that cause deaths. If you crash only at home and not on mobile data, your Wi-Fi or router is the issue.

Why does my ping spike only when someone else downloads?

That is bufferbloat — your router lets the download monopolize the connection and your game packets queue behind it. Enable QoS and prioritize your PC, or schedule large downloads for off-peak hours. This is a router setting, not an ISP problem.

What is the single fastest fix I should try first?

Reboot your router and modem, then flush your DNS and set it to 1.1.1.1. That pair resolves the two most common faults — stale NAT state and login-timeout DNS — in about two minutes, before you touch any drivers or call your ISP.

Does a "gaming router" actually help OSRS?

Only if your current router lacks QoS or has broken NAT handling. A gaming router will not fix a buggy NIC driver or ISP throttling. The settings in this guide matter far more than the hardware badge. Try the free fixes first; buy hardware only if QoS is missing.

My disconnects follow a router reboot but return after an hour — what now?

That is a state problem, not hardware. The NAT table or DNS cache is corrupting over time. Flush DNS, update the router firmware, and enable UPnP. If it persists, the router's firmware is faulty and a replacement (not necessarily "gaming") is warranted.

Can a VPN make OSRS worse?

Yes, if you pick a distant or overloaded VPN server, the extra hop adds latency. Use the VPN only as a diagnostic: if a nearby, reputable VPN lowers your ping, your ISP is the cause. For daily play, fix the routing or consider a better ISP rather than routing every packet through a VPN.

I play on mobile at home and keep disconnecting — is it the app or my network?

If it happens only on Wi-Fi and not on mobile data, your home network is the cause — work §9 (5 GHz, close to router) and §7 (QoS). If it happens on both, the app or account is the issue; see the mobile best-settings and can't-login guides linked above.

12. 🎯 Final Tips — Fix the Cause, Not the Symptom

  • Reboot first, always. A router/modem reboot clears stale NAT and DNS state that causes half of all "random lag" reports.
  • Flush DNS and use 1.1.1.1. This is the only fix for login timeouts; it does nothing for in-game ping, so don't expect it to.
  • Update your NIC driver. The 20–30s cyclic drop is a driver, not Jagex — five minutes here beats hours of "server lag" complaints.
  • Whitelist Jagex in your antivirus. Silent packet drops with no error are almost always a security suite, not the network.
  • Turn on QoS and prioritize your PC. Kills the "ping spikes only when someone else uses the net" problem via bufferbloat control.
  • Test throttling with a VPN. Before blaming Jagex for peak-hour lag, prove it is your ISP with a VPN comparison and a traceroute.
  • Use Ethernet for PvM. Wi-Fi jitter causes deaths no gear can prevent; a CAT6 cable is the cheapest "upgrade" you will ever buy.
  • Never leave DMZ on. It is a diagnostic only — replace it with a precise firewall allow-rule once you confirm the firewall was the cause.
  • Work the checklist in order. Symptom-matching (§2) gets you to the right section faster than applying fixes at random.
  • Log evidence before calling your ISP. A timestamped ping -t log and a traceroute turn "my game lags" into a fixable ticket.
