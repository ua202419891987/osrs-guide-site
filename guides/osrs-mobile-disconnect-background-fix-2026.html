OSRS Mobile Keeps Disconnecting in the Background? 5 Scene Fixes for 2026

You tap away to answer a text, lock your phone to walk somewhere, or drop from Wi-Fi to mobile data — and when you come back, OSRS Mobile has logged you out. This is the single most reported mobile annoyance in 2026, and almost none of the fixes you'll find elsewhere actually explain why. Below we break it into the exact 5 scenarios that cause it and fix each one.

📅 Stage 0 · Beginner Troubleshooting — Updated July 2026 · 13 min read
🎓 Level: Beginner

⏱️ Quick Verdict — 30-Second Read

  • 📌 OSRS Mobile has no "true" background mode — Jagex's app holds an active socket that the OS closes the moment it decides the app is idle.
  • 📌 Scenario 1 (switch apps): Android "kill background app" + iOS "Background App Refresh off" cuts the link in seconds.
  • 📌 Scenario 2 (Doze / Low Power): Battery savers suspend network sockets; the game can't keep the heartbeat alive.
  • 📌 Scenario 3 (Wi-Fi → data): Your IP changes, and the server sees a "new" connection and boots the old session.
  • 📌 Scenario 4 (idle timeout): After ~5 minutes of no input, the world server force-logs an idle character — independent of your phone.
  • 📌 Fix the per-brand battery whitelist (Samsung, Xiaomi, iPhone) and you'll stay connected far longer.

1. 🔍 Why OSRS Mobile Drops the Connection at All

Before we fix anything, understand the architecture. The official OSRS Mobile app keeps a single, continuously-open network connection ("socket") to the game world server. That connection sends a tiny "heartbeat" packet every few seconds to prove you're still there. The moment your phone stops letting the app send that heartbeat — because it went to sleep, lost network, or got killed for memory — the server decides you're gone and tears the session down. You come back to a login screen.

This is different from a crash. A crash or black screen is the app itself dying; a background disconnect is the network session dying while the app is technically still installed and open. Knowing the difference tells you which fix to reach for.

Most sites tell you "just stay on Wi-Fi" and stop there. That advice is useless because the three biggest causes are not your router — they're your phone's own power management, your carrier's IP behavior, and the game's idle timer. The rest of this guide attacks each one separately.

🧩 The Four Real Triggers

  • Process death — the OS closes the app when you switch away (Android) or suspends its network (iOS).
  • Socket suspension — Doze / Low Power / battery optimization pause all background network traffic.
  • IP reassignment — swapping Wi-Fi to 4G/5G gives you a new IP; the server treats the old session as a duplicate and drops it.
  • Idle force-logout — the world server itself logs out characters that haven't acted in ~5 minutes.
💡 The Core Truth

you cannot make OSRS Mobile behave like a music streaming app that plays forever in your pocket. Jagex's client is not designed for indefinite backgrounding. What you can do is push each of the four triggers from "drops in 10 seconds" to "survives a 3–4 minute errand." That's the realistic goal, and it's very achievable.

2. 🔄 Scenario 1 — Switching Apps / Locking the Screen

This is the #1 complaint: "I opened my banking app for 5 seconds and OSRS logged me out." Here's what's actually happening on each platform.

② Android — the app gets killed for RAM

Android is aggressively multitasking. When you switch to another app, the OS keeps OSRS Mobile "cached" in the background — but if memory gets tight, Android silently kills it to free RAM. Even before a full kill, Android may freeze the app's network thread the instant it's not on screen, so the heartbeat stops and the server drops you within ~30–60 seconds.

What to do on Android

  • Open Settings → Apps → Old School RuneScape → Battery and set it to "Unrestricted" / "Don't optimize" (see §6 for brand specifics).
  • After switching away, don't swipe OSRS Mobile off the recent-apps list — that force-closes it. Just leave it in the tray.
  • On phones with 4 GB RAM or less, close a few other background apps first so the OS doesn't need to reclaim memory.
  • Enable "lock the app" in the recent-apps view (long-press the card, tap the lock icon) on Samsung/Xiaomi so it's never auto-killed.

③ iPhone / iPad — Background App Refresh & suspension

iOS is even stricter: unless an app is explicitly allowed, it gets suspended the moment you leave it, and its network socket is closed within seconds. OSRS Mobile is not whitelisted by default.

What to do on iOS

  • Open Settings → General → Background App Refresh and make sure it's ON (at least for OSRS Mobile).
  • Do not force-close the app (swipe up from the app switcher) before walking away — a freshly-launched-but-suspended app reconnects worse than a warm one.
  • Keep the screen on if you expect to be away only briefly: use the in-game "screen always on" / disable auto-lock (Settings → Display & Brightness → Auto-Lock → Never) while playing.
  • Disable Low Power Mode (see §3) — it disables Background App Refresh entirely.
⚠️ Don't Trust "Recent Apps"

Just because OSRS Mobile is still shown in your recent-apps list does not mean it's alive. On both platforms, an app in that list can be frozen or killed. The only guaranteed-alive state is "currently on your screen." Plan errands accordingly.

3. 🔋 Scenario 2 — Doze, Low Power Mode & Battery Savers

Both operating systems have a "save battery by killing background network" feature, and it is brutal to a game that relies on a constant heartbeat.

④ Android Doze & Adaptive Battery

When your screen is off for a while, Android enters Doze mode, which defers all network traffic to infrequent "maintenance windows." OSRS Mobile's heartbeat can't wait for the next window — the server sees silence and disconnects you. Adaptive Battery makes this worse by learning to restrict "unused" apps (and it may decide a game you only open at night is unused).

Disable the battery killers (Android)

  1. Settings → Battery → Battery optimization → find Old School RuneScape → set to "Don't optimize".
  2. Settings → Battery → Adaptive Battery → consider turning it off (or at least it won't target OSRS once you've whitelisted the app).
  3. Turn off any manufacturer "Game Booster / Device Care / Memory cleaner" that force-closes idle games.
  4. Keep the phone plugged in if you're AFK-fishing at your desk — charging disables Doze.

⑤ iPhone Low Power Mode

On iOS, Low Power Mode pauses Background App Refresh and mail fetch and reduces network activity — exactly what kills OSRS's socket. It also kicks in automatically at 20% battery.

Disable Low Power Mode (iOS)

  • Control Center → tap the battery icon so it's not yellow, or Settings → Battery → Low Power Mode → OFF.
  • If you genuinely need battery savings, accept that the game will drop when backgrounded — there's no way around it on iOS while Low Power is on.
💡 The "Plug It In" Trick

Charging disables Doze on Android and disables Low Power Mode behavior on iOS. If you're at home AFK-skilling on mobile, keep it on the charger — you'll stay connected dramatically longer because the OS simply won't bother suspending a plugged-in app.

4. 📶 Scenario 3 — Mobile Data vs Wi-Fi (The IP Swap)

This scenario confuses everyone: "I never left the app, never locked the phone, but it disconnected when I walked out of the house." The cause is your IP address changing.

When you're on Wi-Fi, your phone has one public IP. The moment you leave Wi-Fi range and the phone falls back to 4G/5G, your carrier assigns a different IP. To the game server, that looks like two clients trying to use the same account from two locations. The server protects you from session hijacking by invalidating the old session immediately — so you get booted. Walking back into Wi-Fi and re-grabbing yet another IP can do it again.

🎯 How to Minimize the IP-Swap Drop

  • Pick one network and commit. Don't hop between Wi-Fi and data mid-session if you can avoid it. Start on the network you'll stay on.
  • Prefer Wi-Fi for long sessions. Home/office Wi-Fi keeps a stable IP for hours. Mobile data IPs can also rotate when the phone hands off between cell towers.
  • Use a stable mobile connection if Wi-Fi isn't an option. Stand still — walking between towers triggers carrier-side IP reassignments even without changing network type.
  • Turn off "Wi-Fi Assistant / Adaptive Wi-Fi / Switch to mobile data" features (common on Google Pixel, OnePlus, Xiaomi) that auto-hop networks behind your back.
  • Enable "Wi-Fi Calling" off if your carrier's Wi-Fi calling tunnels data in a way that flips your apparent IP.
🚨 Don't Blame Your VPN/Accelerator Here

If you're outside the game's supported regions and run an accelerator (common for players in China — see our VPN/accelerator login guide), the IP swap is doubly disruptive: the accelerator's exit node changes and the anti-cheat may flag the new IP. Keep the accelerator on a fixed node and never let the phone auto-switch it off.

💡 The 5-Second Rule

If you must change networks, do it in under 5 seconds of "no connection" if possible, or just accept the reconnect. OSRS Mobile auto-reconnects to the login screen fast; the pain is losing your spot, not losing your account. Bank/gear are server-side.

5. ⏳ Scenario 4 — Background Idle Timeout / Auto Logout

Even with a perfect phone and perfect network, OSRS will eventually log you out if you don't act. This is not a mobile bug — it's the game's own idle timer, and it works identically on desktop.

⏱️ The Idle Timers You're Fighting

  • ~5 minutes of zero input → the world server force-logs your character to free the slot.
  • Some minigames / dangerous areas log you faster or don't auto-log at all (you can die AFK).
  • The lobby also times out, but more slowly.

On mobile this bites because "switching apps for a minute" easily becomes "10 minutes gone" when someone texts you. The fix is to either keep sending input or accept the logout.

Stay-alive tactics for mobile

  • AFK-skilling that counts as input: activities like fishing, woodcutting, mining register actions and reset the idle timer as long as the action repeats — but only while the app is active on screen. Backgrounded, no input is sent, so the timer runs.
  • Use the "logout timer" warning: OSRS shows a "You will be logged out in 1 minute" notice — but you must be looking at the screen to see and click it.
  • Best practice: if you'll be away more than ~3 minutes, just log out cleanly from the in-game logout button. A clean logout is safer than a forced one and avoids "lost connection" streaks.
  • Never AFK in dangerous content expecting the background to hold. If the app drops, your character sits still and can be killed or looted.
💡 Set a Real Reminder

Mobile players underestimate how fast 5 minutes passes. Use a phone timer for 4 minutes when you step away, so you can tap back in before the server logs you. It's low-tech but it works better than any setting.

6. 📱 Per-Brand Settings: Samsung, Xiaomi, iPhone

Generic "turn off battery optimization" advice fails because every manufacturer hides the real switch in a different menu. Here are the exact paths.

⑥ Samsung (One UI)

  • Settings → Battery → Background usage limits → turn off "Put unused apps to sleep" and "Sleep apps."
  • Add OSRS Mobile to "Never auto sleeping apps" (the + button).
  • Settings → Apps → Old School RuneScape → Battery → set to "Unrestricted".
  • Open the app in recent-tasks, tap the app icon → "Lock this app" so memory cleaning won't kill it.

⑦ Xiaomi / Redmi / POCO (MIUI / HyperOS)

  • Security app → Battery → App battery saver → set OSRS Mobile to "No restrictions".
  • Settings → Apps → Permissions → Autostart → enable for OSRS Mobile.
  • Recent-tasks → tap the lock icon to lock the app.
  • Disable "MIUI optimization" side-effects by setting "App behavior" to "Show all activities."

⑧ iPhone / iPad (iOS)

  • Settings → General → Background App Refresh → ON (for OSRS at minimum).
  • Settings → Battery → Low Power Mode → OFF.
  • While playing, Settings → Display & Brightness → Auto-Lock → Never (remember to revert later to save battery).
  • Keep iOS updated — older iOS versions had stricter background-socket limits.
🔋 The Battery Trade-Off

Whitelisting OSRS Mobile from battery optimization will use more battery and the phone may run warmer. That's the unavoidable cost of staying connected. If battery life matters more than staying logged in, accept background disconnects and just re-log in — your account is never at risk either way.

8. ❓ FAQ — Mobile Background Disconnects

Will OSRS Mobile ever stay connected forever in the background like a chat app?

No. The client is built around a single active game socket with a heartbeat, and both mobile OSes suspend background network for battery reasons. You can extend survival to a few minutes with the settings above, but indefinite backgrounding isn't supported. Treat "stay logged in while I do a 2-minute chore" as the achievable target.

Does using mobile data instead of Wi-Fi cause more disconnects?

Not by itself — a stable 4G/5G connection is fine. The problem is the transition: each time your IP changes (Wi-Fi↔data, or handoff between towers) the server may drop the old session as a duplicate. Staying on one network is what matters more than which network it is.

My phone is plugged in and still disconnects — why?

Then the cause is likely the idle timeout (§5), not power management. Even on charge, after ~5 minutes of zero input the world server logs your character out. Keep the screen on and tap occasionally, or accept the logout. Also confirm Low Power Mode / Doze are truly off, since some phones re-enable them after updates.

Will whitelisting the app from battery optimization harm my phone?

It increases battery drain and heat for that app — that's the trade-off. It does not harm the device. If you'd rather save battery, simply let it disconnect and re-log in when you return; your account, bank, and progress are all server-side and completely safe.

Is there a setting inside OSRS Mobile to "stay connected"?

There is no in-app toggle for background persistence. The closest controls are the auto-logout warning and logout timer preferences in the in-game Settings → Gameplay, which only help you react before the server logs you. The real fixes are all in your phone's OS settings, covered in §2–§6.

Why does it disconnect more on my new Xiaomi than my old Samsung?

Each manufacturer tunes background-kill aggressiveness differently, and MIUI/HyperOS is among the strictest. Follow the §6 Xiaomi steps (Autostart on, no battery restrictions, lock the app) and it will behave like the Samsung did. Default brand settings — not the game — are almost always the culprit.
