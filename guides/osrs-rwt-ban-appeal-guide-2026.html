RWT Ban Appeal: How to Fight a False Real-World Trading Ban (2026)

Updated August 19, 2026  |  15 min read  |  By Yanchun Wu

You logged in, saw the red "Account Disabled — Real-World Trading" screen, and you know you never bought or sold GP. This guide is the decision playbook: what RWT actually covers, the exact appeal letter to send, the realistic timeline, and — most importantly — what to do if your first appeal gets rejected. Pair it with our companion breakdown of what counts as a false RWT ban and how to prove your innocence.

🛡️ Level: All
🆕 NEW

A real-world trading (RWT) ban means Jagex believes gold, items, or membership on your account changed hands for real money outside the game. The problem: the detection systems that flag RWT are automated and pattern-based, and they make mistakes. A mule account flipping gold between your own characters, a friend "gifting" you a bond, or an unusually large trade from a clanmate can all trigger the same alarm as an actual RuneScape gold seller. If you are innocent, the ban is appealable — but how you write the appeal and what you do after a rejection decides whether you win.

This guide assumes you have already read our false RWT ban explainer and want the operational step-by-step: the letter, the calendar, and the decision tree. If your ban is actually a macro or botting flag instead, jump to our false macro ban appeal guide because the evidence you need is completely different.

⚡ Quick Verdict

  • 📌 Most "false" RWT bans are pattern flags — mule transfers, bonds, gift cards, or big trades — not actual cash-for-GP deals.
  • 📌 Write the appeal like a fraud investigator — dates, amounts, counterparties, and a plain denial of cash.
  • 📌 One appeal at a time. Spamming appeals or arguing with the bot gets you auto-closed faster.
  • 📌 Rejection is not the end. A denied first appeal can still be won on a clean second submission with better evidence.
  • 📌 Secure your other accounts. RWT flags chain — see our chain ban guide.

1. 🧾 What Counts as RWT (and Why You Got Flagged)

Before you write a single word of your appeal, you need to understand the four patterns Jagex's systems watch for. Not one of them requires proof that real money changed hands — they are behavioral triggers. That is good news for you: if your ban came from a pattern rather than a cash transaction, your appeal is about explaining the pattern, not proving a negative.

① Gold transfer between your own accounts (the "mule" flag)

This is the single most common false-positive. You moved GP or items from a main to an alt, or between two alts, or dumped a boss alt's loot into a bank alt. Jagex's trade graphs treat one-sided, high-volume transfers as the signature of a gold-selling operation moving stock between mules. If you did this with your own characters, say so plainly: list the account display names, the rough dates, and the purpose (gear hand-me-downs, boss loot consolidation, skill supplies). The key phrase that helps is "these are all accounts I personally own and operate."

② Membership, bonds, and gift cards

Paying for membership with a bond someone "gifted" you, or redeeming a prepaid OSRS card bought from a third-party key shop, can flag. Bond gifting between real friends is allowed, but a bond that originated from a bulk RWT seller's stock still carries that taint on the ledger. Prepaid cards bought from grey-market resellers are a known injection point for stolen payment methods, and redeeming one links your account to that cluster. In your appeal, name where the membership came from and acknowledge if it was a gift — honesty about the source reads better than pretending you bought it direct.

③ Unusually large or lopsided trades

A clanmate gave you 50M GP "for helping at TOB." A stranger paid you 20M for a services trade. A drop party dumped millions on you. Any trade where one side receives far more than it gives, especially repeated, looks like a cash-settled deal settled in-game. Explain the relationship and the reason. "We were clanmates for two years and he owed me for raids" is a real explanation; "I don't know who that was" is not.

④ The actual cash-for-GP deal (the real ban)

If you did buy or sell GP, bonds, or services for real money through a site or a Discord seller, that is a real RWT ban and this guide's templates will not save the account — Jagex treats admitted RWT as terminal. Be honest with yourself before appealing: sending a "I didn't do it" letter when there is a cash trail behind the ban is a waste of your one good shot. Our false RWT explainer helps you tell which camp you are in.

💡 The Pattern Test

Ask: "Did real money leave my bank account for OSRS value, or did OSRS value move between accounts/people I actually know?" If the answer is the second one, you are in false-positive territory and this guide applies. If the answer is the first one, no appeal template will recover the account — focus instead on what to do when recovery is denied for any linked clean accounts.

2. 🔍 Before You Appeal — Read the Ban & Gather Facts

Roughly half of weak appeals fail because the player appealed emotionally before they understood what was alleged. Spend 30 minutes here and your letter will be 10x stronger.

① Read the exact ban reason on the login screen

The disabled-account screen tells you the category: "Real-World Trading," "Macroing," "Serious" (usually RWT/chargeback), or "Bug Abuse." RWT and "Serious" both relate to trading and are appealed the same way. If the screen says "Macroing" but you are here, you are in the wrong guide — use our macro ban appeal guide. Screenshot the exact wording; you will reference it.

② Pull your own trade and login memory

Before writing, reconstruct the last 30–60 days mentally (or from clan chat logs, Discord, or a spreadsheet if you track GP). Note: the big trades, the bond gifts, the alt transfers, the new "friend" who paid you. You do not need Jagex's internal logs — you need your own honest timeline so the appeal is specific. Vague appeals ("I never traded anyone") get rejected because the system knows you did trade; contradicting the data makes you look like a liar.

③ Decide your single, consistent story

Appeals are reviewed for consistency across submissions. Pick the true explanation and stick to it across every message. If your first appeal says "my brother used the account" and your second says "it was a clanmate gift," you have confessed to account sharing (also bannable) and contradicted yourself. The strongest story is the true one, told the same way every time.

⚠️ Do Not Contact "Unban" Services

Any Discord, Telegram, or website promising to "get your RWT ban reversed for a fee" is a scam that also typically asks for your login details — which turns a recoverable false positive into confirmed account sharing and kills any appeal. Only ever use the official support form linked from the ban screen or account recovery page.

3. ✉️ The Appeal Letter Template (Copy-Paste)

This is the core of the guide. Copy the block below, fill the brackets, and send it through the official appeal form. Keep it under 400 words, factual, and free of anger. Reviewers skim; a tight, specific letter beats a furious essay.

① The subject / category line

Pick "Real-World Trading" or "Serious" as the reason, then state plainly: Appeal — false RWT flag, account owned and operated by me, no real-money trades.

② The body template (fill the brackets)

Account display name: [your main name]
Account creation year: [e.g. 2018]
Ban category shown: Real-World Trading
Date of ban: [DD/MM/YYYY]

I am appealing a permanent RWT ban. I have never bought or sold
GP, items, or membership for real money, and I have never used
a gold-selling website, Discord seller, or third-party service.

The trading activity that likely triggered this flag:
- [e.g. I transferred GP from my main to my own alt "NAME" on
  DATE to buy construction supplies. Both accounts are mine.]
- [e.g. A clanmate "NAME" gifted me a bond on DATE; we have
  known each other since YEAR and raided together.]
- [e.g. I received X GP from "NAME" as repayment for a TOB
  split on DATE; no real money was involved.]

All characters involved are accounts I personally own and play,
or real people I know in game. No real-world payment was made
or received on my part. I can confirm login locations, recovery
questions, or purchase history if useful.

I respect the RWT rules and am not asking for leniency on a
real trade — only that this automated flag be reviewed against
my actual account history, which shows normal personal play.

Thank you for your time.

③ What makes this template work

ElementWhy it helps
Names and dates filled inShows you engaged with the actual data instead of denying all trades
"Accounts I personally own"Directly addresses the mule-transfer pattern
"No real-world payment" stated once, clearlyClean denial without over-swearing (repeating "I swear" reads as guilt)
Offer to confirm purchase historySignals confidence; real buyers fear this check
Under 400 words, no rageReviewers process hundreds daily; brevity gets read
💡 The "I Swear" Trap

Do not open with "I SWEAR I didn't RWT!!!" fifteen times. A reviewer who sees panic-swearing reads either guilt or a scripted unban-service template. State the denial once, calmly, then spend the rest of the letter on the facts that explain the pattern. Facts overturn flags; emotions do not.

4. 📅 The Appeal Timeline — What Happens After You Submit

Knowing the clock stops you from panicking and from spamming. Here is the realistic 2026 flow for an RWT appeal submitted through the official form.

First-line automated reviewDay 1–7A script checks your account against the flag evidenceWait. Do not email again — it resets nothing but wastes their time
Human reviewer (if escalated)Day 7–21A person reads your letter against the trade graphBe patient; this is where a good letter wins
Response: Upheld or DeniedDay 14–30 typicalYou get "ban stands" or "account restored"If restored, secure it (see chain-ban section). If denied, go to Section 6

The single most important behavioral rule: one appeal in the queue at a time. Submitting five appeals in a week does not speed anything up; it marks the ticket as abusive and can get all of them closed as "already reviewed." Submit once, wait for the response, then decide.

5. 🚫 Common Rejection Reasons (and What They Really Mean)

When Jagex denies an RWT appeal, the message is usually short and final-sounding. Here is what each common response actually means and whether it is truly terminal.

"Our decision is final."First-line auto-close; often a template, not a deep reviewNo — a clean second appeal with new evidence can still win
"Evidence shows RWT activity."The trade graph matched a known RWT cluster or patternMaybe — explain the cluster link (e.g. a gifted bond from a tainted source)
"Account sharing detected."Logins from mismatched locations/IPs were seenIf true sharing occurred, yes; if it was you on VPN/travel, explain it

⚠️ "Final" Usually Means "First Pass"

For false positives, a "decision is final" reply after a vague first appeal is frequently an automated close, not a human verdict. Treat it as a signal to improve your evidence and submit once more — not as proof the account is gone forever. Our recovery-denied reapply guide covers the same mindset for hacked accounts.

6. 🌲 After Rejection — Your Decision Tree

This is the part most guides skip and the part that actually saves accounts. When the first appeal is denied, run this decision tree instead of rage-quitting or spamming.

① Did your first letter name the specific trades?

If your first appeal was vague ("I never traded anyone"), the denial is expected — the system knows you traded. Your move: submit a second appeal that names the exact trades, the counterparties, and confirms they are your own accounts or real friends. New, specific facts are the only thing that re-opens a human review.

② Is there a tainted-source explanation?

If the flag came from a bond or card bought on a grey-market site, name it: "I purchased this bond from a third-party key reseller, not realizing it was flagged." That converts "criminal RWT" into "naive purchase from a bad source," which is far more likely to be reviewed sympathetically. Pair this with our membership refund and chargeback ban guide if a payment dispute is involved.

③ If a second appeal also fails

Two denied, well-written appeals with real evidence almost always mean the underlying data shows a cash trail Jagex will not reverse. At that point, stop appealing the banned account and pivot to protecting what is left: secure your other accounts (next section), and if any linked account is clean, follow the denied-recovery reapply process for those. Do not let frustration push you into a "I'll just buy a new max main" purchase from an RWT site — that is how people lose a second account the same week.

7. 🔗 Preventing a Chain Ban on Your Other Accounts

RWT investigations do not stop at the banned account. Jagex's trade and payment graphs connect accounts that moved value together, so a banned mule can pull down the main it fed. Act before the chain spreads.

① Identify what is linked

List every account that traded GP/items with the banned one, every account on the same email or payment method, and every account that shared a home IP. Those are the at-risk set. Our chain ban guide walks the full graph so you do not miss a sleeping alt.

② Stop the behavior that triggered it

If mule-transferring caused the flag, stop consolidating GP across your characters for a while. If a gifted bond was the source, do not accept bonds from that person again. Breaking the pattern is what keeps the linked accounts from accruing their own flags while the review is pending.

③ Pre-secure the clean accounts

For accounts that are genuinely clean: enable the authenticator, use a unique password, and make sure the registered email is one you control. A clean account with good security is far less likely to be swept up, and if it ever is, our Ironman ban appeal guide and recovery guides cover the separate paths. Do not log the clean accounts in from the same suspect setup that got the first one flagged until you are sure the pattern is broken.

💡 One Banned Account Is Recoverable; a Chain Is Not

The difference between losing one alt and losing your whole portfolio is usually whether you kept moving value between them after the first ban. Freeze cross-account transfers the moment you see the RWT screen. That single action protects more accounts than any appeal letter.

8. ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Can I really win an RWT appeal if I never paid cash?

Yes. The majority of overturned RWT bans are false positives from mule transfers, bond gifts, or large personal trades. The key is naming the exact trades and confirming the accounts are yours or real friends — not denying all trading, which contradicts Jagex's data.

How many times can I appeal an RWT ban?

There is no hard published limit, but the effective rule is one appeal in the queue at a time, then one clean follow-up after a denial. Spamming multiple appeals gets them batched and closed as "already reviewed." Two well-written, evidence-backed appeals is the realistic maximum that helps.

Will admitting I used a grey-market bond hurt me?

Less than hiding it. Disclosing "I bought this bond from a third-party reseller, not a cash-for-GP deal" reframes the flag from criminal RWT to a naive purchase from a tainted source, which reviewers treat far more leniently. Lying about the source and getting caught in the payment ledger is worse.

My first appeal was denied with "decision is final" — is the account gone?

Usually not. For false positives that wording is frequently an automated first-pass close, not a deep human verdict. Improve your evidence (name the trades, disclose any tainted source) and submit one clean follow-up after waiting.

Should I use a paid "unban" service?

Never. They cannot access Jagex's internal review, they often ask for your login details (which confirms account sharing and kills the appeal), and many are outright scams. Only use the official support form from the ban screen.

Will my other accounts get banned too?

Only if they are linked by trades, shared email/payment, or shared IP to the banned account, and only if the pattern continues. Freeze cross-account transfers immediately and secure the clean accounts to break the chain. See our chain ban guide.

How long does an RWT appeal take in 2026?

Realistically 14–30 days for a first decision, sometimes longer during backlog. Automated review is days; human review is weeks. Do not interpret silence at day 10 as a denial — no response is not a rejection.

What if the ban was actually for macroing, not RWT?

Then this guide's evidence is the wrong kind. Macro bans need login/play-pattern evidence, not trade explanations. Use our dedicated false macro ban appeal guide, which covers the different template and evidence you need.

Can I get a refund on membership if the ban is false?

If the ban is overturned, membership access returns with the account. If it stands and you paid by card, a chargeback can itself trigger a separate "Serious" ban, so read our membership refund and chargeback ban guide before disputing anything.
