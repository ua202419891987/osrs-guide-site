OSRS Returning Player Guide September 2026 — What Changed, How to Catch Up & Your First 7 Days Back

Published September 5, 2026  |  18 min read  |  By Yanchun Wu

If you last logged out of Gielinor anywhere between 2021 and early 2025, you are returning to a game that has quietly been rebuilt around you. OSRS got its first-ever new skill, an entire third continent finished its three-part rollout, a wave of endgame bosses arrived, and the way you log in changed. This guide tells you exactly what shipped while you were gone, what is safe to ignore, and the fastest path to feeling at home again — without wasting a single bond.

1. The Headline Change: Sailing Is a Real Skill Now

The single biggest thing that happened while you were away is also the biggest in the game's history: on 19 November 2025, Old School RuneScape launched Sailing, the 24th skill and the first entirely new skill the game has ever received. If you left before late 2025, the ocean was just a blue backdrop you charter-shipped across. Today it is a full, playable skill with its own quest line, progression, and endgame.

Sailing is a utility skill. You captain your own vessel, build and upgrade it at the Shipyard, recruit a crew of up to 10 NPCs (or your friends), and explore uncharted waters full of new resources, monsters, and rewards. There is no skill requirement to begin — you start by speaking to Will and Anne at the Port Sarim docks to begin the introductory quest Pandemonium, which grants you a basic Raft and your Captain's Log.

⛵ What Sailing Actually Adds

  • A brand-new introductory quest (Pandemonium) with no requirements — the natural first stop for any returning player
  • Ship building and upgrades: three boat sizes (raft, small, large), stronger components, and facilities you build with Construction
  • Port tasks and Barracuda trails — after level 30 Sailing you can attempt three Barracuda trails for chunky XP
  • New threats at sea: krakens, sharks, orcas, frost dragons, gryphons, and brand-new enemy types
  • The Sailing Skill Cape — maxing the skill gives you the first new skill cape since launch, and a new pet to chase

One detail catches veterans off guard: because Sailing is a real skill, your max cape can no longer be worn until you have 99 Sailing. The moment the skill went live, anyone wearing a max or completion cape had to disassemble it (use a knife on the combined cape) until they trained the new skill. It is a small thing, but it is the clearest signal that the game you remember has moved on.

For a returning player the practical advice is simple: do the Pandemonium quest on day one. It is free, fast, and unlocks the framework for everything else the skill offers. You do not need to grind to 99 — even a few levels open port tasks and travel options that make the rest of the game smoother.

💡 The Golden Rule of Coming Back

Treat Sailing like Runecrafting was in 2013 — optional for most content, but a permanent part of the achievement landscape. Get the quest done, taste the early training, and decide later how deep you go. Do not skip it entirely or you will keep hitting "requires Sailing" walls you do not understand.

2. Varlamore Is Fully Built — The Three-Part Continent

When you left, Varlamore may have been a teaser, a single zone, or not exist at all. As of 23 July 2025 it is complete: a whole new kingdom delivered in three chapters, each loaded with quests, bosses, and training methods. If you have never touched it, think of it as a second Kourend — a self-contained progression hub that rewards both skillers and bossers.

① Varlamore: Part One — 20 March 2024

The foundation. You unlock the continent by completing the quest Children of the Sun, which opens Civitas illa Fortis, the capital city. From there the headline activities are the Hunter's Guild and its Hunter Rumours (a repeatable, profitable hunting loop), the Moons of Peril boss fight (a trio of bosses better known as Perilous Moons), and the Fortis Colosseum, a wave-based combat challenge.

② Varlamore: The Rising Darkness (Part Two) — 25 September 2024

This chapter added the island of Aldarin, the group boss Hueycoatl, the Colossal Wyrm agility course, and Mastering Mixology, a Herblore training activity that is now one of the fastest ways to train the skill. Hueycoatl in particular is a popular early-to-mid group boss because it is forgiving and drops useful supplies.

③ Varlamore: The Final Dawn (Part Three) — 23 July 2025

The finale. It brought a forested northern region, the Doom of Mokhaiotl — a new style of encounter called a Delve Boss that you push deeper into for scaling rewards — and the Vale Totems fletching minigame. A second delve boss, Osto-Ayak, also lives here. The Doom of Mokhaiotl is the kind of content that defines the current endgame, so it is worth understanding even if you are not ready to fight it yet.

🗺️ Varlamore at a Glance for Returnees

  • Start here: Children of the Sun quest — no huge requirements, opens the whole region
  • Best early win: Hunter Rumours at the Hunter's Guild (steady GP and Hunter XP)
  • Best boss to learn: Hueycoatl (group-friendly, low pressure)
  • Best Herblore method: Mastering Mixology
  • Top-tier reward to know: Dizana's Quiver, from the Sol Heredit (Perilous Moons) fight — a powerful ranged off-hand

If you are deciding where to spend your first month back, Varlamore is the safest bet: it has content for every level, it is all in one place, and it hands you some of the best mid-to-late game gear in the game (the quiver alone is a goal worth chasing).

3. Bosses You Missed: Yama, the Moons & the Forgotten Four

The boss roster exploded while you were gone. If your idea of endgame is Corp, Nex, or the God Wars Dungeon, you have a lot of catching up to do. Here are the names you need to recognise.

① Yama, Master of Pacts — 14 May 2025

Yama is a demonic, duo-focused boss in the Chasm of Fire beneath Great Kourend, reachable via fairy ring DJR. You must complete A Kingdom Divided to access him. The base fight is built around movement and prayer switching and is described by Jagex as a step up from the post-Desert Treasure II bosses. His rewards are significant: Oathplate Armour (a new high-tier melee set forged from Crimson Lovakite and Oathplate shards), Demonic Tallow for the new Surge Potions, Aether Catalysts, and the Rite of Vile Transference upgrade to Death Charge. Like the Royal Titans, Yama is easier and faster with a partner, and his stats do not scale with player count.

② The Moons of Peril (Perilous Moons)

A three-boss fight (Blood, Eclipse, and Blue Moon) unlocked through the Perilous Moons quest in Varlamore. It drops the materials for Dizana's Quiver and is a staple mid-game bossing step. If you remember the Fight Caves or the Royal Titans, this sits in a similar "learn the rotations" bracket but rewards better gear.

③ The Forgotten Four (Desert Treasure II)

The Desert Treasure II — The Fallen Empire quest added four bosses: The Leviathan, Vardorvis, Duke Sucellus, and The Whisperer. Each drops a powerful weapon (the Vestige rewards feed into top-tier gear). These are the gatekeepers to a lot of modern best-in-slot equipment, so even if you are not bossing yet, know that "DT2" is shorthand every PvM guide now assumes you understand.

④ The Royal Titans

A beginner-to-mid duo boss pair that became a go-to early PvM introduction. If you want a gentle on-ramp back into bossing, this is where many returning players start now.

💡 Don't try to learn all of them at once

Pick ONE boss as your re-entry fight. The Royal Titans or Hueycoatl are the kindest. Save Yama and the Forgotten Four until your gear and mechanics are sharp — they punish rusty prayer switching hard.

4. Your Login Changed: The Jagex Account Mandate

This is the part that surprises returning players most. The old "username and password" login you remember is on its way out. Jagex now runs a unified Jagex Account system, and it is the default way to play.

Here is what changed, in plain terms:

  • One login, up to 20 characters. A Jagex Account lets you group all your Old School (and RuneScape) characters under a single email and password.
  • Two-factor authentication is mandatory. You verify new-device logins by email code and/or an authenticator app, with backup codes you must store safely.
  • The Jagex Launcher is required on PC. To play on a computer you now launch through the Jagex Launcher, not the old client.
  • New accounts are Jagex Accounts by default. Since 9 January 2024 every new account is created this way, and since 7 November 2023 new players needed a Jagex Account to use the Launcher.
  • The RuneScape Authenticator is retired. If your account still used the old authenticator, it is replaced the moment you move to a Jagex Account.

🔐 What to Do Before You Try to Log In

  • If your old login still works: great — but expect to be prompted to upgrade to a Jagex Account. Do it from a trusted device and save your backup codes immediately.
  • Importing is permanent. Linking an old character to a Jagex Account voids its previous login details and cannot be undone. Make sure the email you use is one you control and can recover.
  • Use the same email you can access. Account recovery is far easier when your Jagex Account email is current and your backup codes are stored offline.
  • Set a Bank PIN as a last line of defence the moment you are back in, especially if the account sat idle for years.

The most common returning-player failure is trying the old client or old credentials, getting stuck, and assuming the account is gone. Nine times out of ten the character is fine — it just lives behind a Jagex Account now. Recover through the official account support page, never through a "helpful" Discord DM or a paid recovery service.

5. Economy & Meta Shifts You Will Feel at the GE

You do not need to be an economist to notice the game's money changed. The clearest, verified shift is the Grand Exchange convenience fee: on 29 May 2025 the tax rate on new Grand Exchange offers rose from 1.0% to 2.0%. That doubles the cut Jagex takes on trades, which matters if you flip items or move high-value gear. At the same time, several items were added to or removed from the item sink list, and more low-value and early-game items were exempted from the tax entirely.

On the gear side, the "best in slot" landscape you knew has moved. New standout items include Oathplate Armour (Yama), Dizana's Quiver (Perilous Moons), and the various Vestige weapons from the Forgotten Four. If your bank still thinks the Toxic Blowpipe and Armadyl Crossbow are the ranged ceiling, it is time to re-read a current best-in-slot overview — the meta has genuinely shifted underneath you.

✅ Healthy habits for the 2026 economy

  • Check the live GE graph before buying or selling anything worth more than a few million — prices for new-content items move fast after updates
  • Factor the 2% tax into flips — what used to be a break-even margin is now a loss
  • Ignore "guaranteed profit" DMs — real-world trading is policed harder than ever and buying gold can get the account banned
  • Re-read a BIS guide before investing in expensive gear you last priced years ago

None of this should intimidate you. The Grand Exchange still works exactly as you remember; only the maths on the edges changed. The bigger adjustment is knowing which items are now actually worth pursuing.

6. Your First 7 Days Back — A Step-by-Step Catch-Up Plan

A returning player who tries to "do everything" burns out in a weekend. A returning player with a plan is having fun by Friday. Here is the sequence we recommend.

Day 1 — Secure and Orient

Log in (through the Jagex Launcher), confirm your 2FA and backup codes, set a Bank PIN, and skim your bank and stats. Note what you were working toward — an incomplete quest, a half-trained skill, a boss you were learning. That unfinished business is your anchor.

Day 2 — Do the Sailing Intro

Run Pandemonium at Port Sarim. It is free, fast, and opens the new skill framework. You will immediately understand what Sailing is and whether you want to pursue it.

Day 3 — Open Varlamore

Complete Children of the Sun and walk around Civitas illa Fortis. Do one Hunter Rumour. You will see why this continent is the modern starting point for returning players.

Day 4 — Update Your Knowledge

Read a current best-in-slot overview and skim the boss roster (Yama, the Moons, the Forgotten Four). You are not learning to fight them today — you are mapping the territory so future guides make sense.

Day 5 — Pick One Goal

Choose a single catch-up target: finish Varlamore content, push Sailing, or learn one boss. One goal, not five. Momentum beats ambition here.

Day 6 — Easy Wins

Do something that pays off immediately — a birdhouse run, a couple of Hunter Rumours, or a few Sailing port tasks. Tangible progress rebuilds the habit faster than reading wikis.

Day 7 — Find Your People

Join a clan chat or Discord built for learners. OSRS in 2026 is far more social and guided than the game you left; a good community turns confusion into momentum.

💡 The 1-5 Year Gap Rule

If you have been gone 1-2 years, you missed Sailing and Yama but your gear knowledge is mostly current — focus on the new skill and the new bosses. If you have been gone 3-5 years, you also missed Varlamore, the Forgotten Four, and the whole modern BIS shift — start with Children of the Sun and a BIS refresh before touching any boss.

7. Common Pitfalls Returning Players Fall Into

These are the mistakes that waste the most time and GP for people coming back:

  • Assuming the max cape means you are "done." Since Sailing launched, the max cape requires 99 Sailing too. Yours is disassembled until you train it.
  • Ignoring Sailing entirely. It is now woven into travel, ports, and achievements. Skipping it just creates mystery walls later.
  • Getting stuck on the old login. The Jagex Account is the path. Recover through official support, not third parties.
  • Trusting real-world trading offers. RWT is banned aggressively; buying gold or items from sellers can lose you the account you just recovered.
  • Rushing endgame bosses cold. Yama and the Forgotten Four punish rusty prayer switching. Learn on the Royal Titans or Hueycoatl first.
  • Forgetting the 2% GE tax. Old flipping margins no longer hold; re-check prices before committing capital.
  • Chasing every update at once. Pick one catch-up goal. The game is not going anywhere — you have time.

8. Returning Player FAQs

Q: Do I really need a Jagex Account to play in 2026?

In practice, yes. New accounts are Jagex Accounts by default, the Jagex Launcher is required on PC, and legacy logins are being phased toward the new system. If your old credentials still work you can keep using them for now, but you will be prompted to upgrade — and importing your character is permanent, so use an email you fully control.

Q: What is Sailing and do I have to train it?

Sailing is the first new skill in OSRS history, launched 19 November 2025. It is a utility skill about captaining and upgrading a ship. You do not have to grind it, but you should at least complete the Pandemonium intro quest — and note that your max cape stays disassembled until you reach 99 Sailing.

Q: I left before Varlamore existed. Where do I even start?

Complete the quest Children of the Sun to unlock the continent, then explore Civitas illa Fortis and the Hunter's Guild. Varlamore is the most returnee-friendly content in the game because it serves every level and hands you strong mid-to-late game rewards like Dizana's Quiver.

Q: What are the "must-know" new bosses?

Recognise at least these: Yama (Chasm of Fire, needs A Kingdom Divided), the Moons of Peril (Perilous Moons), the Forgotten Four from Desert Treasure II (Leviathan, Vardorvis, Duke Sucellus, Whisperer), and the Royal Titans as a gentle entry point. Hueycoatl in Varlamore is the kindest real boss to learn first.

Q: Did the Grand Exchange economy change?

Yes — the GE convenience tax rose from 1.0% to 2.0% on 29 May 2025, and the item sink and tax-exempt lists were adjusted. The trading interface itself is unchanged, but old flipping margins no longer hold, so check live prices before moving value.

Q: My best-in-slot gear feels weak now. What changed?

The modern BIS landscape moved with new content: Oathplate Armour (Yama), Dizana's Quiver (Perilous Moons), and the Vestige weapons from the Forgotten Four are all now reference points. Read a current best-in-slot overview before investing in expensive gear you last priced years ago.

Q: Is Leagues still a thing I should plan around?

Leagues remain a recurring temporary game mode. The most recent, Leagues V: Raging Echoes, ran from late November 2024 to January 2025. A new Leagues is expected in late 2026, so if you enjoy the high-speed mode, watch for the announcement — but it is separate from the main game and not required for catching up.

Q: What should I absolutely NOT do when I return?

Do not buy gold or items from real-world sellers (ban risk), do not trust DMs offering "account recovery help" (scam risk), and do not rush endgame bosses cold. Also avoid spreading your effort across five goals — one catch-up target at a time is the fastest route back.

Final Tips Before You Set Sail

  • 🔑 Recover and secure first. Jagex Account, 2FA, backup codes, Bank PIN — do this before anything fun, or a hijacker does it for you.
  • ⛵ Pandemonium on day two. The Sailing intro quest is free and unlocks the framework for a whole new skill.
  • 🗺️ Open Varlamore. Children of the Sun is the single best returnee on-ramp in the modern game.
  • 📚 Refresh your BIS knowledge. The gear ceiling moved; read a current overview before spending.
  • 🎯 One goal at a time. Pick Sailing, Varlamore, or one boss — not all three at once.
  • 👥 Join a learner clan. OSRS in 2026 is more guided and social than the game you left.
