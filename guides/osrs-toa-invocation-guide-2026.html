🏛️ ToA Invocations: Which to Enable for Best Loot vs. Difficulty (2026)

Updated August 19, 2026  |  15 min read  |  By Yanchun Wu

Tombs of Amascut lives or dies on your invocation selection. The right mix turns a 100 raid-level stroll into a 300 raid-level loot printer; the wrong mix turns a run into a wipe factory that your team quits after two attempts. This guide does NOT re-teach the raid (our ToA solo guide does that). It is purely about choosing invocations: what each one does, how hard it is, and which combinations maximize loot per hour at every raid level.

📅 Updated: August 2026 — Raid Strategy · 24 min read
🛡️ Level: 110+
🆕 NEW

Invocations are the difficulty dial of Tombs of Amascut. Every toggle adds points, and those points raise your raid level, which raises both enemy stats and your unique drop chance. The art is finding the line where added difficulty still pays for itself in loot per hour. If you have only fought Vorkath or Moons of Peril, ToA's invocation system is a different kind of optimization — it rewards planning, not just gear.

The mistake nearly every new ToA team makes is either invoking too low (safe but terrible loot) or too high (good loot but constant wipes that erase the gains). This guide gives you a defensible raid-level target for your team's skill, a difficulty score for every meaningful invocation, and three ready-to-copy loadouts. By the end you will know exactly which boxes to tick.

💡 Quick Verdict

For most teams, the sweet spot is raid level 250–350. That range captures the high-value invocations (Volatile, Totem, the boss "Harder Version" lines, and the free points like Pathfinder and Slower than Molasses) without stacking so many crippling debuffs that your kill time balloons. Over-invoking past 400 usually lowers GP/hr because wipes and slow clears cost more than the marginal loot bump.

1. ⚙️ How Invocations & Raid Level Work

Before touching a single toggle, you need the underlying math. Everything else in this guide is just applying it.

① Points → Raid Level

Each invocation has a point value (anywhere from +5 to +10). Your total invocation points are added to a base raid level of 100. So a run with 150 points of invocations is raid level 250. The game lets you stack up to 400 invocation points, which is the practical ceiling most sweaty teams use; beyond that you simply cannot toggle more.

The reason this matters: raid level is the single variable that controls both enemy strength and loot potential. Higher raid level = tougher rooms, but also better unique drop rates and more total reward potential. You are literally trading survivability for loot with every point.

② Loot scales with raid level

Your "reward potential" equals your raid level. At the end of a successful raid, the game rolls uniques based on that potential. The commonly cited unique drop rates (community-verified, illustrative) look like this:

Raid Level Invocation Points Unique Drop Chance (approx) Notes
1000~1/22Entry mode, terrible loot
15050~1/16Light invocation
200100~1/12Solid casual target
250150~1/9.5Meta sweet spot begins
300200~1/7.5High-invoke norm
350250~1/6.2Sweaty teams
400300+~1/5.3Max efficiency ceiling

These are approximations of the community-understood curve, not exact Jagex-published fractions, but they are close enough to plan around. The key shape: each step up in raid level improves your unique odds, but the marginal gain shrinks while the marginal difficulty often jumps — especially once you enable the crippling debuffs.

③ The core tradeoff

Loot per hour = (unique chance × unique value + alch floor) ÷ (average clear time in hours). Invocations raise the numerator (better loot) but can raise the denominator (slower, wipe-prone clears). Your job is to find the raid level where the numerator grows faster than the denominator. For most teams that is 250–350. For newer teams it is 150–250. For solo learners, often 100–200.

This is exactly why a separate invocation guide exists instead of burying the info in a raid walkthrough — the optimization is its own skill. Pair your loadout thinking with the weapon math in our Soulreaper Axe rework guide if you plan to melee the Wardens.

④ How raid level scales enemy stats (not just loot)

Beyond loot, raid level directly scales enemy difficulty. At higher raid levels, room enemies gain HP, hit harder, and attack faster — roughly a few percent per 25 raid levels. This is why the loot curve and the difficulty curve rise together: every point that improves your unique odds also makes the rooms tougher. Concretely, a 100 raid-level Ba-Ba might have ~300 HP and hit 10–15, while a 300 raid-level Ba-Ba has noticeably more HP and hits harder, demanding better gear or slower, safer play. The scaling is not linear and not published exactly, but the direction is certain: points buy both loot and pain. Players who only look at the loot column and ignore the stat column are the ones who over-invoke. Budget your raid level against your team's effective DPS and survivability, not against the unique table alone.

A useful mental model: each +25 raid levels is a small "gear check." If your team clears 200 comfortably but struggles at 250, the gap is telling you a specific upgrade (more DPS, better prayer bonus, or cleaner mechanics) is required before climbing further. Treat raid level as a progress meter for your team, not just a loot dial. When the rooms start feeling unwinnable, that is data — drop the level, get the upgrade, return.

2. 📜 Every Invocation Explained (by Boss)

Below is every meaningful invocation, grouped by where it applies, with exactly what it does. Point values are in parentheses. "Free" means it adds points with no real downside.

① Path invocations (the free points)

Pathfinder (+10, FREE): Lets you skip the path puzzle entirely. Zero downside. Always enable it. This is the single most important toggle for loot-per-effort because it costs you nothing and saves time.

Path of the Glowing / Walk the Path / Not Just a Head / Both Home and Away / The Fremennik Exiles: these are path variants that change the route or add a path challenge. Most add points but also add time or risk. For loot efficiency, Pathfinder alone is usually enough; the others are for points-maxing teams that can clear fast.

② Ba-Ba (the Goat) invocations

Harder Version (+5): Ba-Ba gets more HP and hits harder. Cheap difficulty, almost always worth it.
Keep Back (+5): Ba-Ba's knockback is stronger, pushing you off tiles. Annoying but manageable; enable for points.
Up to the Gods (+10): the falling god-icons hit much harder and there are more of them. This is the real Ba-Ba difficulty spike — only enable once your team reliably dodges the icons.

③ Kephri (Scarab) invocations

Harder Version (+5): more HP. Enable always.
Scaring the Little Ones (+10): the small scarabs become aggressive and hit harder. Moderate difficulty.
Swarming (+10): many more scarabs spawn. Can overwhelm a team that does not AoE; high difficulty for newer groups.
More Flies (+5): extra fly adds during the transition. Minor; enable for points.

④ Akkha (Guardian) invocations

Harder Version (+5): more HP. Enable always.
Shadows (+10): Akkha spawns shadow clones that mirror attacks. The signature Akkha difficulty — enable only when your team can track clones.
Shrine of the Devoted (+10): shrines periodically heal Akkha. Forces you to destroy shrines fast; adds a task without adding damage taken. Good points for skilled teams.

⑤ Zebak (Snake) invocations

Harder Version (+5): more HP. Enable always.
Crawling (+10): Zebak moves more aggressively and the acid spreads faster. Moderate difficulty.
Reflection (+10): Zebak reflects a portion of damage back at you during certain attacks. This is a genuine DPS check and a common wipe source — enable only with a comfortable team.

⑥ Wardens (Final) invocations

Need Some Balance (+10): the Wardens enrage (damage ramp) faster. Real pressure on long phases.
On the Shoulders of Giants (+10): the Wardens have more HP. Pure DPS tax, no new mechanics — safe points if your team has damage.
Slower than Molasses (+10, FREE / COSMETIC): only slows the Wardens' death animation. Zero combat effect. Always enable — it is free points.

⑦ Bloat (optional room) invocations

Bloaty Boy (+5): Bloat has more HP.
Twisted (+10): Bloat's special attack rotates unpredictably. Adds a read.
The Bloat (+5): Bloat deals more damage. Only enable Bloat's room if you want the extra points and can handle him; many speed teams skip Bloat entirely to save time.

⑧ Elidinis' Ward (statue) invocations

Elidinis' Ward (+5) / Elidinis' Ward Enhanced (+10): upgrades the ward reward. These are "free-ish" points because the ward fight is optional and the upgrade just improves the drop. Enable if you do the ward room.

⑨ Global "crippling" invocations (high risk)

Volatile (+10): all special attacks deal more damage. Across every room this adds up — high difficulty but high value. Enable for sweaty runs.
Totem (+10): more totems spawn in rooms, adding objectives. Moderate difficulty, good points.
Parasite (+10): you cannot heal above the HP you had when afflicted until cured. Punishes mistakes hard — only for confident teams.
Menaphite Remedies (+10): only Menaphite potions restore HP (brews weakened). Forces a specific inventory. Skilled teams use it for points.
Ancient Remedies (+10): brews restore less HP. Similar tradeoff to Menaphite.
Overlord / Poe / Book of the Dead / Silent / No More Running / Jarta / Deadly Game / Stepping In / Underway / Hmm… / Foote / Humble: a large family of +10 global modifiers (more enemies, more shadows, restricted actions, etc.). Most add points with meaningful friction; enable them only when pushing toward 400 points with a practiced team.

⑩ Stacking interactions (which combos to avoid)

Individual invocations are one thing; their combinations are where teams get punished. Some pairs multiply difficulty far beyond their point sum because they hit the same weakness at once. Avoid these early:

Combo Why it spikes Recommendation
Swarming + ReflectionKephri adds overwhelm while Zebak reflects your damage — two rooms become unwinnable together for mid teamsStagger: pick one, not both, until expert
Parasite + Menaphite RemediesBoth attack your healing — you cannot recover from any mistakeNever run together below sweaty tier
Up to the Gods + VolatileBa-Ba icons hit hard AND all specials hit harder — the icon dodge becomes a one-shotEnable Volatile only after Ba-Ba icons are trivial
Shadows + Shrine of the DevotedAkkha clones plus healing shrines = long, confusing roomSkilled teams only; needs clear callouts
Need Some Balance + On the Shoulders of GiantsWardens enrage fast AND have more HP — pure time taxFine if your DPS is strong; skip if clears are slow

The pattern: avoid stacking two invocations that punish the same resource (healing, dodging, DPS) in the same or adjacent rooms. Spread your difficulty across different failure modes so no single mistake chain ends the run. This is the difference between a "hard but fair" 350 build and a "why are we wiping" 350 build.

⑪ Reading the invocation menu efficiently

The in-game invocation menu is long and easy to mis-click. Develop a routine: open the menu, enable the free points first (Pathfinder, Slower than Molasses), then the four Harder Versions, then your target raid level's remaining points in order of lowest difficulty-per-point from the table in Section 3. Do not scroll randomly — a mis-toggle of Parasite when you meant Totem is a run-ender. Many teams save a screenshot of their exact toggle list and replicate it every raid. That consistency is itself a loot multiplier because it removes the "did I enable the right ones" anxiety that causes mid-raid hesitation.

4. 🎯 Recommended Combos by Raid Level

Here are three copy-paste loadouts. Pick by your team's confidence, not your ambition.

① Low raid level (100–200): the learner build

Toggle: Pathfinder, Slower than Molasses, all four "Harder Version" (+20), Keep Back, More Flies, Bloaty Boy, The Bloat, Elidinis' Ward. Total ≈ 90–110 points → raid level ~190–210. This gives you real loot without any mechanical spikes. Perfect while learning the raid with a group that has mostly fought Moons or low-gear Vorkath.

② Mid raid level (250–300): the meta build

Add to the above: On the Shoulders of Giants, Need Some Balance, Shrine of the Devoted, Totem, Up to the Gods, Shadows, Crawling. Total ≈ 200–230 points → raid level ~300–330. This is the commonly cited sweet spot — strong uniques, manageable wipes. Most pub teams should stop here.

③ High raid level (350–400): the sweaty build

Add: Volatile, Swarming, Reflection, Menaphite Remedies (or Ancient Remedies), Parasite, plus the remaining global modifiers to push toward 400. Only for teams that clear sub-20-minute consistently and rarely wipe. The loot is best, but one bad night of wipes erases a week of gains.

④ Solo invocation note

Solo ToA is a different animal — you cannot cover every mechanic at once, so many players run LOWER invocations solo than in a team (raid level 150–250) to keep clears fast and safe. Do not copy a 4-man 350 build into solo; you will hate the raid. Our ToA solo guide covers the solo-specific toggles in detail.

⑤ Exact toggle lists (copy-paste builds)

Rather than describe the builds abstractly, here are the precise invocations to tick for each target raid level. Copy these into the menu:

Learner build (~raid 190–210): Pathfinder, Slower than Molasses, Ba-Ba Harder Version, Kephri Harder Version, Akkha Harder Version, Zebak Harder Version, Keep Back, More Flies, Bloaty Boy, The Bloat, Elidinis' Ward. ~90–110 points.

Meta build (~raid 300–330): all of the above, plus On the Shoulders of Giants, Need Some Balance, Shrine of the Devoted, Totem, Up to the Gods, Shadows, Crawling. ~200–230 points.

Sweaty build (~raid 350–400): all of the above, plus Volatile, Swarming, Reflection, Menaphite Remedies (or Ancient Remedies), Parasite, and the remaining global modifiers (Overlord, Poe, Silent, No More Running, Jarta, Deadly Game, Stepping In, Underway, Hmm…, Foote, Humble) to push toward 400. Only for proven teams.

Notice the learner and meta builds are pure "safe points" — free points, Harder Versions, and mild modifiers. The sweaty build is where the crippling globals enter, and that is exactly where wipes climb. If you are not clearing the meta build in under 35 minutes with no wipes, do not add the sweaty globals; you are buying loot you will lose to resets.

⑥ Adjusting for your team's weak room

Most teams have one room they dread. If your weak room is Ba-Ba, do NOT enable Up to the Gods until the icons are trivial — drop that points source and compensate with a safe +10 elsewhere (e.g., another global you handle well). If your weak room is Akkha, delay Shadows and Shrine of the Devoted. The art of a good build is not maxing points; it is maxing points while avoiding your team's specific failure room. Audit your last five wipes, find the room, and remove the invocation that feeds it before adding more elsewhere.

5. 💰 Loot vs. Difficulty: The GP/hr Math

Let us put numbers on the tradeoff. Assume a unique (Osmumten's Fang, Tumeken's Shadow, Elidinis' Ward, Masori pieces, etc.) averages ~40M GP value across the unique table, and the per-raid alch floor is ~150K.

① Worked example

At raid level 200 (~1/12 unique): expected unique value per raid = 40M ÷ 12 ≈ 3.33M, plus 150K alch = ~3.48M per successful raid. If your team clears in 25 minutes (2.4 raids/hr), GP/hr ≈ 8.4M. At raid level 300 (~1/7.5): expected unique ≈ 5.33M + alch = ~5.48M per raid; if clears slow to 35 minutes (1.7 raids/hr) due to difficulty, GP/hr ≈ 9.3M. The higher raid level wins — but only because clears stayed under ~38 minutes.

② Where it breaks

Push to raid level 400 (~1/5.3 ≈ 7.5M expected unique) but if difficulty drops you to 50-minute clears (1.2 raids/hr) and a 20% wipe rate, your effective GP/hr collapses to ~7.2M — worse than the 300 build. This is the trap: the loot table looks better, but the denominator (time + wipes) eats it. The chart below shows the shape.

⑤ Wipe-cost math (the hidden tax)

A wipe costs more than the lost attempt. It costs the consumables you burned, the time of four players, and the morale that leads to "one more and we stop" quitting. Quantify it: a 30-minute attempt that wipes at the Wardens wasted ~30 minutes of four players' time = 2 player-hours, plus ~500K in brews. At a conservative 5M GP/hr value of that time, a single late wipe costs ~10M in opportunity plus supplies. That is why a 300 build that wipes 20% of the time can underperform a 250 build that never wipes. Track wipes explicitly; if your wipe rate at a raid level exceeds ~10%, drop the raid level until it doesn't.

⑥ Re-evaluating your raid level over time

Your optimal raid level is not fixed. As your team's gear and coordination improve, the same invocation set that once caused wipes becomes comfortable, and you should climb. A good cadence: every two weeks, attempt a +25 raid level test run. If clears stay under 38 minutes with <10% wipes for three consecutive raids, adopt it. If not, revert. This incremental climb captures loot gains without the ego-driven over-invoke that sinks most teams. The raid level is a dial, not a trophy.

Invoke to the raid level your team can clear in under ~38 minutes with a low wipe rate. For most, that is 250–350. Re-evaluate weekly as your team improves — the right number climbs as your skill climbs. Do not set it once and forget it.

6. 🚫 What to Skip & Common Mistakes

A few invocations are traps for the unaware, and a few habits quietly cost teams loot.

① Invocations to skip early

Parasite: brutal, and most teams do not need the points. Menaphite/Ancient Remedies: force an awkward inventory; skip until expert. Swarming + Reflection together: the combo can overwhelm even good teams — stagger them. Deadly Game / Stepping In / Underway: niche global modifiers that add friction without matching the value of the core set.

② Mistake: invoking to a number, not a kit

Players see "400 points = max loot" and toggle everything, ignoring that their gear cannot survive it. Invoke to your team's ceiling, verified by a few clean runs, not to the theoretical max.

③ Mistake: forgetting Pathfinder

It is free +10 and saves time. Every run should have it on. The same goes for Slower than Molasses — free points, enable always.

④ Mistake: over-invoking solo

Solo players who copy team builds drown. Solo raid level 150–250 is the sane range. Push higher only after consistent sub-30-minute solo clears.

⑤ Mistake: ignoring clear time

A pretty invocation list means nothing if your clears are slow. Track your raids/hour, not just your raid level. GP/hr is the only scoreboard that matters.

⑥ The one habit that beats every mistake

If you adopt a single habit, make it this: after every raid, write down the raid level, the clear time, and whether you wiped. Over ten raids a pattern emerges — a specific raid level where clears slipped, or a specific invocation that caused the wipe. That log is worth more than any generic advice, because it is your team's data. The players who climb raid levels safely are not the ones who read the most guides; they are the ones who measured their own runs and adjusted. Treat the invocation menu as experimental, not permanent, and let your log tell you where the loot-per-hour peak actually sits for your group.

7. ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

What raid level should a new ToA team aim for?

Start at 100–200 (learner build: free points + the four "Harder Version" lines). Learn the rooms, then climb to 250–300 once clears are comfortable. Do not start at 300+.

Which invocations are completely free?

Pathfinder (+10) and Slower than Molasses (+10) add points with zero combat downside. Always enable both. Elidinis' Ward upgrades are also near-free if you do the ward room.

Is higher raid level always better loot?

Better per-raid loot, yes — but not necessarily better GP/hr. Once difficulty slows your clears or causes wipes, effective hourly profit can drop below a lower raid level. The peak is usually 300–350 for teams.

Should I enable Parasite?

Only as an expert, max-point speed team. It prevents healing above your afflicted HP, which punishes any mistake hard. Most teams skip it entirely and lose almost no loot.

What is the best single invocation for value?

Pathfinder — free +10 and it skips the path puzzle, saving time on every raid. Slower than Molasses is tied for value (also free +10). After those, the four "Harder Version" lines give cheap, safe points.

How many invocations can I stack?

Up to 400 invocation points total. That is the practical ceiling; beyond it you cannot toggle more even if individual invocations remain. Most teams run 150–300.

Do invocations change the unique drop table?

No — the table is fixed (Fang, Shadow, Ward, Masori, etc.). Invocations only change how often you roll a unique and the overall reward potential, via raid level.

Can I use the same invocations solo and in a team?

You can, but you should not. Solo you cover every mechanic alone, so run lower (150–250). In a 4-man team you can afford 300–350 because roles split the load. Adjust per mode.

Which boss invocations are the biggest difficulty spikes?

Up to the Gods (Ba-Ba), Shadows (Akkha), Swarming (Kephri), and Reflection (Zebak) are the four that add real mechanical pressure. Enable them only after your team handles the base fights cleanly.

Where do I learn the actual raid rooms?

This guide is invocation-only by design. For room mechanics, pathing, and per-boss strategy, use our ToA solo beginner guide. The two guides are meant to be read together.
