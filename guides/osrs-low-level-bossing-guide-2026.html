1. What Counts as "Low-Level" Bossing

For this guide, "low-level" means accounts roughly combat 40–90 that have not yet assembled end-game gear. The bosses below are chosen because they have low or no skill gates, forgiving mechanics, and safe deaths (you keep your items if you misplay) — exactly what a new bosser needs to build confidence and bankroll.

① The Requirements That Actually Matter

🎯 What gates a boss

  • Quest locks: Some bosses need a short quest (e.g., Children of the Sun for Moons/Huey, partial Dragon Slayer for KBD).
  • Key drops: Bryophyta and Obor require a dropped key from their lesser variants.
  • Combat stats: Most "low" bosses are fine at 50–70 combat with decent gear.
  • Prayer: 43 Prayer is the universal floor for survivability.
💡 Why bossing early pays off

Bossing is where OSRS money and gear really accelerate. A fresh account scraping GP from thieving or low-level slayer might earn a few hundred K per hour; even the gentlest bosses here clear a million-plus once you are comfortable. That income funds the gear upgrades that make the next, harder boss reachable — a self-reinforcing progression loop that turns a new account into an end-game one.

2. The 5 Easiest Bosses to Start

② Scurrius — the no-requirement teacher

🐀 Edgeville Dungeon (safe, no requirements)

Scurrius is the ideal first boss. There are no requirements, the fight is in a safe instance (keep your items on death), and it teaches core bossing habits — positioning, prayer switching, and special attacks. Drops a cosmetic giant rat bone and useful supplies. Perfect for your very first kill.

③ Bryophyta — the moss giant boss

🌿 Moss Giant Lair (needs a Mossy key)

Kill moss giants for a Mossy key, then enter the Moss Giant Lair. Low requirements (roughly 20+ combat, though 50+ is more comfortable) and a safe fight. A great early herb and seed source.

④ Obor — the hill giant boss

⛰️ Hill Giant Lair (needs a Giant key)

Kill hill giants for a Giant key to access Obor. Similar entry bar to Bryophyta, safe instance, and a solid early drop table including the giant's bones and occasionally a clue scroll.

⑤ Amoxliatl — no-req Varlamore boss

🐍 Varlamore (no skill requirements)

Amoxliatl is an instanced boss in Varlamore with no skill requirements, designed to teach mechanics. Safe, repeatable, and a good stepping stone before the region's bigger bosses.

⑥ King Black Dragon — first "real" boss (Wilderness caution)

⚠️ Wilderness risk

The KBD lair entrance sits in the Wilderness, so the trip there carries PK risk. Bring an anti-dragon shield (or dragonfire ward) and only what you can lose. Recommended 70+ combat. If you want a safe alternative, the Giant Mole (below) is comparable without the Wilderness.

💡 The on-ramp design pattern

Notice the theme across this list: every boss here is either free of requirements (Scurrius, Amoxliatl) or gated only by a dropped key from a weaker variant (Bryophyta, Obor). Jagex built these as deliberate on-ramps so that even an account only a few days old can taste real bossing without first grinding for an expensive setup. Use them to learn the rhythm before chasing the mid-tier fights.

3. Beginner Gear Setups (Melee / Ranged / Magic)

You do not need best-in-slot to start. Here is a sensible budget-to-mid setup for each style.

⑧ Ranged

🏹 Ranged starter

A Magic shortbow (or Rune crossbow) with Adamant/Rune bolts, Black d'hide armour, an Amulet of power, and Ava's accumulator. Cheap, effective, and enough for every low-tier boss.

⑨ Magic

🔮 Magic starter

A Staff of the dead / Trident of the seas (or battlestaff to start), Mystic / Ahrim's armour, and an Amulet of magic. Magic is strong against several low-tier bosses and pairs well with thralls later.

💡 The one upgrade that matters most

Before any gear, get 43 Prayer and use the three Protect Prayers. A player in Rune armour with proper prayer will out-survive a player in Bandos who ignores prayer switching every time.

🎒 Non-gear essentials to carry

  • Emergency teleport: A teleport tablet or charged amulet — never boss without one.
  • Anti-dragon shield: Mandatory for any dragon (KBD, Vorkath later) to survive dragonfire.
  • Runes for magic: If using magic, pack enough runes for the whole trip plus thralls at 76 Magic.
  • Weight reducer: Graceful or boots of lightness speed up trips between bank and boss.

4. The Mid-Tier Step-Up

Once you are comfortable and sitting around 70+ combat with decent gear, these open up real money and uniques.

⑩ Giant Mole

🐁 Falador Mole Lair (safe)

  • Requires a spade and a teleport to the lair (or the Falador hard diary shortcut).
  • Recommended 60+ Attack/Strength/Defence; melee with a whip is standard.
  • Drops the Mole skin (valuable) and seeds; a classic early money maker.

⑪ Barrows

⚰️ Barrows brothers (needs 43 Prayer)

Solve the tunnel puzzle and defeat the six brothers. Recommended 70+ combat and 43 Prayer for Protect prayers. Drops Barrows armour (strong mid-tier sets) and the occasionally lucrative clue scrolls.

⑫ Moons of Peril

🌙 Varlamore progression boss (needs Children of the Sun + 70+ combat)

A four-boss fight that is the natural mid-game step before end-game PvM. Safe, repeatable, and a strong source of early upgrade gear.

⑬ Hueycoatl & Sarachnis

🐉 Group money & reliable mid boss

  • Hueycoatl: Join a mass world for free, safe, beginner-friendly big-money (see our Hueycoatl guide).
  • Sarachnis: Recommended 70+ combat, a consistent mid-tier earner with straightforward mechanics.

5. Combat Basics: Prayer, Food & Escape

Mechanics differ per boss, but three habits apply to all of them.

⑭ Prayer switching

Learn the right Protect prayer

Most bosses attack with one style at a time. Activate the matching Protect prayer (Melee / Missiles / Magic) to cut damage to near zero. Flicking it only when the boss attacks stretches your prayer points through long fights.

⑮ Food & overloads

What to bring

  • Food: Sharks or anglerfish; bring enough for the whole trip.
  • Potions: Super combat (or divine) and prayer potions / restores.
  • Emergency teleport: A teleport tablet or charged amulet — always.

⑯ Know your escape

💡 Don't die to bad luck

If a fight goes wrong, teleport out. Most low-tier bosses are instanced and safe, so leaving costs only time — not your bank. The players who "accidentally" die are usually the ones who forgot a teleport.

7. Low-Level Bossing FAQs

Q: What is the very first boss I should do?

Scurrius in the Edgeville Dungeon. It has no requirements, is a safe instance, and teaches the habits every other boss expects — prayer switching, positioning, and special-attack timing.

Q: What combat level do I need to start bossing?

You can start at very low levels with Scurrius. For Bryophyta/Obor, ~20+ combat is the gate; for the mid-tier bosses (Giant Mole, Barrows, Moons), 70+ combat with decent gear is the comfortable floor.

Q: Do I need 43 Prayer before bossing?

Strongly recommended. The three Protect Prayers (Melee/Missiles/Magic) cut boss damage to near zero and are the difference between a smooth farm and a death spiral. Get 43 Prayer before anything past Scurrius.

Q: Which boss makes the most money for a low-level account?

Once at 70+ combat, Giant Mole and Barrows are classic earners, while Hueycoatl mass worlds offer strong GP with almost no risk. Bryophyta and Obor are gentler but lower-value starting points.

Q: Is the King Black Dragon safe?

The KBD fight itself is fine, but its lair entrance is in the Wilderness, so the approach carries PK risk. Bring an anti-dragon shield, only risk what you can lose, or use the Giant Mole as a safer alternative.

Q: What gear should a beginner bosser aim for?

A budget melee setup (Dragon scimitar, Rune armour, Amulet of strength) or a Ranged setup (Magic shortbow/Rune crossbow, Black d'hide, Ava's) is plenty for low-tier bosses. Reinvest drops into Bandos / Barrows / better weapons.

Q: Are these bosses safe if I die?

Most in this guide are safe, instanced deaths — you keep your items and just respawn. The main exception is the KBD approach through the Wilderness. Always carry a teleport so a bad fight only costs time.

Q: How do I get to the Moons of Peril and Hueycoatl?

Both are in Varlamore. Complete Children of the Sun to access the region, then use the Quetzal network (unlocked via Twilight's Promise) to reach them. See our Varlamore guide for the full path.
