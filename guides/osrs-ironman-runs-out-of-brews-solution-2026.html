1. Why Ironmen Specifically Run Out of Brews

On a normal main account, a Saradomin brew is a number in a spreadsheet. You finish a trip, hop to the Grand Exchange, and buy another five hundred for a few hundred thousand coins. The cost is trivial, the refill is instant, and you almost never think about it. An Ironman lives in a completely different world, because the single rule of the mode is that you cannot trade with other players or use the Grand Exchange. That one restriction turns every potion from a purchase into a manufactured object, and it changes the entire economics of bossing.

When a main account runs out of brews, the problem is solved in thirty seconds at a bank. When an Ironman runs out of brews, the problem is a broken supply chain that can take days to repair. This is the core reason Ironmen specifically run out: the bottleneck is never combat skill, it is throughput. You are fighting a permanent gap between three numbers that only line up if you build systems around them. Those three numbers are your herb yield per day, your potion consumption per boss, and the time you are willing to spend rebuilding your stock.

① The Production Line You Inherited

To understand the drought, you have to understand what a brew actually costs an Ironman. A single three-dose Saradomin brew requires a clean Ranarr herb, a Snape Grass secondary, and a Vial of Water. The Ranarr has to be grown from a seed in a farm patch, tended with compost, waited on through its growth cycle, harvested, and cleaned. The Snape Grass has to be farmed or picked from its own patches. The Vial of Water comes from collecting water in a vial, which itself came from a glassblowing run or a drop. None of these steps involve coins. Every one of them involves time, attention, and a renewable resource that you personally control.

This is why the Ironman mindset has to shift from "buyer" to "logistics manager." A main account treats potions as a purchase. An Ironman must treat them as a manufacturing supply chain with farms, secondaries, and a bottling step. The moment you start thinking like a production manager instead of a shopper, the droughts become predictable and fixable. You are not "unlucky with supplies" — you have a temporary bottleneck in tier two of your production line, and bottlenecks have known, repeatable fixes.

🎯 The Math That Sinks Most Ironmen

  • A heavy Vorkath trip at low to mid gear eats 12 to 20 brews plus 8 to 12 prayer restores across a full inventory before banking
  • One disciplined Farm run across nine herb patches yields roughly 60 to 110 grimy herbs of mixed types, of which only a fraction are Ranarr
  • Realistically that is enough for 6 to 12 brews per full Farm run from Ranarr alone — far below a dedicated daily bossing schedule
  • The remaining gap is filled by boss drops, Slayer rewards, and your banked stock — which always runs out at exactly the worst moment

② Why It Hits at the Worst Possible Time

The drought almost never appears during your first boss. It appears the moment you transition from casual bossing into a sustained grind. That is the point where you stop treating each kill as an event and start treating bossing as a repeatable source of loot or progression. As soon as you are doing five, ten, or twenty kills per session, your consumption outpaces the casual herb trickle you were living on, and the bank buffer you built during questing evaporates. Most Ironmen hit this wall at exactly one moment: the first real attempt at a sustained grind such as Vorkath, Zulrah, the Corrupted Gauntlet, God Wars Dungeon, or a raid.

The good news is that the problem is fully solvable and, once solved, stays solved. Unlike a main account that can ignore the system forever, an Ironman who builds the right loop ends up permanently self-sufficient. The rest of this guide walks through every layer of that solution: how many to bring, how to make them, how to waste fewer, and how to refill between trips so the buffer never hits zero.

💡 The Buffer Mindset

Treat your brew bank like fuel in a tank. The goal is not "enough for today" — it is a standing buffer of 150 plus brews and 80 plus Super Restores at all times. When the buffer drops below 50, you pause bossing and refill. You never let it hit empty, because an empty buffer is what forces the panic substitutes that slow your whole account down.

Before we move on, internalize one more truth: running out of brews is not a skill failure. It is a planning failure, and planning is a skill you can learn. The chapters below give you the exact numbers and routines to learn it, and the Ironman Late-Game Supply Guide covers the same systems at the raid scale.

2. How Many Brews & Restores to Bring Per Trip

The single most common Ironman mistake is packing by guesswork. You throw a random stack of brews and prayer potions into the inventory, discover mid-trip that you brought too few or far too many, and either die or waste bank space. The fix is to pack by the boss, not by vibes. Every major boss has a known consumption profile based on your gear, your kill speed, and how often you get hit. The table below gives a realistic budget for an intermediate Ironman with solid but not best-in-slot gear.

④ How to Read the Table

The numbers above are intentionally a range, because the dominant variable is kill speed. A Vorkath kill that takes forty seconds consumes a fraction of the brews of a kill that takes ninety seconds, because you spend less time taking damage and because your DPS gets you out of the dangerous phases faster. This is why gear upgrades and tighter rotations are the highest-leverage supply savers in the game: every point of DPS and every point of defence converts directly into brews you did not drink. If you are consistently at the high end of these ranges, the answer is almost never "farm more herbs" — it is "get better at the fight or upgrade your gear."

🎯 The 70 Percent Rule

  • Pack 70 percent brews and 30 percent Super Restores for most magic and ranged bosses where prayer drain is moderate
  • Flip to 40 percent brews and 60 percent Super Restores at prayer-heavy bosses like GWD or ToA where prayer uptime wins fights
  • Always leave 2 to 4 inventory slots for food (Karambwan or Anglerfish) as an emergency top-up that does not touch your brews
  • Bank the moment your brew count drops below the number needed for one safe kill — do not gamble the trip

⑤ Brews Versus Food: Which Slot Does What

A common confusion is when to use a brew and when to use food. The cleanest rule is: use food (Karambwan, Anglerfish, Sharks) for the steady, predictable chip damage, and reserve brews for the burst moments — a big hit, a mistake, or a phase transition where you need a large instant heal plus a defence boost. Brews also lower your Attack, Strength, Ranged, and Magic stats, so you do not want to brew-spam during a DPS race. Food heals a flat amount and carries no stat penalty, which makes it the better default. The karambwan plus brew combo, covered in section four, is the exception that lets you get both heals in a single game tick during emergencies.

💡 Bank Before You Gamble

The most expensive death an Ironman can have is the "one more kill" death. If your remaining brews cannot safely cover one more full attempt, teleport out and refill. A single death at a dangerous boss can cost you the entire trip's loot plus the brews you brought, which is far worse than ending the session five minutes early.

If you want the combat fundamentals that let you stay at the low end of these ranges, the Combat Training Guide and the Boss Survival Guide both cover the mechanical habits that cut your consumption the most.

3. Making Your Own Brews From Scratch

Once you accept that every brew is a manufactured object, the next step is building the factory. This section is the complete path from zero Herblore to a self-sustaining brew pipeline, with the real costs expressed in time and farm runs rather than GP, because an Ironman pays in effort, not coins.

⑥ The Brew Recipe (Memorize This)

A Saradomin brew is made by combining a clean Ranarr herb with a Snape Grass secondary in a Vial of Water. The Herblore level requirement is 40. Each combination produces a three-dose potion, and each dose heals roughly 15 percent of your maximum Hitpoints plus a flat five, while also restoring a small amount of prayer and boosting your Defence substantially. That heal-over-percentage is why brews scale so well as your Hitpoints grow — a brew at 99 Hitpoints heals far more than a brew at 70 Hitpoints.

⑦ Getting to 40 Herblore as an Ironman

You cannot buy grimy herbs, so you have to generate every experience point yourself. The shortest practical route for an Ironman is a layered one. First, clean every herb you already have banked from Slayer and clue drops — cleaning is free XP and many players sit on thousands of grimy herbs without realizing it. Second, make low-tier potions such as Attack and Strength potions from Guam leaf and Eye of Newt, both of which are common monster drops, to push through the early levels cheaply. Third, unlock the Wilderness herb patch south of the Bandit Camp, which is disease-free and drops Ranarr frequently, giving you a free trickle of your brew herb while you build Farming. Fourth, use the Nightmare Zone, where you can spend points on herbs and secondaries — a lifesaver when patches are dry. Fifth, once you reach 43, the Tears of Guthix minigame grants a large chunk of Herblore XP weekly and should be a permanent part of your routine.

🎯 Always Make Three-Dose Brews

  • One herb + one secondary + one vial = a three-dose brew, which is three separate heals
  • Never waste a dose by making one-dose potions unless you specifically need the inventory space
  • A three-dose brew is the most efficient form factor for an Ironman who cannot top up at the Grand Exchange
  • If you need one-doses for a tight inventory, decant at a bank using the potion-combining interface rather than brewing fresh

⑧ Farming the Herb (Ranarr, Toadflax, Snapdragon)

Your Farm runs are the only renewable herb source that does not depend on random boss drops, and they are the true engine of your supply chain. Ranarr seeds, Toadflax seeds, and Snapdragon seeds all come from a mix of bird nests, timed drops, and Master Farmers. Once planted, each herb patch follows the same loop: rake the weed, plant the seed, apply ultracompost, and wait through the growth cycle. The key insight is that you are not farming for one harvest — you are farming for a permanent daily income of herbs that feeds your Herblore indefinitely.

Ranarr is your brew herb, Toadflax is your antivenom and Sanfew herb (critical at venom bosses like Zulrah), and Snapdragon is your Super Restore herb. You want all three growing on a rotation. The herb patches worth using are Catherby, Ardougne, Falador, Port Phasmatys, Trollheim, Harmony Island, the Kourend patches, Weiss, and the Farming Guild. A full loop across nine patches, done on the roughly eighty-minute herb growth timer, is the backbone of every self-sufficient Ironman's potion economy.

⑨ The Secondary Ingredients (Snape Grass, Limpwurt, Vials)

Brews need Snape Grass, Super Restores need Limpwurt Root, and everything needs a Vial of Water. None of these come from the Grand Exchange, so you build small side loops for each. Snape Grass grows at its own patch near the Crafting Guild east of Rimmington and can also be picked at the top of the Wizards' Tower; a single patch gives a healthy harvest that sustains your brew secondary without a full Farm run. Limpwurt Root comes from farming Limpwurt seeds in flower patches and from certain monster drops, and Vials come from collecting water in vials or from your own glassblowing. The point is that each secondary has a renewable source, and part of being a self-sufficient Ironman is knowing all of them so no single missing ingredient stalls your bottling.

🌿 Ultracompost Is Not Optional

Skipping ultracompost is the number one reason Ironmen stay herb-starved. Ultracompost raises the yield of a herb patch from roughly three herbs per seed to eight or more, and it prevents disease entirely. The compost comes from the Fertile Soil spell or a compost bin, both fully Ironman-accessible. One extra harvest per patch, every cycle, is the difference between a drought and a surplus over a month of farming.

The cost of this whole system is time, not GP. A dedicated Ironman spends a few minutes per hour on Farm runs and converts the harvest into potions while watching TV or between boss trips. Expressed in coins, the "cost" of a brew is zero, but expressed in attention, it is a daily habit. That habit, done consistently, is what separates Ironmen who perpetually run dry from Ironmen who never think about supplies again.

4. Stretching Supplies: Flick, Combo & Overheal

The fastest way to never run out of brews is to need fewer of them. Most intermediate Ironmen waste thirty to fifty percent of their supplies on three fixable habits: holding the wrong prayer, never flicking, and ignoring the karambwan combo. Fixing these habits before you plant another seed will save more brews than any Farm run ever could.

⑩ Prayer Flicking: The Highest-ROI Habit

Holding a Protect prayer drains your prayer points continuously, which means you drink restores continuously, which means you burn through your Super Restores and eventually your brews. Prayer flicking — turning the prayer on for only the game tick a hit lands and off again immediately — extends your prayer supply by three to five times. At any boss where you are hit on a predictable timer, flicking is effectively free brews. It takes practice, but it is the single highest return-on-investment habit for a herb-constrained Ironman, and the Prayer Training Guide explains the underlying prayer mechanics in detail.

⑪ Super Restore Over Prayer Potion

Plain Prayer Potions are made from Ranarr — the exact same herb your brews need. Super Restores are made from Snapdragon and Limpwurt, which frees your Ranarr entirely for brews. Even better, a Super Restore restores all of your combat stats (Attack, Strength, Defence, Ranged, Magic) in addition to prayer, which counters the stat-drain that brews themselves cause. At almost every boss, Super Restores are strictly better than Prayer Potions for an Ironman, and they protect your brew herb at the same time. The only time to use a Prayer Potion is when you have spare Ranarr and no Snapdragon — which, with a proper Farm run, should rarely happen.

🎯 The Karambwan + Brew Combo

  • A cooked Karambwan heals 18 Hitpoints and can be eaten in the same game tick as a brew
  • That gives you a brew heal plus an 18 HP food heal in a single tick — a massive instant recovery during burst damage
  • Karambwan require the Tai Bwo Wannai Trio quest and 65 Fishing plus 65 Cooking to prepare reliably
  • Use the combo only for emergencies; for steady chip damage, eat Karambwan alone and save brews for big hits
  • Because Karambwan do not lower your stats, they are a cleaner heal than a second brew back to back

⑫ The Overheal Mechanic

Saradomin brews heal a percentage of your maximum Hitpoints plus a flat amount, and crucially they can heal you above your normal maximum Hitpoints. This "overheal" lets you bank extra effective health going into a dangerous phase. A common technique is to brew up to overheal right before a known big hit or a phase transition, so that even after the hit lands you are still comfortably above your base max. Understanding overheal changes how you time brews: you do not wait until you are low, you brew preemptively to ride the overheal through the danger window. Combined with a Karambwan in the same tick, you can convert a near-death moment into a safe one.

⑬ Gear Upgrades Beat Herb Runs

Every point of Defence reduces the damage you take, and every point of DPS shortens the fight, and both directly reduce brew consumption. A single meaningful upgrade — moving from Dragon to Bandos, adding Rigour or Augury, or finishing a quest that unlocks better gear — can cut your brew usage at a boss by twenty to thirty percent. That saving compounds across every trip for the rest of the account's life, which no Farm run can match. Treat gear progression as a supply strategy, not just a DPS strategy.

💡 Flick the Safe Moments, Brew the Dangerous Ones

The efficient loop is: flick prayer during predictable, telegraphed attacks to preserve restores; eat Karambwan for steady chip damage to preserve brews; and reserve brews for the burst moments where you need the defence boost and the overheal. Ironmen who mix these up waste both restores and brews at the same time.

Mastering these habits is what lets a moderate Farm run support serious bossing. The supply you save here is the supply that builds your buffer, and the buffer is what lets you attempt the hardest content without fear of running dry mid-raid.

5. POH Pool & Altar to Refill Between Trips

Even with perfect stretching, you will eventually return from a trip low on Hitpoints, prayer, and stats. The free, permanent solution is your Player-Owned House. A properly built POH lets you refill to full — Hitpoints, prayer, combat stats, run energy, and special attack energy — in a few seconds, for zero cost, between every single trip. This is the Ironman equivalent of the Grand Exchange bank refill, and every serious bossing Ironman should build it.

⑭ The Ornate Rejuvenation Pool

The centerpiece is the Ornate Rejuvenation Pool, built in the Superior Garden at 90 Construction. Using the pool instantly restores your Hitpoints to full, restores your prayer points to full, restores all drained stats, restores your run energy, cures poison and disease and venom, and restores your special attack energy. In other words, it is a complete reset button. After any trip, you teleport to your house, step into the pool, and walk out as if you had just banked — except you also recovered prayer and special attack, which a normal bank does not give you.

The cost is in GP, not herbs, which is exactly what an Ironman wants: a one-time coin investment that removes a permanent herb drain. The marble blocks come from the Keldagrim Stonemason, and the gold leaves come from crafting or the construction suppliers. If 90 Construction is out of reach today, the lower-tier pools still restore the essentials and are a perfectly good stopgap; the Ornate version simply adds special attack energy, which matters for spec-weapon bosses.

⑮ The Chapel Altar

The second piece is a chapel with an altar. Praying at your own altar recharges your prayer points to full, and a higher-tier altar such as the Gilded Altar (75 Construction) also grants double prayer XP when you offer bones, which doubles as a prayer training method. For supply purposes, even a basic altar recharges prayer, so you can combine it with the pool routine: pool for HP and stats, altar for a final prayer top-up if needed, and you are full. Building a chapel is cheap and low-level, so there is no reason not to have one from early mid-game onward.

🎯 The Between-Trips Reset Routine

  • Finish the trip, teleport to your POH with a house teleport tab or spell
  • Enter the Ornate Rejuvenation Pool to restore HP, prayer, stats, run, and special attack
  • Pray at your chapel altar for a final prayer top-up if you skipped the pool
  • Withdraw your next trip's brews and restores from the bank (or a conveniently placed storage)
  • Teleport back to the boss and start the next trip at full, having spent zero supplies on the refill

⑯ Why This Ends the Drought

Without a POH reset, Ironmen often waste brews and restores simply recovering between trips, which quietly drains the buffer. With a pool and altar, the only supplies you ever consume are the ones spent inside the fight itself. That single change can stretch a given brew stock by a huge margin, because the "dead" consumption between trips disappears entirely. It is the closest thing an Ironman has to the main-account convenience of infinite free refills, and it is available to anyone willing to train Construction to 90 and spend the marble.

💡 Portal Nexus Saves Seconds

Add a Portal Nexus with a Vorkath, Zulrah, or Weiss portal (depending on your grind) so you can refill at the pool and teleport straight back to the boss in two clicks. The less friction between trips, the more kills per hour, and the more value you get from every brew you made.

6. Bosses Where Brews Matter Most + Budget Routes

Not all bosses tax your supply chain equally. Knowing which content is brew-hungry and which is gentle lets you route your Ironman through a budget-friendly progression that builds the buffer instead of draining it. This section ranks the pressure and gives the cheap routes that keep you self-sufficient.

⑰ Where Brews Matter Most

The bosses that consume brews fastest are the long, high-damage, prayer-heavy fights: Tombs of Amascut at high invocation, Theatre of Blood, Corporeal Beast, and sustained God Wars Dungeon grinds. These are also the bosses that demand the most gear and practice, so they are naturally late-game. By contrast, the Corrupted Gauntlet is surprisingly cheap because you self-supply brews from resources found inside the instance, and Zulrah, while venom-heavy, is short enough per kill that a disciplined Ironman can sustain it from Farm runs alone. Vorkath sits in the middle: brew-hungry at low gear, trivial at high gear.

⑱ Budget Ironman Routes That Build the Buffer

If you are currently dry, the smart move is to temporarily shift to content that pays you in supplies rather than costing you supplies. The Corrupted Gauntlet is the perfect example: it costs nothing to attempt, it teaches you to self-supply brews from inside, and it rewards you with enhydrix and unique loot that you can use or convert. Similarly, Zulrah at a modest invocation gives steady herb and resource drops that refill your Farm run indirectly. Even Slayer, though slow, drops grimy herbs, vials, and food that quietly stock your bank. The principle is to climb the supply ladder: start where the fight pays you, build the buffer, then spend it on the brew-hungry bosses once you can afford to.

🎯 The Budget Progression Ladder

  • Step 1: Corrupted Gauntlet and Zulrah — cheap to attempt, pay you in resources, teach self-supply
  • Step 2: Vorkath and GWD with ecumenical keys — moderate cost, big loot to fund Construction for the pool
  • Step 3: Build the Ornate pool and chapel so between-trip refills are free
  • Step 4: Only then attempt ToA, ToB, and Corp, where the buffer finally supports the consumption

⑲ The Permanent Structural Fix

If you intend to raid seriously, Farm runs alone will eventually fail to keep up, because raid consumption simply exceeds what one account's patches produce. The structural solution that ends droughts permanently is a second account feeding your main. The cleanest form is Group Ironman, where a group shares a bank and one member specializes in Herblore and Farming while the others boss. The alternative is a herb alt — a second Ironman that does nothing but Farm runs and Herblore, then trades finished potions to your main (allowed within GIM, or via the drop method for two separate Ironmen). The vast majority of end-game Ironmen run some version of this, because it converts the throughput problem from "your time" into "your accounts' combined time."

💡 Bank a Buffer, Not a Trip

The goal is a standing buffer of 150 plus brews and 80 plus Super Restores at all times, not just enough for today. When the buffer drops below 50, pause bossing and refill. The bosses that matter most are exactly the ones that will bankrupt a thin buffer in a single bad raid, so the buffer is your insurance, not your surplus.

For boss-specific loot and money routes that fund your Construction and herb alt, the Zulrah Money Guide and the Gauntlet Meta Guide both show how to turn these fights into supply-positive grinds rather than supply-negative ones.

7. Ironman Brew Supply FAQs

I have zero Ranarr. How do I make my first brews?

Get Ranarr from the disease-free Wilderness herb patch south of the Bandit Camp, the Trollheim patch, or as a common drop from Herbiboar, a level 31 Hunter activity that requires no Farming level and can supply your first twenty to thirty Ranarr in an afternoon. Combine the clean Ranarr with Snape Grass, which you pick from the patch near the Crafting Guild or the top of the Wizards' Tower. That first batch of brews gets your foot in the door, after which Farm runs take over.

Can I use Super Restores instead of Prayer Potions permanently?

Yes, and you should. Super Restores are made from Snapdragon and Limpwurt rather than your brew herb, so they protect your Ranarr, and they also restore your combat stats, which brews and Prayer Potions drain. At almost every boss, Super Restores are strictly better for an Ironman. The only edge case is when you are completely out of Snapdragon but sitting on spare Ranarr, in which case a few Prayer Potions are better than nothing.

How does the karambwan plus brew combo actually work?

A cooked Karambwan is coded so that it can be eaten in the same game tick as a Saradomin brew, giving you both heals simultaneously. The brew provides a percentage-based heal plus a Defence boost, and the Karambwan adds a flat 18 Hitpoints with no stat penalty. In a burst-damage moment — a big hit or a phase transition — using both in one tick can be the difference between living and dying, and it lets you get a large instant recovery without drinking two brews back to back, which would double your stat penalty.

My brews keep disappearing after I die. Where did they go?

On death you keep your three most valuable items with Protect Item, or you lose everything else. Brews are low value, so they are almost always lost on death at dangerous bosses. Always use Protect Item, keep your bank buffer off-site rather than on your person, and accept that losing a trip's worth of brews is normal while losing your standing buffer is the real disaster. The POH pool refill in section five exists precisely so you do not carry your buffer into danger.

How many Farm runs per day does it take to sustain raids?

High-invocation Tombs of Amascut can consume ten to fifteen brews per raid, which a single account's nine patches cannot sustain alone. Realistically you need three to four full Farm runs per day plus boss-drop Ranarr, or a herb alt feeding you. Most solo Ironmen cannot sustain top-end raids without a second account, so factor that into your progression early: build the alt or join a Group Ironman before you commit to a raid grind, not after you go dry.

Is an Ornate Rejuvenation Pool worth the 90 Construction?

For any Ironman who bosses seriously, yes. The pool removes all between-trip supply drain by restoring HP, prayer, stats, run energy, and special attack for free, which directly stretches your brew stock. The one-time marble cost is paid back in saved herbs within weeks, and the special attack restore alone is worth it for spec-weapon bosses. If 90 is out of reach, the lower-tier pools still restore the essentials and are a strong stopgap.

What is the overheal trick and when should I use it?

Because brews heal a percentage of max Hitpoints plus a flat amount, they can push you above your normal maximum. The overheal trick is to brew up before a known big hit or phase transition so that even after the hit lands you remain above your base max. It is most useful at bosses with telegraphed bursts like Zulrah phases or ToB Verzik, and it pairs perfectly with the karambwan combo for an even larger same-tick cushion.

UIM — how do I keep a brew buffer with no bank?

Ultimate Ironmen cannot bank potions, so you convert excess into three-dose potions to minimize space, use the looting bag and resource management carefully, and plan trips around your twenty-eight slot limit. Many UIMs keep a supply stash in a safe boss rest area or use the cache tricks available in certain content. The core lesson is the same as for every Ironman: never over-commit to a long grind without a planned refill, and use the POH pool the moment you can access a house.
