OSRS YouTube Shorts Guide 2026 — Grow an OSRS Channel & Drive Site Traffic

YouTube Shorts is the cheapest distribution OSRS content has ever had. A 40-second clip of a clean Vorkath kill, a "this method still works in 2026" money tip, or a single surprising fact can reach more people in a week than a written guide reaches in a month — and it can do it from an account with zero subscribers. This guide covers the format rules YouTube actually enforces, which OSRS topics travel furthest, a step-by-step production workflow using entirely free tools, how to legally send viewers to your own website, and what realistic growth actually looks like.

⏱️ Quick Summary — 30-Second Read

  • 📌 Shorts do not need subscribers. The feed distributes by watch-through rate, not by follower count.
  • 📌 Vertical 9:16 is non-negotiable. A horizontal clip is not a Short, regardless of how short it is.
  • 📌 Captions are mandatory in practice. A large share of feed viewers watch with sound off.
  • 📌 The hook lives in the first 3 seconds. If the tip does not start by second 3, the swipe already happened.
  • 📌 Best OSRS topics: GP-per-hour methods, item unlocks, boss kills with no commentary, "this still works in 2026".
  • 📌 Linking out is allowed via a pinned comment or the first line of the description — spammy link farms are not.
  • 📌 Consistency beats polish. One Short a day for 90 days outperforms ten perfect Shorts in a month.

1. 📈 Why Shorts Work So Well for OSRS

OSRS is unusually well suited to short-form video, and it comes down to three properties of the game.

📜 The Three Structural Advantages

  • Every answer is short. "Which method makes the most GP per hour at 70 Slayer?" is a 40-second answer. Long-form video has to pad it; a Short does not.
  • The game is visually legible. An XP drop, a loot beam, a pet roll, a big hit splat — these read instantly on a phone screen without narration.
  • The audience is permanently searching. OSRS players constantly look up methods, and short-form video is now where a large slice of those searches resolve.

The distribution side matters just as much. The Shorts feed does not primarily rank by subscriber count — it ranks by how many people who saw your clip kept watching. That means a brand-new channel with a genuinely good 40-second clip can reach an audience a written post or a long-form upload would never find.

For a site owner the strategic value is not the views themselves. It is that Shorts give you a legal, low-friction place to put your domain in front of OSRS players who are actively looking for the exact topic your guide covers.

2. 📐 The Format Rules YouTube Actually Enforces

Most failed OSRS Shorts fail for format reasons before the content is even considered. Get these right first.

RuleWhat It Means in Practice
Vertical 9:16 aspect ratioA horizontal 16:9 recording is not delivered as a Short. Record however you like, then crop to 9:16 in the edit.
Short durationShorts allow clips of a few minutes, but retention is what the feed rewards. Our recommendation: keep the core clip under 60 seconds and let the tip finish before the viewer's attention does.
No letterboxingDo not post a horizontal clip with black bars top and bottom. It reads as "recycled" and tanks swipe-away rate.
Captions / on-screen textAssume sound is off. Burn in text or use YouTube's caption tools — either works, silence does not.
Audio you can legally useYouTube's own audio library is the safe route. Trending audio is fine while it is available in the Shorts creation flow; ripping audio from another video is how channels get claims.
Accurate title and descriptionDo not clickbait a GP figure you did not actually demonstrate. Misleading metadata is a policy issue, not just an engagement issue.

💡 Record Wide, Deliver Tall

Capture your gameplay in full resolution, then crop. A 9:16 crop of OSRS gameplay usually wants to be centred on your character, with the HP/prayer orbs and any loot beam kept inside the frame — those are the visual cues viewers read without reading.

4. 🎬 Step-by-Step Production Workflow (Free Tools)

You can run this entire pipeline at zero cost. That is deliberate — the bottleneck on an OSRS channel is consistency, not equipment.

Step 1 — Record

  1. OBS Studio (free, open source) is the standard capture tool for OSRS. Record at your native resolution with high bitrate.
  2. Turn the interface down, not off. Hide chat and the noisy overlays, but keep your chatbox and the XP bar — the XP drop is half the payoff.
  3. Record a clean run. If a kill takes three attempts, the third is the one you upload. Nobody wants to watch you walk back from Lumbridge.
  4. Capture more than you need. Ten minutes of footage usually yields one good 40-second clip. That is normal.

Step 2 — Edit for the Phone Screen

  1. CapCut (free, mobile and desktop) or DaVinci Resolve (free, desktop) both handle vertical projects properly.
  2. Set the project to 1080×1920 (9:16) before you import anything. Retrofitting a horizontal timeline is wasted time.
  3. Lead with the payoff. Show the loot beam, the XP drop or the GP total in the first frames, then explain it.
  4. Burn in captions. Big, high-contrast, one line at a time.
  5. Cut dead air ruthlessly. Every "um" between two sentences is a swipe.

Step 3 — Publish

  1. Title = the promise. "3M GP/hr at 70 Slayer (still works in 2026)" beats "OSRS money making method".
  2. First line of the description = your link. That is the placement viewers actually see without expanding.
  3. Pin a comment restating the link and the single most useful fact from the clip.
  4. Add hashtags sparingly. A handful of relevant ones; a wall of them reads as spam.
  5. Upload on a schedule you can actually keep. Daily beats weekly beats "when inspiration strikes".

5. 🪝 Writing a 3-Second Hook That Works

The feed decides in roughly three seconds. Everything you were told about introductions is wrong here — you do not warm up, you do not introduce yourself, and you do not say "hey guys".

📜 Hook Patterns That Fit OSRS

  • The number first: "2.4 million an hour. Level 70 Slayer. No boss."
  • The contradiction: "Everyone farms this. It's the wrong one."
  • The unlock: "This quest takes 20 minutes and gives you 13,750 Attack XP twice."
  • The visual cold open: Start mid-kill on a big hit, then cut back to how you set it up.
  • The count: "Three money methods that still work in 2026 — number three is the one nobody uses."

What all five have in common: they withhold nothing essential and promise something checkable. A hook that cannot be paid off in forty seconds is a hook that produces a swipe, not a follow.

6. 🔗 Driving Traffic to Your Website — Legally

This is usually the actual reason an OSRS site owner starts a channel. There are three placements that work within YouTube's rules, and one pattern that gets you penalised.

MethodHow It WorksRisk
Pinned commentPin a comment immediately after publishing pointing at the full written guide. This is the safest placement and the one viewers are used to seeing.None, if you are linking relevant content.
First line of the descriptionPut the URL on line one, before the hashtags and before the boilerplate.None, as long as the description is not a link list.
End screen / linked long-form videoPoint a longer upload at your site, then link the Short to that video.Requires a longer upload and channel eligibility for end screens.
Link spamRepeated bare links across many Shorts with no relation to the clip.Platform penalty — this is the pattern to avoid.

💡 The Relevant-Link Rule

Link the page that actually continues the video. A Short about a specific money method should link to that method's guide, not the homepage. Matching the click to the promise is what turns a view into a session, and sessions are what your site is actually optimised for.

One more thing worth knowing: Jagex publishes a fan content policy covering videos and monetisation of OSRS content. If you are going to make a business of it, read the policy once and stay inside it — the rules are permissive for commentary, guides and gameplay, but they do exist.

7. ⚙️ Algorithm Notes for 2026

📊 What Actually Moves the Needle

  • Swipe-away rate is the first filter. A clip that holds people past the first few seconds gets shown to more people. Nothing else you do matters if they leave immediately.
  • Rewatches are the strongest positive signal. A tip so dense it needs a second watch is worth more than a long clip people finish once.
  • Consistency compounds. Channels that publish regularly give the system more data and more chances to find an audience.
  • Reply to comments early. The first hours drive the engagement signals that determine the next round of distribution.
  • Post when your audience is awake. Test a few time slots and keep the one that performs; do not blindly copy someone else's schedule.
  • Do not delete underperformers. Old Shorts keep getting discovered. A clip that looked like a flop can wake up months later.

8. 🚫 Common Mistakes That Kill Reach

  • Posting horizontal footage. The single most common reason an OSRS clip never enters the Shorts feed at all.
  • No captions. Large numbers of feed viewers have sound off, especially on mobile.
  • Long, slow openings. An 8-second intro on a 45-second clip has already lost most of the audience.
  • No payoff. Teasing a method and never showing the number is the fastest way to lose a follower.
  • Faceless with no text. No commentary is fine — no commentary and no on-screen text is not.
  • Clickbait figures. If the title says 3M/hr and the clip shows 1.2M/hr, viewers notice, and the engagement drop is permanent.
  • Ignoring the comments. Comments are your content research. "What about X method?" is your next Short.
  • Quitting at 20 videos. Short-form growth is not linear. The clip that works is usually not the first one you make.

💡 Measure the Right Thing

If your goal is website traffic, views are a vanity metric. Track clicks from the pinned comment and description, and the landing-page engagement on your site. A Short with 4,000 views that sends 200 people to the right guide is worth far more than a 60,000-view clip that sends everyone to a dead end.

Monetisation on the platform itself is a longer project: YouTube's Partner Programme requires both a subscriber threshold and an unusually large volume of Shorts views within a rolling window. Treat platform revenue as a possible later outcome, and treat your own website's traffic as the reason you are doing this now.

10. ❓ OSRS Shorts FAQs

Q: Can I grow an OSRS channel with zero subscribers?

Yes. The Shorts feed distributes primarily on watch-through rate rather than subscriber count, so a well-made clip from a brand-new account can reach a large audience. What you cannot skip is consistency — the breakthrough clip is usually somewhere in the first few dozen uploads, not the first one.

Q: Do I need a microphone or a facecam?

No. Commentary-free boss and gameplay clips perform well, and caption-driven method clips work without a voice. If you do not have audio, double down on on-screen text, because a silent clip with no text has very little for a viewer to hold on to.

Q: How long should an OSRS Short actually be?

The platform permits longer clips than it used to, but for OSRS specifically the practical ceiling is when your tip stops being useful. Most effective OSRS Shorts land in the 30–60 second range, because that is enough time for one method, one unlock, or one boss kill — and short enough that viewers often rewatch.

Q: Is it allowed to put my website link in a Short?

Yes, when it is relevant. A pinned comment and the first line of the description are both accepted placements. What gets penalised is repetitive bare-link spam across many unrelated uploads, so keep the link matched to the clip and you are fine.

Q: Can I monetise OSRS videos?

OSRS gameplay, guides and commentary are broadly supported under Jagex's fan content policy, and platform monetisation is possible once you meet the requirements. Read the fan content policy once so you know where the lines are, and do not use assets you cannot license — copyrighted music is the usual culprit.

Q: Which editing software should I use?

CapCut (free, mobile and desktop) is the fastest route for vertical edits done on the fly. DaVinci Resolve (free, desktop) is more powerful if you are willing to learn it. OBS Studio remains the standard free capture tool. Nothing in this workflow requires paid software.

Q: Should I post every day?

A daily cadence is the fastest way to build the data you need to find what works, and it is easier than it sounds once your workflow is tight — one recording session can produce several days of clips. If daily is genuinely impossible, a fixed schedule you can sustain beats a burst followed by silence.

Q: How do I get ideas that are not just "money making"?

Read your comment sections and your site's search data. Every question a viewer asks and every long-tail query your guides receive in search is a Short waiting to be made. Your own analytics are a better idea generator than any trend list, because they are specific to your audience.

Q: Do Shorts cannibalise my long-form videos?

Generally no. They serve different behaviours: Shorts capture discovery, long-form captures depth. The practical pattern is to use Shorts to demonstrate a method and long-form or written guides to explain it fully — and to link between them so the Short's audience has somewhere to go.

Q: What is the biggest mistake new OSRS channels make?

Quitting before the format is right. Almost every "my Shorts get no views" problem traces back to horizontal footage, no captions, or a slow opening — three fixable format issues, not a lack of content quality. Fix the format before you conclude the niche is dead.

Q: Does this work for other OSRS-adjacent games too?

Yes. The format rules, hook patterns and traffic tactics here apply equally to content around similar titles, since they are properties of short-form distribution rather than of any one game. Only the topic list changes.
