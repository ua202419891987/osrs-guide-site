1. 🤔 Why Your Gold Looks "Missing" After a GE Sale

The first time most players notice the tax is a small moment of panic. You set a sell price, the item sells, and the gold that lands in your bank is a little less than the price you entered. Your brain jumps to "the Grand Exchange ate my gold" or "there is a bug." It is neither — it is the Grand Exchange trading tax, a deliberate game mechanic Jagex introduced to remove gold from the economy and slow inflation.

If you are still learning how the market works, start with our Grand Exchange beginner guide, which walks through buy/sell offers, price limits, and the "instant sell vs. wait" choice. The tax only matters once you actually complete a sale, so understanding the sell side is what clears up the confusion.

Here is the key mental model: the number you type is the price a buyer pays; the tax is deducted from that before the gold reaches you. So a 100,000 GP sale does not put 100,000 GP in your bank — it puts 98,000 GP there, because 2,000 GP (2%) went to the tax sink. Nothing vanished. It was always going to be taken; you just were not shown the subtraction until the sale completed.

💡 The Tax Is Shown — If You Look

When you set a sell offer, the Grand Exchange interface displays the tax as a separate line before you confirm. New players skim past it. Next time, watch the "Tax: X GP" line appear as you raise your price — that is the exact amount that will be removed.

2. 💸 The 2% Tax, Explained Simply

The Grand Exchange tax is a flat 2% of the sale price, charged only to the seller. Let us strip it down to the bone:

  • Who pays it: Only the person selling an item. The buyer pays the full listed price and owes nothing extra.
  • How much: 2% of the price you set. 100 GP → 2 GP tax. 10,000 GP → 200 GP tax. 1,000,000 GP → 20,000 GP tax.
  • Where it goes: Into a gold sink (removed from the game). It is not kept by another player and is not refundable.
  • When it applies: On every Grand Exchange sale of 50 GP or more. Below 50 GP, there is no tax at all.

Why does the tax even exist?

Jagex added the GE tax to fight gold inflation. Every item sold feeds a tiny amount of GP into a sink, which gently reduces the total gold in the economy over time. Players do not feel it on small sales, but at scale — billions of GP traded daily — it removes a meaningful amount of gold. Knowing this helps you stop treating it as a personal loss and start treating it as a cost of doing business, the same way our beginner money-making guide treats raw materials as a cost.

⚠️ It Is NOT a Bug

If support tickets existed for "my GE gold is missing," they would be the most common ticket in the game. The tax is intentional and working exactly as designed. Do not report it as a bug — you will be pointed back to this exact mechanic.

3. 🆓 The 50 GP Tax-Free Threshold (Rounding Breakpoints)

This is the part almost no new player knows, and it is the secret to the "why did I lose nothing?" feeling on cheap sales. Any Grand Exchange sale priced below 50 GP is completely tax-free. Sell something for 49 GP and you receive all 49 GP. Sell for 50 GP and a 1 GP tax appears (2% of 50 = 1).

The rounding breakpoint table

Because the tax is rounded down to a whole GP, the take-home amount jumps in steps. Here are the breakpoints that matter most to new players:

List Price (GP) 2% Tax (raw) Tax Charged (rounded down) You Receive
25 0.5 0 25
49 0.98 0 49
50 1.0 1 49
99 1.98 1 98
100 2.0 2 98
149 2.98 2 147
500 10.0 10 490

The takeaway: on very cheap items the tax rounds to 0 or 1 GP, so it feels like "nothing was taken." That is correct — at 49 GP and below, literally nothing is taken.

💡 Practical Use of the Threshold

If you are bulk-selling worthless drops (bones, low herbs, junk), they usually sit under 50 GP and cost you zero tax. The tax only starts to bite once individual items cross 50 GP — which is why high-value flipping, covered in our GE flipping guide, must always price the 2% into your margin.

4. 🔝 The >250M / 5M Cap Misconception

There is a persistent myth that the GE tax "stops at 250M" or that "once you pass 250M total the tax disappears." Both are wrong, and the confusion comes from mixing up two separate limits. Let us clear it up.

The real cap: 5,000,000 GP per item

The tax is capped at 5,000,000 GP per single item sold. The 2% keeps growing with price until the tax would exceed 5M, and then it stops rising — you never pay more than 5M GP in tax on one sale. That means:

  • A 100M GP item → 2M tax (well under the 5M cap).
  • A 250M GP item → 5M tax (exactly the cap, since 2% of 250M = 5M).
  • A 500M GP item → still only 5M tax (capped).
  • A 2.1B GP item (near the max cash stack) → still only 5M tax.

Where the "250M" idea comes from

Players notice the cap is reached at 250M (because 2% of 250M = 5M), and half-remember it as "the tax stops at 250M." In reality the cap is on the tax amount (5M), not on an account total, and it never stops — it just stops growing. Every sale above 250M still pays the full 5M.

⚠️ Max Cash Stack Is Separate

The 2.147B max cash stack is a different limit entirely (it is the largest number the game can store in one inventory slot). It has nothing to do with the tax cap. Do not conflate "I hit max cash" with "my tax disappeared" — the 5M-per-item tax still applies on every sale.

5. 🧮 Worked Examples: How Much You Really Receive

Numbers beat theory. Here are realistic new-player sales and the exact gold that lands in your bank:

  • Dragon bones, 25 GP each × 100 = 2,500 GP sale → tax 0 (under 50 GP each) → you receive 2,500 GP.
  • Nature rune, 200 GP each, instant sell × 1,000 = 200,000 GP → tax 4,000 GP → you receive 196,000 GP.
  • Dragon Scimitar, 100,000 GP → tax 2,000 GP → you receive 98,000 GP.
  • Abyssal whip, 1,000,000 GP → tax 20,000 GP → you receive 980,000 GP.
  • Twisted Bow, 1,200,000,000 GP → tax capped at 5,000,000 GP → you receive 1,195,000,000 GP.

Notice the pattern: on cheap drops the tax is invisible; on a mid-tier weapon it is a rounding error; only on truly expensive items does it become a number worth planning around. For most new players grinding no-requirement money methods, the tax will rarely cost more than a few thousand GP per trip — annoying, not devastating.

💡 Price the Tax Into Your Margin

If you buy an item for 100K hoping to sell for 102K, you actually net 99,960 GP after the 2% tax — a loss, not a profit. Always subtract the tax from your expected sale price before you decide a flip is worth it. Our flipping guide builds this into every calculation.

6. 🔄 Does the Tax Apply to Buying? (And Other Edge Cases)

New players often ask whether the tax hits both sides of a trade. It does not. Here is the full edge-case list:

Buying on the GE

No tax. If you place a buy offer for 50,000 GP, you pay exactly 50,000 GP when it fills. The seller pays the 2%, not you. This is why "buy low, sell high" still works — only the sell side is taxed.

Player-to-player trades

No GE tax. Trading another player directly, or using the trade screen, is not a Grand Exchange transaction, so no 2% is taken. (Be careful of scams in direct trades — that is a different risk entirely.)

Shop sales (NPCs)

Selling to a non-player shop (a general store, a skill master) is a separate system with its own prices and no GE tax. You usually get far less than GE value, so the "tax" there is just a low buy price.

Instant sell vs. offer sell

Both are GE sales and both pay the 2% tax. "Instant sell" just matches the lowest current buy offer; it is not a tax-free shortcut.

Tax on the tax?

No. The 2% is calculated on the list price only. You are never taxed on the tax.

7. 📈 Tips to Stop the Tax Hurting Your Profits

You cannot avoid the GE tax on sales, but you can stop it from eating your margins:

Always subtract the tax before flipping

Take your expected sale price, multiply by 0.98, and that is your real take-home. If that number is below your buy cost, do not do the flip.

Use buy offers, not instant buys, when collecting

Buying is untaxed, so there is no penalty for patience. Place a buy offer at a slightly lower price and wait — you lose nothing to tax and often save GP versus instant-buying.

Sell in chunks you can price correctly

Because the cap is per item, selling one 250M item costs the same 5M tax as selling it in pieces would (roughly). The cap actually helps you on big-ticket sales — you are never taxed more than 5M per item, so very expensive items are relatively tax-efficient.

Know your threshold for junk

Bulk junk under 50 GP each is tax-free, so do not waste time "avoiding tax" on low-value drops. Just sell them and move on.

Track profit after tax, not before

The single habit that turns a confused new player into a profitable one is writing down post-tax profit. Our beginner money-making guide shows how to log real earnings instead of optimistic pre-tax numbers.

8. ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Is the missing gold a bug?

No. The 2% Grand Exchange tax is intentional. When you sell, Jagex removes 2% of the price as a gold sink. The gold is not lost — it is the tax, shown as a separate line before you confirm the sale.

Do I pay tax when I buy an item?

No. Only the seller pays the 2% tax. Buyers pay exactly the listed price with nothing extra taken.

Why did a cheap sale cost me zero tax?

Sales priced below 50 GP are tax-free, and the 2% is rounded down to a whole GP. So a 49 GP sale loses nothing, and even a 99 GP sale loses only 1 GP.

Does the tax stop after 250 million?

No. The tax is capped at 5,000,000 GP per item, which is first reached at a 250M price. Above that, the tax stays at 5M — it does not disappear, it just stops growing.

Can I avoid the GE tax?

Not on Grand Exchange sales — it applies to every sale of 50 GP or more. You can avoid it only by trading players directly or selling to NPCs (usually at a worse price), or by keeping sales under 50 GP.

Is the tax taken on the tax too?

No. The 2% is calculated once on the list price. You are never taxed on the tax itself.
