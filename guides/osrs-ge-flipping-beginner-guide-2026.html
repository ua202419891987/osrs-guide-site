1. What Is GE Flipping (And Why It Works in 2026)

Grand Exchange flipping, usually called margin flipping or just flipping, is the act of buying an item below its true market value and selling it above that value to capture the difference. You never use the item, you never train a skill with it, and you never fight a monster for it. The Grand Exchange itself is your shop, and the gap between what buyers are willing to pay and what sellers are willing to accept is the profit you collect. In Old School RuneScape the Grand Exchange is a central order book where every tradeable item has a live buy price and a live sell price, and that spread is where flippers live.

The reason flipping remains one of the best money makers in 2026 is simple: it scales with almost no requirements. A brand-new member with 5 million GP can flip the exact same items as a maxed player with 5 billion GP, and both earn a percentage return on their capital. Unlike skilling or PvM, flipping does not depend on your combat level, your total level, or your gear. It depends on your understanding of the market, your patience, and your discipline. That is why this guide is written for intermediate players who already have a small bank and want to grow it without grinding for hours each day.

There are two broad styles of flipping. The first is margin flipping, where you look for items that have a stable gap between buy and sell price and you trade a moderate volume of them. A nature rune might only give you a one or two GP margin per item, but because the buy limit is huge you can move tens of thousands of them, and the pennies add up to millions. The second style is high-margin flipping, where you target items with a large per-item gap but lower volume, such as certain potions, rune items, or supplies that swing after updates. Beginners should start with margin flipping on liquid staples because the risk of a price crash is far lower.

① How the Grand Exchange Order Book Actually Works

When you place a buy offer at the current market price, you are matching against the cheapest existing sell offers. If your buy price is at or above the lowest sell offer, your trade fills instantly. If you set your buy price slightly below the lowest sell offer, your offer sits in the order book and only fills when a seller lowers their price to meet you. The same logic applies in reverse for selling. The trick of flipping is to place your buy offer a little below the instant-buy price so you wait for a motivated seller, then place your sell offer a little above the instant-sell price so you wait for a motivated buyer. You earn the spread between those two patient offers.

🎯 The Flipper's Golden Rule

  • Never buy at the instant-buy price unless the margin still works after tax.
  • Never sell at the instant-sell price unless you are exiting a bad position.
  • Patience is the profit. Waiting a few minutes for the right price is what separates winners from break-even traders.
  • Always subtract the 1% Grand Exchange tax before you decide an item is worth flipping.

Think of flipping as the savings account of Gielinor: boring, safe, and always growing.

2. The Flipping Profit Formula — Margin x Volume minus 1% Tax

Every flip lives or dies by one equation. If you internalize nothing else from this guide, internalize this:

🧮 The Core Formula

  • Gross margin per item = sell price you expect to get minus buy price you paid.
  • Tax per item = 1% of the item's value, charged on the selling transaction, for items worth more than 100 GP (capped at 5,000,000 GP per item).
  • Net profit per item = gross margin minus tax.
  • Daily profit = net profit per item multiplied by the number of items you can realistically trade per day.

In plain numbers, imagine you flip a clean rune item. You buy it for 18,000 GP and you believe you can sell it for 19,500 GP. Your gross margin is 1,500 GP. The Grand Exchange tax is 1% of the value of the item on the sell side, which works out to roughly 195 GP. Your net profit is therefore 1,305 GP per item. If you can move 200 of those per day thanks to buy limits and timing, your daily profit is about 261,000 GP. That single calculation is the entire business of flipping.

① Understanding the 1% Grand Exchange Tax

Jagex introduced the Grand Exchange tax to slow extreme price manipulation and to skim a small fee from high-value trades. The rule is straightforward: on the transaction that completes a trade, the seller pays a tax equal to 1% of the item's Grand Exchange value. For most flipping, you are the seller when you close the position, so the tax comes out of your sell side. The tax only applies to items with a Grand Exchange value above 100 GP, which means very cheap items like bones or low-value resources are effectively tax-free. The tax is also capped at 5,000,000 GP per item, so even a 4 billion GP party hat only costs you 5 million GP in tax, not 40 million.

Beginners make the classic mistake of calculating profit as sell minus buy and forgetting the tax. A flip that looks like 1,000 GP per item can quietly become 800 GP or less after the 1% bite, and on thin-margin items that difference is the entire profit. Always build the tax into your target sell price. If you need 1,000 GP net and the item is worth 20,000 GP, you must sell at roughly 21,200 GP so that after the 200 GP tax you keep your 1,000 GP margin. The tax is not a penalty for bad play; it is a fixed cost of doing business that you simply account for.

💡 A Quick Tax Shortcut

Because the tax is 1% of the sell value, you can approximate your required sell price with this mental math: take your desired net margin, add the buy price, then add 1% on top. Or flip the formula around — net margin equals about 99% of (sell minus buy) when margins are small. For a 1,000 GP gross gap, your net is roughly 990 GP after tax. Train your brain to shave roughly 1% off every margin you see and you will never be surprised at the bank.

② Volume Is the Multiplier That Makes Margins Matter

A two GP margin sounds useless until you realize you can trade 40,000 nature runes per four-hour window. Two GP times 40,000 is 80,000 GP per cycle, and if you run three or four cycles a day that is 240,000 to 320,000 GP from a single item with almost no risk. Volume is controlled by the Grand Exchange buy limit, which caps how many of an item you can buy from the exchange within a rolling four-hour window. High-volume items like runes, ores, logs, and food have buy limits in the tens of thousands, while rare items might have a limit of just a few. As a beginner you should gravitate toward high-limit items so your capital is never sitting idle waiting for a limit to reset.

③ Worked Example: From 1M GP to a Bond

Let us say you start with 1,000,000 GP and you flip coal. Coal buys around 200 GP and sells around 215 GP, a 15 GP gross margin. Tax does not apply because coal is under 100 GP in value on the sell transaction? Wait, coal value is around 200 GP, which is above 100 GP, so tax does apply: 1% of 215 is about 2 GP. Net margin is about 13 GP. Your 1,000,000 GP buys roughly 5,000 coal (limited by the 20,000 buy limit, so you could buy more, but let us keep the example modest). Selling 5,000 coal at 13 GP net gives 65,000 GP per flip cycle. Run three cycles in a day and you make about 195,000 GP. In roughly nine days of casual flipping you earn a bond's worth of GP without ever leaving the Grand Exchange interface. That is the power of the formula done consistently.

⚠️ The Margin Crash Trap

Your formula assumes the sell price holds. If a massive supplier dumps 100,000 of your item onto the exchange, the buy price can collapse below what you paid. Suddenly your "13 GP margin" becomes a 50 GP loss. The formula cannot protect you from a crash; only diversification, small position sizes, and watching the market can. Never put your whole bank into one item, and never chase a margin that looks too good to be true.

The profit formula is the skeleton of flipping, but the meat is knowing which items to apply it to. In the next section we list the items that are safe, liquid, and beginner-friendly in 2026, with real margins and limits you can build a routine around.

3. Beginner-Friendly Items to Flip Safely

The safest items to flip share four traits: they are consumed constantly by the player base, they have deep buy limits, their prices are stable week to week, and they are hard to manipulate because the volume is too large for any single trader to move. Runes, ores, logs, and food are the classic beginner staples. Below we break them down with realistic 2026 margins so you can start today.

① Runes — The King of Beginner Flips

Runes are the single best starting point for any new flipper. Nature runes, law runes, death runes, and astral runes are bought by almost every player who trains Magic, does clues, or bosses. Their prices are stable because demand is constant and supply is huge. Nature runes typically show a 1 to 3 GP margin, law runes a 2 to 6 GP margin, and death runes a 3 to 8 GP margin. None of these are taxed because each individual rune is worth under 100 GP, so your entire gross margin is yours to keep. With buy limits of 15,000 to 25,000 per four hours, a focused flipper can clear several hundred thousand GP a day just rotating rune offers between limits.

The best way to flip runes is to set a buy offer a few GP below the current instant-buy price and a sell offer a few GP above the instant-sell price, then walk away and let the order book fill. Because runes are so liquid, your offers usually fill within minutes. You can run nature runes, law runes, and death runes at the same time, which keeps your capital working across three order books instead of one.

② Ores, Bars, and Logs — Bulk Staples

Coal, iron ore, and pure essence are the next tier of safe flips. Coal margins sit around 5 to 20 GP, pure essence around 1 to 4 GP, and these items move in enormous volume because smithers and runecrafters buy them constantly. Maple logs and yew logs are also flippable, though yew margins are thinner and more volatile. The advantage of ores and essence is that their prices barely move, so you can hold a position for a day without fear of a crash. The disadvantage is that margins are small, so you need capital and volume to make them worthwhile. A player with 5M to 10M GP can do very well here.

③ Potions and Consumables — Mid-Margin Plays

Once you are comfortable, prayer potions, super restores, and saradomin brews offer larger per-item margins because they are worth more than 100 GP and therefore taxed, but the volume is still healthy. A prayer potion might show a 200 to 500 GP margin after tax, and with a 10,000 buy limit that is a meaningful daily return. The catch is that potion prices swing more around leagues, events, and boss releases, so you must watch them. Treat potions as your "intermediate" flip once the rune and ore basics are automatic.

④ Items to AVOID as a Beginner

Just as important as knowing what to flip is knowing what to avoid. Stay away from rares and discontinued items such as party hats, Santa hats, and Halloween masks. Their buy limits are tiny, their prices are manipulated by whales, and a single bad flip can wipe your bank. Avoid items you have never heard of with suspiciously huge margins — that gap usually exists because nobody is buying at the higher price, so your sell offer will never fill. Avoid items tied to a single boss or a single quest that could be nerfed in the next update. And never flip an item whose price chart looks like a spike: if it rose 300% in a day, it will fall just as fast, and you do not want to be holding it.

4. Timing, Buy Limits & Inventory Management

Flipping is not just about what you trade; it is about when you trade it and how you organize your bank. A player who understands buy-limit resets and keeps a clean, liquid bank will earn two or three times more than a player with the same capital who trades randomly. This section turns flipping from a hobby into a routine.

① The 4-Hour Buy-Limit Reset

Every tradeable item on the Grand Exchange has a buy limit that refreshes on a rolling four-hour timer from the moment you last bought it. If you buy 10,000 sharks at 10:00 AM, your limit resets at 2:00 PM, not at a fixed server time. This means the smart flipper staggers purchases so that limits are always partially available. Instead of dumping your whole bank into one item and waiting four hours, spread purchases across several items with different reset clocks. That way, at any given moment, at least one of your flips is ready to be rebought.

A common advanced tactic is the "limit block": buy exactly the buy limit of an item, wait for it to sell, then rebuy the limit again the moment it resets. Repeat this four or five times across a day and you have multiplied your volume far beyond a single purchase. The discipline is in showing up at reset time. Set a phone alarm if you must; the players who capture the reset window are the ones who max out their daily volume.

② Best Hours to Flip

The Grand Exchange is most liquid when the most players are online, which in 2026 means the evening hours in the US and EU time zones, plus the late-night hours in Australia and Asia. During these peaks, your sell offers fill faster because more buyers are active, and your buy offers fill faster because more sellers are dumping. Margins are often slightly thinner at peak because competition is fierce, but the higher fill rate more than compensates. The worst time to flip is the dead of the server's night, when offers can sit for hours. If you can only play at off-peak times, focus on the most liquid items (runes) that trade even when the world is quiet.

💡 Flip Around Updates, But Carefully

When a new boss, skill, or quest drops, the items it uses spike in price for a day or two. This is where the biggest margins appear, but also where the biggest crashes happen. The safe play is to buy the related consumables in the hours before the update (when nobody else is thinking about them) and sell into the spike on release day. Never hold a post-update position longer than a day, because the crash is inevitable once the hype passes.

③ Keeping Your Bank Liquid and Organized

A flipping bank should stay mostly liquid. The more of your GP is tied up in items you cannot sell today, the less you can deploy into the next opportunity. A good rule is to never let more than 70% of your total net worth sit in unsold inventory; keep at least 30% as cash so you can pounce on a margin when it appears. Organize your bank with a dedicated flipping tab: one tile for each item you are actively trading, your cash stack separate, and your "pending sell" items clearly grouped so you always know what is in the market versus what is in your bank.

As your bank grows, decide deliberately whether to reinvest profits or cash out for gear and bonds. Cashing out for a bond or a piece of gear is fine too, but understand it resets your compounding. Most intermediate players keep a "trading bank" that is never touched for spending and a separate "spending stack" they withdraw from. That separation keeps the flipping engine running while still letting you enjoy the game.

5. Common Scams That Drain Flippers' Banks

Flippers are prime targets for scammers because everyone knows flippers carry liquid GP. A scammer does not need to hack your account; they only need to trick you into handing over the items or GP yourself. In 2026 the most common attacks are social rather than technical, which means awareness is your best defense. Learn these patterns cold before you trade with anyone outside the Grand Exchange.

① The Item-Switch (Trust Trade) Scam

The oldest trick in the game is the "trust trade" or item-switch. A scammer offers to buy your flipped items for a great price in a player-to-player trade, then quickly swaps the valuable item for a nearly identical junk item at the last second — a dirty rune arrow for a rune sword, or a white berry for a valuable one. Because the trade screen updates fast, your eye misses the swap and you confirm. The defense is simple: never do large trades outside the Grand Exchange, and if you must, double-check every item slot on both sides before you click accept. The Grand Exchange exists precisely so you never need to trust a stranger.

② Fake Middleman and "Official" Dispute Scams

Scammers pretend to be an official middleman or a Jagex moderator to "secure" a trade between you and another player. They will send a message that looks like a system notice, link a fake website, or ask you to trade them the items "for safekeeping." Jagex moderators never trade with players, never ask for your password, and never use Discord or WhatsApp to resolve disputes. Any "middleman" who contacts you first is a scammer. Use only the official Grand Exchange or a middleman service you verified through the official forums, and even then, be suspicious of anyone who approaches you.

③ Phishing, Clone Sites, and "Double Your GP"

The most dangerous scam for flippers is the phishing link. You receive a message or see a comment claiming "Jagex is doubling GP this weekend, claim at this link" or "free bonds for flippers." The link leads to a clone of the OSRS login page that steals your credentials the moment you type them. Within minutes your account is stripped of every item and every GP you flipped for. Real Jagex communications never ask you to log in through a shortened or strange URL. Bookmark the official site and only ever log in there. Turn on two-factor authentication so that even a leaked password cannot move your bank.

⚠️ If It Sounds Too Good, It Is

No legitimate event doubles your GP. No player can multiply your stack by sending it to them. No moderator will ever trade you. Any message that creates urgency — "act in 10 minutes or lose the offer" — is engineered to bypass your judgment. Slow down, close the chat, and verify through official channels. The few minutes you spend checking will save a bank you spent months flipping to build.

④ Investment and Pump-and-Dump Scams

A newer 2026 variant is the "investment group" scam. Someone invites you to a Discord or clan chat where they promise to coordinate buying an item to pump its price, then sell into the spike. In reality, the organizers already bought cheap and are using you as the exit liquidity; when you buy, they dump, and the price crashes with your GP inside it. This is the exact margin-crash trap from section two, weaponized by bad actors. Never take flipping tips from unsolicited group chats, and never buy an item just because a stranger said it will moon.

⑤ Account Buying, Power-Leveling, and Malware

Finally, beware offers to "buy a maxed flipping account" or "let us bot your flips for you." These almost always end with your payment taken and no account, or with malware installed on your machine that steals your saved passwords. OSRS accounts are non-transferable under the rules, so any sold account can be recovered by the original owner, leaving you with nothing. Keep your own account, secure it properly, and flip it yourself. The slow, honest compounding described in this guide is safer and, over a year, far more profitable than any shortcut.

💡 Build a Scam-Proof Habit

Enable two-factor authentication today. Set a bank PIN. Never click links from in-game strangers. Trade only on the Grand Exchange for anything valuable. If a deal requires leaving the Grand Exchange, assume it is a scam. These four habits cost you nothing and block every scam in this section. Your flipping profit is only real if you still control the bank at the end of the week.

Scam-proofing is the unglamorous half of flipping, but it is what keeps your gains. With safe items, good timing, and a secure account, the only remaining question is how much you should aim to earn. The final section sets realistic daily GP goals by bank size.

6. Setting Realistic Daily GP Goals

The fastest way to burn out or make reckless trades is to chase a number you cannot realistically hit. Flipping returns a percentage of your capital, not a fixed GP per hour, so your goals must scale with your bank. This section gives you target ranges for common bank sizes so you know whether your routine is on track.

① Returns Scale With Capital

A healthy flipping bank returns roughly 2% to 5% per day for an active player and 1% to 2% for a casual player who only checks a few times. That sounds small until you compound it. A 10M bank at 3% daily is 300,000 GP per day, or about 9 million GP in a month — enough to buy multiple bonds or a strong gear set. A 100M bank at 3% is 3 million GP per day, which rivals high-level PvM without the risk of death. The key insight is that flipping gets more powerful as you get richer, which is the opposite of most money makers that cap out at a fixed GP per hour.

② Start With a 100K-a-Day Habit

If you are new, do not aim for millions on day one. Set a floor of 100,000 GP per day. That is low enough to hit reliably with a couple of rune flips and high enough to feel real. Hit that floor for two weeks straight, and you will have built the habit of logging in, checking offers, and rebuying at reset. Once 100K is automatic, raise the floor to 250K, then 500K, then 1M as your bank grows. Goals that rise with your skill keep you motivated without pushing you into risky items.

③ Track Your Results Like a Business

Treat your flipping like a small business and it will behave like one. Keep a simple spreadsheet of each item, your buy price, your sell price, the tax paid, and the net profit. Review it weekly to see which items are pulling their weight and which are just tying up capital. You will quickly notice that two or three items do most of the work, and you can drop the rest. Tracking also exposes mistakes early — if your "profit" row keeps going negative on one item, you have found a margin crash before it eats your bank.

④ When to Stop Flipping and Spend

Flipping is a means, not the game itself. Once your trading bank is large enough to fund your goals — a bond each month, a voidwaker, a Torva set — move those GP into the things that make the rest of the game fun. The point of flipping is to free you to play the game you came for.

💡 Compounding Beats Grinding

A player who flips 3% a day and reinvests turns 10M into roughly 24M in a month, just from the market. The same player grinding a 500K GP per hour method for two hours a day makes 30M in a month but pays with their time and attention. Flipping is not about replacing grinding; it is about building a base of GP that works while you sleep, so your active playtime goes further. Let the exchange compound for you.

7. GE Flipping FAQs

How much GP do I need to start flipping?

You can start with as little as 100,000 GP by flipping nature runes or coal, but a 1M to 5M GP starting bank makes the routine far smoother because you can fill buy limits properly and run several items at once. The more capital you have, the more volume you can deploy, and the more your percentage return is worth in absolute GP. Do not wait until you are rich to begin; begin now with what you have and let compounding do the rest.

Is GE flipping bannable or against the rules?

No. Flipping on the Grand Exchange is a normal, accepted playstyle and is explicitly part of how the economy is designed to work. What is bannable is using real-world trading, botting, or price manipulation through exploits. Simply buying low and selling high as a player is completely legitimate and will never get you banned. Just stay inside the official Grand Exchange and avoid any third-party automation.

How do I find items to flip beyond this list?

Use the RuneLite Grand Exchange plugin to sort items by margin and by volume, and watch the live price graphs for stable items with a consistent gap between buy and sell. Community price-tracking websites also list top movers, though you should verify any number inside the game before committing GP. Start with the staples in this guide, then expand to one new item per week once you understand its price behavior. Never jump into an item you have not watched for at least a few days.

Why did my flip lose money when the margin looked good?

Almost always it is one of three causes: you forgot the 1% tax and the true margin was thinner than you thought; the buy price crashed after you bought because a large supplier dumped stock; or your sell offer never filled and you panic-sold at the instant-sell price, accepting the worst possible value. Fix all three by subtracting tax first, diversifying across items, and setting patient sell offers instead of dumping. A lost flip is tuition; write it down so you do not pay it twice.

How much tax will I actually pay?

You pay 1% of the item's value on the sell side, but only for items worth more than 100 GP, and never more than 5,000,000 GP per item even if the item is worth billions. Cheap staples like runes, coal, and essence under 100 GP are effectively tax-free, which is a big part of why they are ideal beginner flips. For higher-value items, just shave 1% off your expected sell price during planning and you will always know your real net margin.

Can I flip on a free-to-play account?

Yes, but the F2P Grand Exchange has far fewer liquid items and thinner margins, so progress is slower. The best F2P flips are runes, iron ore, and pure essence, which work the same way as in members. Many players use F2P flipping specifically to save toward their first bond, then switch to members where the real volume and variety open up. If you are on F2P, treat flipping as your bond fund and stay patient.
