1. What Is Group Ironman (and How It Differs From Solo)

A Group Ironman (GIM) is an Ironman account that belongs to a group of two to five players. Like all Ironmen, the group cannot use the Grand Exchange or trade with outsiders, but instead of each player having a personal bank, the entire group shares a single group bank. That one change turns the mode from a solo slog into a cooperative project.

① GIM vs solo Ironman

🎯 The key differences

  • Shared bank: One group bank holds all items; individual members have no private bank.
  • Co-op progression: Members can split the grind — one trains Smithing, another farms, a third bosses.
  • Social accountability: The group moves at the pace of its slowest or most active member, depending on how you organize.
  • Leaving penalty: A member who leaves becomes a permanent solo Ironman and cannot return to the group.
💡 The mental model

Think of a GIM group as one household with a shared pantry. Everyone contributes and everyone eats from the same shelf. The household progresses faster than a solo player because the work is parallelized — but you have to actually coordinate, or the pantry goes stale.

2. Forming a Group: Codes, Size, and the Founders

Forming a group is permanent and binds you to the other founders, so do it deliberately.

② How group creation works

StepWhat happens
Choose a founderOne player creates the group and gets a unique invite code.
Set the sizeThe group is fixed at 2–5 members at creation; you cannot change it later.
Invite the restOther players join with the code before anyone plays past tutorial island.
Lock it inOnce the group is formed, membership is locked; new members cannot be added after the fact.
⚠️ Pick your group like a business partner

You are tied to these people for the entire life of the account. Form a group with players you trust to stay active and to communicate. A group that fractures mid-progression is one of the most common GIM regrets.

3. Shared Storage Rules (the Part Everyone Gets Wrong)

The shared storage system has quirks that trip up nearly every new group. Get these right and the mode clicks; get them wrong and you will lose items.

③ The storage mechanics

🏦 How the group bank behaves

  • One shared bank: All members deposit and withdraw from the same group bank, accessible at any bank booth.
  • No personal bank: Individual members cannot hide items from the group; everything is communal.
  • Tradeable-only deposit: Only tradeable items can be stored; untradeable drops stay on the individual.
  • Trust is the system: There is no permission layer — any member can withdraw anything, so the group must self-police.
💡 Avoid the "who took my stuff" fights

Because there is no per-member permission, establish a rule early: high-value items (bis gear, rare drops) are group assets, not personal property. A clear agreement prevents the most common GIM drama.

4. Roles and Division of Labor

The single biggest advantage of GIM is parallel progression. A group that divides work out-levels and out-gears any solo Ironman of equal total hours.

④ Example role split

✅ A proven 5-person split

  • The bosser: Pushes combat and raids for group gear and supplies.
  • The skiller: Trains the slow buyables (Smithing, Runecrafting, Construction) so the group gets gear and teleports.
  • The farmer: Runs Farming and Herblore for food and potions the whole group uses.
  • The quest runner: Clears quests and diaries to unlock content for everyone.
  • The flexible fifth: Covers gaps — Slayer, Hunter, or money making as needed.
⚠️ Don't double up on the same grind

Two people training the same skill wastes the group's parallelism. Coordinate so each member covers a distinct pillar. The point of GIM is that the group does five things at once, not five people doing one thing.

5. Early Game Group Progression

The opening weeks of a GIM set the group's culture. Use the shared bank to snowball fast.

⑤ The early-game playbook

🚀 First priorities

  • Quest together, bank together: Every quest reward lands in the shared bank, so one person's progress helps all.
  • Pool starter gear: Deposit spare tools, runes, and food so newer members gear up instantly.
  • Unlock group teleports: Quests like Fairytale II and the lodestone network speed up every future trip.
  • Set a meeting cadence: Decide how often you play together versus solo, so the shared bank stays stocked.
💡 The shared bank is a force multiplier

When one member farms a few hundred thousand noted fish and dumps them in the group bank, the whole group eats free. That kind of pooling is why GIM groups hit late-game content months ahead of solo Ironmen.

6. Mid and Late Game

Once the group is established, the late game is about raiding and maxing as a coordinated unit.

⑥ Scaling the group

🏰 From starting group to raid team

  • Raids as a team: The Chambers of Xeric and Theatre of Blood are far smoother with a coordinated group that shares drops.
  • Distribute bis fairly: Agree on a loot policy (needs-based or rotation) before the first big drop.
  • Cross-train for flexibility: As members max, they can fill any role a raid team needs.
  • Use group chat and tools: External voice or text chat is nearly mandatory for organized raiding.
💡 Loot policy before loot

The single most common late-game GIM breakup is a fight over a rare drop. Decide the policy (rotation, need, or bank-value split) before you get the item, not after. Write it down in your group chat so there is no ambiguity.

7. Leaving the Group and the Permanent Unlock

This is the section every GIM player should read before they need it. Leaving has real, permanent consequences.

⑦ What leaving does

ActionResult
Member leavesThey become a permanent solo Ironman and lose access to the group bank.
RejoiningNot allowed — the departure is final and the group cannot be rejoined.
Last member leavesThe group is disbanded and remaining assets are lost to the group structure.
Shared itemsLeaving members forfeit everything in the shared bank; only their personal untradeables come with them.
⚠️ Treat leaving as irreversible

Unlike some other mode switches, leaving a GIM group is effectively permanent. Only do it if you are certain you will never want to play with that group again. Many players keep a struggling group alive just to preserve the option.

8. GIM Money Making & Bossing as a Team

The entire point of Group Ironman is that the shared bank turns divided labor into compound progress. One member's grind feeds five accounts at once.

⑧ Divide by strength

Assign roles early: a maxed crafter supplies gear and bolts, a skiller funds the group with resources, a PvM main clears bosses, and a runner handles shops and clues. The shared bank means nobody re-grinds what a teammate already made.

⑨ Best group money makers

Chambers of Xeric, Tombs of Amascut, Nex, Hueycoatl, and the Corporeal Beast are all stronger with a coordinated team than solo. Profit that would take one account weeks is split across roles and banked jointly.

⑩ Early-game group grinds

Before end-game, run Tempoross and Wintertodt as a group. The rewards stack in the shared bank and bootstrap everyone's early gear simultaneously.

⑪ Shard and loot rules

Decide up front how raid points, unique drops, and construction supplies are split. A written (or at least agreed) rule prevents the resentment that breaks most groups.

⑫ Construction and the group POH

One member building a shared Player-Owned House (portal, rejuvenation pool, and a Combat Ring) gives the whole group free teleports and stat restoration between trips. Because the house is account-bound but the benefit is shared, it is the highest-ROI group investment after the first raids — and it removes a surprising amount of bank-standing downtime.

9. GIM Traps & Group Etiquette (What Breaks Groups)

⑬ No tax or death rules

Without a light "tax" into the shared bank, one member funds everything and burns out. Agree on a contribution norm before you need it.

⑭ The hoarder

A single member parking rare drops in the shared bank "for later" starves the rest. Transparency about what is reserved avoids suspicion.

⑮ Leaving is permanent

Once you leave a GIM group you can never rejoin it, and the other founders keep playing. Treat a leave as a one-way door — discuss it as a group, not solo.

⑯ Mixing in a Hardcore

Putting a Hardcore Ironman inside a GIM group adds a death-risk that can gut the team's progress. Keep account types aligned.

⑰ Inactive members

A dead account clogging the roster blocks recruitment and dampens morale. Set an activity expectation and a graceful exit path.

⑱ The permanent-unlock trap

Leaving grants permanent GIM status (you keep playing solo with the cosmetics), but you forfeit the shared bank forever. Weigh that trade carefully before any dramatic exit.

10. Group Ironman FAQs

Q: How many people can be in a GIM group?

Between two and five, fixed at creation. You cannot add or remove members after the group is formed, so choose your size and roster deliberately.

Q: Can a GIM trade with other Ironmen?

No. Like all Ironmen, a GIM cannot trade with players outside its own group or use the Grand Exchange. The only sharing is within the group bank.

Q: What happens to the group bank if I leave?

You forfeit access to the shared bank and become a permanent solo Ironman. Only your personal untradeable items leave with you; everything in the shared bank stays with the group.

Q: Is GIM easier than solo Ironman?

Generally yes, because the shared bank and divided labor let the group progress faster. But it adds coordination overhead and social risk, so it is "easier" only if your group works well together.

Q: Can we add a friend later if we started with three?

No. Group size and membership lock at creation. Plan your full roster before forming the group.

Q: How should we split rare drops?

Agree on a policy before the first raid: rotation, needs-based, or bank-value split are all common. The key is deciding in advance, not arguing after a rare item drops.

Q: Does the group share a hiscore entry?

GIM groups have their own leaderboard, but each member also has an individual Ironman hiscore entry reflecting their own progress. The shared bank helps individuals rank faster.

Q: Can a Hardcore Group Ironman exist?

Yes — the Hardcore variant (HCGIM) applies the one-death demotion to the whole group. That is a distinct, harsher mode with its own redemption mechanics, covered in our HCIM guide.
