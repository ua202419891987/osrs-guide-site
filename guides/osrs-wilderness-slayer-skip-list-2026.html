OSRS Wilderness Slayer Skip List 2026 — What to Block, What to Rush

Published September 18, 2026  |  16 min read  |  By Yanchun Wu

Krystilia pays more Slayer points per task than any other master in the game, and every kill inside the Wilderness can roll a Larran's key. That is the real reward loop — and it is not the one most guides describe. This article breaks down what skipping and blocking actually cost, the full task pool by weight, which assignments are worth turning down at 90+ and 95+ Slayer, and the key drop-rate formula so you can pick tasks that actually pay.

1. How Wilderness Slayer Actually Works

Krystilia stands in the Edgeville guardhouse, in the jail north-east of the bank. She has no combat level requirement, which is the first thing that catches people out: she will assign you any monster in her pool regardless of your combat level, and she warns you about that before your first task.

The second thing that catches people out is that her assignments use a separate task counter from every other Slayer master. You must complete five tasks from Krystilia before her point streak begins. You also cannot hold a normal Slayer task and a Wilderness task at the same time.

Critically, only kills inside the Wilderness count. A monster's death must physically occur while you are in the Wilderness. That includes the safe-zone buffer immediately outside Ferox Enclave, but the Enclave itself does not count — so resting at the pool of refreshment mid-task and letting a cannon finish a kill will not give you credit.

💡 Two useful oddities

Krystilia refuses to assign anything to a player still at level 1 Slayer. The Natural history quiz at the Varrock Museum awards 1,000 Slayer and 1,000 Hunter experience, which takes both skills to level 9 and clears that block. She can also move your respawn point to Edgeville for a one-time fee of 5,000,000 coins.

One practical escape hatch: if Krystilia gives you a task you cannot or will not do, Turael can remove it. The catch is that this resets your Wilderness task streak, and you will need another five Krystilia tasks before earning points again.

2. The Real Reward Loop — Keys, Enchantments, Points

Wilderness Slayer pays out through three channels, and none of them are emblems.

RewardSourceWhy It Matters
Slayer pointsTask completion at Krystilia25 per task at base — the highest of any master — rising to 125, 375, 625, 875 and 1,250 at 10th, 50th, 100th, 250th and 1,000th task intervals
Larran's keyAny monster killed in the Wilderness while on her taskOpens Larran's small and big chests for noted ores, seeds, runes and a Dagon'hai chance
Slayer's enchantmentWilderness monsters killed on taskEnchants a Slayer's staff into a Slayer's staff (e) with 2,500 charges

The point rate is the headline. Krystilia gives 25 points for every task, and the milestone bonuses stack on top: 125 at every 10th, 375 at every 50th, 625 at every 100th, 875 at every 250th and 1,250 at every 1,000th. For comparison, Nieve pays 12 points per task and Duradel pays 15. If you are grinding Slayer points for unlocks, task extensions or block slots, Krystilia is simply the fastest source in the game — you just have to accept the Wilderness risk.

The key and enchantment drops ride along with the kills you were already doing. Neither requires a separate activity, and both are tradeable, so they convert directly into coins.

3. What Skipping and Blocking Actually Cost

These are two different actions and they are priced differently.

💰 The two costs

  • Cancel (skip) a task: removes your current assignment without penalty and without permanently changing your list. The wiki lists the cost as a 30-100 point range that varies by master. The exact price at Krystilia is not separately published, so confirm it on the in-game prompt before committing.
  • Block a task: cancels the current assignment and permanently removes that monster from your list. With Krystilia this costs a confirmed 100 Slayer reward points — the joint-highest block price in the game alongside Duradel.

Two structural facts change how you should think about that price. First, since the 27 August 2025 update every Slayer master has its own block list. Blocking Hill Giants at Krystilia does not free up a slot or change what Duradel assigns you. Second, block slots are granted one per 50 Quest Points, up to seven — most masters cap at six usable slots, and Lumbridge & Draynor Elite grants an additional slot once you own a quest cape.

Because a Krystilia block costs 100 points and she only pays 25 per task, a single block is worth four tasks of income. That is a real cost, and it is why the correct play at high Slayer levels is usually to skip a mediocre task rather than block it — unless the task reappears often enough to justify locking it out permanently. Blocking also does not refund anything if you later change your mind.

💡 Block economics at Krystilia

With six block slots and roughly a dozen genuinely bad assignments in the pool, you cannot block your way out of every poor roll. Reserve permanent blocks for the most frequent bad tasks by weight, and skip the rest on a case-by-case basis.

5. The Skip List — What to Turn Down at 90+

At 90+ Slayer your time is worth more than the points you would save by grinding out a bad task. These are the assignments to cancel.

🚫 Skip these

  • Magic axes (weight 7). High frequency, low value. They sit in the Magic Axe Hut at Wilderness levels 54-56 — genuinely deep, with a long walk and heavy PK traffic. You also need 23 Thieving and a lockpick just to get in.
  • Fire giants (weight 7). The Deep Wilderness Dungeon sits at Wilderness level 53. Slow kills, low Slayer XP per kill, and one of the worst places to be caught by a PKer.
  • Ice warriors (weight 7). Frozen Waste Plateau at levels 44-55. Ice warriors give very little Slayer experience and the task is long at 100-150 kills.
  • Lava dragons (weight 3). Low weight, but 35-60 kills on Lava Dragon Isle at levels 36-43 with a dragonfire requirement. Only worth it if you specifically want the noted bones and hides.
  • Hill Giants, Pirates, Zombies and Black Knights (weight 3 each). The bottom of the pool. Low combat level means the worst Larran's key odds in the game, no Slayer XP to speak of, and they are common enough to clog your block slots.
  • Bears and Scorpions (weight 6 each). Deceptively frequent and deceptively bad. Low-level monsters, poor key rates, and the boss variants that also count are not something you can reliably farm on task.
  • Chaos druids and Dark warriors (weight 5 and 4). Low Slayer experience and no meaningful key rate. Chaos druids are worth it only if you personally value the herb drops.

The common thread is that every task on that list shares at least two of three problems: the monster's combat level is low enough to wreck your Larran's key odds, the location is deep enough in the Wilderness to be dangerous, and the Slayer experience per kill is negligible at 90+. Skipping one of these costs a fraction of the income it would take to grind it out.

Where you should spend permanent block slots depends on your own assignment history. Track which of the above you roll most often over a few dozen tasks — Magic axes, Fire giants and Ice warriors are the natural first candidates purely because their weight-7 entries make them frequent.

6. The 95+ Optimal Task Pool

At 95+ Slayer the entire calculus changes, because you now have access to the best key-farming environment in the game: the Wilderness Slayer Cave. Every monster killed there gets a further 15% increased chance to drop a Larran's key, and the cave contains several Slayer monsters that already carry a 20% key-rate bonus on top of that.

TaskWhy It Belongs Here

Nechryael (80 Slayer)Combat level 200 and in the cave. The highest-combat task in the pool, which means the best base key odds available without touching a boss. Extended amounts go to 200-250.

Hellhounds (weight 7)Cave hellhounds at combat levels 122-136, plus the Deserted Keep spawns. Solid XP and no supplies needed.

To make this pool reachable, buy the two cheap unlocks first. I Wildy More Slayer costs 0 points and is the single most valuable free toggle at Krystilia — it lets her assign Jellies, Dust Devils, Nechryaels and Abyssal Demons. Bigger and Badder costs 50 points and gives superior slayer monsters a spawn chance; on Krystilia tasks a superior always drops a Larran's key, which makes it one of the highest-value 50 points you can spend. Like a Boss costs 200 points, and Watch the birdie for Aviansie costs 80.

Because the toggle tasks only appear once you have them enabled, buying them actively dilutes the pool away from filler and toward Slayer monsters. That is the whole strategy: spend points to make the bad rolls rarer.

7. Larran's Key Maths — Where the Money Is

Larran's key drop rate is not fixed. It scales with the monster's combat level, and there are two multiplicative bonuses on top.

📐 Key drop rate

  • Combat level 1-80: 1 in (100 + floor(0.3 x (80 - level) squared)) — from 1/1972 at level 1 improving to 1/100 at level 80.
  • Combat level 81-350: 1 in (115 - floor(5/27 x level)) — from about 1/99 at level 81 down to 1/50 at level 350, then capped at 1/50 above that.
  • Slayer monster bonus: multiply the chance by 1.2. This applies to any monster that requires a Slayer level to kill.
  • Wilderness Slayer Cave bonus: a further 1.15 multiplier on any kill inside the cave.
  • Superior slayer monsters: always drop a key on Krystilia tasks.

This is why the skip list and the optimal pool look the way they do. A Hill Giant and an Abyssal demon do not give you remotely comparable numbers of keys, even though they occupy the same task slot and cost the same to skip.

Where you cash in matters too. Larran's small chest sits north-east of the Chaos Temple and pays roughly 30% less than the big chest, with no chance at Dagon'hai robes. Larran's big chest is on the ship west of the Pirates' Hideout and adds a 1/256 chance each at the Dagon'hai hat, robe top and robe bottom, alongside higher-quantity versions of the same noted ores, seeds and runes. The big chest also announces the name of whoever opens it to a wide area, which is a direct invitation to PKers — open it with nothing else on you worth losing.

💡 Keys are always dropped on death

Larran's keys leave your inventory when you die, even if you would normally keep them under the three-item rule. If another player kills you, they take the keys. If you die to a monster, the keys go to your gravestone. Since keys became tradeable in April 2023 you can also just sell them on the Grand Exchange instead of running the chests at all.

8. Emblems and Blighted Items — The Common Myth

Two rewards get attached to Wilderness Slayer constantly in older guides and forum posts. Neither is part of the current reward loop, and getting this wrong leads people to farm the wrong content.

Mysterious emblems no longer exist. They were purchased from the Emblem Trader, upgraded by killing your Bounty Hunter target, and came in five tiers worth 75,000 to 1,200,000 coins on the sell-back. They were removed on 23 April 2020 with the Bounty Hunter Returns update. They were never a Wilderness Slayer drop in the first place — they belonged to Bounty Hunter, a PvP system, not to Krystilia's tasks. Any guide telling you to farm emblems through Wilderness Slayer is describing content that has been gone for years.

Blighted items are Wilderness-only consumables, not Slayer drops. A blighted potion or food item can only be used inside the Wilderness, Ferox Enclave, the Mage Arena bank, PvP worlds, Castle Wars and Clan Wars. They are bought from the Last Man Standing reward shop for 1 point, or from the Soul Wars Reward Shop for 10 Zeal Tokens. Larran's chests do not contain them, and Krystilia's tasks do not drop them.

What blighted items are good for is supporting your Wilderness Slayer trips — the 4-dose blighted super restore is the only variant tradeable on the Grand Exchange, and it restores Prayer at a boosted rate if you are wearing a Prayer cape or a ring of the gods (i), or carrying a Prayer cape or holy wrench. Buy them for the task, do not expect to farm them from it.

9. Risk Management in the Wilderness

Every hour you spend on a Krystilia task is an hour you can be attacked, and the deep-Wilderness tasks on the skip list are deep for a reason. Four habits cover most of the risk.

  • Bring only what the task needs. Cheap food, a cheap weapon, and no item you would be upset to lose. The Wilderness is not the place for your best gear.
  • Use the looting bag. Any Slayer master sells one for 10 Slayer reward points, and inside the Wilderness it lets you carry more loot per trip without a bank run.
  • Bank keys and enchantments often. Both are tradeable and both are always lost on death. A full inventory of keys is exactly what a PKer is hoping to find.
  • Prefer the Wilderness Slayer Cave for anything on the keep list. It has better key odds, denser spawns and a more predictable escape route than the open deep-Wilderness locations.

If you set your respawn to Edgeville, Krystilia's 5,000,000 coin service, you cut the run-back time after a death dramatically. Whether that is worth the fee depends entirely on how much Wilderness Slayer you plan to do — but if it is going to be your main Slayer master, it pays for itself in travel time.

10. Wilderness Slayer FAQs

Q: How much does it cost to skip or block a task at Krystilia?

Blocking a task with Krystilia costs a confirmed 100 Slayer reward points — one of the two highest block prices in the game. Cancelling (skipping) a task is listed on the wiki as a 30-100 point range that varies by master, but the exact Krystilia figure is not separately published, so check the confirmation prompt in-game before you commit.

Q: Which Wilderness Slayer tasks should I skip at 90+?

Magic axes, Fire giants and Ice warriors first — all weight 7, so they come up often, and all sit deep in the Wilderness with poor Slayer XP. Then the weight-3 filler: Hill Giants, Pirates, Zombies and Black Knights. Lava dragons, Bears and Scorpions are also weak value unless you specifically want their other drops.

Q: What is the best task pool at 95+ Slayer?

Anything inside the Wilderness Slayer Cave. Abyssal demons, Nechryael, Dust devils and Jellies all stack the 1.2 Slayer-monster key multiplier with the 1.15 cave multiplier. Greater demons, Black demons, Ankou and Hellhounds are the other strong picks. Enable I Wildy More Slayer (free) and Bigger and Badder (50 points) first so those tasks can actually be assigned.

Q: Do Mysterious emblems drop from Wilderness Slayer?

No. Mysterious emblems were removed on 23 April 2020 with the Bounty Hunter Returns update, and they were a Bounty Hunter item rather than a Wilderness Slayer reward. The current Krystilia reward loop is Slayer points, Larran's keys and Slayer's enchantments.

Q: Where do blighted items come from?

Blighted potions and food are bought from the Last Man Standing reward shop for 1 point, or the Soul Wars Reward Shop for 10 Zeal Tokens. They are Wilderness-only consumables and are not found in Larran's chests or dropped by Krystilia's task monsters.

Q: What is the Larran's key drop rate?

It scales with the monster's combat level: roughly 1/100 at level 80, improving steadily to 1/50 at level 350. Slayer monsters get a 1.2 multiplier, kills inside the Wilderness Slayer Cave get a further 1.15, and superior slayer monsters on Krystilia tasks always drop a key.

Q: Can I hold a Wilderness task and a normal Slayer task at the same time?

No. You can only hold one Slayer task, and Krystilia uses a separate completion counter from other masters. You must finish five tasks from her before her point streak starts, and having Turael remove a Wilderness task resets that streak.
