1. ⛵ Sailing Overview — What the Skill Is

Sailing is the first completely new skill added to Old School RuneScape in many years, and it plays differently from almost every other skill in the game. Instead of standing in one spot clicking a tree, rock, or altar, you command a ship that travels between ports, charts unexplored water, dives on wrecks, races through trial courses, and trawls the deep sea. Your Sailing level measures how well you handle that vessel, and nearly every training method is tied to the boat itself rather than to a fixed location.

The skill begins at level 1 for every account, main or Ironman, member or free-to-play where available, and it scales all the way to 99. What makes Sailing unusual is that it is both a destination skill and a transport skill at the same time. Early on, you train it for the XP. Later, a higher Sailing level and a bigger ship unlock content that feeds other parts of your account: better travel, passive resource generation, and access to high-end bosses and activities that simply are not reachable from a small raft.

Because Sailing is new, the exact numbers have shifted since launch. Jagex has adjusted XP rates more than once, most notably trimming some of the lowest-effort Port Task bounty gains in a 2026 balance pass. The practical takeaway is that you should treat any single XP per hour figure as a range rather than a promise, and you should lean on method choice and consistency rather than chasing a precise hourly number. For a broader picture of how Sailing slots into a 23-skill plan, see our complete skill training guide, and for the condensed 1–99 route, our full Sailing 1–99 guide.

💡 The One Sentence Version

Sailing rewards players who understand which method fits their playstyle more than players who blindly chase the highest XP per hour. Pick a rhythm you can sustain for 100+ hours, and the 99 takes care of itself.

2. 🗺️ The Five Core Activities Explained

Almost all Sailing XP comes from five repeatable activities. Understanding what each one is, what it rewards, and how active it is will let you build a plan instead of guessing. The table below summarizes them; the sections after it go deeper.

Activity Rough Level Start Style XP/Hr (range) Side Reward
Port Tasks 1 Passive / casual Low – moderate Bounties, reputation, small GP
Sea Charting 1 Exploration (one-time per zone) One-time lump XP Unlocks routes, navigation bonuses
Shipwreck Salvaging ~20 Semi-AFK Moderate (pays GP) Planks, ores, gems, salvage
Barracuda Trials ~30 Active, skill-based High (fastest) Minimal – pure XP
Deep Sea Trawling ~50 Semi-AFK Moderate Raw fish (trains Fishing too)

② Port Tasks — Your Daily Sailing Bread and Butter

Port Tasks are short errands given at docked ports: deliver cargo, ferry passengers, scout a stretch of coast, or run a supply line. They are the gentlest entry point and they are where most players earn their first Sailing levels. Because they are casual and often tied to a port you are already visiting, they are a good way to trickle XP in without a dedicated grind. Note that the 2026 balance pass reduced the bounty XP on the very lowest-effort tasks, so leaning only on Port Tasks is no longer the most efficient path — but they remain a solid, low-pressure supplement.

③ Sea Charting — Free Levels You Should Not Skip

Sea Charting is exploration. You sail into uncharted water and map it, earning a large one-time XP reward for each zone you complete. Because it is one-time, it is pure free progress that you should grab early. Charting also improves your ship's navigation and unlocks faster travel between regions, which pays off every single time you train later. A common mistake is to put off charting until high level; the smarter move is to chart aggressively in your first weeks so that every later trial and salvage run happens at the highest available tier.

④ Shipwreck Salvaging — The AFK Money Printer

Salvaging sends your ship to dive on wrecks. You locate a wreck, send down salvaging equipment, and pull up planks, ores, gems, and assorted salvage. It is semi-AFK: you interact every few minutes, then let the dive run. Crucially, salvaging pays you in both GP and raw materials. On a main account that means steady profit while you train; on an Ironman it means the exact stacks of planks and ore you need to keep upgrading. This is the method most second-monitor and Netflix players should default to. Full detail in our Sailing AFK training guide.

⑤ Barracuda Trials — The Active Speed Route

Barracuda Trials are timed, skill-based courses where you pilot your ship through gates, around hazards, and across checkpoints as fast as possible. They reward repetition and muscle memory: a course you fumble on your first attempt can become dramatically faster after a few hours. Trials are the fastest XP in the skill and the default choice for focused sessions, but they demand attention. The crown jewel trial unlocks at level 72 and is where the very highest XP per hour lives.

⑥ Deep Sea Trawling — Two Skills At Once

Trawling deploys a net from your ship and pulls up fish. It trains Sailing and Fishing simultaneously and is semi-AFK. If you still need Fishing levels, trawling is effectively free Sailing XP because you were going to fish anyway. It is a classic synergy win and a great way to fill AFK windows once you reach the level requirement.

🎯 How the Five Fit Together

  • Chart everything early — one-time XP and better travel forever
  • Trials when focused — push the bulk of your XP in active sessions
  • Salvage when busy — AFK XP plus materials and GP
  • Trawl when you need Fishing — two skills, one timer
  • Port Tasks as filler — casual XP between dedicated sessions

3. 📈 Training Methods by Level Bracket (1–99)

Sailing's optimal method changes as you level, mostly because higher tiers of each activity unlock and your ship grows. The table below is a practical roadmap. Treat the XP per hour figures as ranges — your exact rate depends on ship tier, facilities, and how cleanly you run trials.

Level Bracket Recommended Method XP/Hr (range) Why
1 – 20 Port Tasks + early Charting Low – moderate Easy entry, free chart XP, no ship upgrade needed yet
20 – 50 Salvaging (Skiff) + Charting gaps Moderate AFK-friendly, pays materials, bridges to the Sloop
50 – 72 Salvaging (Sloop) + intro Trials Moderate – high Sloop's crew slots boost everything; trials ramp up
72 – 99 Barracuda Trials (top course) + salvage filler High (fastest) Top trial unlocks; focus here for the fastest finish

⑦ Levels 1–20: Establish the Habit

In the opening bracket you are on a basic raft with minimal capacity. Do not overthink it. Run Port Tasks at whatever port you are near, and chart the zones you sail through. The combination of light tasks and one-time chart XP gets you to 20 with almost no friction and teaches you the controls. This is also the time to start your support skills gently (see the shipbuilding section) so they do not block you later.

⑧ Levels 20–50: The Salvaging Bridge

Once salvaging opens, it becomes your workhorse. On a Skiff you will have limited crew and facility slots, but salvaging still pays materials and GP, which funds the eventual Sloop. Mix in charting whenever you unlock a new zone. By the time you approach 50, your goal is simple: have the support skills ready so you can build the Sloop the moment you ding 50.

⑨ Levels 50–72: The Sloop Era

The Sloop is the turning point. Its extra crew and facility slots meaningfully raise every activity's output, and it is when trials start to rival salvaging for efficiency. Many players run a hybrid here: salvage during AFK windows, attempt trials during focused sessions, and keep charting any remaining zones. This bracket is where a sustainable rhythm matters most — it is long, and burning out here is the most common failure point.

⑩ Levels 72–99: Sprint on Trials

At 72 the top Barracuda Trial unlocks and becomes the fastest XP in the skill by a wide margin. If your schedule allows focused play, pour your active hours here. Salvaging remains a fine filler for busy days, but the XP ceiling is now clearly highest on trials. A main that focuses here finishes 99 noticeably faster than one that keeps salvaging; an Ironman may still prefer salvaging for the materials, trading some speed for self-sufficiency.

💡 Rule of Thumb

Use active time for the 72–99 stretch if you can, and let AFK cover 1–72. You cannot easily "catch up" trial XP if you only ever AFK'd, because the rates diverge sharply after 72.

4. ⚡ Fastest vs Profitable vs Ironman Routes

The three "best" routes are best at different things. Picking the wrong one for your account is how players waste dozens of hours. Here is the honest comparison.

① The Fastest Route (Main, Focused)

If you want the Sailing Cape as quickly as possible and you have uninterrupted sessions, Barracuda Trials are the answer. They deliver the highest XP per hour in the skill and scale hard with practice. The trade-off is attention: trials are not something you do while watching a movie. For a main that just wants 99 on the way to a Max Cape, this is the obvious play. Budget a focused, repetitive grind and use the trial helper plugins to draw optimal lines.

② The Profitable Route (Main, Casual)

If your play is spread across a second monitor, Shipwreck Salvaging wins despite a lower XP rate. You click a wreck every few minutes and otherwise live your life, and you get paid in GP and materials the whole time. Over a 99 the profit can be substantial, which effectively offsets the slower training. This is the route for players who would otherwise quit a pure trial grind out of boredom.

③ The Ironman Route (Self-Sufficient)

An Ironman cannot buy its way past gates, so the profitable/main distinction collapses into one path: salvage, chart, and self-supply. Salvaging generates the planks, ores, and gems an Ironman needs, so training and self-sufficiency reinforce each other. Front-load support skills, build the Sloop at 50, and let salvaging carry you. The slower pace is irrelevant because the materials are the reward. See our Ironman money making guide for bootstrapping those early skills without the Grand Exchange.

🎯 Which Route Is Yours?

  • Main + free time: Trials-heavy hybrid, finish fast
  • Main + busy life: Salvage-heavy hybrid, finish paid
  • Ironman: Salvage + chart, self-supply everything

5. 🛠️ Shipbuilding, Barrels & Crew

Your ship is not handed to you fully built, and its upgrades are gated by support skills. Understanding these gates early prevents the awkward moment where you have the GP for a hull but cannot build it. This section covers hulls, barrels and cargo, and crew.

① Hulls: Raft → Skiff → Sloop → Warship

You begin with a Raft. The Skiff unlocks around level 15 and adds a crew slot and a facility slot. The Sloop unlocks around level 50 and is the critical milestone — multiple crew and facility slots raise the output of every activity. Larger hulls at higher levels add even more capacity and access. The key habit: never delay a hull upgrade once you hit its level. The Skiff's two slots cap your efficiency hard, so building the Sloop at 50 is one of the highest-value actions in the whole grind.

② Barrels & Cargo Holds

Barrels are Sailing's storage layer. Your ship carries cargo — planks, ores, salvage, and other goods — in barrels and hold space, and the size of that storage determines how long you can run a method before returning to port. Bigger hulls and certain facilities increase barrel capacity, which directly extends your AFK windows and your per-trip profit from salvaging. Managing barrels well means fewer bank runs and more time actually training. Think of barrels as the skill's version of a larger inventory: the more you can carry, the more efficient every trip becomes.

③ Facilities & Crew Slots

Facilities are installable ship modules — things like resource extractors, teleport focuses, and rigging improvements — that passively boost your voyages or generate materials. Crew slots let you staff the ship with specialists who improve speed, yield, or survivability. Both are gated by support skills (detailed below) and by hull tier. A Sloop with zero facilities is just an expensive raft, so plan your support skills so you can fill slots the moment a hull unlocks. For the deep detail on staffing, see our Sailing ship & crew guide.

④ The Support-Skill Gates

Every hull and most facilities consume planks (Woodcutting), metal (Mining → Smithing), and sailcloth (Crafting). Construction behaves like a floating Player-Owned House for interior slots. You do not need 99 in any of them — only thresholds that stop blocking your next upgrade.

Support Skill Why Sailing Needs It Suggested Threshold
Woodcutting Planks for hulls & facilities 50 (Teak), 60 ideal (Mahogany)
Smithing Hooks, fittings, metal parts 40 – 50
Crafting Sailcloth & rigging 30 – 40
Construction Interior facility slots 33+ (oak interiors)
Mining Ore for Smithing parts 40 – 50
Fishing Deep Sea Trawling synergy 50 (optional but efficient)
⚠️ Don't Over-Level for the Sake of It

These are thresholds, not goals. You do not need 99 Woodcutting to sail. Train the support skill only to the point where it stops blocking your next upgrade, then return to Sailing. If you want targeted help, our Woodcutting 1–99 guide, Mining guide, and Crafting guide cover the exact paths.

6. 💰 Cost Comparisons & Best Training Spots

Sailing's "cost" is unusual: the active route is nearly free (you supply a ship and time), while the profitable route literally pays you. The real cost is opportunity cost — the GP and hours you trade for speed or materials.

① Cost / Profit Comparison

Route Direct GP Cost GP Return Net
Barracuda Trials (focused) Low (consumables only) Minimal ~Break-even, fastest
Shipwreck Salvaging Low High (GP + materials) Profitable
Deep Sea Trawling Low Fish sales Profitable + Fishing XP
Port Tasks None Small bounties Minor profit

On a main, the "cheapest" 99 is the one that pays you — salvaging — because it offsets the time you spend. On an Ironman, GP is irrelevant and materials are everything, so salvaging wins for the same reason. Trials cost the least in attention-resistant focus but the most in forgone profit. For exact money-making math, the dedicated Sailing money making guide breaks it down.

② Best Training Spots by Goal

"Spots" in Sailing are really activity locations: the wreck fields for salvaging, the trial courses for speed, the trawling grounds for dual training, and the charting zones for free XP. The best spot is the one matching your current goal:

Fastest XP

Run the highest trial course you have unlocked, especially the level-72 course, during focused sessions. Use helper plugins with optimal route lines.

Best Profit / AFK

Salvage at the highest-tier wreck field your ship can reach. Bigger barrels and a Sloop keep trips long and payouts high.

Best Dual Training

Trawl in the deep-sea grounds once you meet the level requirement — you train Sailing and Fishing at once.

7. 🧭 Step-by-Step: Your First 99

A plan you can actually follow beats a theoretical optimum you abandon at level 55. Here is a sustainable, numbered path from level 1 to 99.

① Levels 1–20 — Learn the Controls

Start with Port Tasks at the nearest port and chart every zone you sail through. Do not rush a hull upgrade yet; just build the habit and grab the free chart XP. Gently begin Woodcutting so planks are accumulating in the background.

② Levels 20–50 — The Salvaging Bridge

Unlock salvaging and run it on your Skiff during AFK windows. Chart any new zones as they open. Push Woodcutting toward 50 (Teak) and Smithing/Crafting toward their thresholds so the Sloop is buildable the moment you hit 50.

③ Hit 50 — Build the Sloop Immediately

This is the most important single step. The moment you reach level 50, build the Sloop and fill its crew and facility slots. Delaying it wastes every session afterward. From here, salvaging and trials both improve at once.

④ Levels 50–72 — Hybrid Rhythm

Salvage when busy, attempt trials when focused, and keep charting gaps. Use helper plugins to draw optimal trial lines so your active sessions are efficient. This long bracket is where burnout happens — protect your rhythm with AFK filler.

⑤ Level 72 — Unlock the Top Trial

The highest Barracuda Trial unlocks and becomes the fastest XP in the skill. If your schedule allows, shift your focused hours here. Keep salvaging as filler on busy days.

⑥ Levels 72–99 — Sprint

Pour active time into trials. A main finishes fastest this way; an Ironman may keep salvaging for materials. Either path reaches 99 — the difference is speed versus self-sufficiency. The condensed route in the companion Sailing training guide is a useful reference.

💡 The "Honest Hours" Test

Before committing, estimate how many of your weekly hours are truly focused. Under five? Build around salvaging and treat trials as a bonus. Forcing active training onto an AFK lifestyle is how accounts stall at level 50.

8. ❓ Sailing FAQs

What level does Sailing start at?

Sailing starts at level 1 for every account. There is no prerequisite skill or quest gate that blocks you from beginning — you simply start with a basic raft and train from there. The skill scales to 99 like any other.

What are the five core Sailing activities?

The five repeatable activities are Port Tasks (casual errands), Sea Charting (one-time exploration XP), Shipwreck Salvaging (semi-AFK, profitable), Barracuda Trials (active, fastest XP), and Deep Sea Trawling (semi-AFK, also trains Fishing). Most training is some mix of these five.

What is the fastest way to 99 Sailing?

Barracuda Trials are the fastest XP in the skill, especially the course unlocked at level 72. A main that focuses active sessions on trials finishes 99 well ahead of any AFK route. The trade-off is attention: trials are not a second-monitor activity, and the rates reward practice and clean routing.

What is the most profitable way to train Sailing?

Shipwreck Salvaging is the profitable route. It is semi-AFK and pays you in GP and raw materials (planks, ores, gems, salvage) while you train. Over a full 99 the cumulative profit can be substantial, which effectively offsets the slower XP rate versus trials.

Which support skills should I train before building a serious ship?

Woodcutting first (planks gate every hull and facility — aim for 50 Teak, 60 Mahogany ideal), then Smithing 40–50 and Crafting 30–40 for fittings and sailcloth, with Construction 33+ for interior slots. Mining 40–50 supplies the ore Smithing needs. Train each only to its threshold, then return to Sailing.

Can an Ironman realistically 99 Sailing without the Grand Exchange?

Yes. Salvaging generates the planks, ores, and gems an Ironman needs, so training and self-sufficiency reinforce each other. Front-load support skills, build the Sloop at 50, and salvage your way to 99. The slower pace is irrelevant because the materials are the real reward.

What are barrels and why do they matter?

Barrels are your ship's cargo storage. Bigger hulls and certain facilities increase barrel capacity, which extends how long you can run a method before returning to port. More barrel space means fewer bank runs and longer, more profitable salvaging trips — directly boosting efficiency.

Should I chart everything early or save it for later?

Chart early. Sea Charting gives a large one-time XP lump per zone and improves navigation permanently. Skipping it means grinding later activities at a lower tier than necessary. Knock out charting in your first weeks so every future trial and salvage run happens at its highest available tier.

Did the 2026 Sailing changes make trials useless?

No. The 2026 balance pass mainly trimmed the lowest-effort Port Task bounty gains, not the trial ceiling. Trials remain the fastest XP by a wide margin. If speed matters, trials; if lifestyle and materials matter, salvaging.

Is Deep Sea Trawling worth it if I already have Fishing?

If your Fishing is already 99, trawling's dual-skill appeal drops, but it is still a perfectly fine semi-AFK Sailing method. If Fishing is not done, trawling is excellent because it trains both skills at once. Treat it as a filler activity for AFK windows rather than your primary 99 push.

Final Tips — Your Sailing Deep-Dive Checklist

→ 1: Honestly sort your hours into "focused" vs "AFK" before picking a method.
→ 2: Train support skills only to their Sailing gate — Woodcutting 50, Smithing 40–50, Crafting 30–40.
→ 3: Chart every sea zone early for the one-time XP boost and better travel.
→ 4: Build the Sloop the moment you hit level 50 — do not delay.
→ 5: Ironmen: salvage for materials, not GP — the stockpile is your real reward.
→ 6: At 72, shift focused hours to the top Barracuda Trial for the fastest finish.
