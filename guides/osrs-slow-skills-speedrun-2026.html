OSRS Slow Skills Speedrun 2026 — Blitz Agility, Slayer & Mining

Updated August 19, 2026  |  23 min read  |  By Yanchun Wu

Three skills punish impatient players hardest: Agility, Slayer, and Mining. This speedrun plan chains the correct rooftop courses, tunes your Slayer block list for point efficiency, and picks the right Mining method for your account — so you cut dozens of hours off the grind without burning out.

🏃 Skill Training Series · 18 min read · Updated for 2026
🛡️ Level: 70+

Every intermediate OSRS account eventually hits the same wall: a few skills that feel like they take forever. Agility, Slayer, and Mining are not the absolute slowest skills on paper — that title usually goes to Runecrafting or Construction — but they are the ones players most often dread, because the early levels are tedious and the payoff feels distant. This speedrun guide shows you how to compress those hours using the right courses, the right block lists, and the right mining method for your account. If you are still building the combat foundation these methods assume, start with our complete skill training guide before attempting these optimized routes, and if you later pick up the newest skill, the Sailing 1–99 guide follows the same "fastest path" logic.

The core idea is that these skills are not slow because of their XP curves — they are slow because most players use the wrong method for their level band, or they grind them in a way that fights the game's mechanics instead of using them. Agility rewards course chaining and Marks of Grace. Slayer rewards a disciplined block list and cannon usage. Mining rewards picking Motherlode, Amethyst, or 3-tick manipulation at exactly the right moment. Get those three decisions right and the "slow" skills become puzzles you can solve in a fraction of the time most players spend.

⚡ Quick Verdict — The 30-Second Read

  • 📌 Agility is fastest when you ladder rooftop courses by band and buy Graceful at 60 — do not waste time at the Brimhaven Arena.
  • 📌 Slayer is only "slow" with a bad task list. Block the weak tasks, cannon everything you can, and extend the high-XP ones.
  • 📌 Mining is near-AFK via Motherlode Mine to 99; Amethyst (92+) and 3-tick manipulation are the speed options.
  • 📌 The single biggest lever is combat 70+ — below that, efficient Slayer is locked and your whole speedrun stalls.
  • 📌 RuneLite plugins (Agility, Slayer, Motherlode, Tick bar) are mandatory, not optional, for a real speedrun.

1. Why These Three Feel Slow (and the Mindset Fix)

The reason Agility, Slayer, and Mining feel like a slog is not their XP curve — it is the lack of a single obvious fast method at low levels, combined with how punishing it is to pick the wrong route. Combat skills have Sand Crabs and NMZ. Fishing has fly fishing. But these three punish impatience in a way that is invisible until you have already wasted hours:

  • Agility has a brutal 1–30 band where the only safe course is Gnome Stronghold, and even that is click-heavy with slow XP. Players who stay on Gnome too long, or who dabble at the Brimhaven Arena, lose the most time here.
  • Slayer is gated by which master you use and which tasks you block. A bad task list — or no block list at all — can double your time to a target level and leave you broke instead of profitable.
  • Mining below level 30 is just iron ore, which is fine for GP but slow for XP unless you know the Motherlode trick. Players who mine iron by hand for 90 hours are doing it the hard way on purpose.

The speedrun mindset is simple: treat each skill as a logistics problem, not a grind. You are optimizing routes, resets, and reset RNG. When you frame it that way, the "slow" skills become puzzles you can solve in a fraction of the time most players spend. The same logic applies if you later pick up Sailing — every skill has a fastest path if you look for it, and the Sailing guide lays out exactly where that path is.

The real cost of inefficiency

To make the point concrete, consider what "10% slower" actually means across a speedrun. Agility to 99 is roughly 110 focused hours for an average player; a 10% drag is eleven extra hours. Slayer to 95 is similar. Mining via hand-iron versus Motherlode is not 10% — it can be 40–60% slower. So the methods you pick in Sections 2–4 are not marginal tweaks; they are the difference between a two-week push and a two-month slog. That is why this guide leads with routing decisions instead of generic "just do it" advice.

💡 The 10% Rule

If a method feels 10% slower than the wiki's "fastest" column, you are probably using the wrong route for your level band. Always check the level band, not the skill's overall best method. The overall best method is usually locked behind a level or quest you do not have yet, and forcing it early just wastes time.

One more mindset point before the methods: these three skills reward consistency over intensity. A modest daily Agility session across two weeks outperforms one heroic 8-hour lap marathon that you never repeat, because the XP does not decay but your motivation does. Build the speedrun around a routine you can actually keep, then let muscle memory do the rest.

2. Agility Speedrun — Rooftops, Graceful & Boosts

Agility is the classic "slow skill" because most of its XP comes from repeating rooftop courses. The trick is to chain the correct course for each band and stack every boost available. There is no AFK path to 99 Agility — it is active clicking the whole way — which is exactly why routing discipline matters more here than in any other skill on this list.

Optimal course ladder

The rooftop courses are ordered by when they unlock, and each one is meaningfully faster than the last. The single biggest mistake is overstaying on a low course because you do not want to walk to the next one. Walk. The XP difference is worth it.

Level Band Course Approx XP/Hr Why
1–10Gnome Stronghold~8,000Only safe low-level option; use the sneaky tree shortcut
10–30Draynor Village Rooftop~12,000First rooftop with Marks of Grace; short laps
30–60Al Kharid Rooftop~18,000Best XP/hour for the 30s; good Mark rate
60–70Seers' Village Rooftop~30,000Unlocks the coal truck shortcut; steady XP
70–90Pollnivneach Rooftop~45,000High XP/hour; can be camel-herded for near-AFK
90–99Prifddinas / Ardougne (after unlock)~60,000+Highest XP/hour in the game

Get Graceful the moment you can

At 60+ Agility you can collect enough Marks of Grace to buy the full Graceful set (~260 marks). Graceful gives +30% run energy recovery and reduces your weight to zero, which means you almost never walk. This does not just help Agility — it speeds up every other skill because you spend less time walking between spots. Prioritize finishing the set before pushing past 70, and remember that rooftop Marks are how you fund it. If you are short on marks, the Al Kharid and Pollnivneach courses have the best Mark rates for their bands.

Boosts that matter

  • Summer pie (+5 Agility) lets you enter a course 5 levels early, so you can start Pollnivneach at 70 instead of 75 and Prifddinas/Ardougne as soon as the requirement is almost met.
  • Agility cape perk (from a skiller alt or once you hit 99) gives a 1-in-6 chance to double a lap's XP. On a maxed account this is a free ~17% XP boost.
  • Agility mix / other temporary boosts exist but are rarely worth the inventory space compared to summer pies.
⚠️ Don't Train Agility at the Brimhaven Arena

The Agility arena is a fun distraction but it is dramatically slower than rooftop courses for XP. Only use it for the ticket rewards, never as your primary training method. Players who "take a break" at the arena routinely lose more progress than they gain.

When to actually push to 99

Agility is the skill on this list where 99 is least urgent for account progression — the cape perk is nice but not account-defining. The speedrun philosophy is to get to ~85 (Prifddinas/Ardougne unlocked, good daily marks) during focused bursts, then finish the last 14 levels in dead time. For the full method depth, the Agility training guide covers every course, shortcut, and XP rate in detail.

3. Slayer Speedrun — Block Lists & Point Efficiency

Slayer is "slow" only when your task list is bad. A well-tuned block list turns Slayer into one of the fastest combat-XP methods in the game while also making you money. The goal is simple: never get stuck on a task you hate or that is weak to the wrong style. Because you also train Hitpoints, Strength, Attack, Defence, Ranged, or Magic while doing it, Slayer is the most "multi-tasking" skill here — which is why it belongs in every speedrun.

Pick the right master

Your master decides your task pool and your points-per-hour. Below level 50 Combat, use Turael for easy tasks or skip Slayer entirely until your stats climb. From ~70 Combat up, switch to Konar (task location assignments, good points, and a chance at Konar-only drops) or Nieve/Steve (highest-level tasks, best points). Duradel is the end-game master for the best points-per-hour once your stats are high and you can handle his task list. Krystilia (Wilderness) is only worth it if you want risk and the unique loot, which is the opposite of a clean speedrun.

Combat Level Master Why
<50TuraelEasy tasks; build the habit, not the XP
70–85Konar / NieveGood points; Konar adds location variety
85–99Nieve / DuradelBest task pool and points-per-hour
MaxedDuradelHighest-level tasks, fastest point gain

Block list strategy

You get a limited number of blocks (up to 7 with the quest unlocks and a high Slayer level). Block tasks that are slow, low-XP, or travel-heavy, and prefer tasks that are cannon-friendly or have good drops. Our Slayer block & skip list is the full, current recommended setup — but the speedrun core is:

  • Block: Cave crawlers (until you need the herb), Dagannoth (slow), Lizards/Suqah (travel-heavy), Spirits (annoying), and Wyrms if you hate them.
  • Prefer (via Slayer reward points): tasks with good cannon spots — Gargoyles, Nechryael, Dust Devils, and Abyssal Demons.
  • Skip, don't block, the short ones you can tolerate occasionally — skipping is cheaper than blocking when the task is only mildly annoying.

Spend points wisely

Slayer reward points are the engine of the speedrun. The priority order is: buy the Slayer Helmet (big damage boost) first, then Bigger and Badder (chance at superior creatures with rare drops), then extend high-value tasks like Dust Devils and Nechryael. Extending a good task is the single highest-XP/hour lever in Slayer because it multiplies the time you spend on the tasks you actually want. For the exact points math, the Slayer points fast guide breaks down how to accumulate them without wasting tasks.

💡 Cannon Everything You Can

Where a task allows a dwarf multicannon (most tasks do), use it. A cannon triples your kill speed on clustered monsters and is the difference between a 4-hour task and a 90-minute one. The cannonballs are a tiny fraction of the drops you collect, so the GP math almost always favors bringing one.

Why Slayer pays you to train it

Unlike Agility and Mining, Slayer is rarely a net GP sink during a speedrun. Superior creatures from Bigger and Badder drop valuable loot, and the task monsters themselves drop alchable gear and seeds. A blocked, cannoned, extended Slayer setup commonly earns 300K–500K GP/hr on top of the combat XP — which means Slayer can fund the rest of your account while it trains you. That dual return is why it sits at the center of this speedrun rather than at the edge.

4. Mining Speedrun — Motherlode, Amethyst & Tick Manipulation

Mining's reputation for being slow comes from players who mine iron by hand for 90 hours. The speedrun version uses two methods that are both faster and more relaxed, plus an optional third for players who want the absolute ceiling.

Motherlode Mine (30–99)

From level 30, the Motherlode Mine in the Falador Dwarven Mine is the AFK king. You mine ore veins, pay nuggets to wash them, and collect the cleaned ore from the sack. It is slower in raw XP than 3-tick granite at high levels, but it is banked automatically, safe, and almost fully AFK. Most intermediate players should just do MLM to 99 and not think about it — you can watch a show, do other account tasks between rock refills, and still gain steady XP. The Prospector kit from the MLM reward shop adds +2.5% XP and pays for itself quickly.

Amethyst (92–99, or earlier with boost)

At level 92 (or 87 with a +5 boost from a spicy stew / mining potion trick), Amethyst in the Mining Guild is the fastest conventional Mining XP. Each amethyst takes a while to mine but grants huge XP, and the amethyst itself is valuable, so this method is also the most profitable. Combine it with the Varrock armour 4 (from the medium diary — see the achievement diary guide) for a chance at double ore, and the Mining cape for an additional double-ore chance.

Tick manipulation for the sweaty

If you truly want the fastest possible route, 3-tick mining using a hammer/knife swap with a stone or gem rock lets you mine granite (50+) or amethyst at roughly triple the speed of MLM. It requires a RuneLite tick timer and practiced muscle memory, but it is the only method that rivals MLM's convenience while being much faster. Expect a steep learning curve, and accept that it is mentally demanding — this is the "speedrun flex," not the recommended default. The Mining 1–99 guide has the full tick-manipulation walkthrough if you want to learn it properly.

📋 Mining Method Picker

Want AFK: Motherlode Mine 30–99.
Want fast + profit: Amethyst 92–99.
Want absolute fastest: 3-tick granite/amethyst with tick manipulation.

5. Method Comparison — Time, Effort & Profit

Before you commit the speedrun to a schedule, it helps to see the three skills side by side. The numbers below are illustrative ranges for an average account following the routes in this guide — your exact result depends on stats, gear, and how cleanly you execute. The point is the direction of each trade-off: which skill is hardest to AFK, which pays you back, and which is the safest to do "in the background."

Skill Hours to 99 (avg) AFK Possible? GP/Hr While Training Speedrun Priority
Agility ~110h No — active clicking None (costs nothing, earns nothing) Focused bursts; finish last 14 levels in dead time
Slayer ~120h to 95 Partly (cannon spots) +300K–500K Core — trains combat and pays you
Mining (MLM) ~130h Yes — near AFK ~50K–100K (ore) Background grind; pair with other tasks
Mining (3-tick) ~70h No — full attention ~150K+ (granite/amethyst) Optional ceiling for sweaty players

The pattern is the whole strategy: do Agility in short focused sessions because it cannot be AFKed and earns nothing; do Slayer as the spine because it multitasks your combat stats and banks GP; and do Mining (MLM) in the background because it is near-AFK and still pays a little. Stack those three correctly and the "slow skills" stop being a wall — they become the most efficiently spent hours on your account.

6. Gear, Inventory & RuneLite Setups That Save Hours

The difference between an average speedrun and a great one is the setup. These small things compound across hundreds of laps and tasks, and most of them are free. The theme is: remove every avoidable second of running, banking, and menu searching.

Agility

  • Full Graceful (obviously) — zero weight means you almost never walk.
  • RuneLite Agility plugin — shows lap counts, course marks, and the next-obstacle highlight so you never misclick a lap.
  • Summer pies in the bank for early unlocks and the +5 boost.
  • A ring of endurance (charged) extends run energy even further once you have the diary for it.

Slayer

  • Slayer helmet (i), a cannon + cannonballs, and a dwarf multi-cannon.
  • Best melee or ranged gear you can afford; bring a Salve amulet (ei) for undead tasks and a black mask (i) synergy with the Slayer helmet.
  • RuneLite Slayer plugin — task highlighting, target minimap markers, and a loot tracker so you never miss a drop.
  • A teleport tab set matched to your block list's common task locations (Slayer ring, fairy rings, or a house teleport with portals).

Mining

  • Prospector kit (from MLM reward shop) for +2.5% XP — buy it before you start MLM in earnest.
  • Varrock armour 4, Mining cape for a chance at double ore at Amethyst.
  • RuneLite Motherlode Mine plugin and the Tick bar for 3-tick methods.
  • A gem bag and coal bag once unlocked, to reduce banking trips at MLM.
💡 One Inventory Trick for All Three

Keep a teleport tab set (House, Xeric's, or Skills necklace) and a run-restoring food (like strawberries) in every setup. Saving 20 seconds of running per reset adds up to hours over a speedrun. The reset is where most players silently waste time, and it is the easiest to fix.

If you are below 70 combat, spend the first few days on the combat foundation before starting — forcing Slayer early just wastes the speedrun.

  5. Finish Agility and Mining in dead time; push Slayer toward 95 for unlocks.

8. Frequently Asked Questions

Which of the three is actually the slowest to 99?

Agility is usually the slowest in wall-clock time for an average player because it has no AFK path and requires active clicking the whole way. Mining can be near-AFK via Motherlode Mine, and Slayer trains your combat stats at the same time, so it "multi-tasks." If you only have limited play time, do Agility in focused bursts and Slayer/Mining while doing other things. The hours-to-99 chart in Section 5 shows the rough gap.

Is 3-tick Mining worth learning?

Only if you care about saving ~30–40% of the time on Mining and you enjoy tick-perfect gameplay. For most intermediate players, Motherlode Mine to 99 is perfectly fine and far less stressful. Learn 3-ticking only after you have already done the other two skills and want a personal best, or if you are an ironman who needs the resources anyway.

Do I need a high Combat level before starting efficient Slayer?

Yes, meaningfully. Below 70 Combat you are limited to weak masters and weak tasks, which kills XP rates and removes the profit that makes Slayer worth doing. Get your melee stats to ~70/70/70 and Ranged or Magic to 70+ first, then Slayer becomes both fast and profitable. The complete skill guide covers that combat foundation in order.

Can I speedrun these on a free-to-play account?

Partially. F2P has Agility courses (Gnome, Wilderness), F2P Slayer (Turael/Krystilia with limits), and F2P Mining (copper/tin/iron/coal). But the fastest methods — Graceful, MLM, Amethyst, cannon Slayer — are members-only. Expect F2P to take roughly 2–3x longer, and the Slayer profit mostly disappears without members' tasks and cannons.

Why is Graceful such a big deal for a speedrun?

Graceful removes your weight and gives +30% run energy recovery, so you almost never walk between spots. Because Agility, Slayer, and Mining all involve running between courses, tasks, and banks, Graceful quietly speeds up every one of them at once. Buying it the moment you hit 60 Agility is the highest-leverage early purchase in the entire speedrun.

Should I block or skip a task I dislike?

Block the tasks you never want to see (travel-heavy or genuinely slow ones like Lizards/Suqah or Cave crawlers once you don't need the herb). Skip the merely annoying short tasks instead, because skipping is cheaper in points and keeps your block slots free for the worst offenders. The block & skip list has the exact recommended set.

How important are RuneLite plugins to the speedrun?

They are mandatory, not optional, for a real speedrun. The Agility plugin prevents misclicked laps, the Slayer plugin tracks tasks and loot, the Motherlode plugin streamlines banking, and the Tick bar makes 3-tick Mining possible at all. None of them are "cheating" — they are quality-of-life tools that remove avoidable seconds, and those seconds compound into hours across a speedrun.

Is Agility worth taking to 99 during the speedrun?

Not urgently. The Agility cape perk (1-in-6 double lap XP) is a nice boost but not account-defining, and Agility earns no GP while training. The speedrun approach is to reach ~85 (Prifddinas/Ardougne unlocked) in focused bursts, then finish the last levels in dead time. Spend your prime play hours on Slayer and Mining, which pay you back.

What if I only have an hour a day — can this still work?

Yes, but rebalance toward background skills. An hour a day is perfect for Motherlode Mine (near-AFK) and a Slayer task or two (which trains combat and banks GP). Save Agility for days when you can focus, since it demands active clicking. Consistency beats intensity: a modest daily session across two weeks outperforms one heroic marathon you never repeat.

9. Final Tips — The Speedrun Cheat Sheet

  • Agility: Ladder the courses by band, buy Graceful at 60, use Summer pies to skip ahead, and skip the Brimhaven Arena.
  • Slayer: Block the tasks you hate, prefer cannon-friendly tasks, extend the good ones, and rush the Slayer Helmet.
  • Mining: MLM 30–99 for AFK, Amethyst 92+ for fast and profitable, 3-tick only if you love tick manipulation.
  • Universal: Teleport tabs + run food in every inventory; RuneLite plugins are mandatory, not optional.
  • Mindset: These are logistics problems. Optimize the route, then let muscle memory do the rest.
  • Priority: Combat 70 first, then Graceful, then Slayer as the spine, then Mining in the background.
  • Depth: For every method above, the Agility, Slayer, and Mining full guides have the complete XP tables.
