1. What the Ground Items Plugin Does

The Ground Items plugin is one of the most useful and most installed plugins in the entire RuneLite client. In the default Old School RuneScape game view, items dropped on the floor are shown only as small, identical sprites. When you kill a monster or open a chest, the loot appears as tiny icons that all look alike until you walk close and hover each one. This is slow, confusing, and it causes real mistakes. Every experienced player has, at some point, walked away from a pile of loot and later realized they left a rune item or a clue scroll behind. The Ground Items plugin fixes this problem completely.

When the plugin is enabled, it draws the name and value of every ground item directly on the game screen, positioned right on top of the item sprite. You no longer need to hover. You can see at a glance that the grey pile in front of you is worth 3 coins while the green text a tile away is a 25,000 coin drop. The plugin pulls price data from the Grand Exchange and the RuneLite price cache, so the numbers you see are the real tradeable value of the item, not a guess.

This single change turns a messy floor into a readable loot map. For a brand new player, that readability is the difference between a profitable hour of Slayer and an hour where you quietly lose thousands of coins because you ignored items you could not identify. The plugin does not change game mechanics. It does not give you an unfair advantage in the sense of extra stats or faster attacks. It simply presents information the game already has in a way your eyes can actually use. That is why it is allowed, trusted, and recommended for every account from fresh level 3 to maxed ironman.

1.1 Why Beginners Need It Most

New players often assume they will remember to check every drop, but reality is different. During your first few Slayer tasks or boss attempts, your attention is split between prayer flicking, eating food, moving out of attacks, and watching your run energy. A tiny unidentified bone or seed is easy to miss. The Ground Items plugin removes that mental load by making value visible. You stop asking "should I pick that up?" and start reacting to color instead. Bright text means grab it. Dim text means ignore it. That simple rule scales from your first cave goblin to your first Corporeal Beast trip.

1.2 What You Need Before Starting

You need the RuneLite client installed and logged in. RuneLite is a free, open-source, third-party client approved by Jagex for use with Old School RuneScape. You do not need a membership to use most of its features, and the Ground Items plugin works on free-to-play worlds as well as members worlds. You also need a stable internet connection the first time you launch, because the plugin downloads the current price list and icon cache from RuneLite servers. After that, prices update quietly in the background while you play.

🎯 How To Enable Ground Items In 4 Steps

  • Open the RuneLite client and log into your account as normal.
  • Click the wrench (Configuration) icon at the top-right of the RuneLite sidebar.
  • Type "Ground Items" into the search box and click the plugin name to open its settings.
  • Make sure the toggle at the top is switched on, then close the panel and return to the game.
💡 First-Run Tip

If item names do not appear immediately, walk to a fresh drop or reload the area by teleporting. The plugin draws names when items enter your view, so existing items already on the floor may need a small reposition to trigger the label. Logging out and back in also forces a full refresh.

2. Basic Highlight Settings & Value Tiers

Once the plugin is on, the most important job is setting up value tiers. A value tier is a price threshold that controls how an item is colored and displayed. The idea is simple: you decide that anything worth less than 1,000 coins is junk, anything between 1,000 and 50,000 is "okay," and anything above 50,000 is important. The plugin then paints each drop according to which bucket its price falls into. This is the foundation of a clean loot screen.

RuneLite Ground Items comes with sensible default tiers, but every account plays differently, so you should adjust them. A main account with millions in the bank may not care about 5,000 coin drops, while a brand new ironman values every nature rune. Set your tiers to match your current wealth and goals. The panel where you edit tiers is labeled Highlighted Items / Value tiers, and each tier has three controls: a price floor, a text color, and sometimes a border or background option.

2.1 Recommended Starter Tiers

For a beginner, a three-tier system is enough and stays easy to read. The exact numbers below are a good starting point; tweak them after your first few trips. The key is contrast. You want cheap junk to nearly disappear and valuable loot to shout at you.

🎯 A Clean Three-Tier Setup

  • Tier 1 (Junk): 0 to 1,000 coins — dim grey text, no highlight. You will mostly ignore these.
  • Tier 2 (Worth grabbing): 1,000 to 25,000 coins — white or light blue text so you notice it during a trip.
  • Tier 3 (Important): above 25,000 coins — bright green text with a colored outline so it is impossible to miss.

As your bank grows, push Tier 3 higher. Many mid-game players move the important threshold to 100,000 coins because picking up every 25,000 drop between boss kills slows them down. There is no single correct number. The correct number is the one that matches what you would actually walk over to collect. If a drop is below your effort threshold, let it stay dim.

2.2 Item Value Display Options

Inside the settings you will find options that change how the price itself is shown. The most useful ones are Show item value, which prints the GE price next to the name, and Show GE price versus Show HA price. GE price is the street trade value, while High Alchemy price is what you get if you alch the item. For most money-making and looting decisions, GE price is the number you care about, because that is what you would receive if you sold it. Some players prefer HA price when they plan to alch drops on the spot.

💡 Keep The Floor Readable

Turn on Hide under 1k or set your lowest tier to a very dim color. A screen full of bright names for bones, ashes, and empty vials is just as bad as no names at all. The goal is signal, not noise. Less bright text means your eyes find the real loot faster.

When something gold or red appears, you stop what you are doing and make sure it is safe. This single habit prevents the most painful losses new players experience.

3. Custom Colors & Specific Item Rules

Value tiers are great for bulk decisions, but some items matter for reasons other than price. A clue scroll might only be worth a few coins if dropped, but it can lead to millions in rewards. A zulrah scale or a bird nest may be cheap individually yet important to your bank. The Ground Items plugin lets you create specific item rules that override the tiers for chosen items. This is where the plugin becomes truly personal.

To add a specific rule, open the plugin settings and look for Highlighted items / Menu / Add or the "Items" list depending on your RuneLite version. You type the exact item name, choose a color, and optionally a border or background. From that moment, every time that item hits the floor, it uses your custom style no matter its price. This is perfect for items you never want to miss even if they look cheap.

3.1 Items Worth A Permanent Rule

Think about what you personally care about. Below is a starter list that works for almost every account. Add these as bright, distinct colors so they stand out from your value tiers.

🎯 Starter "Never Miss" Rules

  • Clue scroll (any tier) — bright yellow, because clues are free money and easy to overlook.
  • Ring of wealth / looting bag — orange, so you notice if they drop on death or slip from inventory.
  • Barrows equipment pieces — cyan, because even one piece is worth real GP.
  • Herb seeds and tree seeds — light green, useful for farming and often dropped in batches.
  • Rune items (rune scim, rune kite) — red outline, classic valuable drops from mid-level monsters.

You can also color by border and background instead of just text. A colored border is less intrusive than a full background and works well in busy areas like the Grand Exchange or a popular Slayer cave. A background fill is best reserved for the single most important items, because too many filled boxes turn the floor into a candy store of colors and defeat the purpose.

3.2 Naming And Matching

The plugin matches item names exactly, so spelling matters. "Clue scroll (easy)" is different from "Clue scroll (medium)." If your rule does not seem to work, check the spelling against the in-game drop name. You can also use the plugin's right-click menu on a ground item directly: hover the item, right-click it in the RuneLite overlay, and choose Highlight to add a rule for that exact item without typing. This is the fastest way to build a custom list during real play.

💡 Color Blind Friendly

If you have trouble telling red from green, use shape cues as well as color. Give important items a thick border and less important ones plain text. Border thickness carries meaning even when hue is hard to read, so your loot map stays useful regardless of color vision.

4. Filter Junk & Show Only Valuables

The opposite of highlighting is hiding. Sometimes you do not want to see junk at all. The Ground Items plugin can filter out low-value items so they never get a name on your screen. This is the single best setting for keeping fast content clean. When you are killing demons or running Slayer tasks, the floor fills with bones, ashes, and low herbs. If every one of those gets a label, your screen becomes noisy and you start missing the good stuff. Filtering solves that.

The main control is Hide below value (sometimes called "Only show loot above value" depending on version). Set it to a number, and any drop worth less than that threshold simply will not be labeled. A common beginner setting is to hide everything under 1,000 coins. That removes bones, bat bones, ashes, and most junk from view while keeping everything you would actually bank. You can raise this number during intensive money-making so only the big drops show.

4.1 The "Valuables Only" Mode

For pure profit trips, many players switch to a valuables-only mindset: set the hide threshold high, keep your custom rules for clue scrolls and seeds, and let the plugin show only what matters. This is especially strong when you are using a looting bag at the Wilderness or doing long Slayer tasks where inventory space is tight. You see exactly the items worth your bag slot, and the rest fades away.

🎯 Switch To Clean Mode In Seconds

  • Open Ground Items settings and find Hide below value.
  • Set it to 5,000 (or higher for rich accounts) so only real loot is named.
  • Keep your specific rules for clue scrolls and seeds so they still appear.
  • Close the panel; the floor is now quiet except for worthwhile drops.

Remember that hiding an item does not delete it from the game. The sprite is still there; it just has no text. If you later want to grab junk for a reason (such as bones for Prayer), you can lower the threshold or turn the filter off. Filtering is a display choice, not a permanent loss, which makes it safe to experiment with.

4.2 Combine Hide With Tiers

The strongest setup uses both tools together. Hide everything under your junk threshold, then use tiers and custom rules for everything above it. The result is a floor where onlyvaluable drops are visible, and among those visible drops, color tells you how urgently to act. This combination is what lets experienced players loot at speed without thinking. They are not reading prices; they are reading color and presence.

⚠️ Do Not Hide Too Much

If you hide everything under 50,000 coins, you may miss seeds, herbs, and task-only drops that add up over a session. Start conservative at 1,000 to 5,000, then raise it only after you confirm you are not losing meaningful income. Let your hourly profit, not your impatience, set the number.

5. PvM & Slayer Scenarios

Different activities need different Ground Items setups. The plugin is flexible enough to serve a calm Slayer task and a chaotic boss kill with the same core logic, but the thresholds should shift with the content. Below are practical setups you can copy, then fine-tune. The guiding principle is always the same: match the floor to how you actually loot in that situation.

5.1 Slayer Tasks

Slayer is where most beginners first feel the value of this plugin. You kill hundreds of similar monsters, and the floor fills with a mix of junk and worthwhile drops. A good Slayer setup hides the obvious junk (bones, ashes, low herbs) but keeps herbs, seeds, and runes visible. Set your hide threshold around 1,000 to 2,000 coins, use light blue for the mid tier, and keep bright green for anything above 25,000. Add custom rules for unsired, ancient shards, and clue scrolls if your task can drop them.

Because Slayer tasks often run long, also turn on show item quantity so you can see how many of an item are stacked. Seeing "(28)" next to a herb tells you at a glance whether a pile is worth banking. This small detail saves dozens of wasted clicks per task.

5.2 Bossing And PvM

During boss fights the screen is busier and your attention is split. Here you want fewer, louder signals. Raise your hide threshold so only meaningful loot is named, and give your top tier a bold border. For raids or multi-drop bosses, a gold background for rare drops is worth the visual space because missing one is expensive. If you are learning a boss from our beginner combat training guide, keep the loot rules simple so they do not distract from learning the mechanics.

🎯 Boss Loot Preset

  • Hide below value: 10,000 coins to cut the noise during fast kills.
  • Important tier (green border): 25,000 to 250,000 coins.
  • Massive tier (gold fill): anything above 250,000 or a specific rare drop.
  • Custom rule: every unique boss drop in bright cyan so you never walk past it.

5.3 Money Making Runs

If your goal is pure GP, pair this plugin with a solid method from our beginner money making guide. For activities like collecting runes, picking herbs, or killing creatures for drops, set the hide threshold to match your lowest acceptable per-item value and let tiers do the rest. The plugin turns "is this worth it?" into a color check, which keeps your runs efficient and your hourly rate honest.

6. Share Your Config With Friends

One of the most underused features of RuneLite is that your plugin settings can be exported and shared as text. The Ground Items plugin, like many others, stores its configuration in a plain format that you can copy, paste into a message, and import on another account. This means your whole clan, your Ironman group, or your friend can run the exact same clean loot screen without anyone retyping dozens of rules.

6.1 How To Export

Open the RuneLite settings, find the Ground Items plugin, and look for the Export or Config button depending on your version. RuneLite will produce a block of text containing your tiers, colors, and item rules. Copy that block. You can paste it into Discord, a forum post, or a private message. The text is small, usually only a few lines, because it stores only your choices, not the whole game.

6.2 How To Import

On the receiving account, open the same plugin settings and choose Import or Load, then paste the text block and confirm. RuneLite applies every tier and rule instantly. If a friend shares a well-tuned setup, you inherit their experience in seconds. This is also how many content creators distribute their "recommended" loot profiles to viewers.

🎯 Share In 3 Steps

  • On your account: open Ground Items settings and click Export, then copy the text.
  • Send the text to your friend through any chat app or paste it into a shared note.
  • On their account: open Ground Items settings, click Import, paste, and confirm.

6.3 Why Sharing Matters For Groups

When a group raids or Slayers together, consistent loot labels reduce confusion. Everyone sees the same green for "grab it" and the same gold for "rare." That shared language prevents the awkward moment where one player ignores a drop the other assumed they would take. It also helps new players learn good habits by copying a veteran's tuned setup instead of starting from a blank panel. If you built your config using the tiers in this guide, export it and pass it to a friend who is still looting blind.

💡 Back Up Your Own Config

Keep a copy of your exported config in a notes app. If you reinstall RuneLite, switch PCs, or accidentally reset plugins, you can restore your perfect loot screen in under a minute instead of rebuilding it from memory. Treat the export like a save file for your eyes.

7. Ground Items FAQs

Is the Ground Items plugin allowed by Jagex?

Yes. Ground Items is a standard RuneLite plugin and RuneLite itself is an approved third-party client for Old School RuneScape. The plugin only displays information the game already has, such as item names and Grand Exchange values. It does not automate clicks, modify game data, or give statistical combat advantages. Using it is safe and widely accepted across the community from new players to top-ranked ironmen.

Why are item names not showing on my screen?

The most common cause is that the plugin is enabled but the item is below your hide threshold, so it is intentionally not labeled. Check the Hide below value setting and lower it. If names still do not appear, make sure the plugin toggle is on, that you are logged in, and that RuneLite finished downloading the price cache on first launch. Walking to a new drop or relogging usually forces the labels to render.

Should I use Grand Exchange price or High Alchemy price?

For almost all looting and money-making decisions, use Grand Exchange price because it reflects what you would actually receive if you sold the item on the street. Choose High Alchemy price only if your plan is to alch drops on the spot rather than bank and sell them. Mixing the two can make cheap alchables look more valuable than they are, so pick one and stay consistent.

Can I have different settings for Slayer and bossing?

RuneLite does not auto-switch Ground Items profiles by activity, but you can quickly change the hide threshold and tiers between sessions, or keep a moderate universal setup. Many players keep one solid all-round config and only raise the hide threshold for intensive bossing. You can also export a Slayer profile and a bossing profile as two text blocks and import the right one before each trip.

Will filtering junk make me lose items?

No. Hiding an item only removes its text label; the item sprite is still on the floor and can still be picked up. Filtering is a display preference, not a deletion. If you later need junk, such as bones for Prayer or vials for decanting, simply lower the hide threshold or turn the filter off. The risk is only that you forget the item is there because you cannot see its name, so review your threshold after each session.

How do I stop the screen from being too colorful?

Reduce the number of custom item rules and use borders instead of background fills for most items. Reserve bright background fills for only one or two truly important drops. Also keep your lowest value tier dim grey and consider hiding items under a small threshold so cheap drops never get bright text. A clean loot map uses few, high-contrast signals rather than many competing colors.
