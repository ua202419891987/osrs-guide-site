OSRS Doom of Mokhaiotl Delve Tiers Guide 2026 — When to Stop & Risk/Reward

Doom of Mokhaiotl is a delve boss: you sink one tier deeper every time you win, and every floor multiplies both your reward and your risk. This guide breaks down how the delve system actually works, how HP and the danger scale per tier, the exact drop-rate curve, and the mathematically sane place to stop and claim before a death resets you to one.

1. 🔢 How the Delve System Works

Doom of Mokhaiotl is the first true delve boss in Old School RuneScape. Unlike a raid where you pick a difficulty up front, or a fixed boss where every kill is identical, Doom asks one question after every victory: go deeper, or bank what you have? The entire risk/reward puzzle of the fight lives in that single decision, and understanding the loop is the foundation for everything else in this guide.

The core loop: defeat → descend

When you enter the Ruins of Mokhaiotl you start at delve 1. You fight the boss, and if you defeat it you are given the choice to sink one tier deeper. Each successful kill increments your delve counter by one. There is no separate "difficulty selector" — your current delve is the difficulty. The deeper you are, the more dangerous the next floor becomes, but the richer its rewards.

Claim, continue, or reset

After a win you face three practical options, and only one of them is safe:

  • Continue deeper. You push onward without banking. Your unclaimed loot stays on the table and your delve counter climbs. This is where the money is — and where the risk lives.
  • Claim your loot. You bank everything you have accumulated so far and end the run. The run is "closed" and your delve resets to 1 on your next entry.
  • Die or teleport out. Both of these reset you to delve 1 immediately, and — crucially — any loot you had not yet claimed is lost. Teleporting is not a safe exit; it is a forfeit.

This is the single most important rule in the whole fight: claiming, dying, or teleporting all reset your delve to 1. Only by continuing do you stack depth, and only by claiming do you secure the stack. The gamble is whether one more floor is worth more than everything you already hold.

Uniques unlock by delve, not by luck floor

Unlike bosses where every unique can drop at any kill, Doom gates its headline loot behind delve thresholds. The uniques are unlocked per tier, which means a low delve simply cannot hand you the best items no matter how lucky you are:

  • Mokhaiotl cloth — unlocks at delve 2 (used to convert a tormented bracelet into confliction gauntlets)
  • Eye of Ayak — unlocks at delve 3 (the demonbane-powered staff)
  • Avernic treads — unlocks at delve 4 (the hybrid boots)
  • Dom pet — unlocks at delve 6 and above (the rare pet drop)

Because of this gating, the "best" drops are invisible until you reach the relevant depth. That is the engine behind the optimal-stop math later in this guide: you are not just chasing a higher number, you are chasing the unlock that makes deeper delves worth the climb. For the full item-by-item breakdown of values and uses, see our Doom of Mokhaiotl rewards & drops guide.

⚠️ The one sentence to remember

Every dive is a bet that the next floor pays more than the floor you already cleared. The house always wins the moment you die with loot unclaimed — so the skill is knowing when the bet stops being worth it.

2. 📈 HP & Difficulty Scaling

The most common misconception about Doom is that deeper delves make the boss hit harder. They do not. The scaling model is deliberately narrow, and understanding exactly what changes — and what does not — tells you where your preparation should go.

HP: 525 at the entrance, 625 in the depths

Doom's health pool grows as you descend. At delve 1 the boss has 525 HP, and by the deepest practical delves that pool rises to 625 HP. The growth is steady rather than explosive, which means a deeper Doom is longer to kill, not suddenly unkillable.

Delve bandBoss HPWhat it means for you
1 – 4 (base)~525The shortest fights; you learn the core rotation here.
5 – 7 (mid)Rising toward 600Longer burns; extra mechanics start layering in.
8 – deepUp to 625The longest fights; every added phase compounds the time pressure.

Because the HP ceiling is only about 100 above the floor, raw DPS checks are never the wall. A player who can clear delve 1 can, in principle, out-damage delve 9 — the reason deeper delves fail people is the mechanics, not the health bar.

Stats stay the same — the danger persists

Doom's attack accuracy, max hit, and defence do not scale with delve. What changes is which mechanics are active and how many of them are stacked at once. A delve 1 prayer-flip is the same prayer-flip at delve 9; the difference is that at delve 9 it arrives alongside a burrowed phase, larvae, acid blood, and a shielded phase you cannot out-damage with normal weapons.

This is why our Doom gear & inventory guide stresses a demonbane weapon as mandatory rather than optional: the deep-delve shielded phase can only be true-damaged by demonbane, so the scaling that actually kills you is mechanical gating, not a bigger health bar. Plan your setup around mechanics, not around DPS.

💡 Where to spend your effort

Since stats never change, do not over-invest in max DPS expecting deep delves to demand it. Invest in the utility that survives mechanics: demonbane damage, prayer sustain, and the mobility to handle unpredictable movement.

3. 🗺️ Mechanics Added per Delve

This is the heart of the delve system. Doom does not get faster or harder-hitting as you descend — it gets busier. Each band of delves introduces a new mechanic, and the deeper you go the more of them run at once. Below is the layering model so you know exactly what you are walking into at each depth.

Delves 1–4: the base fight

The first four delves teach the foundation. You will see the core rotation only:

  • Orb prayer flips — blue orbs indicate magic, green orbs indicate ranged; you must swap Protect from Magic / Ranged accordingly or eat the hit.
  • Adjacent melee — the boss closes to melee range and threatens a crush blow if you stand still.
  • Rock throw — a rock shatters and disables your protect prayer briefly before launching; you must re-activate it fast.
  • Shockwave — roughly once per minute a shockwave sweeps the arena; a volatile earth node can be used to negate it.
  • Earthen shield — a shield phase where positioning matters.

At this band the uniques begin unlocking (cloth at 2, Eye at 3, treads at 4), so it is also where a careful player starts to feel the reward curve bite.

Delves 5+: burrowed, freeze immunity, larvae

From delve 5 the fight changes character. Three new systems appear:

  • Burrowed phase — Doom burrows and gains 100% freeze immunity plus unpredictable movement. You can no longer rely on freeze to control the boss; you must track its erratic path.
  • Demonic larvae — adds spawn that must be killed before they are absorbed by the boss. If you let them reach it, the boss is healed and empowered.
  • Acid blood — ground damage that punishes standing in the wrong tile during the burrowed phase.

Delve 8+: merged larva and deep-delve phases

At delve 8 the larvae mechanic escalates: the separate larvae merge into a single high-HP larva. This is a meaningful difficulty spike — instead of several small, fast targets you must clear, you face one tanky add that demands focused burst or it heals the boss for a large chunk.

Beyond the merged larva, the deep-delve phases stack the hardest systems of the fight:

  • Shielded phase — only demonbane deals true damage; poison and venom are immune, and thralls are immune. A non-demonbane setup literally cannot finish this phase.
  • Charging phase — the boss charges an attack that must be interrupted with a melee hit, or it lands a devastating blow.
  • Grubs — small healers that restore the boss's health if left alive; they must be prioritized.

The takeaway: by delve 8 you are juggling burrowed movement, a merged larva, acid blood, a shielded demonbane-only phase, a melee-interrupt, and grub healing simultaneously. The HP only went up by ~100; the mechanical load went up by an order of magnitude. That is the real scaling.

⚠️ The delve 8 wall

The merged larva is where many strong teams suddenly wipe. Practice bursting the merged larva down before it reaches the boss — a demonbane switch and focused damage here pays for itself many times over.

4. 💰 Drop Rate Curve

Knowing the reward side of the gamble is just as important as knowing the danger. Doom's loot follows a clear curve: better drops and a higher quantity multiplier the deeper you go, but both improvements cap at delve 9. Past that point you are taking more risk for no extra loot — a hard ceiling that defines the "deep end" of the optimal range.

Unique unlock tiers set the floor

As covered in section 1, the headline uniques are gated by delve. The curve is therefore not smooth luck — it is a staircase:

DelveUnique now possibleNotes
1None of the headline uniquesCommon drops and the base loot table only.
2Mokhaiotl clothFirst meaningful unique becomes available.
3+ Eye of AyakThe valuable staff enters the pool.
4+ Avernic treadsThe highest-value unique unlocks here.
6++ Dom petThe rare pet becomes possible.
9Rate cap reachedDrop rate and quantity multiplier stop improving.

The quantity multiplier and the delve 9 cap

Beyond uniques, Doom uses a quantity multiplier on its common drops. The number of items you receive scales with delve following the formula Qn = Q3 + trunc(Q3 · Mn), where Mn is the delve multiplier. The key fact for planning: Mn caps at delve 9. Delves 10 and beyond give no additional quantity — they only add danger.

DelveMultiplier stateExpected value read
1 – 4Low, risingCommon drops only; uniques begin unlocking.
5 – 8Mid, risingBest balance of unlock completeness and quantity.
9MaximumThe peak of the curve — no deeper delve improves loot.
10+Capped (same as 9)More risk, identical loot ceiling.

The practical lesson is blunt: delve 9 is the loot ceiling, not delve infinity. Anything past 9 is pure vanity or a personal challenge, never an efficiency play. If your goal is profit per hour, you should be claiming well before the point where extra depth buys you nothing.

5. 🎯 Optimal Stop Point

Everything in this guide converges on one question: at what delve should you claim? The answer is not "as deep as possible" — it is "as deep as the rate bonus justifies before the death penalty catches up." That sweet spot is delve 6 through 8.

Why 6–8 and not 9

Delve 9 is the loot ceiling, so you might assume you should always push to 9 before claiming. But two forces push the smart stop lower:

  • The death penalty is binary. Die at delve 9 with loot unclaimed and you lose everything and return to delve 1. The deepest delves are exactly where mechanical load is highest, so your chance of death rises precisely when the stake is largest.
  • The rate bonus flattens. Most of the unique unlocks (cloth, Eye, treads, pet) are already open by delve 6, and the quantity multiplier is near its cap by delve 8. The marginal loot gained from 8 → 9 is tiny compared to the marginal risk.

Expected value, then, peaks in the delve 6–8 band: you have captured essentially all the unlocks and most of the multiplier, while keeping the fight in a range where a single mistake does not automatically cost you a full deep-delve haul.

A simple way to think about it

Treat each delve as a bet. Early delves are "free" learning reps with small stakes. Mid delves (6–8) are where the payout is richest relative to risk. Past 8 the payout stops growing while risk keeps climbing — that is the mathematical signal to stop pushing and start claiming.

Delve bandRiskRewardVerdict
1 – 4LowPartial unlocksLearning / warm-up only.
5 – 6ModerateMost uniques openSolid, safe claim point.
6 – 8Moderate–highNear-max multiplierOptimal claim band.
9HighMax (capped)Only if you are consistent.
10+Very highNo extra lootVanity only — not efficient.
💡 A rule of thumb

If you are not confident you can clear the next delve without panic, claim where you are. A claimed delve 7 haul beats a "could-have-been" delve 9 haul you died at every single time. Discipline > greed.

6. 🧠 Risk Management

Doom is unusually punishing because the cost of failure is not just a gravestone — it is the erasure of an entire run's unclaimed loot. Good risk management is therefore less about survival stats and more about when you bank and how cheap a mistake is.

Claim early, claim often

The single biggest lever you control is the claim button. Because dying or teleporting resets you to delve 1 and forfeits unclaimed loot, the optimal behavior is to claim as soon as you hit your target band (section 5's delve 6–8). Do not "one more dive" your way into a reset. The loot you bank is the only loot that counts.

  • Set a hard rule before you enter: "I claim at delve 7 no matter what." Then follow it.
  • If a delve goes badly — low prayer, bad RNG, a missed interrupt — claim immediately rather than praying the next one is cleaner.
  • Never teleport out thinking it "saves" your run. Teleport = reset to 1 with loot lost.

The 20-delve reclaim discount

Doom shares a key mercy with Yama: when you die, your items are sent to a reclaim point rather than permanently lost, and the reclaim fee is reduced by 75% for your first 20 delves. This dramatically lowers the cost of early mistakes — the first 20 times you die, getting your gear back is cheap.

Use that window wisely. The first 20 deaths are "tuition": cheap to recover from, and the perfect time to learn the burrowed movement, the larva timing, and the shielded-phase demonbane requirement without a brutal gold sink. Once you are past 20 delves, reclaim gets expensive, so by then your consistency should already be high.

Death prevention fundamentals

Because the penalty is total, invest in the basics that stop deaths entirely. Our boss mechanics & death prevention guide covers the universal habits — prayer flicking discipline, inventory layout for instant brews, and panic-rotation drills — that matter most here. Pair that with a sane boss gear checklist so you are never under-prepared for the deep-delve phases.

⚠️ The reclaim trap

The 75% discount lulls people into dying "for free." It is not free — you still lose the run's unclaimed loot. Treat every death, discounted or not, as a full reset of progress, because that is exactly what it is.

7. 🆚 Doom vs Other Endgame

To place Doom in context, it helps to compare it directly with the other marquee endgame bosses players weigh it against: Yama, the Colosseum (Fortis), and Tombs of Amascut (ToA). Each scales difficulty differently, and Doom's delve model is the odd one out.

Doom vs Yama

Yama is the closest cousin. Both send you to a lobby on death rather than a hard gravestone walk, and both offer a reduced reclaim fee in their early encounters — Doom's 75% discount inside 20 delves mirrors Yama's learner-friendly reclaim. The difference is structure: Yama is a fixed, learnable duo fight, while Doom is an open-ended delve ladder where you decide how deep the danger goes. If you liked Yama's "death is a cheap reset" feel, Doom will feel familiar — but Doom adds the unclaimed-loot gamble Yama does not have. See our Yama boss guide for the side-by-side on reclaim and pacing.

Doom vs Fortis (Colosseum)

The Colosseum's Fortis waves are a fixed gauntlet: the difficulty is in the scripted wave order and the no-teleport pressure cooker. There is no "go one wave deeper for better loot" dial — you either clear the full run or you do not. Doom instead lets you opt into more risk for more reward, which makes it more forgiving to learn (you control the ceiling) but more tempting to overreach. Our Colosseum Fortis guide is the better read if you want a bounded, all-or-nothing endgame instead of a scaling one.

Doom vs ToA (invocations)

ToA is the other "choose your difficulty" boss, but it scales via invocations — you set the raid's modifiers before entry, then run a fixed instance. Doom is the inverse: difficulty is emergent from your own decision to descend, decided mid-run rather than pre-set. Both reward pushing higher, but ToA's scaling is transparent up front while Doom's is a live gamble where the next floor's exact load is only partly predictable. For a broader ranking of where Doom sits among all bosses, our boss difficulty tier list places it against the full endgame field.

The throughline: Doom rewards discipline more than raw stats. The boss does not out-level you — you out-greed yourself. Players who internalize the claim rule consistently outperform players with better gear who keep diving one floor too far.

8. 📚 Doom of Mokhaiotl Delve FAQs

Q: How do I go deeper in the Doom delve?

You go deeper by defeating the boss. Each successful kill gives you the option to sink one tier lower; choosing to continue increments your delve counter by one. There is no separate difficulty menu — your current delve is the difficulty.

Q: What resets the delve back to 1?

Three things reset you to delve 1: claiming your loot (which ends and banks the run), dying, and teleporting out. All three forfeit any unclaimed loot, so only continuing keeps your depth — and only claiming secures what you have gathered.

Q: Does Doom's HP keep increasing forever?

No. The boss scales from 525 HP at delve 1 up to 625 HP in the deepest delves, but its attack stats, accuracy, and max hit never change. The real difficulty comes from stacked mechanics, not a growing health bar.

Q: Which uniques unlock at which delve?

Mokhaiotl cloth unlocks at delve 2, the Eye of Ayak at delve 3, Avernic treads at delve 4, and the Dom pet from delve 6 and above. Below its unlock delve a unique simply cannot drop, no matter your luck.

Q: When does the Doom drop rate stop improving?

Both the unique drop rate and the common-drop quantity multiplier cap at delve 9. Delves 10 and beyond give no additional loot — they only add mechanical risk. Dive 9 is the loot ceiling.

Q: What is the best delve to claim at?

The optimal claim band is delve 6–8. By delve 6 most uniques are unlocked, by delve 8 the quantity multiplier is near its cap, and the death penalty still resets you to one anyway — so expected value peaks there before deeper delves add risk without adding loot.

Q: How does the Doom reclaim fee work?

On death your items go to a reclaim point. For your first 20 delves the reclaim fee is reduced by 75%, making early deaths cheap to recover from. Past 20 delves the discount expires, so consistency must already be high by then.

Q: Is Doom harder than Yama?

Structurally they are similar — both send you to a lobby on death and both offer a learner-friendly reclaim discount. Doom adds the unclaimed-loot gamble and an open-ended delve ladder you control, which makes it more about self-discipline than raw difficulty. Compare directly in our Yama boss guide.

Q: Can I use thralls, poison, or venom in deep delve?

Not effectively. During the shielded phase only demonbane weapons deal true damage, and poison, venom, and thralls are all immune. A demonbane weapon is therefore mandatory for the deep-delve phases, not optional. See the gear guide for the required setups.

Q: How does Doom compare to ToA invocations?

ToA scales via pre-set invocations chosen before a fixed instance, so the difficulty is transparent up front. Doom scales emergently from your live decision to descend, making it a mid-run gamble rather than a planned one. Both reward pushing higher, but the risk model is opposite.
