🐉 Vorkath & Demonic Gorillas: Prayer Flicking & Switch Drills (2026)

Updated August 19, 2026  |  15 min read  |  By Yanchun Wu

This is a teaching guide, not a walkthrough. If you want the full kill routes, read our Vorkath guide and the Kraken guide for context. Here we drill the two mechanical skills that separate players who "survive" from players who "efficiently farm": flicking prayers on Vorkath (especially the freeze/poison and fireball specials) and executing the three-piece combat-style switch on Demonic Gorillas. No gear required to learn — just rhythm, keybinds, and reps.

📅 Updated: August 2026 — Mechanics Training · 23 min read
🛡️ Level: 70+
🆕 NEW

Two bosses teach the two most transferable PvM skills in OSRS. Vorkath teaches you to flick prayers against an alternating attack pattern and to handle an unprayable environmental special. Demonic Gorillas teach you to switch — prayer, weapon, and target read — on a tight timer. Master both and you are qualified for nearly every mid-to-high boss in the game, because every later fight is some combination of "flick" and "switch." If you can already do Zulrah rotations, this is the natural next step.

The fastest way to learn is to isolate each skill, drill it slowly, then speed up. This guide gives you the timing, the keybind layout, and a 30-minute daily drill routine you can run on a spare account or a low-stakes trip. Do not try to learn flicking mid-fight on your main — learn the rhythm first, then apply it. The setup details also help if you are building toward a low-gear Vorkath setup.

💡 Quick Verdict

Flicking Vorkath correctly cuts your prayer drain roughly in half and removes the food cost of his magic hits. The Gorilla three-piece switch (Protect prayer + attack style + read) is the single best DPS-and-survival drill in the game. Spend one week drilling both and every other boss gets easier. Neither requires max gear — they require keybinds and repetition.

1. 🎓 Why Flicking & Switching Matter

Before the how, the why. Both skills exist because OSRS bosses are designed around resource pressure: prayer points, food, and time. The players who win are the ones who spend those resources only when necessary.

① Flicking = free prayer

A protect prayer drains 1 prayer point per game tick (0.6s) while active. If you leave Protect from Magic on for a whole Vorkath fight, you burn points continuously and run dry in a couple of minutes, forcing expensive prayer potions or an early teleport. If you instead toggle the prayer ON only for the single tick the hit lands, you drain maybe 1 point every 1.8 seconds — roughly one-third the cost. Over a long trip that is the difference between 2 brews and 10 brews.

② Switching = more DPS and less damage

Demonic Gorillas change their defensive weakness every few attacks. If you keep attacking with the wrong style, you hit through their full Defence and do a fraction of your potential damage — and the kill drags on, costing food and time. Switching to the style they are weak to can double or triple your damage. At the same time, you must switch your Protect prayer to the style they are attacking with, or you eat unblocked hits. Switch correctly and the fight is short and safe; fail and it is a food drain.

③ They compound

Most end-game bosses combine both. ToA's Akkha, the Moons of Peril bosses, and even Barrows brothers all reward flicking and/or switching. Learning the two fundamentals here pays off everywhere. That is why this guide exists separately from the boss walkthroughs — the skill transfers.

2. 🐲 Vorkath Prayer Flicking (Freeze, Poison, Fireball)

Vorkath is the perfect flicking teacher because his pattern is simple and repetitive. He attacks on a roughly 3-tick interval and alternates between two styles: a Ranged attack (the icy/breath projectile) and a Magic attack (the fireball). Every fourth attack is a special you cannot fully pray.

① The basic flick (Ranged ↔ Magic)

Because Vorkath alternates, you flick between two prayers:

  • When the Ranged projectile is in the air and about to land, activate Protect from Missiles for that landing tick, then turn it off.
  • When the Magic fireball is incoming, activate Protect from Magic for that landing tick, then turn it off.
  • Since only one hit lands at a time, only one prayer is ever active — your drain drops from "1/tick" to "1 per 1.8s."

The rhythm is: Missiles-on, off, off, Magic-on, off, off, repeat. The two "off" ticks between each hit are where you save everything. Practice the toggle timing on a dummy first (see the drill section) before doing it live.

② The Freeze / Poison special (every 4th attack, type A)

On the 4th attack in the cycle, Vorkath fires a poison projectile that lands in the center and creates a poison pool. This attack cannot be blocked by prayer — you must move. The projectile also freezes you briefly if it hits, which is why players call it the "freeze attack."

Counter routine:

  1. Watch the 4th attack telegraph — Vorkath rears and the projectile is green/poison-colored, not the normal blue or orange.
  2. When it lands, run to the opposite side of the arena (Vorkath is at the north; run to the south edge).
  3. Drink an antipoison (or keep a Serpentine helm / anti-venom active) so the pool's venom does not stack.
  4. Do NOT stand in the pool — it deals damage and venoms every tick you remain.

The freeze can root you for a moment, so the key is to already be moving when it lands, not after. Many deaths happen because the player tried to keep attacking and got caught mid-pool. Drop your weapon's attack for one cycle and reposition — the DPS loss is nothing compared to a poison death.

③ The Fireball special (every 4th attack, type B)

The other 4th-attack variant is a large fireball. This one IS prayable: turn on Protect from Magic as it lands to nullify most of the damage. Additionally, you can attack the fireball with a Crush weapon (or any weapon) to pop it and reduce the damage further — a nice efficiency trick once you are comfortable. The fireball special does not require movement, only the correct prayer and (optionally) a hit to shrink it.

④ The Zombified Spawn special (every 4th attack, type C)

The third 4th-attack variant spawns a zombified spawn next to you. If left alive, it heals Vorkath. Kill it quickly (a single hit usually suffices) or it undoes your DPS. This special is not a prayer issue — it is a priority target. The lesson: on every 4th attack, glance at what spawned, not just at your prayer tab.

⑤ Lazy alternative (when learning)

If you cannot flick yet, the safe fallback is to keep Protect from Missiles permanently on (it blocks the freeze/poison attack, the most dangerous one) and simply eat the magic fireballs, or keep Protect from Magic on and move for the freeze. This costs more prayer and food but keeps you alive while you learn. Graduate to flicking as soon as the rhythm clicks — the food savings are large.

⑥ Phase-by-phase flick calls (what to say aloud)

Talking through the fight cements the rhythm. Here is the call script for one full 4-attack cycle, which you can mutter (or, in a team, say) to stay on beat:

  • Beat 1: "Missiles" — toggle Protect from Missiles on for the landing tick, off after.
  • Beat 2: "Magic" — toggle Protect from Magic on for the landing tick, off after.
  • Beat 3: "Missiles" — same as beat 1.
  • Beat 4: "Special — read it" — glance at the projectile: green = run + antipoison, orange fireball = Protect from Magic, zombified = click it dead.

Saying the style name as you toggle binds the action to the word, which is why drill instructors have you call it out. Within a session the calls become internal and you stop needing to voice them — but until then, vocalizing prevents the autopilot mistake of leaving one prayer on.

⑦ Common Vorkath flick failure: the double-toggle

A frequent beginner error is toggling the prayer twice per beat — on, off, on again — which drains double points and serves no purpose. The correct pattern is one toggle ON as the hit lands, then one toggle OFF after it resolves, then nothing until the next beat. If your prayer points vanish faster than expected, film your hands (or watch the prayer orb) and confirm you are not double-tapping. One clean toggle per beat is the entire skill.

3. 🦍 Demonic Gorilla Three-Piece Switch

Demonic Gorillas (Crash Site Cavern) are the best switch drill in the game because they force you to change two things at once: your Protect prayer (to block their attack) and your attack style (to exploit their weakness). Get both right and the gorilla dies fast and safely.

① The style cycle

A gorilla attacks with one of three styles and shows it via the overhead prayer icon and its body color:

  • Melee (attacks when you are in melee range; shows the melee icon). Weakness: Ranged.
  • Ranged (blue projectile; shows Protect from Missiles icon). Weakness: Magic.
  • Magic (red projectile; shows Protect from Magic icon). Weakness: Melee.

After about three attacks the gorilla switches to a new style. The rule that ties it together: the style it attacks with is the style it is WEAK to is false — the correct pairing is below. Read carefully:

② The weakness table (memorize this)

Gorilla attacks with Pray (to block) Gorilla is weak to Attack with
MeleeProtect from MeleeRangedBlowpipe / Bow / Ranged
Ranged (blue)Protect from MissilesMagicTrident / Splinter / Magic
Magic (red)Protect from MagicMeleeWhip / Barrelchest / Melee

So the three-piece switch is: (1) switch your Protect prayer to match what the gorilla attacks with, and (2) switch your weapon/attack style to what it is weak to. The "third piece" is (3) reading the switch early — watch the overhead icon and the body color so you swap before the next attack lands, not after you take a hit.

③ The switch order (do it fast)

When the gorilla's icon changes, the optimal sequence is:

  1. Swap Protect prayer to the new attack style (1 key press).
  2. Swap weapon to the weakness style (1 preset key or 1 inventory swap).
  3. Resume attacking. The whole thing should take under ~1 second with good keybinds.

If you are even one attack late on the prayer swap, you eat a hit you could have blocked. If you are late on the weapon swap, you waste the gorilla's vulnerable window. The drill below builds the muscle memory so the switch becomes reflexive.

④ Why this is the best drill

Unlike Vorkath (single rhythm), gorillas force you to read and react on a timer, which is exactly what every raid boss demands. Players who can cleanly three-piece a gorilla pack can pick up ToA, COX, and most other bosses far faster. It is the highest-leverage 20 minutes you can spend training.

⑤ Gorilla pack routing & target priority

The Crash Site Cavern usually presents gorillas in pairs or a small pack, which changes your switching strategy slightly from a 1v1. The rules:

  1. Never tank two gorillas melee at once. If both are in melee range, one will hit you unprayed. Kite so only one is in range, or step out entirely between switches.
  2. Switch to the gorilla currently attacking you. If gorilla A shows Magic and gorilla B shows Ranged, you cannot block both — prioritize the one whose attack is about to land, and reposition so the other whiffs.
  3. Weapon swap targets the weaker target. If one gorilla is in its Melee-weak (red) state and the other in its Ranged-weak (blue) state, your weapon can only exploit one; kill the lower-HP one faster by matching its weakness, then switch.
  4. Use the terrain. The cavern has pillars; line-of-sight a second gorilla so you only ever manage one set of switches. This turns a 2-target nightmare into two sequential 1-target drills.

Pack switching is just single-target switching plus prioritization. Players who master the 1v1 three-piece adapt to packs within a session; players who skip the 1v1 drill drown in packs. Learn the fundamental first (Section 3), then apply the routing rules here.

⑥ Reading the switch early (the 1-attack buffer)

Gorillas telegraph their style change ~1 attack before it lands: the body color and overhead icon update a beat ahead of the actual hit. The elite habit is to swap the instant the icon changes, not after you take the first unblocked hit of the new style. That one-attack buffer is the difference between flawless switches and a slow food drain. Drill it by watching the icon during the metronome cycle in Drill C — swap on the visual change, not on the damage.

4. ⌨️ Keybind & Preset Setup

You cannot switch fast by clicking the prayer book and inventory every time. You need keybinds. This section is OSRS-client and RuneLite only — no automation, no macros (those are bannable). Manual keybinds are 100% allowed.

① Prayer keybinds

Open the prayer tab (default F6) and note that each prayer can be toggled with number keys 1–N once the tab is open. Better: use RuneLite's Prayer keybinds (or the built-in "Prayers" keybind in the keybind plugin) to map each Protect prayer to a single comfortable key. A common layout:

  • Protect from Melee → a side key (e.g., `Q` or a mouse side-button).
  • Protect from Missiles → `E`.
  • Protect from Magic → `R`.
  • Quick Prayer (your most-used protect) → a single key or spacebar via the quick-prayer setting.

The goal: each protect prayer is one keypress, no tab navigation. Practice toggling them in a safe area until the timing is automatic.

② Weapon swap keybinds (gear presets)

If you have a Presets setup (members, via the bank preset pin or RuneLite's Presets plugin), bind each combat style to a function key:

  • F1 → Melee preset (whip + melee gear).
  • F2 → Ranged preset (blowpipe + ranged gear).
  • F3 → Magic preset (trident + magic gear).

With presets, a full weapon+gear swap is one keypress. Without presets, keep the three weapons in adjacent inventory slots and use number keys (1–3) to equip them, accepting a slightly slower swap. The drill section works either way.

③ The combined layout

Your left hand owns prayers (Q/E/R), your right hand owns weapon presets (F1–F3) and attack. During a gorilla switch you press one prayer key and one preset key almost simultaneously. That is the whole skill — two keys, under a second, repeated until it is reflex. Once this layout is automatic, you will find that every other style-switching boss in the game feels like the same two-key motion with different art — the underlying skill is identical, only the icons change.

④ Mouse-click vs keybind: which to use

Beginners often ask whether to click the prayer book and inventory or use keybinds. The answer depends on your speed target:

Method Switch speed Best for
Mouse-click prayer + inventory swap~1.5–2.5sLearning the logic; casual gorillas
Function-key prayers + number-key weapons~0.8–1.2sRegular farming; most players
Keybind plugin (Q/E/R + F1–F3 presets)~0.3–0.6sSpeed kills; raid bosses

Clicking is fine to learn the logic, but you will outgrow it fast because the gorilla switches every few attacks — a 2-second click-switch means you eat at least one unblocked hit per rotation. Move to keybinds as soon as the logic is solid. The keybind plugin layout in Section 4 is the standard; adopt it early so the muscle memory carries into every later boss.

⑤ Mobile considerations

On mobile (see our mobile guides), keybinds do not exist, so you rely on the prayer quick-tap and the action bar. The principle is identical — tap the correct Protect prayer and the correct weapon — but the input is slower, so favor content with longer switch timers. Vorkath flicking is awkward on mobile (the flick window is tight); gorillas are more manageable because the switch cadence is slower. If you mainly play mobile, drill gorillas there first and treat Vorkath flicking as a desktop skill until you are very comfortable.

5. 🏋️ The 30-Minute Daily Drill Routine

Learning by doing it live is slow and expensive. Instead, isolate the skill and drill it. This routine takes 30 minutes and needs no boss kill — you can do most of it on a low-level or spare account.

① Drill A: flick timing (10 min)

Go somewhere safe (the Barrows brothers are too dangerous; use a low-level monster or a friend's dummy). Set a metronome to ~33 beats per minute (one beat ≈ Vorkath's 1.8s attack gap). On each beat, toggle Protect from Missiles ON then OFF on the next; alternate Missiles/Magic each beat. Goal: clean, no early/late toggles, for 10 minutes without breaking rhythm. This builds the exact Vorkath flick cadence.

② Drill B: the special reaction (5 min)

Mental drill: every 4th beat, say the special type aloud ("POOL — move!", "FIREBALL — pray magic", "SPAWN — kill"). You are training the "glance at the 4th attack" reflex so you do not tunnel on the prayer tab during the real fight. Pair it with a quick weapon-swap keypress to simulate dropping attack for a reposition.

③ Drill C: gorilla three-piece (10 min)

Use the Kraken or any multi-style trainer if available, or simply cycle the switch manually: call out "MELEE → switch to Ranged weapon + Protect Melee", then "RANGED → Magic weapon + Protect Missiles", then "MAGIC → Melee weapon + Protect Magic", looping. Press the prayer key and weapon key together each time. Goal: sub-second, clean, no hesitation. Do 20 full cycles.

④ Drill D: live application (5 min)

Now actually fight a few gorillas or a Vorkath attempt, focusing ONLY on clean switches/flicks — ignore DPS. If you break rhythm, stop and restart the drill. After a week of this, the skills are permanent.

6. 🚫 Common Mistakes & Fixes

These are the failure modes we see most, and the one-line fix for each.

① Mistake: leaving a prayer on "just in case"

Players keep Protect from Magic on permanently at Vorkath "so they don't forget." That burns prayer and still eats the ranged freeze. Fix: flick — off between beats. The whole point is the off time.

② Mistake: switching weapon but not prayer (or vice versa)

On gorillas, players swap to the right weapon but forget the Protect prayer, or pray correctly but keep the wrong weapon. Fix: drill them as a single combined action (one prayer key + one weapon key). They are never separate.

③ Mistake: reacting after the hit lands

Reading the icon after you already took damage wastes the switch. Fix: watch the overhead icon and body color; swap the instant it changes, before the next attack. The drill trains early reads.

④ Mistake: panicking on the freeze and standing in the pool

Players keep attacking through the poison special and get frozen in the pool. Fix: on the 4th-attack telegraph, stop attacking and move first, antipoison second. One cycle of lost DPS beats a poison death.

⑤ Mistake: over-engineering keybinds

Some players install macro tools to "auto-switch." That is bannable and unnecessary. Fix: manual Q/E/R + F1–F3 is fast enough for any human-speed boss. Stay manual.

⑥ Mistake: drilling on the main account under pressure

Learning live wastes supplies and builds bad habits under stress. Fix: do the metronome and cycle drills first, in safe spaces, then apply. The routine in Section 5 exists for exactly this.

7. ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Is prayer flicking allowed by Jagex?

Yes. Manual prayer flicking is a legitimate mechanic and explicitly permitted. What is bannable is using software (macros, auto-clickers) to flick for you. All keybinds in this guide are manual and safe.

How much prayer does flicking Vorkath actually save?

Leaving one protect prayer on drains 1 point/tick (~1.67/sec). Flicking (one prayer on only for the landing tick every ~3 ticks) drains roughly 1 point per 1.8s — about one-third the cost. Over a long trip that is the difference between a few brews and a full prayer potion stack.

Can I just keep Protect from Missiles on at Vorkath?

You can, and it blocks the dangerous freeze/poison attack — but you will eat the magic fireballs and burn more prayer. It is a valid learning fallback. The efficient method is to flick both so you block everything and save prayer.

What is the Demonic Gorilla weakness cycle again?

Melee-attacking gorilla is weak to Ranged; Ranged-attacking (blue) is weak to Magic; Magic-attacking (red) is weak to Melee. Pray the style it attacks with, weapon the style it is weak to. That pairing is the entire switch.

Do I need presets to three-piece switch?

No. Presets make it one keypress, but you can keep the three weapons in inventory slots 1–3 and equip with number keys. Presets are faster and recommended if you have them, but they are not required to learn the skill.

How do I handle the Vorkath freeze if I get poisoned?

Keep an antipoison active (Serpentine helm, anti-venom, or sip antipoison the moment the pool spawns). The freeze roots you briefly, so be moving before it lands. Never stand in the pool — step to the far edge.

What keybinds do you recommend for beginners?

Prayers on Q/E/R (Melee/Missiles/Magic) and weapon presets on F1/F2/F3. One prayer key plus one weapon key per switch. Keep it simple and symmetric so the muscle memory transfers to every boss.

How long until the switch is automatic?

Most players reach clean, sub-second switches after about a week of the 30-minute daily drill (Section 5). The skill then transfers to ToA, COX, and other style-switching bosses without re-learning.

Should I learn flicking or switching first?

Flicking first — it is a fixed rhythm and easier to internalize. Then switching, which adds the read-and-react layer. The routine orders them this way on purpose. Once both are solid, combine them.

Where do these skills apply beyond these two bosses?

Everywhere. Flicking helps at Zulrah, Vorkath, and most raids; switching helps at ToA (Akkha), the Moons of Peril, and Barrows. They are the core PvM fundamentals — see the broader boss hub for where to apply them next.
