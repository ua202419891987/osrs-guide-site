Bank PIN: Setup, Delay Window & Recovery Rules Explained (2026)

Updated August 19, 2026  |  15 min read  |  By Yanchun Wu

The Bank PIN is the cheapest, highest-impact anti-theft setting in Old School RuneScape — and the one most players either skip or set up wrong. This is the full decision guide: how to set it, which tabs to lock, precisely how the 7-day delay window stops a hijacker, what to do if you forget the PIN, and the myths that get banks cleaned out.

  • 📌 Forgot the PIN? You wait 7 days — there is no instant bypass; plan recovery around that window.

1. 🏦 What a Bank PIN Actually Protects (and What It Doesn't)

Before you set anything, understand the boundary of what a Bank PIN does. Players who overestimate it get a false sense of safety; players who underestimate it skip it entirely. Both groups lose banks.

① What the PIN protects

A Bank PIN gates access to your bank tabs. When you set it, you choose which of your bank's tab pages require the PIN before items can be withdrawn or moved. The PIN itself is a 4–8 digit number you enter at the bank interface. With the PIN active and tabs locked, anyone — including a hijacker who has your password — cannot pull items out of those tabs without the code. That is the entire point: it is a second gate that sits between a logged-in intruder and your stuff.

② What the PIN does NOT protect

This is the part players miss. A Bank PIN does not:

  • Protect items you have equipped or in your inventory — a hijacker can drop or trade what you are wearing and carrying without touching the bank.
  • Protect unlocked tabs — if you only PIN-lock some tabs, the rest are open.
  • Stop a hijacker from playing the account, training, or interacting — it only gates the bank.
  • Replace 2FA or a secure email — those stop the login; the PIN only slows the theft after a login.
  • Prevent RWT or botting bans — a PIN has zero effect on macro or real-world-trading detection.
So the PIN is a damage-limiter, not a wall. Its job is to buy time — especially via the 7-day delay (Section 3) — during which you can recover the account before anything moves.

③ Why "buy time" is the real value

A hijacker who gets in wants speed: grab, liquidate, log out. The Bank PIN with the 7-day delay turns that speed run into a week-long waiting game they will almost never bother with. Combined with our 1-Hour Emergency Checklist, that week is more than enough to end their session and recover. The PIN is the single setting most likely to make a thief give up and move to an easier target.

💡 Think in layers, not silver bullets

The PIN is layer three of four: (1) a unique password, (2) app-based 2FA, (3) the Bank PIN with 7-day delay, (4) a secured email that also has 2FA. Skip any one and the others work harder. The PIN's special power is that it keeps working even if layer 2 fails — which is exactly why you enable the delay.

2. 🔧 Step-by-Step: Setting Your Bank PIN

Setup takes under two minutes. Do it on a trusted device and network, and decide the tab coverage before you start.

① Open the Bank PIN interface

Go to any bank, right-click the bank booth or banker, and choose "Set a PIN" (or, inside the bank interface, use the Bank PIN options). You can also reach PIN settings from the in-bank "PIN" button. The game prompts you to enter a new PIN of 4 to 8 digits. Pick something memorable but not obvious (not 1234, not your birthday) — and not the same as any PIN you use elsewhere.

② Choose which tabs to protect

This is the decision that makes or breaks the setup. You can lock individual tab pages or choose to protect the entire bank. Best practice: lock ALL tabs. If you only lock, say, your "raids" tab, a hijacker cleans out your unprotected GP and consumables tab in seconds. The small inconvenience of entering the PIN each bank visit is nothing compared to the protection of a fully locked bank. If you truly need a fast-access tab (e.g., food during a session), lock everything except that one — but know it is a deliberate hole.

③ Enable the 7-day delay (critical)

In the Bank PIN settings, find "Require a 7-day waiting period before allowing my PIN to be changed or deleted" and turn it ON. This is the setting that converts a simple PIN into an anti-theft wall. Without it, a hijacker who learns or resets the PIN can remove it instantly. With it, any attempt to change or delete the PIN starts a 7-day countdown they cannot shortcut. Enable it during setup — do not leave this off.

④ The "don't ask on this device" option — use with care

OSRS lets you tick a box to stop the PIN prompt on a specific, trusted computer. This is convenient but it means that device is now a soft spot: anyone with access to that machine skips the PIN. Only enable it on a personal, password-protected, malware-free PC — never on a shared or public computer. If you are unsure, leave it unticked; entering the PIN is a two-second habit.

3. ⏳ The 7-Day Delay Window — How It Works

This is the mechanism players ask about most, and the one most misunderstood. Here is exactly what the 7-day delay does and does not do.

① What triggers the 7-day window

When the delay option is enabled, any attempt to change or delete your Bank PIN starts a mandatory 7-day countdown before that change takes effect. The countdown begins the moment the request is made — typically the instant someone (you or a hijacker) clicks "delete PIN" or "change PIN" with the delay toggle on. During those 7 days, the existing PIN keeps working as normal, so your own play is unaffected; only the removal/change is held.

② Why it stops hijackers

A thief who gets in wants to disable the PIN so they can move everything freely. With the delay on, their "delete PIN" does not take effect for a week. They are stuck behind the PIN for that whole time — and a week is far longer than a hijacker will linger on one account when there are easier targets. More importantly, the delay gives you time: if you spot the hijack (or get the "PIN change requested" notice), you can cancel the pending change and recover the account well before the window closes. The delay is a timer that favors the defender.

③ What the delay does NOT cover

Be precise, because overclaiming gets players burned:

  • The 7-day delay applies to Bank PIN change/delete — not to your password, not to 2FA, not to the registered email.
  • It does not freeze withdrawals if the hijacker already knows the PIN. The PIN still gates tabs; the delay only gates removing the gate.
  • Separate Jagex Account settings (email change, 2FA removal, DOB) have their own cooling-off rules — do not assume the bank PIN delay protects those. Secure them independently per our Account Security Guide.
So the delay is a targeted, powerful tool — not a blanket account freeze.

⚠️ Do not disable the delay "for convenience"

Some players turn the 7-day delay off because they worry about being locked out of their own PIN change. That reasoning is backwards: the delay is what protects you during a hijack, and the "forgot PIN" recovery (Section 4) already gives you a legitimate 7-day path if you need to remove it. Leaving the delay ON costs you nothing day-to-day and denies a thief their fastest exit. Keep it on.

4. 🔓 Recovery: Forgot Your PIN?

You set the PIN, then life happened, and now you cannot remember it. Here is the rule and the process — and the one number you must accept: seven days.

① There is no instant bypass

Jagex does not provide a "reset my PIN by email" button, and anyone offering one is a scammer. If you forget the PIN, the only sanctioned path is the in-game "Forgot PIN" / recover option, which starts the same 7-day countdown described in Section 3. After 7 days the PIN is removed and you can set a new one. There is no shortcut, no support override, and no "answer your security question" fast lane. Plan around the week.

② How to start the recovery

At the bank PIN interface, choose the recover/forgot option. The game confirms it will remove the PIN after the waiting period. You can keep playing normally in the meantime — you just cannot change or remove the PIN until the timer ends. Make sure you actually want the removal, because once started it runs its course. If you suddenly remember the PIN mid-week, you can still use it; only the change/delete is delayed, not your access.

③ If a hijacker started the deletion

Worst case: the thief initiated a PIN delete (with the delay on) and you noticed. Because the delay exists, the PIN is still active for 7 days — so your bank is still gated that whole time, which is your opening to recover the account via the emergency checklist before the window closes. If you can log in, you can also cancel the pending PIN change from the bank settings, slamming the door shut immediately. The delay is doing exactly the job it was built for.

④ Preventing your own lockout

The cleanest "recovery" is never needing it: write the PIN in your password manager or a sealed paper note kept offline, separate from your devices. If you run multiple accounts, our Multi-Account 2FA Management Guide approach applies to PINs too — track each account's PIN in one encrypted place so a memory lapse is a lookup, not a week-long wait. And keep the 7-day delay ON so a forgotten-PIN week never becomes a hijacker's opportunity.

💡 Recovery math

Forgotten PIN = 7-day wait to remove. Hijacker tries to delete your PIN = also 7-day wait (because the delay is on) = your bank stays gated that week. Same timer, opposite outcomes depending on who set it. That symmetry is the whole design — use it.

5. 🧭 Bank PIN vs Other Security Layers

Players often ask "which security thing should I do first?" The answer is a stack, not a choice. Here is the decision guide for where the Bank PIN sits and what to pair it with.

① The four-layer model

LayerWhat it stopsSetup effortPair with PIN?
1. Unique passwordGuess / credential-stuffing loginLowYes — baseline
2. App-based 2FALogin without your deviceLow–MedYes — critical
3. Bank PIN + 7-day delayBank drain after a loginLow— (this guide)
4. Secured email + 2FAReset-link interceptionLowYes — often forgotten

The Bank PIN is layer 3. It is the only layer that keeps working even if layer 2 fails — which is exactly why enabling its 7-day delay matters so much. But it cannot compensate for a weak password or an open email; those are upstream of the bank entirely.

② Bank PIN vs Authenticator — not either/or

A common myth is "I have 2FA, so I don't need a Bank PIN." False. 2FA stops the login; if a hijacker ever gets past it (lost device, SIM swap, phishing your code), the Bank PIN is the next wall — and the 7-day delay is the wall they cannot climb quickly. Conversely, "I have a Bank PIN, so I don't need 2FA" is equally wrong: the PIN does nothing against an unauthorized login and does not gate equipped/inventory items. Run both. The Authenticator guide covers layer 2; this guide covers layer 3.

③ When the PIN alone is "good enough"

Realistically, never as a solo measure — but the PIN is the single highest-leverage setting for players who do only one thing. If you do just one security action today, enable the Bank PIN with the 7-day delay and lock all tabs. It is the cheapest protection per second of effort, and it directly defends the wealth you grinded for. Then, tomorrow, add 2FA. The ordering matters less than doing both.

💡 Decision in one line

If you are logged in and worried: set the PIN + 7-day delay now (2 minutes). If you are locked out: that is a 2FA/email problem, not a PIN problem — go to the Authenticator recovery guide. The PIN protects the bank; the other layers protect the door.

6. 🚫 Common Mistakes & Myths

These are the misconceptions that get banks drained. If you believed any of them, fix your setup today.

① Myth: "A Bank PIN prevents all theft"

No. As Section 1 covered, the PIN gates bank tabs only. Equipped gear, inventory items, and unlocked tabs are still exposed, and the PIN does nothing against the login itself. It is a damper, not a vault. Players who think "I have a PIN, I'm safe" skip 2FA and then lose everything the PIN never covered.

② Mistake: locking only some tabs

Setting a PIN but leaving your GP tab or main-stack tab open is the most common setup error. A hijacker goes for the unlocked tab first and is gone before the PIN ever matters. Lock all tabs unless you have a specific, understood reason for one fast-access tab — and even then, keep the valuable tabs locked.

③ Myth: "The 7-day delay freezes my account"

It does not. The delay only holds PIN change/delete requests; your normal play, including using the bank with the current PIN, is unaffected. Some players avoid the delay out of fear it will lock them out — but the only time it "delays" you is when you yourself try to remove the PIN, which is exactly the scenario the forgot-PIN recovery already handles.

④ Mistake: disabling the delay for "convenience"

Turning the 7-day delay off to make your own PIN changes easier is a net loss. You trade a once-a-year minor inconvenience for a permanent, fatal hole a hijacker will exploit in minutes. Keep the delay ON. The forgot-PIN path already covers the legitimate case where you need to remove it.

⑤ Myth: "I can bypass the wait by contacting support"

Jagex will not override the 7-day Bank PIN timer for a forgotten PIN, and will not give you the code by any channel. Anyone who says otherwise is running a recovery scam. The only path is the in-game recover option and its 7-day window. Protect your own access by storing the PIN in a password manager so you rarely need the recovery at all.

⑥ Mistake: using the same PIN everywhere

Reusing your bank PIN as your phone lock, your email PIN, or another game's code means one leak exposes all of them. Use a unique PIN for the bank, stored only in your password manager. The PIN's strength comes from secrecy, not from being easy to remember by reusing it.

7. ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

How many digits should my Bank PIN be?

OSRS allows 4 to 8 digits. Use the full 8 if you can remember it — more digits mean more brute-force resistance, though in practice a hijacker cannot brute-force the PIN at all (it is gated by login). The bigger point is choosing a non-obvious number and storing it safely, not maximizing length for its own sake.

Can a hijacker change my PIN if they know my password but not the PIN?

They can request a change, but with the 7-day delay enabled that change will not take effect for a week — during which your existing PIN keeps protecting the bank. If you can still log in, cancel the pending change immediately. If you cannot, recover the account within that window. This is exactly why the delay exists.

Does the Bank PIN protect items I'm wearing or carrying?

No. The PIN only gates bank tabs. Equipped gear and inventory items can still be dropped or traded by someone with account access. That is why the PIN must sit on top of 2FA and a secured email — it limits bank loss, not total loss. Never assume "PIN = fully safe."

What happens to the 7-day delay if I recover a hacked account?

When Jagex returns a hijacked account, they strip the unauthorized changes (email, 2FA) and restore your access; your Bank PIN settings, including the enabled delay, generally remain as you left them. After recovery, verify the delay is still ON and re-lock all tabs if anything looks off. Our After-the-Hack Recovery Walkthrough covers the post-recovery checklist.

I enabled "don't ask on this device" and now I'm worried. What do I do?

You can reverse it from the bank PIN settings on that device — turn the trust off so the PIN is required everywhere. Only enable device trust on a personal, password-protected, malware-free PC. If that device is shared, public, or ever compromised, keep the trust OFF and enter the PIN each time; the few seconds are worth the safety.

Is the 7-day delay the same as the Jagex Account email/2FA cooldown?

Similar idea, different systems. The Bank PIN delay governs PIN change/delete specifically. Jagex Account-level changes (registered email, Authenticator removal, date of birth) have their own separate cooling-off protections. Do not assume one covers the other — secure each layer independently using our Account Security Guide.

Can I set a Bank PIN on a F2P account?

Yes — the Bank PIN is available regardless of membership, and F2P banks still hold valuable GP and items worth protecting. There is no reason to wait for membership to set it up. The setup steps in Section 2 are identical for F2P and members.

Will setting a Bank PIN get me banned or flagged?

No. The Bank PIN is a legitimate, Jagex-provided anti-theft feature. It has no effect on botting, RWT, or macro detection. Using it is expected good practice, not a behavior signal. The only "flag" risk is if you use the PIN to shuffle wealth in a way that looks like muling — but the PIN itself is clean.

My PIN "isn't working" at the bank — what's wrong?

Common causes: you are on a device where you ticked "don't ask" (so it skips the prompt — that is expected), you are entering it on the wrong tab, or the game client needs a restart. If the prompt appears but rejects the code, you may have the wrong PIN remembered — use the forgot-PIN recover (7-day wait) to reset. If a hijacker changed it, the 7-day delay protects you; recover the account instead.
