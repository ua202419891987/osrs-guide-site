OSRS Tears of Guthix Guide 2026 — How to Unlock It and Maximise Your Weekly Tears

Published September 18, 2026  |  16 min read  |  By Yanchun Wu

Tears of Guthix is the cheapest repeatable experience in Old School RuneScape — a two-minute weekly ritual that dumps XP straight into your lowest skill and asks for almost nothing in return. It is also the activity most players set up wrong: they never check which skill will receive the XP, they play on a world with a bad tear sequence, and they miss the 10% diary bonus entirely. This guide covers the unlock requirements, Juna's location, the real weekly reset rule, the XP formula, and the world-selection trick that maximises your tear count.

1. What Tears of Guthix Actually Is

Tears of Guthix is both a quest and a minigame. The quest released on 4 May 2005, is members-only, rates Intermediate difficulty and is flagged Very Short. Completing it unlocks the minigame of the same name, and the minigame is the part that matters long-term.

Once a week you tell Juna, an ancient serpent and Guardian of Guthix, a story about your adventures. In exchange she lets you into a cavern where the walls weep, and for a short, Quest-Point-scaled stretch of time you collect blue tears while dodging green ones. When the timer expires you drink the bowl and receive experience in the skill with the least experience on your account.

That design choice is why the activity stays relevant at every account stage. Every other training method lets you pour XP into whatever you feel like grinding; Tears of Guthix does the opposite and patches up your most neglected skill for free. For an Ironman it is one of the only genuinely free XP sources that reaches Runecraft, Agility or Herblore with no materials at all.

The quest is also a hard prerequisite for two pieces of content: While Guthix Sleeps cannot be started without it, and it is a required quest for the hard Lumbridge & Draynor Diary — which, as Section 6 explains, feeds straight back into the minigame.

2. Unlocking It — Full Quest Requirements

The bar to entry is low, but there is a Quest Point gate, so a brand new account cannot rush it on day one.

RequirementLevel / ValueBoostable?
Quest Points43No — must be earned
Firemaking49Yes
Crafting20Yes
Mining20Yes

The 43 Quest Points are the real gate, and a fresh account clears it naturally during the early-to-mid questing grind — exactly when a free boost to your weakest skill starts being valuable. Firemaking 49 is the highest single skill number and it is boostable, so a spicy stew or a mature dwarven stout can slip you through a few levels early.

Items you need to bring

  • A lit sapphire lantern — the one non-negotiable item. Combine a bullseye lantern (or bullseye lantern (unf)) with a cut sapphire, fill it with lamp oil, then light it with a tinderbox. Lamp oil comes from the lamp oil still at the chemist's house in Rimmington, or the lamp still in the Dorgesh-Kaan market.
  • A chisel — shapes the stone bowl that collects the tears.
  • A pickaxe — mines the magic stone from the rocks across the chasm.
  • A rope — only needed the first time you enter the Lumbridge Swamp Caves.

Build the sapphire lantern before you start the quest to save a round trip — the cavern will not let you extinguish a bullseye lantern once inside, so arriving with a plain candle is a wasted trip.

🎒 Ironman routes to a sapphire lantern

  • Buy it: Miltog sells one for 600 coins after Death to the Dorgeshuun.
  • Smith it: 49 Smithing (boostable) for a bullseye lantern (unf).
  • Pickpocket it: 36 Thieving (boostable) on Dorgesh-Kaan cave goblins.
  • Kill for it: 35 Slayer (boostable) on wall beasts, or a low-level Skeleton in Tarn's Lair.
  • Item spawn: Darkmeyer, after Sins of the Father.

The quest reward is modest: 1 Quest Point, 1,000 Crafting experience and weekly minigame access. Keep the lantern afterwards — While Guthix Sleeps uses one again, and you can recover the sapphire by extinguishing it and using the lantern lens on it.

3. Getting to Juna — Location and Fastest Routes

Juna lives in the Chasm of Tears, reached from the south end of the Lumbridge Swamp Caves. She counts as an NPC but has no minimap icon, so only the first trip needs directions.

Route A — the games necklace (use this every week)

Since November 2014, players who finished the quest can use a games necklace to teleport directly to Tears of Guthix. That is the entire weekly routine: rub the necklace, arrive at the chasm, right-click Juna and choose Tell Story. No cave navigation, no wall beasts, no agility obstacle.

Route B — walking the caves (first time only)

  • Wall beasts lurk in the wall holes and grab you for damage. The reliable trick is to stop one square short of each hole and then run past — that always avoids the grab.
  • Cave crawlers inflict heavy poison. Take the southern path to avoid them.
  • Swamp gas south of the middle will ignite any exposed light source. A covered sapphire lantern makes this a non-issue.
  • The agility obstacle into the chasm can be failed, but failing deals no damage, still puts you on the far side, and will not extinguish a covered lantern.

If you have completed The Lost Tribe you can enter through the Lumbridge Castle cellar instead, skipping the wall beast stretch entirely. Inside the chasm, climb down the rocks to reach Juna.

💡 Turn on the login notification

Juna can toggle a message that tells you the moment you become eligible to drink tears again. Enable it, and also note that she creates a staff of balance from a staff of the dead plus a Guthixian icon, and hosts a Master clue step requiring three or more Guthix-affiliated items.

4. The Weekly Reset Rule Most Players Get Wrong

Tears of Guthix is not a simple once-per-calendar-week activity. Two separate conditions must both be satisfied before Juna lets you in.

🔁 The two conditions

  • Time: seven days must have passed. Since the April 2015 change, Juna resets at the start of the 7th day after your last completion, rather than allowing access at 6 days and 23 hours.
  • Activity: since your last visit you must have gained 100,000 total experience points or 1 Quest Point. Either satisfies it. This rule does not apply to a first-ever visit.

The activity condition is the one that catches people out: a player who logs in purely for Tears of Guthix each week, gains nothing else, and returns seven days later will be turned away. Fortunately 100,000 XP across a week is trivial for an active account — an hour of decent training clears it — and a week spent questing counts too.

You can read the Tears of Guthix entry in your Quest Journal at any time to see exactly how many days and how many Quest Points or experience points you still need. Do not chain the visit to a strict seven-day timer — let Juna's login message be your trigger instead.

5. Blue Tears vs Green Tears — How the Cave Works

When you tell Juna a story she hands you a bowl that requires two free hands to wield, so bank your shield and any two-handed weapon before you go in. She then lets you into the arena and the timer starts.

ElementWhat It Does
Blue tearsWhat you want. Collecting them increases your bowl count, which the reward is calculated from.
Green tearsWhat you avoid. Touching a green stream reduces the blue tears you have already collected.
Weeping wallsNine in total. Three blue and three green streams are active at any one time.
Stream lifetimeEach stream stays put for 16 game ticks, or 9.6 seconds, before relocating.
Refresh orderStreams move one after another in a fixed sequence, and each world has its own sequence.
Time limitOne game tick (0.6 seconds) of cave time per Quest Point earned.

There is no damage and no way to die in the minigame. The only failure state is collecting fewer tears than you could have. When the timer expires you are removed automatically, drink the tears, and a chatbox message tells you which skill was trained.

6. How Much XP You Actually Get, and the 10% Diary Bonus

Two numbers decide your reward: how many tears you collect, and how much experience the target skill already has.

📐 XP per tear

XP = min( 60, 10 + 0.1 × floor( current XP in skill ÷ 27 ) )

Each tear is worth 60 XP once the receiving skill is around level 30 or higher. Below that the value scales down, bottoming out at 10 XP per tear for a completely untrained skill.

Your time limit, and therefore your maximum possible tears, is driven entirely by Quest Points — one game tick of cave time each, with a theoretical ceiling of roughly one tear per tick minus a little overhead at the start and end.

Quest PointsCave TimeTheoretical Max TearsXP at 60 per Tear
50 QP50 ticks / 30.0 s462,760
100 QP100 ticks / 60.0 s965,760
150 QP150 ticks / 90.0 s1468,760
200 QP200 ticks / 120.0 s19611,760
250 QP250 ticks / 150.0 s24614,760
300 QP300 ticks / 180.0 s29617,760
343 QP (max)343 ticks / 205.8 s33920,340

Treat that as a ceiling rather than a target — it assumes you never waste a tick, and stream repositioning always costs something. At maximum Quest Points the wiki's own realistic estimate is nearer 287 tears, roughly 17,220 XP per visit. That is still an enormous weekly freebie into whichever skill you have neglected most.

There is exactly one multiplier, and it is not tied to the Varrock diary: completing the hard Lumbridge & Draynor Diary grants a permanent 10% bonus to the experience, added in March 2015. When it applies you also get a chatbox confirmation stating that you were awarded an additional 10% for completing the hard Lumbridge achievement diary. If you do not see that message, the diary is not done.

7. Maximising Your Tears — The GGGBBB World Method

Streams refresh in a fixed order that differs per world. Most sequences mix colours, but a minority run Green, Green, Green, Blue, Blue, Blue — GGGBBB. On those worlds every green stream finishes and relocates before the blue streams expire, so it is impossible to clip a green tear once your blue stream ends. That property is worth more than any amount of fast clicking.

🎯 The maximisation checklist

  • Find a GGGBBB world first. Use the ToG Crowdsourcing RuneLite plugin, a crowdsourced Tears of Guthix world list, or the ToG World friends chat. Do not guess.
  • Time your dialogue. Finish Juna's dialogue when the first green stream is roughly halfway through its 9.6-second life, so you enter just as the first blue stream refreshes.
  • Start on the first blue stream. That gives a 5 in 9 chance (about 56%) that another blue stream relocates onto your tile, letting you stay put instead of running.
  • Read the cycle position. If your tile refreshes and it is the 2nd stream, you have a 1 in 3 chance of a third refresh, holding the spot for 48 ticks (28.8 seconds). If it is the 3rd stream, move back to the first blue stream immediately, because nothing will refresh on your tile.
  • Late-game rule. With less than one full cycle (16 ticks) remaining, take the nearest blue stream rather than the first one. You will not have time for a back-to-back refresh, so minimising walking is strictly better.

Everything else is mechanical: keep two hands free, do not bring a shield, and top up run energy. No gear, potion or prayer changes the outcome.

8. Which Skill Receives the XP

This is the most misunderstood part of the activity. The XP does not go to your lowest level — it goes to the skill with the least experience points. Those are usually the same, but they diverge when two skills share a level with different XP totals.

Four skills are excluded unless you meet their access requirements:

SkillRequired to Be Eligible
RunecraftComplete the Rune Mysteries quest
HerbloreComplete the Druidic Ritual quest
ConstructionOwn a player-owned house
SailingComplete the Pandemonium quest

If your lowest-experience skill is one of those four and you have not met the requirement, the reward skips it and moves to the next eligible skill. An account that has not done Rune Mysteries therefore cannot use Tears of Guthix to patch Runecraft at all.

Tie-breaking

If two or more skills have exactly the same experience total, the XP goes to whichever appears first in the game's internal skill order. That order was changed in November 2025 to put combat skills at the front: Attack, Strength, Ranged, Magic, Defence, Hitpoints, Prayer, Agility, Herblore, Thieving, Crafting, Runecraft, Mining, Smithing, Fishing, Cooking, Firemaking, Woodcutting, Fletching, Slayer, Farming, Construction, Hunter, Sailing.

Because combat skills now lead that list, a tie between Attack and Runecraft resolves in favour of Attack. If you are feeding XP to a specific non-combat skill, make sure it is strictly the lowest rather than tied with a combat skill.

⚠️ The pure account warning

Combat pures and skill pures should generally avoid Tears of Guthix. The reward model is XP into your neglected skill, which for a pure means unwanted Attack, Defence or Prayer levels that can permanently break the build — and the XP is not optional once you drink.

9. Is It Worth Doing Every Week?

For a main account the maths is straightforward. At 300 Quest Points you collect a realistic chunk of XP in a skill you would otherwise have to train manually, and it lands in whichever skill you have neglected most — precisely the one you least want to grind. The time cost is about two minutes once the games necklace teleport is in your bank.

For an Ironman the case is stronger. Runecraft, Agility, Herblore and Farming are materially slower without the Grand Exchange, and Tears of Guthix hands out XP in those skills with no input materials.

Account TypeRecommendationReason
Main accountDo it weeklyFree XP into your weakest skill for two minutes of effort
IronmanNever skip itNo materials required, reaches slow-to-train skills like Runecraft and Herblore
Combat pureAvoid itThe award cannot be redirected or refused
Skill pureAvoid itThe same automatic award can push unwanted combat or Prayer levels

The two exceptions are those restricted builds. Everyone else should treat Tears of Guthix as a fixed part of the weekly routine, sitting alongside herb runs, birdhouse runs and other repeatable upkeep. Because the Quest Point gate, the timer scaling and the diary requirement all key off quest completions, the activity also gets better the more you quest.

10. Tears of Guthix FAQs

Q: What are the requirements to unlock Tears of Guthix?

43 Quest Points, 49 Firemaking, 20 Crafting and 20 Mining to complete the quest that unlocks the minigame. All three skill requirements are boostable, so a spicy stew can finish it a few levels early. The Quest Point requirement cannot be boosted.

Q: Where is Juna, and what is the fastest way to reach her?

Juna is in the Chasm of Tears, reached from the south end of the Lumbridge Swamp Caves. Once the quest is complete, a games necklace teleports directly to Tears of Guthix — by far the fastest route, and the one to use every week.

Q: How often can I play Tears of Guthix?

Once every seven days, and only if you also gained 100,000 total experience points or 1 Quest Point since your last visit. Both conditions must be met. A first-ever visit requires neither the wait nor the activity requirement.

Q: Which skill gets the Tears of Guthix experience?

The skill with the least experience points, not the lowest level. Runecraft, Herblore, Construction and Sailing are skipped unless you have met their access requirements (Rune Mysteries, Druidic Ritual, owning a house, and Pandemonium). Ties resolve in favour of whichever skill comes first in the game's internal order, which now leads with Attack, Strength, Ranged, Magic, Defence and Hitpoints.

Q: How much XP is one tear worth, and how do I collect more?

Each tear is worth 60 XP once the receiving skill is around level 30 or higher, scaling down to a minimum of 10 XP per tear using the formula in Section 6. To collect more, play on a Green-Green-Green-Blue-Blue-Blue world so you can never clip a green stream, enter as the first blue stream refreshes, and switch to the nearest blue stream when under 16 ticks remain.

Q: Is there a diary bonus, and should pures play?

The hard Lumbridge & Draynor Diary grants a permanent 10% experience bonus; there is no Varrock diary bonus and no other item multiplier. Pures should skip the minigame entirely, because the XP is placed automatically in your lowest-experience skill and cannot be redirected or refused.
