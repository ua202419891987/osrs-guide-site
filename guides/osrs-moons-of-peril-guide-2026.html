Moons of Peril 2026: Gear Swaps, Tank Setups & Speed Kills

Updated August 19, 2026  |  15 min read  |  By Yanchun Wu
🆕 NEW
🛡️ Level: 70+

Moons of Peril is the boss that punishes players who bring one weapon and hope. Three lunar generals — Blue Moon, Blood Moon, and Eclipse Moon — take turns as the only damageable target, and each one is vulnerable to a different combat style while attacking with yet another. Win here and you are swimming in prayer-boosting and strength gear; lose here and you are usually a victim of a missed weapon swap, not of raw damage. This guide gives you the weakness triangle, the exact gear-swap plan with switch timing, a tank setup that lets you skip most defense, and a speed-kill rotation you can copy. Pair it with our Vorkath guide if you want another fight that rewards clean rotation, and keep the low-gear Vorkath setup in mind if your bank is still thin.

The beautiful part of Moons is that it is mechanically simple once the triangle clicks: you attack the active moon with the style it is weak to, you pray the style it uses, and you swap the instant the active target changes. Everything else — tank gear, speed kills, profit — is built on top of that one loop. Read the weakness section twice before you touch your bank.

💡 Quick Verdict

Moons of Peril is a gear-swap boss, not a gear-check boss. You do not need maxed stats — you need three weapons (or two weapons plus a switch) and the discipline to swap the moment the active moon changes. The single biggest DPS and survival gain comes from using the counter style every time instead of one "safe" weapon, because each moon takes noticeably more damage from its weakness. Build a tank setup that only cares about one defense style at a time, and the fight becomes trivially survivable while your DPS stays high.

1. 🌙 Moons of Peril at a Glance

Moons of Peril is reached through the Lunar Isle, fought at the Lunar Chest beneath the moonlit meadow. You face three lunar generals one at a time; only the "active" moon can be damaged, and the active target rotates on a fixed cadence. Each moon has roughly 300 HP, uses one attack style, and is weak to a different style — which is the entire fight in a nutshell.

① The three moons and their identities

MoonAttack style usedWeak toNotable special
Blue MoonMagicRangedTeleports; magic volley
Blood MoonMeleeMagicBlood pool on ground
Eclipse MoonRangedMeleeEclipse shadow follows you

The mnemonic that saves lives: each moon is weak to the style the previous moon uses, and uses the style the next moon is weak to. Blue uses Magic and is weak to Ranged; Blood uses Melee and is weak to Magic; Eclipse uses Ranged and is weak to Melee. It is a perfect triangle, which is exactly why the fight is a swap drill.

② Requirements & how to get there

You need a modest account to start and almost no skill gates beyond the Lunar questline that opens the area. The practical floor:

RequirementValueNotes
Combat level70+ recommendedEach moon hits for low-to-mid damage
Quest progressLunar Diplomacy doneOpens Lunar Isle travel
Styles neededMelee + Ranged + MagicOne weapon per style, or smart swaps
Prayer43+ (all Protects)You will use all three Protect prayers
Food10+ sharks or karambwanTank setups need almost none

Unlike wilderness bosses, there is no risk on the way in, so you can repeatedly attempt Moons to learn the swap rhythm without losing your bank. That low barrier is why Moons is one of the best early PvM moneymakers for a mid-level account in 2026.

③ Why Moons rewards swapping

Each moon takes meaningfully more damage from its weakness than from a neutral style. Attacking Blue Moon with a crossbow instead of a whip is not a side-grade — it is often a 20–35% DPS increase, and because the fights are short, that DPS difference is the difference between a clean kill and a food drain. The rest of this guide is about capturing that DPS without fumbling the swap.

💡 The One Rule

Attack the active moon with the style it is weak to. Pray the style it uses. Swap the instant the active target changes. If you internalize only one sentence from this guide, make it that one.

2. 🔄 The Weakness Triangle — Style Beats Style

The weakness triangle is the spine of the fight. Miss it and you will over-pray, under-damage, and wonder why the kill is slow. Master it and Moons becomes a comfortable farming loop.

① Blue Moon — Magic user, Ranged weakness

Blue Moon attacks with Magic, so you wear Protect from Magic and answer with Ranged. A crossbow (Rune or Dragon) with broad bolts, or a blowpipe with darts, is the standard answer. Because you are praying Magic, you can wear light ranged gear without worrying about its melee or ranged defense — Blue Moon literally cannot hurt you through the prayer. This is the first hint at the tank philosophy in section 4.

② Blood Moon — Melee user, Magic weakness

Blood Moon attacks with Melee and is weak to Magic. You switch to Protect from Melee and attack with a powered staff (Trident of the Seas/Swamp, or Twinflame if you have it). Blood Moon's signature is a blood pool it drops on the ground that damages you if you stand in it — so you also need to keep moving off the pool while you cast. The pool ignores prayer, so positioning matters even though the melee attacks do not.

③ Eclipse Moon — Ranged user, Melee weakness

Eclipse Moon attacks with Ranged and is weak to Melee. You switch to Protect from Missiles and swing a melee weapon (whip, bludgeon, or better). Eclipse's twist is a shadow that tracks your position; standing still lets it catch you, so keep circling. The melee answer is the highest raw-DPS window of the three because melee weapons hit hardest per tick at this level.

⚠️ Don't Pray the Wrong Style

The most common Moons death is swapping your weapon but forgetting your prayer. If you are on Blood Moon (melee) with Protect from Magic still up, you eat every hit. Re-pray on every swap — it is as important as the weapon change.

3. 🗡️ Gear Swaps & Weapon Switch Timing

Gear swaps are where Moons separates smooth farmers from food-drainers. The active moon changes on a fixed timer, and the window to swap is short — fumble it and you waste hits attacking into the wrong style or, worse, stand unprotected.

① The swap inventory

At minimum you need three weapons: a crossbow (Blue), a powered staff (Blood), and a melee weapon (Eclipse). Many players keep a fourth "spec" weapon (Dragon Claws or DWH) for burst. Your gear can stay mostly constant if you build a tank setup (section 4), but the weapon and sometimes the prayer must change every rotation.

Active moonWeaponPrayerSwap note
BlueCrossbow / BlowpipeProtect from MagicLight ranged gear fine
BloodPowered staffProtect from MeleeMove off blood pool
EclipseMelee weaponProtect from MissilesCircle the shadow

② When to swap

The active moon telegraphs its change with a visual cue (the active target glows, the others dim). The correct time to swap is the instant the cue appears, not after you finish your current attack animation. A one-tick delay costs you a hit; a full missed swap costs you a whole rotation of wrong-style damage. Practice the swap on a dummy or on easy moons until it is muscle memory.

💡 Bind Your Swaps

If you play on RuneLite, use gear presets or function-key binds for each moon's loadout. A one-key swap beats a manual drag every time, and it removes the "wrong weapon" deaths that plague mouse-only players.

4. 🛡️ Tank Setups — Why You Can Skimp on Defense

Here is the counter-intuitive core of Moons: because only one moon attacks at a time, and you are praying that one style, you do not need balanced defense. You only need to not get hit by the style you are not praying — which barely happens. That means you can wear glass-cannon or style-pure gear and still be effectively invincible.

① The "pray one style" principle

While Blue Moon is active you have Protect from Magic up. Blue Moon's only damage is Magic, so its melee and ranged defense on your gear is irrelevant — you take zero regardless. The same logic applies to each moon. The only unblockable damage in the fight comes from Blood Moon's ground pool and Eclipse's shadow, both of which you avoid by positioning, not by defense. So your armor choice is driven by DPS, not by tankiness.

Active moonPrayer upDefense you needGear priority
BlueProtect from MagicNone (Magic blocked)Max ranged DPS
BloodProtect from MeleeNone (Melee blocked)Max magic DPS
EclipseProtect from MissilesNone (Ranged blocked)Max melee DPS

② A workable tank/DPS hybrid setup

Because defense barely matters, a strong Moons setup is just your best DPS gear per style with a single defensive concession: enough Prayer to keep Protect up the whole fight. Many players run Armadyl-range for Blue, Mystic/ancestral for Blood, and Bandos for Eclipse, swapping only the weapon and prayer between them. If your bank is thin, even mixed mid-tier works because the prayers do the defensive work.

③ When a true tank setup helps

A "tank" Moons setup (high defense, low DPS) is only useful if your DPS is so low the fight drags and prayer drains out. In that case, lean into defense so you eat less when a swap is late, and bring prayer restores. But for most accounts past level 70 with three weapons, the DPS-first setup is both faster and safer. The Barrows beginner guide covers a similar "pray one style" mindset if you want to see the principle applied elsewhere.

The budget build uses mixed mid-tier with one defensive nod (a blessed d'hide body for the ranged moon); the BiS build uses the strongest DPS gear per style and relies entirely on prayers. Notice both bring the same three weapons and the same prayer plan — the difference is pure DPS, not survival.

The budget build still clears Moons comfortably because the prayers do the defensive work; the BiS build just kills faster and banks more per hour. If you are deciding where to spend, upgrade your weapons before your armor — Moons rewards the three-style kit, not one flashy piece.

⑤ Mistakes that break the tank plan

Two habits quietly undermine the tank setup. First, over-investing in defense gear you do not need: a player who wears full Bandos to tank Eclipse is wasting the melee DPS they could get from a Scythe, because Eclipse is already blocked by Protect from Missiles. Second, forgetting that Blood's pool and Eclipse's shadow ignore prayer — no amount of defense helps there, only footwork. The tank plan is real, but it is a prayer-plus-positioning plan, not an armor plan. Keep that distinction sharp and Moons stays easy far past the point where your stats say it should be hard.

⚠️ Prayer Drain Is the Real Threat

The one thing that breaks the "pray one style" plan is running out of prayer points mid-fight, which drops your protection and lets the active moon hit you. Bring prayer potions or a holy wrench, and consider a prayer restore in the inventory so a long fight never leaves you unprotected.

5. ⚡ Speed Kills — Rotation, Order & DPS

Speed killing Moons is about two things: never attacking into the wrong style, and never standing in unblockable damage. Do both and your kills drop under a minute per cycle, which is what makes the boss a moneymaker.

① Kill order

The active moon rotates on a fixed order (Blue → Blood → Eclipse → repeat). You do not choose the order, but you can choose which moon to "soften" first if you enter mid-rotation. The standard plan is simply to always damage the active target at full counter-style DPS; trying to "save" a moon for later just wastes time. If you enter on Eclipse, kill Eclipse hard with melee, then swap cleanly to Blue.

② DPS checks per moon

Each moon has about 300 HP. With the correct counter style you should kill one in roughly 15–25 seconds depending on gear. The speed ceiling is set by your worst weapon — if your ranged setup is weak, Blue Moon drags and the whole cycle slows. Invest in a balanced three-style kit rather than one god-tier weapon, because Moons uses all three equally.

MoonCounter weapon (mid)Est. kill timeSpeed tip
BlueRune/Dragon crossbow~20sBind bolts for consistency
BloodTrident of the Swamp~22sKeep moving off blood pool
EclipseAbyssal Whip~16sCircle to dodge shadow

③ The speed-kill loop

Clean loop: (1) confirm active moon, (2) weapon + prayer swap, (3) attack at counter style, (4) dodge the moon's special (pool/shadow/teleport), (5) swap on cue. Repeat. The faster your swaps, the tighter your loop, and the more cycles per hour. Players who master this routinely pull 30+ kills per hour, which is where Moons' profit comes from. Compare the rotation discipline to Zulrah's rotation guide — both reward pattern memory over raw stats.

④ Beginner to speedrunner progression

If you are new to Moons, do not try to hit 30 kills per hour on day one. The realistic path is: first, learn the weakness triangle cold so you never attack the wrong style; second, practice the weapon-and-prayer swap until it is instant; third, tighten your rotation to sub-25-second moon kills; only then chase volume. Most players who cannot speed kill Moons are actually still fumbling swaps or forgetting to re-pray, and no amount of best-in-slot gear fixes that. Record a few kills, watch where your time goes, and you will almost always find the lost seconds in a missed swap or a stood-still moment rather than in your weapon tier. Fix the habit first, buy the gear second, because the gear only amplifies a rotation you have already made clean. The discipline transfers directly to Vorkath and Zulrah, where pattern memory matters even more.

One last note for impatient players: the gap between a 15-kill hour and a 30-kill hour is almost never gear, it is swap cleanly and never stand in a pool or shadow. Film yourself once and the mistakes become obvious, and the fix is free.

💡 Don't Over-Pray

You only ever need ONE Protect prayer up at a time in Moons. Running Piety or Rigour alongside it is fine for DPS, but never waste prayer points on a second Protect — it does nothing and drains your buffer. One Protect, one offensive prayer, that is the whole prayer plan.

6. 📊 Loot, Profit & Drop Table

Moons of Peril drops a focused, high-value table built around prayer and strength gear, which is why it stays relevant deep into an account's life. The uniques are the draw; the consistent alchables fill the gaps.

The uniques are not mega-rares, so your expected value per kill is steady rather than lottery-driven. That makes Moons a reliable earner: you are not waiting on a 1/500 drop, you are banking a stream of mid-value loot plus the occasional unique.

② Profit reality in 2026

Per kill, Moons returns a mix of alchables and unique-value spread across your kills. At 30+ kills per hour with clean speed kills, the hourly is competitive with other mid-level bosses and far safer than wilderness content. The loot scales with how fast you cycle, so every swap you sharpen is direct GP. For a broader money-making context, the Vorkath money guide (if you have it) shows the next step up once your stats climb.

③ What to do with the loot

Alch the junk, keep or sell the uniques based on your account needs, and bank the antlers for any moon-themed upgrades. Because the table is small, inventory management is easy — a stack of food plus your three weapons plus a few alchables is a complete trip. The fight's low complexity is exactly why it is a favorite for passive, long-session farming.

💡 Farm the Weakness, Not the Rare

Do not stall a kill hoping for a unique — the uniques come from volume, and volume comes from fast, clean cycles. The player who speed-kills 200 Moons walks away richer than the one who drags 50 hoping to hit a rare.

7. ❓ FAQs — Moons of Peril 2026

Which combat style is each moon weak to?

Blue Moon is weak to Ranged, Blood Moon is weak to Magic, and Eclipse Moon is weak to Melee. Each moon uses the style the next one is weak to, forming a perfect triangle: Blue uses Magic, Blood uses Melee, Eclipse uses Ranged.

Do I need three separate weapons?

Yes, effectively. You need a Ranged weapon for Blue, a Magic weapon for Blood, and a Melee weapon for Eclipse. You can technically use one style for all three, but you will deal far less damage and the kills will drag. Three weapons is the baseline for a real Moons setup.

Why can I wear glass-cannon gear at Moons?

Only one moon attacks at a time, and you pray that one style, so its hits are blocked regardless of your defense. The only unblockable damage is Blood's ground pool and Eclipse's shadow, both avoided by moving, not by armor. So your gear should maximize DPS, not defense.

When should I swap weapons?

The instant the active moon changes — the target glows to show it is now damageable. Swap weapon and prayer together at that cue. A one-tick delay costs a hit; a missed swap costs a whole rotation of wrong-style damage and can drop your prayer protection if you forget to re-pray.

What is the biggest mistake at Moons?

Swapping the weapon but not the prayer. If Blood Moon (melee) is active and you still have Protect from Magic up, you eat every hit. Re-pray on every swap — it is as important as the weapon change. The second biggest mistake is standing in Blood's pool or Eclipse's shadow.

How many kills per hour is good?

A clean speed-kill loop runs 30+ kills per hour. Early learners might do 15–20 as they build swap muscle memory. The hourly profit scales directly with cycle speed, so sharpening your swaps is the highest-leverage improvement you can make.

Is Moons good money in 2026?

Yes for mid-level accounts. The loot table is small and high-value, the uniques are steady rather than lottery-rare, and there is no risk route to the arena. At 30+ kills per hour it competes with other mid bosses and is far safer than wilderness content.

What stats do I need to start?

Combat 70+ is a comfortable floor, with 43+ Prayer for all three Protect prayers and at least modest levels in melee, ranged, and magic so your three weapons hit. Lunar Diplomacy must be done to reach the area. No huge stat gates — the fight is a skill check, not a level check.

Can I solo Moons or do I need a team?

Solo is the standard and the most profitable per player, since you keep the whole loot table. There is no mechanical reason to bring a team; Moons is a one-player rotation drill. Team up only if you are learning and want a safety net, but expect to split the loot.

How does Moons compare to Zulrah or Vorkath?

All three reward rotation memory over raw stats, but Moons is the easiest entry: no stealth phase, no precise pillars, just a clean swap drill. Zulrah and Vorkath pay more per kill at higher stats. Moons is the best "first real boss" for a mid account before stepping up to Vorkath or Zulrah.
