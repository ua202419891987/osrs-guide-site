Third-Party Tool Risk: A Green / Yellow / Red Rating (2026)

Updated August 19, 2026  |  15 min read  |  By Yanchun Wu

Not every OSRS tool outside Jagex is a scam — but the line between "helpful" and "catastrophic" is sharper than most players think. This guide gives you a simple three-color rating system, a named-tool table you can bookmark, the exact questions to judge any tool yourself, and the recovery steps if you ever touched a red one.

📅 Updated: August 2026 — Tool Safety · 16 min read
🛡️ Level: All
🆕 NEW

The safe rule is short: anything Jagex ships (the official client, the Jagex Launcher, RuneLite from a verified official domain) is Green, and everything that asks for your password or promises banned advantages is Red. For the wide Yellow middle — price trackers, clan tools, reputable databases — follow the judgment checklist below and keep your account security habits tight.

⚡ Quick Verdict

  • 🟢 Green = official ecosystem: Jagex Launcher, RuneLite (runelite.net), the official OSRS client, the official Wiki and Plugin Hub. Use freely.
  • 🟡 Yellow = acceptable, cautious: reputable web tools (price trackers, hi-score sites), known open-source plugins, clan Discords. Safe if you never enter game credentials.
  • 🔴 Red = high risk / scam: fake clients, GP generators, RWT/account selling, botting clients, "recovery" fees, phishing clones. Avoid entirely.
  • 📌 The password test decides most cases: if a tool asks for your Jagex login or 2FA, it is Red, full stop.
  • 📌 Downloaded executables are riskier than web pages — a jar/exe runs on your machine; a website just renders.

1. 🟢🟡🔴 The Three-Color System

Color ratings exist because players keep asking the wrong binary question — "is this a scam or not?" The useful question is "how much risk am I accepting, and what is my exposure?" Three colors capture that spectrum without false precision.

① 🟢 Green — safe by design

Green means the tool is part of the official ecosystem or is universally trusted open-source infrastructure maintained by known parties. Using it carries essentially the same risk as playing the game normally. Green tools never ask for your Jagex password (the Launcher handles auth itself), and their code is public or Jagex-operated.

② 🟡 Yellow — acceptable, treat as untrusted input

Yellow means the tool is helpful and typically reputable, but it sits outside Jagex's control. Web-based price trackers, hi-score databases, clan Discords, and well-known open-source plugins fall here. They are fine to use as long as you never hand them your game credentials and you keep them sandboxed from your account. The risk is not "this will steal from you" but "if it is compromised or turns malicious, your exposure is limited by what you gave it."

③ 🔴 Red — do not touch

Red means the tool is either an active scam (fake client, GP generator, phishing clone) or a rule-breaking service that will get you banned and frequently steals from you too (botting clients, RWT/account selling). There is no safe way to use a Red tool. The cost is always higher than the promised benefit.

2. 🟢 Green: The Official Ecosystem

These are the tools you can use without a second thought. They are operated by Jagex or by the open-source RuneLite project, and they are the baseline against which everything else is judged.

① Jagex Launcher & official client

The Jagex Launcher is the sanctioned way to run Old School RuneScape and RuneLite on desktop. It manages authentication, signs the official clients, and updates them in place, so you never handle a raw executable. This is the Gold Standard of Green.

② RuneLite from runelite.net

RuneLite is the leading open-source third-party client, maintained by a public team and distributed only via runelite.net and the Jagex Launcher. Its source is public, its plugins go through a curated Plugin Hub, and it never asks for your Jagex password (the Launcher does). Note the domain discipline: only runelite.net is Green; every lookalike is Red (see the client-malware guide).

③ Official Wiki, hi-scores & Plugin Hub

The Old School RuneScape Wiki (oldschool.runescape.wiki) is the community encyclopedia Jagex links to and is Green for reference. The official hiscores and the in-client Plugin Hub are Jagex-operated or Jagex-curated and safe. Anything you reach through the official client or jagex.com is Green by definition.

💡 Green rule of thumb

If the tool is operated by Jagex or by the public RuneLite project and you reached it through its official domain or the Launcher, it is Green. When a "Green" tool is offered through a different link, re-rate it — the link, not the brand, is what matters. Verify any domain with our domain checker guide.

④ Why official tools can be trusted

The reason Green tools are safe is not that Jagex is benevolent but that the trust is structural, not promised. The Jagex Launcher authenticates you itself and signs the clients it runs, so a malicious build cannot be substituted without breaking the signature. RuneLite's code is public, meaning any backdoor would be visible to thousands of reviewers and would be caught quickly — the opposite of a closed, anonymous jar from a DM. The Plugin Hub is curated, so plugins cannot silently add a loader the way an external repo can. None of these protections exist for a tool you downloaded from a stranger. That structural difference is exactly why the color system puts official tools in Green and everything else lower: trust is earned by architecture, not by a friendly message.

3. 🟡 Yellow: Acceptable but Cautious

The Yellow zone is where most useful third-party tools live. None are official, but many are reputable and widely used. The discipline is: use them, but never feed them your account.

① Web price trackers & databases

Sites like GE Tracker, the Wiki's Exchange pages, and item databases are Yellow. They are read-only market information you could gather in-game; they do not need your login. They become Red the moment they ask you to "connect your account" with your Jagex password — that is never required to look up a price.

② Hi-score & progress trackers

Tools such as Wise Old Man and Temple OSRS pull public hi-score data (your username is public) to chart progress. Yellow because third-party, but safe as long as you only ever provide your display name, never your password. If a progress tool demands your Jagex login, treat it as Red.

③ Known open-source plugins (outside the Hub)

Some legitimate plugins live on public GitHub with a real contributor history but are not in the official Plugin Hub. Yellow: useful, but you are running unaudited code. Read the source if you can; if you cannot, the safe answer is to skip it. This is the exact boundary discussed in our account security guide — unverified code is untrusted code.

④ Clan Discords & third-party bots

Your clan's Discord, a loot tracker bot, or a scheduling bot are Yellow. They are convenient but outside Jagex's control. Keep them off your main email, never paste account tokens into them, and be alert to the admin-impersonation angle covered in the Discord admin scam guide.

⑤ OSBuddy and similar legacy clients

Clients like OSBuddy are real third-party clients, not outright scams, but they are closed-source and less transparent than RuneLite. Yellow at best — acceptable if you insist, but RuneLite via the official source is the safer pick. Any "OSBuddy" download from a non-official page is Red.

🟡 The Yellow user contract

  • Never enter your Jagex password or 2FA into a Yellow tool.
  • Prefer web tools over downloaded executables when both exist.
  • Use a display name only; never link account auth.
  • Keep a separate, low-value email for third-party sign-ups.
  • If the tool's reputation shifts (bought out, hacked, asks for logins), re-rate it Red immediately.

4. 🔴 Red: High-Risk & Scam

Red tools are never worth it. They fall into two buckets: active scams that steal from you, and rule-breaking services that get you banned (and often steal from you anyway).

① Fake clients & phishing clones

Lookalike RuneLite/OSRS sites, modded mobile APKs, and "fixed client" DMs are Red. They deliver trojans that drain your bank (full mechanics in our client-malware guide). The mobile variant is covered in the fake-app security guide — only official store apps are safe.

② "Free GP" generators & dupe tools

Any site or program claiming to generate GP, items, or bonds is Red. GP cannot be created outside the game; these are either malware droppers or lead-in scams that ask for "verification fees." There is no generator. Full stop.

③ Botting clients

Standalone bot clients (scripting/automation software) are Red on two counts: they violate Jagex's rules and will get your account banned, and they frequently bundle account-stealing payloads. The 2026 bot-client crackdowns (see our bot client shutdown guide) show how aggressively these are pursued. Even "private" bots are a ban and a theft risk.

④ RWT & account selling

Buying/selling GP or accounts (real-world trading) is explicitly banned and is Red: the seller often never delivers, the buyer's account gets banned, and payment details get harvested. "Official-looking" RWT storefronts are phishing fronts. Never engage.

⑤ "Recovery" & "unban" services for a fee

After a hack or ban, actors DM offering to "recover your account" or "unban you" for payment. Jagex does not work through third parties; these are second-stage scams. The only legitimate path is Jagex Support, detailed in the emergency checklist.

⚠️ Red is red even "just to look"

Downloading a Red executable to "check it" still runs the malware. Visiting a phishing clone and typing your password still hands over the token. You do not need to "complete" the scam to be hurt — initiating contact with a Red tool is the damage. Delete, don't inspect.

6. 🔍 How To Judge Any Tool Yourself

You will meet tools this table does not cover. Use this decision flow every time. Answer in order; the first Red answer wins.

① The decision questions

  1. Does it ask for my Jagex password or 2FA? → Red. No legitimate tool needs these; the Launcher handles auth.
  2. Did I reach it from its official domain, or from a DM / ad / link? → If not the official domain, Red until proven otherwise.
  3. Does it promise banned advantages (GP generation, bots, dupes, free membership)? → Red. Those cannot be delivered legitimately.
  4. Is it a downloaded executable (jar/exe) from outside the official source? → At minimum Yellow; treat as Red unless provably the official build.
  5. Is it open source with a real, public contributor history? → Green/Yellow depending on whether it is the official project.
  6. Is it a web tool that needs only public info (prices, display name)? → Yellow; safe if you give no credentials.

② The exposure principle

Even within Yellow, your risk equals what you exposed. A price tracker you never logged into can do nothing to your account. The same tracker, if you typed your Jagex password into it, becomes a full account-takeover vector. Color is the tool; exposure is your behavior. Keep exposure at zero on everything that is not Green.

③ Reputation is not permanence

A Yellow tool can turn Red overnight if it is acquired by a bad actor, suffers a breach, or quietly adds a login requirement. Re-check the questions whenever a tool changes hands or behavior. The prevention guide explains why "it was fine yesterday" is not a security guarantee. Real-world examples are common: a popular modded client gets sold to a new owner who ships a "update" that harvests logins, or a price site adds an innocuous "sync your account for alerts" button that is really a credential phish. The pattern is always the same — trust is built slowly, then exploited in a single change. The only defense is to re-apply the judgment questions on every meaningful update, not just on first use.

7. 🛡️ Safe Habits & If You Slipped

The rating system only protects you if it changes behavior. Here are the durable habits, plus the exact response if you ever used a Red tool.

① Daily habits

  • Bookmark runelite.net and jagex.com; never navigate to clients via links.
  • Run OSRS through the Jagex Launcher; avoid raw executables.
  • Enable Jagex account 2FA and set a bank PIN.
  • Use a unique, strong email password for your Jagex account.
  • Treat any DM offering a tool, GP, or "help" as hostile by default.

② If you used a Red tool

🚑 Red-tool exposure response

  1. Disconnect the machine from the network.
  2. From a clean device, change your email and Jagex passwords and force-logout all sessions.
  3. Enable/replace 2FA; if it was rebound by an attacker, open a Jagex Support recovery ticket.
  4. Rotate every password saved on that machine (the infostealer grabbed them all).
  5. Run a full antivirus scan after reconnecting, then monitor the account for strange logins.
  6. Follow the detailed steps in the emergency checklist if access is already lost.

③ If you only visited, did not run

If you landed on a phishing clone but typed nothing and downloaded nothing, you are likely fine — but change your password anyway as a precaution and clear the browser. If you typed your password, assume it is compromised and follow the full response above. The bank-drained guide covers the phishing side in full.

💡 The 5-second habit that beats every scam

Before installing or logging into any OSRS tool, ask out loud: "Is this Jagex or RuneLite, reached from its official domain, and did it ask for my password?" If the answer is not a clean yes-yes-no, it is Yellow at best and Red at worst. That single question prevents almost every tool-related compromise.

8. ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Is RuneLite safe to use?

Yes — when obtained from runelite.net or the Jagex Launcher. RuneLite is the official open-source third-party client, its plugins go through a curated Hub, and it never asks for your Jagex password. The danger is only the fake "RuneLite" distributed elsewhere.

Are web price trackers like GE Tracker safe?

They are Yellow: reputable and useful, but third-party. Safe as long as you never enter your Jagex login — looking up prices needs no authentication. If such a site ever asks for your password, re-rate it Red and leave.

Is OSBuddy a scam?

Not an outright scam, but it is closed-source and less transparent than RuneLite, so we rate it Yellow at best. The safer choice is RuneLite from the official source. Any "OSBuddy" download from a non-official page is Red.

What makes a tool Red rather than Yellow?

Red means it either steals (fake clients, GP generators, phishing clones, recovery fees) or breaks Jagex rules and commonly steals too (botting clients, RWT/account selling). The password test is decisive: anything asking for your Jagex login or 2FA is Red.

Can a Yellow tool ever become Red?

Yes. A reputable tool can turn Red if it is bought by a bad actor, breached, or adds a login requirement. Re-run the judgment questions whenever a tool changes ownership or behavior, and downgrade immediately if anything looks off.

Are botting clients worth the risk for GP?

No. They are Red: they violate Jagex's rules (certain ban) and frequently bundle account-stealing malware. The 2026 crackdowns show how aggressively these are pursued. The GP you "save" is dwarfed by the value of a banned or drained account.

I only visited a fake site but typed nothing. Am I okay?

Probably yes, but change your password as a precaution and clear the browser cache/cookies. If you typed your Jagex password, assume compromise and follow the red-tool response steps immediately.

Why are downloaded executables riskier than websites?

A website only renders in your browser; a downloaded jar/exe actually executes code on your machine, where it can read files, tokens, and credentials. That is why a non-official executable is at minimum Yellow and usually Red, while a read-only web tool can stay Yellow.

Should I trust a plugin recommended in my clan Discord?

Only if it is in the official Plugin Hub or a public, well-known GitHub project you can read. A repo link pasted in chat is Yellow at best and often Red — exactly the delivery method fake-client scammers use. Verify before installing.

Where do I report a Red tool or site?

Report the in-game actor via the report button, warn your clan moderators, and report the domain to the hosting/registry where possible. If the tool touched your account, follow the emergency checklist and open a Jagex Support ticket. Reporting will not refund you but shrinks the scam's reach.
