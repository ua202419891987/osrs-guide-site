OSRS Raid Scale & Requirements: Stop Getting Kicked From Teams (2026)

You cleared the bosses. You met the gear checks. Then you got removed from the team before the raid even started. This guide explains exactly why — CoX scale math, ToB's mandatory voice rule, ToA's split reality — and gives you the checklist that keeps you in the raid. Pair it with our Raid Entry Requirements page if you are still unsure whether your account is even raid-ready.

🛡️ Level: 1500+

⏱️ Quick Summary — 30-Second Read

  • 📌 Most kicks are scale/role mismatches, not skill. CoX teams size to the loot split; ToB needs 4 roles + voice; ToA splits by points, not effort.
  • 📌 CoX scales 1–100+. Small teams = bigger individual split but harsher gear checks; read the scale before you apply.
  • 📌 ToB is voice-only. No mic = no invite on 99% of teams. Roles (tank / maiden / bloat / nylo / verzik) are assigned before you load in.
  • 📌 ToA splits by raid points. A low-invocation runner earns a thin share — that is the "low split" complaint, and it is by design.
  • 📌 The LFG门槛 is a gear+KC wall. Clan guesting and a clean ToB/CoX learner run beat spamming world 385 every time.

Getting removed from a raid team is the single most common frustration for players crossing from solo bossing into group content. You did the prep, you brought the supplies, you showed up on time — and yet the host removed you before the first room loaded. The instinct is to blame "toxic LFG" or "random bad luck," but almost every kick follows a predictable, mechanical logic that has nothing to do with whether you are a good player. Raid leaders are optimising for one outcome: a clean, fast, split-worthy clear with the lowest possible chance of a wipe. If your presence makes that outcome less likely — because of scale, voice, role, or invocation mismatch — you are out, regardless of how close your stats look on paper. This guide breaks down the three raids individually, shows you the exact mismatch that gets each one wrong, and hands you a reusable checklist so the next application you send is one you actually survive.

The fastest way to internalise this is to stop thinking of raids as "bosses you do with friends" and start thinking of them as contracts. Every raid has an unspoken contract: a scale, a voice rule, a role split, or an invocation level that the team agreed on before you clicked apply. When you honour the contract, you sail in. When you silently violate it — even by accident — you get kicked. The pages linked throughout this guide (Raid Entry Requirements, Boss Gear Checklist) tell you the hard stat and KC gates; this page tells you the softer, human contract that decides whether you keep your slot.

1. 🚪 Why You Keep Getting Kicked

Getting removed from a raid team stings, but it is almost never personal. Raid leaders optimise for one thing: a clean, fast, split-worthy clear. If your presence threatens that, you are out — regardless of how "close" your stats are. The pattern repeats so consistently that you can predict your own kick before it happens once you learn to read the signs.

There are four repeatable reasons players get kicked, and they map cleanly onto the three raids. Learn to spot which one applies to you and the fix becomes obvious instead of mysterious.

💡 The Four Kicks

① Scale mismatch — you applied to a 5-man CoX but the leader wanted a 3-man for a fatter split. ② No voice — ToB teams will not carry a silent runner. ③ Wrong role/KC — you claimed verzik but have 0 ToB KC. ④ Low invocation — you joined a 300+ ToA but only toggle 50, gutting the team's points. Our Raid Entry Requirements page lists the exact thresholds so you apply to the right team the first time.

The fix is not "get better gear" — it is understanding each raid's unspoken contract before you click apply. Most players treat the apply button like a lottery ticket: spam it everywhere, hope someone accepts. That approach guarantees you land in teams where you violate the contract, and those are exactly the teams that remove you. A slower, intentional application process — read the listing, match the stated numbers, confirm voice — cuts your kick rate dramatically without a single new upgrade to your account.

One more framing that helps: a kick is feedback, not an insult. When a host removes you, they are telling you precisely which part of the contract you broke. Write it down. "Kicked from 3-man CoX, had only 75 range." Next time you see a 3-man CoX listing, you now know to check your range level against the stated gear before applying. Players who treat every kick as data climb out of the kick cycle in a week; players who treat it as drama stay stuck for months.

If you want the full stat and quest gates before you even think about applying, read Raid Entry Requirements first. The rest of this guide assumes your account can technically enter the raid and focuses purely on the team-dynamics reasons you lose the slot anyway.

2. 🏛️ CoX: Scale, Team Size & CM Reality

Chambers of Xeric is the most flexible raid in the game, and that flexibility is exactly why players misread it. CoX has no fixed team size — it scales from a solo speedrun to 100-player mass worlds. The leader picks a scale, and the scale dictates the loot split and the gear expectation. Misjudge the scale and you are either carried (annoying the team) or a liability (getting kicked).

① How CoX scale actually works

The raid's difficulty (and therefore its point value and purple rate) scales with team size and the personal bests of the raiders. A 2–3 man team faces a proportionally harder fight but splits the loose end and unique loot between fewer people. A 20-man mass is trivially easy but your individual share is thin. When a leader says "scaling a 4," they mean four people sharing the reward pool. The word "scaling" is doing real work there: it is the team's statement of how the loot will be divided, and it is the first thing you must check before applying.

The trap is that players hear "CoX team, need 1" and assume any scale is fine. It is not. A host advertising "scaling a 3" has already done the loot math for three people. If you show up and it becomes four, every other member's share just shrank. They will almost always remove the fourth rather than re-scale, because re-scaling changes Olm's HP, the puzzle difficulty, and the entire point economy of the run. So the scale in the listing is not a suggestion — it is the run's foundation.

② Team-size tiers and what each expects

Pick the tier that matches your gear, not your ego

Use this as a rule of thumb when applying. The split per person falls as the team grows, and the gear expectation rises as the team shrinks:

Team sizeSplit per personGear expectationBest for
1–2 manVery highNear-BiS, practicedSpeed / profit chasers
3–5 manHighStrong PvM gearMost LFG teams
6–10 manMediumEntry raid gear OKLearners / clans
15–100 manLowAnything goesMass worlds, fun

If you apply to a 3-man with budget gear, you will likely be replaced before Olm — not because you are bad, but because the split math no longer works for the other two. This is the single most common CoX kick, and it is entirely avoidable: only apply to small-scale teams when your gear genuinely matches the tier.

The chart below makes the trade-off concrete. Notice that smaller teams raise your individual payout, but the unspoken cost is that every slot must pull its weight mechanically. A dead slot in a 20-man mass is absorbed; a dead slot in a 3-man is a wipe.

③ Challenge Mode (CM) is a different contract

CoX Challenge Mode requires the whole team to have a recorded CM completion and tight, pre-drilled paths. Showing up to a CM with only normal-mode KC is an instant kick. CM is not "normal mode but harder" — it is a different run with different puzzles, and a player who has never seen the CM path is a guaranteed wipe. Read the CoX loot & profit guide to see which scales actually pay before you commit, and never apply to a CM listing unless your CM cape or CM KC is right there in your profile.

The unifying lesson for CoX: the scale number in the listing is a promise about loot and difficulty. Honour it exactly. Apply only to the scale your gear supports, confirm CM status explicitly, and you will stop being the person removed before Olm.

3. 🩸 ToB: Mandatory Voice & Role Splits

Theatre of Blood is where most "I got kicked for no reason" stories are born — because the reason is almost always voice or roles. ToB is a coordinated, role-locked raid, and 99% of teams run it on Discord voice. Unlike CoX, where you can quietly contribute DPS, ToB punishes silence immediately because every room depends on live callouts.

① No mic = no invite (usually)

ToB demands callouts: "bloat down," "nylocas left," "verzik purple," "tank swapped." A silent player forces three other people to guess, and guesswork wipes the raid. If a listing says "VC required" and you have no mic, do not apply — you will be removed on sight. This is the brutal but fair LFG门槛 of ToB. The hosts are not being difficult; they are protecting a four-hour investment (learner runs aside) from a single point of failure. Buy a £10 headset, or accept that open ToB LFG is closed to you until you do.

There is a small exception: some learner clans run "type-only" ToB where callouts happen in Discord text chat. That is a legitimate on-ramp, but it is explicitly labelled as such. If the listing says voice and you plan to type, that is a contract violation and a kick.

② The five roles and why claiming the wrong one gets you kicked

Know your role before you load in

ToB splits into clear jobs. Claiming verzik with no KC is the fastest way to a kick, because verzik is the room that ends runs:

  • Maiden — clears crabs, manages reds, calls nylos.
  • Bloat — times the down, tanks the slam.
  • Nylocas — wave clear, often the hardest DPS check.
  • Verzik P1/P2 — the DPS and movement phase.
  • Verzik P3 / tank — the endgame, reserved for experienced runners.

Be honest about your KC. A learner who says "I can do maiden and bloat, first ToB" gets invited far more often than a liar who claims verzik. See the Theatre of Blood guide for a full role breakdown and the exact mechanics each room demands.

The room order below is fixed, and each room hands a callout to the next. The moment one player cannot communicate their room's status, the chain breaks. That is why voice is non-negotiable on real teams.

③ Learner runs exist — use them

Many clans host "learner ToB" with reduced expectations and a teacher on voice. That is the correct on-ramp, not a 5-man speed team. Join a learner run, get your first 2–3 KC honestly, then apply to real teams. The learners who survive are the ones who spent a few runs listening to callouts before they ever claimed a hard room. Patience here pays off: a clean learner KC is worth more than ten kicked "experienced" applications.

4. ⚱️ ToA: Invocation & The Low-Split Math

Tombs of Amascut invented a new player complaint: "the split was too low." That is not a bug — it is the invocation system working as designed. Understanding the math is the difference between raging at your team and understanding you simply joined the wrong lobby.

① Invocation sets the raid's point total

ToA difficulty is chosen by toggling invocations (300 = baseline, 500 = max, with the raid level shown as e.g. "ToA 450"). The total raid points are shared among the team at the end. A 4-man 450-invocation raid has a fixed point pool — and everyone is expected to contribute similar invocation levels. The pool is not per-person effort; it is a single team total divided equally. That single design choice explains almost every "low split" argument in the game.

② Why your split feels small

The "low split" is usually a mismatch, not theft

If you join a 500-invocation ToA but only toggle 150, the raid's final points are dragged down by your low contribution — yet you still take an equal share of the (smaller) pool. Teams hate this, and many will kick low-invocation applicants from high-invocation lobbies. Conversely, if you toggle 500 but the team averages 300, your split looks "thin" because the pool was small. Match your invocation to the lobby's stated level. ③ Soloing vs teaming

If splits frustrate you, solo ToA removes the argument entirely — every point is yours. It demands tighter mechanics, but it ends the "why is my split low" debate for good. For many players, a few solo 300s teach the mechanics faster than a dozen teamed 500s where they were carried and confused. Once you understand the raid solo, you return to teams with the invocation discipline that keeps you off the kick list.

5. ⚖️ CoX vs ToB vs ToA: Side-by-Side

Players who only know one raid often misapply its rules to another and get kicked. The three raids share the word "raid" but operate on completely different contracts. The comparison table below is the cheat-sheet you should screenshot before applying to anything.

Dimension CoX ToB ToA
Team size 1–100+ (host picks scale) Usually 4 (fixed) 1–8 (lobby states level)
Voice rule Often optional Mandatory on 99% of teams Recommended, less critical
Split logic Per-person by scale Equal, role-based Equal, by total points
Main kick trigger Scale/gear mismatch No mic or wrong role Low invocation vs lobby
Learner on-ramp 6–10 man clan teams Clan learner runs Solo 300 then low teams

The single most useful row is "Main kick trigger." If you get kicked from CoX, it is almost always scale or gear. From ToB, voice or role. From ToA, invocation. Diagnose which raid you were in, and the reason writes itself. Cross-reference the hard gates in Raid Entry Requirements and the gear checks in Boss Gear Checklist to confirm you meet the mechanical bar before you blame the team.

6. 🔎 Where to Actually Find Teams (LFG门槛)

The "wild" LFG (world 385 ToB, bus worlds, the in-game friends-chat spam) is the harshest environment and the worst place to learn. Get your teams from better sources and the kick rate drops overnight.

① Clan guesting beats world 385

A clan with a raiding branch will guest you, teach you, and tolerate learner KC that open LFG never would. This single change ends most "I can't get into raids" problems. Our best clans & Discord guide shows how to pick one that actually raids rather than one that just has a Discord logo. A real raiding clan runs scheduled teams, has teachers, and tracks your KC — exactly the structure that keeps you in the raid instead of on the kick list.

② Discord LFG with stated requirements

Good LFG posts state scale, KC, gear, and voice rule up front: "ToA 450, 4-man, 150+ KC ea, VC." That transparency protects you — if you do not meet it, don't apply, and you will not get kicked. The listings that say only "toa need 3" with no detail are the ones that will boot you the moment something feels off, because they never set a contract in the first place. Prefer the verbose, specific posts every time.

③ Build your own team once you have KC

The moment you have ~5 ToB KC or a clean CoX PB, you become the host. Hosting flips the power dynamic: you set the scale, you pick the voices, and you never get kicked again. This is the real endgame of raid survival. Post your own LFG with explicit numbers, screen applicants, and run the raid your way. Most players who escape the kick list permanently do it by becoming hosts, not by getting better at begging for invites.

7. ✅ The 7-Step "Don't Get Kicked" Checklist

Run this before every raid application. Miss one and you are a kick risk. This is the operational version of everything above, compressed into a pre-apply routine you can complete in two minutes.

Pre-apply checklist

  1. Read the scale. CoX team size and ToA invocation must match the lobby's stated numbers.
  2. Confirm voice. If "VC required," have a working mic — no exceptions.
  3. Claim only known roles. State your real KC for each role honestly.
  4. Meet the gear check. Cross-reference the boss gear checklist before applying.
  5. Match invocation. ToA: toggle within ~50 of the lobby's level.
  6. Have supplies. Bring your own food, pots, and (runes/spec weapons) — leeches get kicked.
  7. Show KC proof if asked. A screenshot of your rank beats a typed "trust me."

Two of these deserve extra emphasis because they are the ones players skip. Step 1 (read the scale) is where CoX kicks happen — people apply without checking the number. Step 5 (match invocation) is where ToA kicks happen — people join a 500 lobby toggling 150. Both are pure attention errors, not skill errors, which means both are 100% preventable by a 10-second read of the listing.

💡 When in doubt, guest a clan

Ninety percent of raid-kick pain disappears the moment you stop relying on open LFG and join a raiding clan. You learn faster, die cheaper, and stop begging for invites. The clans guide walks you through picking one that actually runs teams.

8. ❓ Raid Team FAQs

What team size is best for CoX if I want profit?

For pure GP/share, smaller is better: 2–3 man scales give each person a fat slice of the loot pool. But they demand near-BiS gear and clean Olm mechanics. If your gear is entry-level, a 6–10 man clan team pays less per run but lets you actually clear. Match scale to gear — applying to a 3-man with budget gear is the most common CoX kick.

Can I do ToB without voice?

Technically the game allows it, but practically almost no LFG team will take you. ToB needs live callouts to survive the nylocas wave and verzik. Get a mic, or stick to learner clans where a teacher carries the calls. Silent ToB is a wipe waiting to happen.

Why was my ToA split so low even though I did my job?

Because ToA splits by total raid points, not by effort. If the lobby's average invocation was lower than yours (or you toggled low in a high-inv lobby), the shared pool was small and your equal share looks thin. Match your invocation to the stated raid level next time, or solo it.

How much KC do I need before applying to real teams?

Roughly: CoX ~50 PBs before small-scale, ToB ~5–10 learner KC before a real 4-man, ToA ~10 completions before a 400+ lobby. But the bigger gate is role honesty — claim what you have done, not what you hope to do. A learner who is honest about KC gets invited more than a liar with inflated stats.

Is hosting a raid hard?

Less hard than it feels. Once you have modest KC, post your own LFG: state scale, KC, gear, voice rule. You control who joins, set the pace, and never get kicked. Hosting is how most players escape the kick list permanently, and it teaches you the contract from the other side.

World 385 ToB keeps kicking me — what do I do?

Leave world 385. Open ToB LFG is the most toxic, highest-bar environment in the game and the worst place to learn. Join a clan learner run or a Discord LFG with stated requirements instead. See our clans guide for how to find a raiding clan that guests learners.

Do I need max gear to not get kicked?

No — you need appropriate gear for the scale. A 10-man ToB welcomes entry raid gear; a 2-man CoX expects near-BiS. The kick happens when your gear mismatches the scale you applied to, not when it is "low" in absolute terms. Read the listing's gear line and match it.

What is the difference between CoX scale and ToA invocation?

CoX scale is the team size the host chose, which sets how the loot pool splits and how hard the raid is. ToA invocation is a difficulty toggle (300–500) that sets the size of the shared point pool. Both are "the number in the listing you must respect," but CoX's number is about people and ToA's is about modifiers. Violating either gets you kicked for the same reason: you changed the contract after applying.

Can I bring a friend who is under-leveled to a raid team?

Only if the listing's numbers include them. Bringing an under-geared friend to a 3-man CoX or a 500 ToA without telling the host is a fast way for both of you to be removed. Either find a learner team that explicitly welcomes two, or be upfront in your application. Surprise +1s are one of the most common silent kicks.

Why do I keep getting kicked right before the raid starts but never during it?

Because the host audits the team in the last 60 seconds: they check gear, KC, CM status, and voice one final time. If something does not match the listing, they remove you before wasting the run. This is actually good news — it means your in-raid performance was never the issue. Fix the pre-raid mismatch (scale, voice, role, invocation) and you will load in.

Should I lie about my KC to get into a team?

Never. ToB and ToA lobbies almost always ask for KC proof (a rank screenshot or a quick profile check) before or during the run, and a lie collapses the moment you fumble the mechanics you claimed to know. A honest learner who says "first ToB, maiden/bloat only" gets invited far more than a liar who claims verzik. Honesty is also the fastest way to actually learn the raid.

9. 🎯 Final Tips — Stay In The Raid, Not On The Kick List

  • Read the scale before you apply. CoX team size and ToA invocation are promises about loot and difficulty — honour them exactly.
  • Confirm voice for ToB. No mic means no invite on 99% of teams; get a headset or join a learner clan.
  • Claim only roles you have KC for. Honesty gets you invited; lying gets you kicked mid-run.
  • Match your invocation to the lobby. A low toggle in a high-inv ToA drags the whole pool down and earns the boot.
  • Use clan guesting over world 385. It ends most "I can't get into raids" problems overnight.
  • Host your own team once you have KC. You set the scale, pick the voices, and never get kicked again.
  • Bring your own supplies. Leeches who show up empty get removed on sight.
  • Treat every kick as data. Write down why you were removed and fix that specific mismatch next time.
