OSRS PvM Group Matching 2026 — Find Good Teams, Set Clear Rules, Dodge Bad Groups

Updated August 19, 2026  |  15 min read  |  By Yanchun Wu
🛡️ Level: 1500+

Most PvM deaths are not caused by the boss. They are caused by the other three people in the room. A raid that should take twenty minutes turns into a forty-minute argument because nobody agreed on the plan, the gear bar was a lie, or one player bailed at the bank. This guide is about the part of PvM that the combat calculators never teach: how to actually match with a group that finishes the kill, splits the loot fairly, and invites you back next time. Whether you are learning your first Chambers of Xeric or pushing high-invocation Tombs of Amascut, the principles below will save you more deaths than any weapon upgrade. Read it alongside our boss gear checklist so your stats match the group you post for, and pair it with the inventory prep guide so you arrive complete, not just armed.

The good news is that OSRS has more ways to find a team than ever. The bad news is that most of them are noisy, and a surprising number of "groups" are quietly scams. Below we break down where to look, how to write a post people trust, how to read someone else's post, and the exact red flags that mean you should close the chat and walk away. Group matching is a skill like any other in this game — and like any other skill, it is learnable.

  • 📌 Eighty percent of a smooth raid is agreed expectations before the portal opens — spend two minutes in lobby chat.
  • 📌 Post specific requirements and a clear plan; vague "lf raid" posts attract leeches and wipes.
  • 📌 Verify every host in a named Friends Chat or Discord before you teleport — five seconds saves a bank.
  • 📌 Keep your own loot rolls. Any request to "trade host for a split" is a scam pattern.

1. 🤝 Why Bother Grouping Up?

Before the mechanics of matching, it is worth remembering why you are doing it. Soloing is romantic, but group PvM is where OSRS makes most of its repeatable money and where most endgame bosses are designed to be beaten. The Theatre of Blood, the Chambers of Xeric, the Tombs of Amascut, and the Fortis Colosseum all assume a coordinated team. Trying to brute-force them solo is slower, riskier, and usually less profitable than running with people who know their job.

Split the danger, split the cost

A four-person Theatre of Blood run shares the healing burden, the damage checks, and the food cost across four players. One bad tick is survivable because three other people can cover you. That safety margin is the single biggest reason new PvMers should group rather than solo. When a Nylocas spawns on you during ToB or a Warden phase bugs out in ToA, the difference between a wipe and a clear is almost always "did someone else notice in time to help." A group is a live safety net; soloing is a tightrope with no net at all.

Efficiency compounds

Two people with 80 KC each are worth far more than one person with 160 KC, because they act in parallel. A well-matched group clears content faster than the sum of its parts, which means more loot per hour for everyone. Our boss profit comparison shows how much group content outearns solo grinds at the same gear tier — and the gap widens the better the team meshes. The math is simple: a clean ToA 300 run in twelve minutes pays more per hour than a sloppy one in twenty, and the sloppy one is usually sloppy because the group was matched on vibes rather than on a clear plan.

Learning by watching

The fastest way to learn Yama, ToA, or the Colosseum is to sit in a team with someone who already knows it. You absorb callouts, positioning, and panic routines that no written guide can fully convey. A good group is a free tutorial. Many of the best PvMers credit a single patient host — the one who explained the Maiden bomb instead of raging at it — with their entire progression. When you group with people better than you and stay humble, you borrow their experience for free.

The hidden cost of bad groups

Not all grouping is good. A mismatched group costs you more than time: it costs you supplies, it costs you deaths (and the XP and item risk that come with them), and it costs you the emotional will to keep trying. The whole point of this guide is to help you filter the good teams from the bad ones before you waste a single inventory. Bad groups are not rare — they are the default — so the skill of matching is what separates players who progress from players who quit raids after three wipes.

💡 The 80/20 of Grouping

Eighty percent of a smooth raid is agreed expectations before the portal opens. Spend the first two minutes in lobby chat agreeing on mode, roles, and split; it saves twenty minutes of wipes inside. The fight is the easy part — the matching is the real boss.

2. 📡 Where to Find Groups: LFG Channels

There is no single "correct" place to find a team. Each channel has a different culture, a different scam rate, and a different pace. Matching the channel to your goal is half the battle, because posting a learner ToA request in a speedrunning FC will get you ignored, while shouting for a chill Vorkath duo in a hardcore raid server will get you laughed at. Know the rooms.

In-game Friends Chat & Clan Chat

The simplest starting point. Join a busy Friends Chat (FC) for your boss — type the FC name in the friend list search and hop in. Many are explicitly recruiting and will take learners who say so up front. The trade-off is that FCs are often casual, so expect a slower pace and looser discipline. For your first handful of raids, that looseness is a gift: you learn the rooms without the pressure of a sweaty team. Use FCs to build your first 30–50 KC, then graduate to tighter spaces.

The official OSRS Discord & Reddit LFG

The official community Discord has dedicated LFG threads per boss, and r/OSRSGuilds is built for exactly this. These are higher-signal than general chat because posters usually state requirements and the communities self-police scammers. Our best clans Discord guide walks through which servers are worth joining and how to read a server's reputation before you post. Treat the Discord LFG as your main hub once you have some KC under your belt.

Third-party LFG bots and websites

Several fan sites run LFG boards with filters for KC, role, and region. These are useful when you need a very specific comp — for example, "ToA 300, need a deob blocker, EU timezone, no mic" — because you can filter instead of shouting into a void. Just remember that anyone can post anywhere, which is exactly why the verification sections later in this guide matter. A filtered board gets you the right comp; it does not get you a trustworthy team. Verification is still on you.

World 1 / World 2 shouts

Old-school but still alive for entry bosses. Shouting "LFM Vorkath, need 1" works for low-commitment content and is a fine way to fill a last slot. Avoid it for raids — the signal-to-noise ratio is terrible and scammers love crowded worlds where they can vanish into the crowd after a lure. If you use shouts at all, keep it to casual bossing and never to anything that routes near the Wilderness.

⚠️ Never Trade "For The Team"

If a group asks you to trade your loot to a "host" who will split it later, that is a scam pattern regardless of how friendly they sound. Legitimate groups use the in-game loot roll or an agreed split at the chest. Keep your own drops in your own inventory until the split is done.

3. 📝 Writing a Group Post People Trust

A trustworthy post is short, specific, and honest. Vague posts ("lf raid" or "need ppl for tob") get ignored or attract leeches who will cost you the kill. A clear post does three jobs at once: it filters out unqualified applicants, it tells good players you are serious, and it sets the expectations that prevent wipes. Use this structure every single time.

The five fields every good post has

  • Boss + mode: "ToA 250 inv, no intended" not just "ToA". Name the invocation level or HM status so nobody shows up expecting a different fight.
  • Roles needed: "need 1 ranged, 1 deob" — name the gap, not the whole team. People fill gaps; they ignore "need 4."
  • Requirements: "300+ KC, Bowfa or TBow, mic preferred". State the floor so under-geared players self-select out.
  • Plan: "learner friendly, will explain, deaths split". Set expectations on pacing and loot before anyone teleports.
  • Contact: FC name or Discord tag, never "pm me" with no handle. Accountability starts with a name.

Copy-paste template

📋 LFG Post Template

[Boss] ToA 300 inv — no intended [Need] 1x melee (Fang or Scythe), 1x ranged (TBow/Bowfa) [Req] 150+ KC, full crystal or better, comfortable with Wardens [Plan] Learner-friendly, callouts given, deaths split at chest [FC] yourfc — or DM @yourtag

Honesty about your own level protects you. If you are a learner, say "learner, 40 KC, will follow calls" — groups that accept learners are far safer than groups that pretend you are experienced and then blame you for a wipe. A learner who admits it gets coached; a learner who lies gets kicked and remembered. The ToB guide in our Theatre of Blood walkthrough pairs well with a learner-friendly post because you can point hosts at the exact rooms you are studying.

💡 Under-Promise, Over-Deliver

Post slightly above what you need, then show up ready. A post saying "300+ KC" that you meet at 305 builds trust. A post saying "any KC" that you meet at 5 gets you kicked and muted. The gap between your post and your reality is your reputation — keep it small and positive.

4. 🎯 Setting & Reading Gear / KC Requirements

Requirements exist to keep the group alive and fast. They are not insults and they are not elitism — they are the host's attempt to guarantee a clean clear. Learning to both set and read them is core group-matching skill, and it is where most first-time hosts go wrong.

KC requirements: what they really mean

KC (kill count) is a lazy proxy for experience. "300+ ToA KC" usually means "you know the rooms without coaching". If you are under the KC but genuinely experienced, say so: "220 KC but 50 ToA clears, know all rooms". Many hosts accept that because they care about competence, not the number. If you are over the KC but rusty, admitting it prevents a blame spiral later — a rusty 500 KC player who claims sharpness and then dies to the Wardens is worse than an honest 220.

Gear requirements: match them to the boss

Different bosses need different floors. A Theatre of Blood team cares about melee BiS and a Scythe; a Vorkath duo cares about a crossbow and dragonfire protection; a ToA team cares about magic damage and a shadow or sibling. Use our boss gear checklist to know the realistic floor for each fight before you post or apply, and the boss profit comparison to understand why the gear floor exists — better gear means faster kills, which means more loot per hour for the whole team.

Reading a requirement you cannot meet

If a post wants "TBow or Bowfa" and you have a RCB, do not apply and hope. Either build toward it or find a learner group that states a lower floor. Applying under-geared wastes the host's time and tanks your reputation — the OSRS PvM community is smaller than it looks, and hosts talk. The inventory prep guide pairs with gear so you show up complete, not just armed; a perfect gear bar with no brews and no teleport is still a wasted slot.

Mic / comms requirements

"Mic required" usually means the fight needs callouts (ToB, Colosseum, high ToA). If you have no mic, look for "no-mic OK" or text-callout groups. Forcing yourself into a voice group silently is how wipes happen, because the team is calling targets you cannot hear. Respect the comms requirement the same way you respect the gear requirement.

💡 Requirements Are Negotiable With Proof

A screenshot of your stats, gear, and a recent kill log beats a protested "trust me bro". Share evidence, not arguments. Hosts who ask for proof are the hosts you want — they are protecting the group, which protects your loot.

5. 🚩 Red Flags vs Green Flags in Group Posts

You can filter ninety percent of bad groups in ten seconds if you know what to look for. Train your eye on every post you read, because the pattern recognition transfers to voice calls and DMs too. The goal is not paranoia — it is a fast, repeatable screen that keeps you out of the obvious traps.

Red flags (close the chat)

  • "Trade host for split" — classic loot scam. The moment items leave your inventory, they are gone.
  • No requirements at all for a hard boss — "any KC ToB HM, pm" is either a troll or a wipe factory. Real hosts set a floor.
  • Rushes you to join — "hurry only 2 min" pressure is a manipulation tactic to stop you thinking. Slow down.
  • Vague identity — no FC, no Discord, just "pm me" — nothing to hold them accountable when they lure you.
  • Asks for a "deposit" or loan — you never pay to join a legitimate group. Ever. Full stop.

Green flags (apply with confidence)

  • Clear comp and plan — they named roles, mode, and split method. They planned, which means they care.
  • Named FC or Discord — you can verify them and review their history before you commit.
  • They ask about your experience — means they care about the team, not just filling bodies.
  • Learner-friendly wording — "callouts given" signals patience, not ego. These are the best groups to learn in.
  • Established loot rule — "roll at chest" or "deaths split" shows they planned fairness in advance.
⚠️ The "Free Raid" Trap

Posts offering a "free ToB carry, just bring nothing" often end with you being lured to the Wilderness or tricked into an item-transfer scam. Carries are real, but verify the host in a known FC first. A genuine carrier wants a reputation; a scammer wants your items and then silence.

6. 🛡️ Avoiding Scam & Leech Groups

Scams in group PvM are less about skill and more about trust. The boss is the same for everyone; the variable is whether the other three people are who they claim to be. Here is how to stay safe without becoming paranoid — a calm, repeatable verification habit beats both naivety and fear.

Loot-rolling scams

At a raid chest, only the game's built-in roll or a transparent split is safe. If someone says "I'll hold it and trade after", your items are gone the second they log. Keep your own rolls and your own drops. The built-in chest roll exists precisely so no single player holds the loot — use it. Transparent splits (everyone keeps what they rolled, then trades agreed items) are fine only when done at the chest, in front of everyone, with no "I'll sort it later."

Wilderness lure groups

A "group boss trip" that mysteriously routes through the Wilderness (Kril, Callisto, Vet'ion, the Revenants) can be a PK setup. Check the location before teleporting. Our PK avoidance world-hop guide explains how to spot and escape these — the single biggest tell is a host who insists on meeting "just outside the wild" for a boss that has a safe teleport. Real groups use the safe teleport.

Leechers

A leech joins, contributes nothing, and still expects a split. Filter them with a real requirement and a quick lobby check — ask one mechanics question; a leech will dodge it or answer vaguely. "What's our call on the Wardens专人?" separates the experienced from the freeloaders instantly. A leech in a learner group is tolerable once; a leech in a sweaty team is a wipe waiting to happen.

Account "verification" phishing

No group needs your password, bank PIN, or authenticator code. Ever. If a "host" links a "verification site", it is phishing — Jagex never asks for these out of game, and no raid group needs them at all. Report and block, then warn the FC. The new player guide covers the basics of account safety that keep you out of these traps in the first place.

💡 Verify Before You Teleport

Five seconds checking a FC's reputation in the official Discord saves a bank. If you cannot verify them, do not go. "I couldn't be bothered to check" is the most expensive sentence in group PvM, because the cost is your whole inventory.

7. 🤝 Etiquette That Gets You Re-Invited

Your reputation travels faster than your KC. The players worth grouping with talk to each other, and a good teammate is remembered and re-invited for months. A bad one is remembered too — and quietly excluded. Etiquette is not politeness for its own sake; it is the mechanism by which you build a network that always has a spot for you.

Be ready before you say "in"

Show up with the right inventory, potions, and gear already set. Nothing kills momentum like "one sec let me bank" after four people already teleported. Being ready is the cheapest respect you can show a group, and hosts notice it immediately.

Call, don't blame

On a wipe, say "my bad, I misclicked the portal" — not "why didn't you heal me". Accountability keeps the group; blame ends the friendship and gets you dropped from the next run. The death prevention guide is full of the exact mistakes that cause wipes, and owning them is how you stop repeating them.

Know your role and shut up otherwise

In voice groups, one shot-caller talks during execution. If you are not the caller, listen. Spamming callouts causes more deaths than silence, because the team hesitates between two contradictory instructions. Speak on your job, listen on everything else.

Stay for the split

Leave only after loot is fairly distributed. Dipping mid-chest looks like you stole something, even when you did not, and it denies you the chance to confirm the split was fair. The split is the payoff — be present for it.

💡 Be the Teammate You Want

Patient, prepared, accountable. Groups that run smooth will tag you next time — and that network of reliable teammates is worth more than any single drop, because it compounds into hundreds of future clears.

8. 🏰 Discord Clans vs Open LFG

Two models dominate, and knowing which fits your goal saves weeks of bad trips. Most players use both at different stages; the mistake is using the wrong one for what you need right now.

Open LFG (Discord threads, Reddit, FC shouts)

Best for: one-off kills, filling a specific role, learning a boss once. Fast to join, no commitment, but inconsistent quality and no accountability beyond the session. Use it to get reps and learn rooms, not to build a long-term profit engine.

Clans & static teams

Best for: progressing a raid, building a reliable ToB or ToA team, long-term profit. You build trust, get coached, and develop a regular group. The downside is commitment and sometimes drama. Our clans Discord guide helps you pick one that fits your schedule and attitude.

Hybrid approach (recommended)

Use open LFG to learn and fill gaps, then graduate into a clan or static once you have the KC and reps. Most endgame PvMers end up in a static — it is simply more efficient than re-matching every night, because the team already knows each other's tells. The transition usually happens around 100–150 KC on a raid, when you are no longer a learner but not yet a teacher.

When to Leave a Group

Leave politely if: requirements were misrepresented, safety rules are ignored, or loot handling is shady. Leave mid-fight only if you feel scammed or unsafe. Your account matters more than politeness — a bad group that has your items is a group you should exit, not endure.

9. 📊 LFG vs Clan vs Static — Compared

Players ask "which is best?" as if it is one answer. It is not — it depends on your goal this week. The table below compares the three main ways to match, so you can pick deliberately instead of defaulting to whatever chat you happened to open.

Method Best For Speed to Join Trust Level Long-Term Profit Risk of Bad Trip
Open LFG (Discord/Reddit) One-off kills, filling a role, learning Fast (minutes) Low–Medium (verify host) Low (no continuity) Medium–High
In-game Friends Chat Learner raids, casual bossing Fast Medium (named FC) Low–Medium Medium
Discord Clan Progressing a raid, community Slow (apply/join) High (reputation) Medium–High Low
Static Team Endgame speed, max efficiency Slow (earn the spot) Very High (known people) High Very Low
World Shouts Entry boss last slot Instant Very Low (anonymous) Low Very High (scams)

The pattern is clear: convenience and safety trade against each other. Open LFG is fast but you must verify; a static is safe but you earn the seat. Most players should slide left-to-right as they improve — start in FCs and LFG, finish in a static. The mistake is staying in anonymous shouts for anything that touches your bank or routes near the Wilderness.

How to choose this week

Ask one question: "do I need a specific comp, or do I need reps?" If you need reps on a boss you barely know, an FC learner group is perfect. If you need a tight ToA 450 push for efficiency, you need a static or a well-vetted clan team. Match the tool to the job and you stop wasting trips on mismatched groups.

10. 🚀 Your First Group: A Step-by-Step Checklist

If you have never grouped for a raid before, the process feels intimidating. It is not. Run this ordered checklist the next time you want a team and you will match cleanly on the first try.

① Pick one boss and one mode (Day 0)

Do not shotgun "any raid." Choose a single target — say ToA 150, no intended — and study its rooms via our ToB walkthrough or the relevant boss page. Knowing the fight is what lets you post honestly and read other posts correctly.

② Confirm gear and inventory (Day 0)

Run the gear checklist and the inventory prep guide. Screenshot both. You will need them for verification and they stop you applying under-geared.

③ Write the post with the template (Day 1)

Use the five-field template from Section 3. State learner status if true. Post in the official Discord LFG or a named FC. Do not post in world shouts for a raid.

④ Verify the host before teleporting (Day 1)

Check the FC name or Discord tag. A thirty-second reputation check prevents a bank loss. If they cannot be verified, decline and repost.

⑤ Agree the plan in lobby, then run (Day 1)

Spend two minutes on mode, roles, and split. Then execute. Call, don't blame. Stay for the split. You just completed your first clean group — and the host will remember you as the prepared one.

11. ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Where is the best place to find a ToA or ToB group?

The official OSRS Discord LFG threads and r/OSRSGuilds are the highest-signal spots, because posters state requirements and the communities self-police scammers. In-game Friends Chats work for casual learners. Avoid crowded world shouts for raids — the scam rate is higher there and there is no name to hold anyone accountable.

How do I prove my KC and gear without doxxing myself?

A simple screenshot of your quest-log kill count, equipment screen, and a recent log is enough. You never need to share your display name publicly or anything account-related beyond what the group agrees. Hosts want proof of competence, not your identity — keep the two separate.

What if I am under the posted requirements but experienced?

Message the host with proof of experience rather than applying blind. Many will accept a slightly under-KC player who is clearly competent — for example, "220 KC but 50 ToA clears, know all rooms." Do not lie about meeting the floor; that gets you kicked and remembered as dishonest.

Is it safe to let a "host" hold loot for a split?

No. Always use the in-game chest roll or an agreed transparent split where you keep your own drops at the chest. Any request to trade loot to a host for later splitting is a scam pattern, full stop. The game provides the roll precisely so no one holds the bag.

Should I join a clan or just use LFG?

Use LFG to learn and fill roles, then move into a clan or static once you have reps. A regular team is more efficient and safer than re-matching nightly, but it asks for commitment. The hybrid path — LFG early, clan later — is what most endgame PvMers follow, usually around 100–150 KC on a raid.

How do I spot a Wilderness lure before I teleport?

Check the meeting location. A real group uses the boss's safe teleport; a lure insists on meeting "just outside the wild" or routes you through it for a boss that has a normal bank. Our PK avoidance guide covers the tells in detail. If the location feels unnecessary, it is a red flag — verify the host or decline.

What should I do if a group misrepresents its requirements?

Leave politely if you are still in lobby; leave immediately if you feel unsafe or scammed mid-fight. Your account matters more than politeness. Report the host in the relevant Discord and warn your FC. Misrepresentation is the clearest sign a group will waste your trip or worse.

Can I group as a total beginner with no KC?

Yes, but target learner-friendly FCs and posts that explicitly say "callouts given" or "learner OK." State your status up front — learners who admit it get coached, while learners who fake experience get blamed and dropped. Use the boss guides to learn rooms before you post so you are not a blank slot.

Why does my reputation matter so much in PvM?

Because the OSRS PvM scene is smaller and more connected than it looks. Hosts talk in Discords and FCs, and a prepared, accountable teammate gets tagged for future runs while a blame-throwing leech gets quietly excluded. Your reputation is the invite that compounds — protect it from the first post.

Is it worth building toward a static team?

For anyone serious about raid efficiency and profit, yes. A static knows each other's tells, clears faster, and splits fairly by default — which is why our boss profit comparison shows static groups outearn open LFG at the same gear. The cost is earning the seat through consistent, clean reps, which the steps in Section 10 help you build.

12. 🎯 Final Tips — Match Smart, Not Fast

  • Post specific requirements and a clear plan — vague posts attract leeches and wipes.
  • Verify every host in a named FC or Discord before teleporting; five seconds saves a bank.
  • Keep your own loot rolls; never trade drops to a "host" for a later split.
  • Match gear to the boss using the gear checklist before applying — under-geared wastes everyone's time.
  • Be prepared, call don't blame, and stay for the split — your reputation is your best invite.
  • Use open LFG to learn, then graduate into a clan or static once you have the reps.
  • Avoid world shouts for anything that touches your bank or routes near the Wilderness.
  • Honesty about your own level protects you — learners who admit it get coached, liars get dropped.
