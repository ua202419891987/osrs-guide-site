The Four Layers of a Survivable Base

Most destroyed Windrose bases fail for the same reason: they have one wall and assume it holds. A real defense has four concentric layers, and a raid has to burn through all four before it touches your loot. In order from outside in:

  • Layer 1 — Detection: Watchtowers and a patrol ship that spot enemies before they reach the wall.
  • Layer 2 — Wall: A tiered perimeter that slows and damages attackers, not just a single fence.
  • Layer 3 — Turrets: Automated fire covering every approach with no blind spot.
  • Layer 4 — Keep: Your storage core, surrounded by a second inner wall and traps.

This guide walks each layer in the order you should build it. Do not skip ahead — a tier-3 turret on a tier-1 wall just gets knocked out by the first raider who walks up.

The Raid Math Most Players Ignore

Raids scale with your visible wealth. The more stacked chests a passing enemy can see from the shoreline, the higher the raid tier you trigger. Two fixes: build storage out of sight (behind the inner wall, not on the dock), and never leave thousands of refined ingots in one open crate. A hidden, split stockpile triggers tier-1 raids; a glowing open vault triggers tier-3. This single habit removes the majority of base wipes.

Wall & Material Tiers

Walls are not equal. Each material tier adds HP and a small damage-reflection, and the jump from wood to stone is the single biggest survivability upgrade in the game.

TierMaterialHP (per segment)Best use
1Wood~220Temp base only; replace by day 3
2Stone~540Outer perimeter — your workhorse
3Reinforced (iron-braced)~980Inner keep wall
4Steel-reinforced~1600Core vault only; expensive

Build the outer ring in stone, not wood. Wood walls get shredded by the first raid and you will spend more time rebuilding than playing. Save tier-3 and tier-4 for the inner keep where your storage actually sits.

Mistake: Do not build the entire base in tier-3. That much iron early game stalls your weapon and tool crafting. Stone perimeter + tier-3 core is the efficient split.

Do not rush to tier-3 on the outer wall. The iron is better spent on your inner keep and your first tools/weapons. A stone outer ring plus a tier-3 core is the efficient split; tier-3 everywhere just starves your offense and leaves you under-geared for the raids you are trying to survive.

Turret Placement & Coverage

Turrets have a fixed range and a firing arc. The classic error is lining them along the wall — that leaves gaps where two turrets' arcs do not overlap. Instead, place turrets on corners and raised platforms so their arcs cross.

One more turret tip: tar traps slow enemies into your spike row, and a tar patch at the gate can stall a whole raid long enough for turrets to finish them. Layer tar (slow) then spikes (damage) then a final spike row — never the reverse, or the slow does nothing.

Rule of thumb: if you can draw a straight line from outside the wall to your core without crossing a turret arc, you have a blind spot. Four corner turrets on a square base eliminate almost all of them.

Fleet Defense — Your Patrol Ship

Land walls do nothing against a ship-based raid. If you built on a coast, cove, or island, you need a patrol vessel circling the approach. A single small ship with a ballista can intercept landing parties before they reach shore.

Patrol loadout

One ballista forward, one swivel cannon aft, and a ram for pushing enemy longboats away from your dock. You do not need a warship — a converted fishing cutter is enough to break a raid landing. See our Combat & Ship Guide for the exact build.

Set the patrol to circle at max range from your dock. If you hear the raid alarm, hop on and intercept; if you are offline, co-op partners or a hired NPC crew can run the loop automatically once you unlock the crew station.

If you are offline often, hire an NPC crew at the crew station so the patrol loop runs without you. A parked, uncrewed ship does nothing — an auto-patrolling cutter is the difference between "base fine" and "base looted" when you return.

Layered Defense & Trap Routing

Traps are the cheapest layer and the most overlooked. A narrow gate funnel lined with spike traps turns a 10-raider push into a 3-raider trickle by the time they reach your core.

1

Funnel the entrance

Build a single narrow gate instead of a wide one. One entry point is easier to cover with turrets and traps than a wall with three gaps.

2

Line the approach

Place spike and tar traps along the 2-tile corridor before the gate. Raiders cluster in the funnel and eat the full trap row.

3

Inner keep

Surround storage with a tier-3 wall and a second turret. Even if layers 1–3 fall, the core buys time for your patrol fleet to return.

Common Base Defense Mistakes

1. One wall, no layers

A single wood or stone ring is a countdown timer to destruction. Build the four layers or accept frequent rebuilds.

2. Turrets on the wall line

Wall-line placement leaves arc gaps. Use corner placement as shown above.

3. Ignoring naval raids

If you are on water, a patrol ship is mandatory. Land-only defense gets flanked from the sea every time.

4. Hoarding in one chest

Split your stockpile across two inner vaults. A raid that breaches one should not wipe your whole economy.

Day 1–3 — Your First Defensible Base

1

Hour 1–6: temp wood ring

Throw a wood wall around your starter chest so wildlife and the first scout raid do not wipe you. This is disposable — replace by day 3.

2

Hour 6–12: stone perimeter

Mine stone (river banks are fastest) and rebuild the ring in tier-2. Add the four corner turrets now, even if basic.

3

Hour 12–24: inner keep

Build a small tier-3 wall around storage and split loot into two vaults. Place a second turret inside.

4

Day 2–3: patrol ship

If on water, convert a fishing cutter and start the patrol loop. This is what stops naval landings cold.

Do not over-build in the first day — a wood ring that you replace tomorrow is fine. The mistake is staying in wood past day 3, not starting in it. Get to stone, get the four corner turrets up, then optimize.

Turret Tiering & Ammo

Turrets also have tiers. A basic turret is fine early, but by coordinated raids upgrade to the ballista turret — longer range and armor-piercing rounds that actually hurt shielded raiders. Put upgraded turrets on the corners (your coverage anchors) and keep basic ones as gate supplements. Turrets draw from your stockpile automatically but chew ammo in a long raid: keep a buffer of 200+ rounds per turret, and park a storage crate near each so resupply never lags. A turret that runs dry mid-raid is worse than no turret.

Two Proven Base Layouts

Compact vault (solo, early-mid)

A 20x20 stone square, four corner turrets, storage dead center inside a tier-3 inner wall. Minimal wall to maintain, maximal coverage. Best for solo players who log in daily.

Expanded fortress (co-op, late)

A 40x40 stone perimeter with an inner tier-3 keep, two funnelled gates, a patrol dock, and six turrets (four corners + two gate). More to build but survives coordinated raids. Best for groups — see our Co-op Guide for role splits.

Whichever layout you pick, the principle is identical: concentrate your defenses on the approaches, not the perimeter length. A small base with perfect corner coverage beats a huge base with blind spots every time. Build tight, build layered, then expand once raids stop landing.

Everything else in this guide is optimization on top of that foundation — do it after your base stops falling, not before.

FAQ

What is the single best base location?

A river-mouth cove: water coverage on two sides cuts wall cost, and it stays close to inland nodes. Inland plateau is marginally safer but slower to supply.

How many turrets do I need?

Four corner turrets cover a standard square base with no blind spots. Add a fifth only if your base is L-shaped or has a second gate.

Stone or reinforced for the outer wall?

Stone for the outer perimeter (cheap, enough HP). Save reinforced/steel for the inner keep where your storage sits.

Do I need a patrol ship if I am inland?

No. Inland and hill bases face only land raids, so a patrol fleet is wasted. Spend that effort on turrets and traps.

Can NPCs defend my base while I am offline?

Yes — once you build a crew station, a hired crew can run the patrol loop and man turrets. They are weaker than you, but they buy time.

How do I stop raids from destroying my storage?

Surround storage with a tier-3 inner wall, a second turret, and split your loot across two vaults. Layer 4 is what protects the core.

Are traps worth the materials?

Absolutely. A funneled spike row is the cheapest DPS in the game and slows raids enough for turrets to finish them.

What kills most new bases?

A single wood wall and no patrol ship. Both are fixed by day 3 if you follow the four-layer plan.

Should I build on an island?

Only if you enjoy hauling resources across a bridge daily. The defense bonus is real but the logistics tax is high for solo players.

Where do I learn ship combat for the patrol?

Our Combat & Ship Guide and Boss Guide cover loadouts and engagement tactics.

Do decorations help defense?

No — they are cosmetic. Spend that time on walls and turrets instead.

Can I move my base later?

Yes, but destroying placed structures refunds only part of the materials. Plan the location once; a rebuild is expensive.
