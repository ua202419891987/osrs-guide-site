Windrose Newbie Progression Guide: XP from Logs, Not Kills — First 10 Hours

Beginner Route

Killing things does not level you up in Windrose. Reading logs and fully clearing points of interest does. Here is the stage-by-stage route through hours 1 to 10, plus fixes for the three ways new players get stuck.

🎮 Beginner Route · 9 min read · Updated August 2026

⏱️ Quick Summary — 30-Second Read

  • 📌 XP does not come from kills. It comes from reading logs/notes and from fully clearing points of interest. Farming enemies is a waste of hours 1–10.
  • 📌 A camp only counts as cleared when you have looted every container and picked up every readable — killing the last enemy is not the completion trigger.
  • 📌 In co-op, quest progress is tracked per-host, not per-player. Play in the same world every session or your friend's journal will drift out of sync.
  • 📌 A death marker that will not clear is usually a stale cache, not lost loot. Section 4 has the fix before you rage-quit the run.

1. The XP System Nobody Explains

Windrose looks like a combat survival game, so new players assume it works like one: fight things, get stronger. It does not. The tutorial never says so clearly, and it is the biggest reason people bounce off in the first three hours — an evening of clearing patrols moves almost nothing.

Progression here is driven by discovery: captain's logs, journals, notes and letters scattered through wrecks and camps, plus the payout for fully clearing a point of interest. A log you walk past is progression you did not collect.

ActivityProgression valueVerdict

Reading a log / journal / noteHigh, one-timeNever skip one
Fully clearing a camp or POIHigh, one-timeMain engine of hours 1–10
Repeat-farming a cleared campMaterials onlyFine for loot, useless for levels
Exploring new map regionsModerateSail wide early
The one habit that fixes everything: treat every readable object as a pickup. Interact with every book, note, plaque, and log you see, even mid-fight if it is safe. New players who do this hit hour 10 substantially ahead of players who focus on combat, using identical playtime.

2. How to Actually Complete a Random Camp

Random camps are the bread and butter of early progression, but many players "clear" them incorrectly and never see the payout. Killing the last defender does not close the objective — the game wants the location emptied, not just pacified.

Step 1 — Circle before you commit

Approach from high ground or the water and count enemies before engaging. Camps do not scale down for solo play, so three versus eight decides whether you fight or pull. If outnumbered, use the pull-and-funnel patterns in our solo & duo 1vN boarding tactics guide.

Step 2 — Clear from the edges inward

Take out the outer patrols first so nothing wanders into your fight at the centre. Fighting at the camp's edge means fewer simultaneous attackers and an escape route to the shoreline.

Step 3 — Loot every container, not just the obvious chest

Barrels, crates, sacks, bedrolls and shelving all count. Camps frequently place one container deliberately out of sight — behind a tent, under a platform, or on a rock shelf above the camp. If the completion did not fire, that container is why.

Step 4 — Sweep for readables last

Do a slow lap with your interaction prompt in mind before you leave. Logs are the highest-value item in the camp and the easiest to miss because they sit on tables and crates that look decorative.

Common trap: leaving a camp because the fight is over. Two minutes of looting is worth more progression than the entire fight was. If you have to leave early, drop a map marker and come back — clearing status persists.

3. The First 10 Hours, Stage by Stage

Hours 0–1: Survive and orient

Do not build anything permanent. Gather wood and fibre, craft your starting tool and weapon, and place a bed for a respawn point. Interact with every readable in the starting area — the tutorial region has several and they are free progression. Do not sail out before the bed is down.

Hours 1–3: First camps, first logs

Clear the two or three nearest low-level camps using the four-step method above. Your goal in this window is not gear, it is completed POIs and collected logs. Expect to die at least once here; that is normal and cheap at this stage, but read the recovery rules in our death & loot recovery guide before your first corpse run so you do not lose a full inventory to a bad retrieval attempt.

Hours 3–5: Get off your starting island

Build the starter boat as soon as materials allow. Sailing to a second island unlocks a fresh set of camps, wrecks and readables, and wrecks are the densest source of logs in the early game. Prioritise breadth here: touching four islands beats fully mining one. Keep a light inventory so a death at sea is not catastrophic.

Hours 5–7: Consolidate a real base

Now that you know what your loop looks like, place a permanent base with a bed, storage, and your crafting stations in one cluster. Keep it near a harbour so ship trips do not cost you five minutes of walking each way. Resist the urge to over-build — you will want to redesign it around hour 20 anyway.

Hours 7–9: Gear tier two and harder camps

With a stable base and stored materials, upgrade your weapon and armour one tier and start clearing the mid-difficulty camps you avoided earlier. These have more containers and more logs per visit, so your progression rate should visibly accelerate here. If it does not, you are almost certainly skipping containers.

Hour 9–10: Set your next objective

By now the story thread should be open to you and you should have a reliable income of materials. Pick a single medium-term goal — a specific ship upgrade, a region to map, or the next story beat — and stop free-roaming. Players who leave hour 10 without a stated goal are the ones who report feeling directionless at hour 15.

4. The Three Ways New Players Get Stuck

Stuck #1 — Co-op quest progress is out of sync

Quest state lives with the host's world, not with your character. Objectives you complete in a friend's world will not show in your own journal, and vice versa. The fix is procedural: agree on one host world and always play there. If journals have already diverged, the joining player should re-visit the relevant POI in the host world. Connection-side problems are covered separately in our co-op setup & troubleshooting guide.

Stuck #2 — A death marker that will not disappear

You recovered your gear, but the death marker stays on the map, sometimes with a phantom loot bag. This is almost always a stale client-side cache rather than a real container. Confirm your inventory is complete first, then fully quit to desktop and reload the world — the marker clears on a fresh session in most cases. Do not keep sailing back to it, and do not assume you lost items until you have verified against your recovery checklist.

Stuck #3 — "I cleared the camp but nothing happened"

Ninety percent of the time this is an unlooted container or an unread log. Return to the camp and do a full perimeter sweep including elevated spots and the inside of tents. The remaining cases are an enemy that pathed away from the camp during the fight and is still technically alive nearby — sweep the treeline before giving up on it.

5. Hour 10 Checkpoint — Are You On Track?

Run this list at the ten-hour mark. Four or more ticks means you are in good shape; two or fewer means you have been playing it like a combat game.

  • You have a permanent base with a bed, storage and clustered crafting stations.
  • You own a working boat and have landed on at least three islands.
  • You have fully cleared at least five camps or points of interest.
  • You read every log you found rather than skipping the text.
  • You have tier-two gear and materials banked for the next upgrade.
  • You can name your next objective in one sentence.
If you are behind: do not restart. Spend one session revisiting camps you rushed and sweeping them properly. Retroactive looting still pays out, and most "stuck at hour 12" cases resolve in a single clean-up run.

FAQ

Q: Do I really get no XP from kills at all?

Combat is not the progression engine. You fight to access containers, logs and objectives — the reward comes from the location, not the corpse. Grinding respawning enemies gives you materials and practice, but it will not move your progression meaningfully.

Q: My friend's quests are behind mine. Did we break something?

No. Progress is tied to the world you played in. Pick one host world as your shared save and stick to it every session. The joining player can re-register missed locations by visiting them in the host world.

Q: Is it worth going back for a log I skipped two islands ago?

If it is on a route you will sail anyway, yes. Making a dedicated trip for a single readable is rarely efficient — instead, batch missed readables into one sweep run once you have three or four marked.

Q: Should I rush the story or explore first?

Explore for the first 10 hours. The story stays available, and arriving at story content with mapped islands, tier-two gear and a stocked base makes those beats far less punishing than hitting them under-geared at hour 6.
