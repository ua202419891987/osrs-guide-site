The One-Sentence Difference

The Wharf makes ships. The Shipwright's Workshop makes ships better. That is the entire distinction, and it is the one nearly every Windrose guide gets backwards.

Heads up: multiple Windrose guide sites describe these two stations with their functions swapped — some tell you to build a hull at the Shipwright's Workshop, others say the Wharf installs your cannons. Both are wrong. If a guide told you a step that did not work in game, this is almost certainly why.

The confusion is understandable: both stations are waterfront, both are upgrade-chain buildings, both appear in the same crafting tree, and both take ship materials. But they never overlap in function. You cannot build a ship at the Workshop, and you cannot install cannons at the Wharf.

Wharf — Build, Repair, Switch

The Wharf is the shallow-water station you build on the shoreline. It is the only place a new hull ever comes into existence, and it costs 10 Wood + 10 Coarse Fabric to construct. That is cheap enough to be one of your first real base structures.

The Wharf handles four jobs:

  • Building new ships. Every hull — from your starter Ketch up through the Brigantine and beyond — is constructed here.
  • Managing your fleet. Every ship you own lives in the Wharf's fleet list.
  • Repairing damaged hulls. Bring a beaten-up ship here to patch it.
  • Switching your active ship. If you own more than one vessel, this is where you choose which one you are sailing.
Repair budget: the damaged Ketch you find during the quest "I Need a Bigger Boat" takes 100 Wood / 20 Nails / 20 Coarse Fabric / 10 Rope to repair, and you must also rescue 7 crew before it is seaworthy. Stockpile that set before you go hunting for wrecks — it is a much bigger bill than the 10 Wood the Wharf itself costs.

One quality-of-life note worth memorizing: if your ship is anchored somewhere inconvenient, press Esc then K to recall it. That is the fastest way to get the vessel you want to modify sitting next to the Wharf. For base placement advice that keeps a Wharf usable year-round, see our Base Layout Guide.

Shipwright's Workshop — Upgrade Only

The Shipwright's Workshop is a crafting station, not a construction site. You use it to manufacture ship equipment and to manage the four equipment slots on a ship that already exists:

SlotControlsWhy it matters
CannonsYour naval gun batteryThe main damage source in ship-to-ship combat
Hull BracingStructural reinforcementHow long you survive under sustained fire
Naval TacticsHandling and combat doctrineShapes how the ship behaves in an engagement
Boarding Party GearEquipment for your boarding crewDecides how a boarding fight goes once you grapple over
The Workshop cannot build a ship. No hull, no matter what tier, is ever produced here. If you are standing at a Shipwright's Workshop looking for a "build Ketch" or "build Brigantine" option, you are at the wrong station — go to the Wharf.

Think of it this way: the Workshop is a factory that makes parts, the Wharf is the drydock that owns the ship. Parts travel from one to the other in your backpack.

Build Order: Wharf First, Always

1

Wharf (10 Wood + 10 Coarse Fabric)

Build it on a sheltered shoreline in shallow water. Until this exists you have no fleet management, no repair option and no way to build anything better than what you sailed in on.

2

Shipwright's Workshop

Only useful once you have a ship to upgrade. Building it before the Wharf means you have a station full of parts and no hull to put them on.

3

Upgrade your crafting chain in parallel

Both stations depend on the same refining backbone, so keep the workbench line moving alongside them — see our Workbench & Crafting Progression Guide.

A Wharf with no Workshop means a stock ship that never improves. A Workshop with no Wharf means a pile of parts and nothing to install them in. Wharf first, every time.

"Why Can't I Build a Ship?" — Troubleshooting Flow

Work down this list in order. Almost every case resolves in the first three steps.

1

Check which station you are at

New hulls are built at the Wharf only. If you are at the Shipwright's Workshop, the build option will never appear — this is the #1 cause.

2

Check the Wharf is actually built and placed in water

A Wharf is a shoreline structure in shallow water. Confirm it finished building and that you are interacting with the completed structure, not a placement ghost.

3

Check you have the blueprint

Higher-tier hulls are gated behind blueprints. The Brigantine, for example, requires a blueprint purchased from a Brethren of the Coast merchant — see our Brigantine Unlock Guide for the full chain.

4

Check your materials are refined, not raw

Ship construction wants refined inputs — planks, ingots and fabric, not logs and ore. If your stockpile is all raw, the option stays greyed out.

5

Check the biome gate

Later hulls require materials from a second biome. The Frigate, for instance, needs Tarred Planks and Tarred Fabric — you cannot skip straight to it from the first biome.

6

Check your ship is docked nearby

Any operation that touches an existing ship — installing gear, repairing, switching — needs that ship at the Wharf. Recall it with Esc then K.

The Upgrade Workflow That Actually Works

Upgrading a ship is a four-step round trip between the two stations. Doing it in this order avoids the two classic dead ends — gear stuck in station storage, and a ship that "won't accept" the part.

1

Craft the part at the Shipwright's Workshop

Pick the slot you want to improve — cannons, hull bracing, naval tactics or boarding party gear — and manufacture the upgrade.

2

Take it into your character inventory

Do not leave it in the station's storage. The item has to be physically on your character for the ship to see it.

3

Walk to the Wharf with the ship docked

The ship must be near the Wharf. If it is anchored elsewhere, press Esc then K to recall it before you start dragging anything.

4

Drag the part into its matching slot

Cannons to cannons, hull bracing to hull bracing, and so on. Refits are only possible while the ship is parked at the Wharf — you cannot re-equip mid-voyage.

Plan refits as a base visit, not a pit stop. Because both stations and the docked ship all have to be in the same place, batch your upgrades: craft everything you need in one Workshop session, then do a single Wharf visit to install it all.

Once you can field more than one hull, the next question is which variant to actually build — that decision is covered in our Ship Variants Comparison Guide.

FAQ

Which station builds ships in Windrose?

The Wharf. It is a shoreline, shallow-water structure costing 10 Wood + 10 Coarse Fabric, and it is the only place a new hull is constructed.

What does the Shipwright's Workshop do then?

It crafts ship equipment and manages the four equipment slots on a ship that already exists: cannons, hull bracing, naval tactics and boarding party gear. It cannot build a hull.

Why can't I see the option to build a ship?

Most often because you are at the Shipwright's Workshop instead of the Wharf. Otherwise: the Wharf is not built or not in shallow water, you lack the blueprint, your materials are unrefined, or a biome gate is blocking the tier.

Why do some guides say the opposite?

Several Windrose guide sites list these two stations with their roles reversed. If a step from another guide did not work in game, test it against this page's comparison table before assuming your save is broken.

Which should I build first?

The Wharf. Without it you have no ship to upgrade, which makes the Workshop useless until the Wharf exists.

How much does a Wharf cost?

10 Wood + 10 Coarse Fabric. It is one of the cheapest high-value structures in the game.

How do I install an upgrade I just crafted?

Craft at the Workshop → move it into your character inventory → walk to the Wharf with the ship docked → drag it into the matching slot. Refits only work while the ship is parked at the Wharf.

Can I re-equip my ship out at sea?

No. Ship equipment changes require the ship to be at the Wharf. Recall it with Esc then K if it is anchored elsewhere.

Where do I repair a damaged ship?

At the Wharf. For reference, repairing the damaged Ketch from "I Need a Bigger Boat" costs 100 Wood / 20 Nails / 20 Coarse Fabric / 10 Rope and requires rescuing 7 crew.

Can I skip straight to the Frigate?

No. The Frigate requires second-biome materials — Tarred Planks and Tarred Fabric — so you have to progress through the tiers.
