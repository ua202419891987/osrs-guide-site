Windrose Connection Region Mismatch Fix 2026

🛠️ Technical
🆕 NEW

You paste your friend's invite code, hit join, and the server list comes back empty. Nine times out of ten it is not your router and it is not the host's firewall — it is that one person in the group picked a different Connection Service region. Here is how to find them and fix it.

The One Rule That Breaks Everything

Windrose does not run one global matchmaking pool. The Connection Service is split into regions, and a session created in one region is invisible to a client sitting in another. That is the entire bug. There is no cross-region lookup, no fallback, no error message that says "region mismatch" — the invite code simply resolves to nothing and the search returns an empty list.

This is not community folklore. The official FAQ at playwindrose.com/faq lists "When I enter a server invite code, I cannot find the server" as a headline symptom and points at the Connection Service region as the first thing to verify. If you have been tearing through router settings for two hours, check the region first. It takes ninety seconds and it fixes the majority of these cases.

The rule in one line: every player in the session — host and every guest — must be set to the exact same Connection Service region. Not "roughly the same continent." The same list entry.

The nasty part is that nothing tells you who is wrong. The host sees a perfectly healthy world. The person on the wrong region sees an empty search box and assumes the host is offline. Both sides blame the network, and the session never happens. That is why this is worth checking before anything else.

Symptom Checklist — Is This Really a Region Problem?

Region mismatch wears several costumes. If you are hitting two or more of the following, stop troubleshooting your router and go straight to the region setting.

  • "When I enter a server invite code, I cannot find the server." The official FAQ wording, and the classic signature. The code is correct, the host is online, and the result is empty.
  • You cannot join a friend's world at all, even though they can see you online in Steam and you are in the same party.
  • You cannot create/host a session, or your hosted world never appears for anyone else despite you sitting in it for ten minutes.
  • The loading screen hangs indefinitely after a join attempt — no error, no timeout, just a spinner that never resolves.
  • All connectivity services display as "Not Available" in the network settings panel. Windrose reports every connectivity service as unavailable rather than naming the specific failure, so this one is easy to misread as a total outage.
  • It works for some people and not others, with no pattern in who succeeds. This is the strongest tell: if one friend connects instantly and another never sees the server, the difference is almost always their region setting, not their hardware.
Do not confuse this with a hard connection failure. If nobody can connect and the host cannot start a world either, you are more likely looking at blocked P2P traffic or a firewall issue. Those have a different fix — see our Co-op Setup Troubleshooting Guide and the Failed to Connect Fix Guide.

The Three Connection Service Regions

There are exactly three options in the Connection Service region selector. Use these names verbatim when you compare settings across machines — it is a dropdown, so there is no room for interpretation, but people still pick the wrong one because they assume two of them overlap.

Region optionCoversWho usually picks it by mistake
EU & NAEurope and North America share one poolSouth Americans, and anyone who thinks "NA" means North America only
SEASoutheast AsiaPlayers in East Asia who assume SEA covers them, and vice versa
CISCommonwealth of Independent StatesPlayers who auto-picked it during setup and never looked again

The single most common real-world failure looks like this: three friends in Germany, one friend who moved to Brazil. Brazil is not in EU & NA. That one person cannot see the server, and nobody works out why because "we're all on the same region" feels true right up until you check.

The second most common is the opposite direction: someone in CIS who picked EU & NA because the English-language client made it look like the default. Geography does not decide this — the dropdown does.

Practical default: for a mixed international group, agree on EU & NA unless the majority of the group sits firmly inside SEA or CIS. It is the largest pool, so it also gives dedicated servers the best chance of being reachable.

Check the Region on Every Single Machine

This is the part groups skip, and it is the only part that actually matters. Do not accept "yeah I'm on EU" over voice chat. Make everyone read the setting out loud.

1

Have the host stop hosting

Close the session completely before changing anything. A region change while a world is live does not propagate to anyone already connected, and half-applied changes produce confusing partial results.

2

Open Settings → Connection (or Network)

From the main menu, not from inside a running world. The Connection Service region selector lives in the connection/network section of the options menu.

3

Read the Connection Service region out loud

Each person reports one of three values: EU & NA, SEA, or CIS. Write them down. Anyone whose answer differs from the group majority is your culprit — not necessarily the person whose connection is worst.

4

Re-check the person who "already checked"

This sounds insulting and it saves hours. The setting is a dropdown that people scroll past on first install, and it does not always match what they remember picking. Have them open it and read the current value, not the value they believe they set.

If you have more than four players, assign one person to collect answers instead of letting everyone shout. With eight players the odds of at least one mismatch are high enough that a mismatch should be your working assumption, not your last resort.

Switch Everyone Over — In the Right Order

Order matters more than people expect. Changing regions while clients are mid-join leaves stale lookups hanging around, so do it cleanly.

The clean sequence

1. Everyone quits to desktop — not to the main menu, fully out of the game.
2. Everyone sets the same region and confirms it.
3. Host launches first and creates or loads the world.
4. Host generates a fresh invite code.
5. Guests launch and enter the new code.

Generate a new code after the switch. An invite code created while the host was on the old region may keep failing even after everyone matches. Regenerating costs nothing and removes a variable you would otherwise chase for an hour.

Once everyone is in, resist the urge to change the region again "to see if it is faster." Every change costs you a full restart cycle and risks reintroducing the mismatch. Pick a region once, write it in your group chat, and leave it alone. If the group later wants to try a different pool for latency reasons, do it as a deliberate session with everyone present, not piecemeal.

If the same person keeps drifting to a different region after reinstalls or updates, note it. Some players report the setting resetting after a fresh install, which means every reinstall reintroduces the problem silently.

Still Nothing: Force Relay, Then Direct IP

If every machine shows the same region and the invite code still finds nothing, you have ruled out mismatch and you are now in genuine network territory. Work down this list in order — do not jump to the bottom.

1. Force Relay Connection

Windrose normally tries peer-to-peer first. If P2P traffic is being blocked by a router, an ISP, or a firewall, the handshake never completes and you get an empty search or a hang. Force Relay Connection routes traffic through relay servers instead, which trades higher ping for a connection that actually establishes. It was added in patch 0.10.0.4.268 for exactly this situation. Full decision logic in our Co-op Setup Troubleshooting Guide.

2. Direct IP connection

If relay does not help, connect by explicit address instead of by code. Direct IP bypasses the lookup entirely, which sidesteps both region mismatch and most discovery problems. Note that Force Relay and Direct IP are mutually exclusive — the official FAQ states Force Relay "doesn't work with Direct IP connections," so pick one, do not stack them.

3. Port forwarding

For Direct IP to work across the internet, the host's router needs a public IP and both TCP and UDP forwarded for the game port. If some group members connect and others cannot, the host's setup is probably fine and the failing clients are the ones worth investigating. See Port 3478 Fix for Co-op Players.

One more thing worth ruling out: a non-English Windows username can break the connection outright. If one specific person cannot connect no matter what you try, check whether their Windows account name uses non-Latin characters. That was a confirmed bug and it still gets searched constantly.

Dedicated Servers, LAN Nights & Edge Cases

A few situations that look like region mismatch but are not quite the same thing.

Dedicated servers

The region still applies. A dedicated server registered in one region will not appear for clients set to another, even with a correctly forwarded port and a healthy box. If you rent a server and nobody can find it, check the client region before you open a ticket with the host — see our Server Setup Troubleshooting Guide and Dedicated Server Admin Commands.

Same-household / LAN sessions

Players on one local network generally do not need a public IP or port forwarding at all — an internal IP plus the port is enough, and you can connect directly rather than through a relay. Region still has to match, though; that check does not get skipped just because you are on the same switch.

Mixed-platform or mixed-store groups

If your group is split across storefronts, treat region as the first check and the platform as the second. Discovery behaves differently across platforms, but the region rule stays the same.

The group that "fixed itself"

Sometimes a session starts working with no intervention. Usually that means the mismatched player restarted the game and their region landed on a new value, or the host regenerated the code. It does not mean the problem was random — it means you got lucky. Set the region deliberately so it does not come back.

FAQ

Why does my invite code find nothing even though the host is online?

Almost always a Connection Service region mismatch. One player is on a different region from the host, so the lookup returns nothing. Verify all three region options (EU & NA / SEA / CIS) match on every machine.

What are the exact region names?

Three options: EU & NA (Europe and North America in one pool), SEA (Southeast Asia), and CIS. Everyone must select the identical entry.

Do I pick the region closest to me geographically?

Not necessarily — you pick the region the rest of your group picked. A player in Brazil joining three friends in Germany should choose EU & NA even though it is not their nearest option.

All my connectivity services show "Not Available" — is the game down?

Usually not. Windrose reports connectivity as unavailable when the handshake fails, and a region mismatch produces exactly that. Fix the region before assuming an outage.

Some friends connect and one cannot. Is it their internet?

Probably not. When the pattern is per-person rather than per-network, the differentiator is that person's region setting — or, less often, a non-English Windows username.

Do I need to restart the game after changing region?

Yes. Quit to desktop on every machine, then relaunch. A region change mid-session does not apply cleanly and produces confusing half-states.

Do I need to make a new invite code after switching regions?

Yes. Regenerate it. Codes created while the host was on the old region can keep failing, and this step costs five seconds.

Should I enable Force Relay Connection?

Only after confirming regions match. Force Relay trades higher ping for a stable connection when P2P is blocked, and it does not work alongside Direct IP. Read the Co-op Setup Troubleshooting Guide for the decision tree.

Does this affect dedicated servers too?

Yes. A dedicated server registered in one region is invisible to clients set to another, regardless of port forwarding or server health.

We are all on the same WiFi — do we still need matching regions?

Yes. LAN removes the need for a public IP and port forwarding, but the region check still applies.

Can a non-English Windows username really break connecting?

It was a confirmed bug that caused connection failures. It has been addressed, but if one specific player fails every other fix, it is still worth checking.

What if nothing on this page works?

Work through the Failed to Connect Fix Guide and the Co-op Setup Troubleshooter in order, then report it with everyone's region and the exact error text.
