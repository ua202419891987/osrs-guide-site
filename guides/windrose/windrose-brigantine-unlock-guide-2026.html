Windrose Brigantine Unlock Guide 2026

🚢 Ships · Mid-Game
🆕 NEW

The Brigantine is the most important mid-game upgrade in Windrose and the one with the longest prerequisite chain — reputation, a boss kill, a 1,000 Piastre blueprint, and a material list that stalls almost everyone at 200 Nails. Here is the whole path, in order, with the numbers.

The Full Unlock Chain at a Glance

The Brigantine is not gated behind one quest — it is gated behind five sequential steps, and skipping ahead is impossible. In order:

1

Brethren of the Coast reputation — level 2

Requires 100 reputation points with the Brethren of the Coast faction.

2

Defeat the pirate boss Richards

The boss gate. This is the step most players underestimate — see our Boss Guide for the fight itself.

3

Buy the blueprint — 1,000 Piastre

Purchased from a Brethren of the Coast merchant. Do this after Richards, not before — see section 4.

4

Buy Shipwright's Tools — roughly 300 Piastre

A separate purchase. You need 30 of them for the build, and they cost Piastre, not materials.

5

Build it at the Wharf

The Brigantine hull is constructed at the Wharf — not the Shipwright's Workshop. If that distinction is new to you, read our Wharf vs Shipwright's Workshop Guide first.

Total cash requirement: about 1,300 Piastre (1,000 for the blueprint plus roughly 300 for the Shipwright's Tools) — on top of the full material list in section 6. Budget for both at the same time.

What the Brigantine actually buys you

It is worth understanding the payoff before you commit to a five-step chain, because the grind is long enough that motivation matters. Moving up from the Ketch is not a straight stat bump — it changes what you are allowed to do:

  • You can pick your fights. The Brigantine is faster than the Ketch tier, which means you get to decline bad engagements instead of surviving them.
  • You can win the ones you take. A proper Brigantine battery out-guns anything the Ketch can mount, which shortens every naval engagement and therefore reduces the amount of damage you take.
  • You stop being transitional. The Ketch is a hull you pass through. The Brigantine is a hull you sail for a long stretch — which is exactly what makes it worth the full material list.

That last point is the argument for doing the chain properly rather than half-finishing it: every hour you save by skipping a step is an hour you spend longer in a hull you were always going to replace.

One caveat on expectations — you cannot skip past the Brigantine to the endgame hull. The Frigate after it requires second-biome materials, so this is a mandatory tier, not an optional upgrade.

Step 1 — Brethren of the Coast Reputation 2

Before anything else, you need reputation level 2 with the Brethren of the Coast, which means 100 reputation points. This is a hard gate: with lower reputation, later steps in the chain do not become available.

Two things worth knowing about reputation in Windrose:

  • Reputation is map-bound. When you join another player's world, you inherit the host's reputation — so you can legitimately buy faction gear in a friend's world that your own save has not unlocked yet.
  • Grind it early and passively. Reputation work overlaps with the general resource grind, so fold it into your normal loop rather than treating it as a separate chore. Our Resource Farming Guide covers routes that produce faction progress alongside materials.

Do not rush past this step to the boss. Players who beeline to Richards without the reputation often arrive, win, and then discover they cannot complete the purchase anyway.

Step 2 — Defeat Richards

Richards is the pirate boss standing between you and the blueprint. He is a genuine wall for a lot of players — the first boss in Windrose already has a reputation as a hard difficulty spike, and Richards comes later than that.

Pre-fight checklist

  • Gear tier must match your biome. Falling two tiers behind spikes difficulty dramatically.
  • Food buffs up. There is no potion system — food is your entire sustain layer, and boss retries become farming errands if you run out.
  • Bank your inventory. Do not walk into a boss fight carrying a haul you cannot afford to lose.
  • Ship in good repair. If the fight has a naval component, a limping hull ends the attempt early.

Full fight specifics live in our Windrose Boss Guide. The point here is sequencing: Richards is step 2 of 5, and he drops what you need for the steps after him.

Step 3 — Buy the Blueprint (Timing Matters)

After Richards falls, buy the Brigantine blueprint from a Brethren of the Coast merchant for 1,000 Piastre.

Do not buy the blueprint before Richards dies. The materials you need for the build drop from Richards — buying early leaves you holding a 1,000 Piastre blueprint you cannot act on, with 1,000 Piastre you no longer have for the Shipwright's Tools. Kill the boss, then shop.

This is the most common self-inflicted delay in the whole chain. The blueprint is not a shortcut that lets you skip ahead; it is the last purchase, not the first.

Also note the faction merchant requirement — you need Brethren reputation high enough to transact, which is exactly why step 1 comes first.

Step 4 — Shipwright's Tools & Building at the Wharf

Separate from the blueprint, you need Shipwright's Tools, which cost about 300 Piastre. The build itself consumes 30 of them.

With the blueprint and the tools in hand, the hull is built at the Wharf — the shoreline, shallow-water station that constructs ships, manages your fleet and repairs hulls. It costs 10 Wood + 10 Coarse Fabric to build if you have not placed one yet.

Not the Shipwright's Workshop. The Workshop crafts and manages the four equipment slots (cannons, hull bracing, naval tactics, boarding party gear) on a ship you already own. It cannot produce a hull. Several Windrose guides state this backwards; if the build option is not appearing, you are at the wrong station.

Full Material List — and the Nail Bottleneck

Here is the complete Brigantine build cost. Nothing here can be substituted, and the Nails row is where runs stall.

MaterialQuantityNotes
Wooden Planks200Refined from wood — steady, predictable
Nails200The bottleneck — see below
Coarse Fabric120Early-tier textile
Copper Ingots80Also consumed indirectly by Nails
Timber40Higher-tier wood product
Linen Fabric40Higher-tier textile
Rope40Standard crafting
Shipwright's Tools30Purchased, roughly 300 Piastre total
Nails are the bottleneck. You need 200 Nails, and Nails are not a raw resource — they are crafted from Copper Ingots and Iron Ingots. That means the 80 Copper Ingots in the table above are on top of whatever copper your Nail production consumes, and you need a parallel iron supply you probably are not running yet.

Everything else on this list scales with time played. Nails scale with how early you build the copper-plus-iron pipeline, which is why they are the difference between a smooth Brigantine and a three-session stall.

Running the Nail pipeline

  • Two smelters, not one. Copper and iron both feed Nails, so running them sequentially instead of in parallel doubles your wall-clock wait.
  • Over-produce copper deliberately. Copper appears twice in this build — 80 Copper Ingots directly, plus whatever your Nail crafting consumes. Under-supplying copper is the more common failure; iron is usually the easier of the two to top up.
  • Craft Nails continuously, not in batches. Turning 200 Nails into one crafting session at the end is what makes the bottleneck feel like a wall instead of a queue.
  • Never spend Nails elsewhere. Hull repairs and Ketch upgrades both want them. Until the Brigantine is built, Nails have exactly one purpose.

Players who finish the Brigantine quickly are almost never the ones who farmed harder at the end — they are the ones whose smelters had been running for days before they needed the output.

Stockpiling Early & Whether to Upgrade Your Ketch

The pre-Brigantine stockpile plan

  • Run copper and iron smelting continuously from the moment you have both, not when you decide you want the Brigantine.
  • Never spend Nails on the Ketch. Every Nail you put into a transitional hull is 200 minus one.
  • Bank Piastre as you go. Target roughly 1,300 Piastre banked before Richards so the two purchases do not bottleneck you after the kill.
  • Keep Timber and Linen Fabric on the craft queue — at 40 each they look small next to 200 Planks, but they are the slowest per unit.

Should you invest upgrades in your Ketch?

Community consensus: no. The Ketch is a transitional hull — you will replace it with the Brigantine, and upgrades you install do not travel with you to the new ship. Keep the Ketch functional (repairs, cannons that let you farm safely) and stop there.

If you do want to spend on a Ketch, spend on the things that make the Brigantine grind faster, not on the Ketch itself. And when you get to pick which Ketch variant to sail during the wait, our Ship Variants Comparison Guide has the numbers and the recommendation.

One last sequencing note: the Frigate after the Brigantine requires second-biome materials — Tarred Planks and Tarred Fabric. You cannot skip tiers to reach it, so the Brigantine is not optional on the way to endgame ships.

FAQ

What are the full requirements to unlock the Brigantine?

Brethren of the Coast reputation level 2 (100 reputation points) → defeat Richards → buy the blueprint from a Brethren merchant for 1,000 Piastre → buy Shipwright's Tools for roughly 300 Piastre → build at the Wharf.

How much Piastre do I need in total?

Roughly 1,300: 1,000 for the blueprint and about 300 for the Shipwright's Tools.

How many reputation points is Brethren level 2?

100 reputation points with the Brethren of the Coast.

Should I buy the blueprint before killing Richards?

No. The build materials drop from Richards, so buying early strands 1,000 Piastre in a blueprint you cannot use. Kill Richards first.

Why am I stuck at 200 Nails?

Because Nails are crafted from Copper Ingots and Iron Ingots, not gathered. You need a sustained copper and iron smelting pipeline running well before you want the Brigantine.

How many Shipwright's Tools does the build need?

30, purchased for roughly 300 Piastre.

Where do I build the Brigantine?

At the Wharf — the shoreline shallow-water station that builds ships, manages your fleet and repairs hulls. The Shipwright's Workshop only upgrades ships you already own.

Should I upgrade my Ketch while waiting?

Community consensus is no. The Ketch is transitional and its upgrades do not carry over to the Brigantine. Keep it functional, bank the materials instead.

Can I skip the Brigantine and go straight to the Frigate?

No. The Frigate requires second-biome materials — Tarred Planks and Tarred Fabric — and the tiers cannot be skipped.

Can I buy the blueprint in a friend's world?

Faction reputation is map-bound: joining another player's world inherits the host's reputation, so you can buy faction items there that your own save has not unlocked.
