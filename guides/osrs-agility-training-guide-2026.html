1. Why Agility Matters (and Why Everyone Hates It)

Agility is the classic OSRS "grind" skill. It gives almost no direct combat benefit, it is slow, and there is no profitable way to train it. So why does every end-game account have 99 Agility? Because the indirect benefits touch everything you do in the game.

Here is what Agility actually buys you:

🎯 The Real Payoffs of Agility

  • Run energy recovers faster — at 99 Agility your run energy regenerates roughly 2.3x faster than at level 1, so you spend far less time walking
  • Map shortcuts — dozens of obstacles (stairs, tunnels, narrow ledges) let you skip huge detours in raids, slayer, and bossing
  • Quest requirements — many high-value quests (Recipe for Disaster, Monkey Madness II, Sins of the Father) gate behind 50-70 Agility
  • Access to content — the Wilderness Agility Course, Hallowed Sepulchre, and Prifddinas Rooftop all require Agility levels
  • Graceful — the weight-reduction set you earn from rooftop courses is worn by virtually every PvM and skilling account

The honest truth: you will not enjoy training Agility. But doing it early — before you have a dozen other 99s competing for your time — is the smart play. Most players knock it out in their first few hundred total level because the alternative (needing 70 Agility mid-raid and not having it) is far worse.

💡 Mental Model

Treat Agility like a toll road. You pay the boring fee once, and every future trip in the game is faster and smoother. The sooner you pay, the more trips you save.

2. Early Courses: Levels 1-30

Before rooftop courses open up (the first is at level 10, but they get good at 20+), you grind the basic courses. These are slow and awkward, but they are the only option at low levels. Your goal from 1 to 30 is simply to reach the point where rooftops become available.

③ Recommended Path 1 → 30

Do the Gnome Stronghold course from level 1 to 10. It is safe, has no failure chance, and teaches you the click rhythm. At 10, move to the Barbarian Outpost course and stay there until 30. The Penguin course is technically better XP but requires a longer run and a boat trip; most players find Barbarian simpler.

Step 1: Gnome Stronghold (1-10)

Located near the Grand Tree. The course is a loop of log balances, nets, and pipes. Each lap is ~86 XP. Expect about 30-40 minutes to reach level 10.

Step 2: Barbarian Outpost (10-30)

West of the Grand Tree. Runs, ropes, and balances. Each lap ~152 XP. This is where you first feel the "click everything fast" cadence that defines Agility. Roughly 2-3 hours to 30.

💡 Use the Camera

Set your camera to a fixed angle and learn the obstacle order by heart. The faster you click the next obstacle the instant the animation ends, the more laps per hour. Fumbling for the next click is what kills your XP rate.

3. Rooftop Courses: The 1-99 Backbone

Rooftop courses are the heart of Agility training from level 10 all the way to 99 for most players. Each one is a themed parkour loop over a major city, and each lap awards Marks of Grace — the currency you spend on the Graceful set. They are safe, repeatable, and the XP scales well with your level.

⑤ Which Course Should I Use?

The rule is simple: always use the highest-level rooftop course you have unlocked. Each new course is a meaningful XP upgrade, and the Marks of Grace you collect fund your Graceful set. Do not "camp" a lower course for Marks — you will hit the Graceful set long before 99 if you progress normally.

🏃 The Standard Rooftop Route

10-20 Varrock → 20-30 Canifis → 30-40 Falador → 40-50 Seers' → 50-60 Pollnivneach → 60-70 Rellekka → 70-85 Ardougne → 85+ Prifddinas (or Summer Garden). This route takes you from 10 to 99 with zero detours and stacks up Marks of Grace the whole way.

💡 Failures Are Fine

Most rooftop obstacles have a small failure chance that drops you and costs a few seconds. Do not panic — failed obstacles still count toward the lap if you recover. The XP loss from occasional fails is negligible compared to the time you would waste being afraid to click fast.

4. The Graceful Set — Your First Priority

If you take one thing from this guide, take this: get the full Graceful set as early as you can. Graceful is a weight-reduction armor set obtainable only with Marks of Grace from rooftop courses. Wearing the full set reduces your weight by 30 kg, which means your run energy drains slower and restores faster — a permanent quality-of-life upgrade for every activity in the game.

⑥ How to Get Graceful

You need 260 Marks of Grace for the full set (including the cape). At typical rooftop drop rates you will accumulate this naturally within your first 10-20 Agility levels of doing rooftops. Spend them at the Graceful tutor in any rooftop course city (or the one in the Grand Exchange basement).

⑦ Why Graceful Beats Everything Else

🎯 Graceful vs Other Weight Gear

  • Boots of Lightness (-4.5 kg) help but only one slot; Graceful covers six slots for -30 kg total
  • Spotted/Spottier capes give -3 / -4.5 kg but occupy your cape slot, blocking Graceful cape
  • Full Graceful is the only set that also boosts run energy restoration, not just weight
  • At 99 Agility + full Graceful, your run energy is effectively a non-issue for most content

Recolor your Graceful at the Graceful tutor if you care about looks — the color is free and purely cosmetic. Many players keep a second set dyed for clue scrolls or just personal preference.

💡 When to Wear It

Wear Graceful for basically all skilling, questing, and travel. Switch out of it only when you need combat stats (e.g., during bossing where defense or strength matters more than weight). Even then, many players keep Graceful on for the run to the boss and swap at the door.

5. Summer Garden: 85+ Speed Running

Once you hit 85 Agility, the Summer Garden in Prifddinas unlocks and becomes the fastest Agility XP in the game — around 90k-100k XP per hour for a focused player, nearly double the best rooftop. The catch: it demands concentration. You must pick fruit from a tree and navigate a tight obstacle course within a timer, and failing wastes the whole attempt.

⑧ How the Summer Garden Works

  1. Enter via the southwest Prifddinas agility gate (85 Agility required, Plague's End quest completed)
  2. Pick a summer pie / fruit — you need to be fed to stay in the garden
  3. Run the obstacle loop — precise clicks; the course is short but punishing
  4. Bank the XP — each successful lap is ~800 XP plus bonus for speed
  5. Repeat for as long as you can stay focused; most players do 30-60 min sessions

Because the Garden is so click-intensive, it burns attention fast. Most players alternate: Summer Garden when fresh, rooftops (Prifddinas) when tired. Both are in the same city, so the transition is instant.

🍎 Summer Garden Tips

  • Eat a summer pie before entering to extend your time window
  • Pre-set your camera angle so every obstacle is visible without scrolling
  • Use run the entire time — energy is irrelevant here, speed is everything
  • If you fail an obstacle, the attempt ends; just re-enter and try again, do not rage
  • Pair with a podcast or show — the loop is mechanical once learned, and that is how 85-99 flies by
⚠️ Is Summer Garden Worth It?

If you only need 85 Agility for quest requirements, skip the Garden and stop at 85. If you are going for 99, the Garden cuts your 85-99 time from ~25 hours of rooftops to ~15 hours. That is ten hours of your life — absolutely worth it for a 99.

The Long Game

Agility is rarely trained in one sitting. Most players chip at it during money making downtime, while watching shows, or as a "background skill" between bosses. The rooftop loop is mindless enough that it pairs perfectly with passive entertainment — that is the secret to not hating it.

7. Agility FAQs

What is the fastest way to 99 Agility in 2026?

Rooftop courses from 10 to 85, then the Summer Garden from 85 to 99. This hybrid is ~35-45 hours of active training and is the universally accepted fastest legitimate route. Hallowed Sepulchre (52+ Agility, unlocks after Sins of the Father) is a popular alternative that adds combat and coins, but the Garden edges it on pure XP.

Can I make money while training Agility?

Not directly — Agility itself pays nothing. However, the Hallowed Sepulchre (52+ Agility) drops coins, ancient emblems, and the Dark Graceful recolor, making it the only "profitable" Agility method. The standard rooftops and Summer Garden are pure XP with zero income. Most players accept this and make their GP elsewhere.

Do I need 99 Agility for bossing?

No. You need specific Agility levels for quests that unlock bosses (e.g., 70 for Sins of the Father), but the skill itself is not a bossing prerequisite. That said, 99 Agility makes every bank run and positioning phase smoother, so it is a strong "quality of life" 99 rather than a "damage" 99.

Why do I keep failing obstacles?

Most rooftop obstacles have a small failure chance that scales slightly with your level (higher level = lower fail chance). Fails cost a few seconds and occasionally drop you to the ground. They are normal and not a sign you are doing anything wrong. Clicking efficiently and not panicking recovers the time quickly.

Should I get Graceful before 99 Agility?

Yes — ideally within your first 20 Agility levels. The Marks of Grace accumulate naturally from rooftops, and the -30 kg weight reduction helps every other skill you train. There is no reason to delay it; you will have the full set long before you finish Agility.

How many hours is 1-99 Agility really?

Using rooftops plus the Summer Garden, budget 35-55 hours of active clicking. Players who watch shows alongside often find it less painful than the raw number suggests. If you only go to 85 for quests, it is closer to 25-30 hours.

Is the Hallowed Sepulchre worth doing for Agility?

The Hallowed Sepulchre (unlocked at 52 Agility after the quest Sins of the Father) is the one Agility method that adds combat and money to the XP. You sprint through a coffin-filled cathedral, dodging obstacles and damaging the Dark Alcove, earning coffin keys, Hallowed marks, and a steady ~70k-80k XP per hour at high levels. It is slightly slower than the Summer Garden on pure XP, but it drops coins, the Dark Graceful recolor, and the powerful Graceful cape upgrade. Most players who dislike mindless rooftops prefer the Sepulchre because the obstacle variety keeps you engaged. If your goal is 99 as fast as possible, the Summer Garden still wins; if you want a break from clicking the same loop, the Sepulchre is the best alternative and pays you while you train.

What should I eat or bring to Agility training?

For standard rooftop courses and the Summer Garden, you technically need nothing — failing an obstacle just costs a few seconds and you never take damage. Most players bring no food at all and simply run. The Summer Garden is the exception: you must stay fed (a summer pie or similar) or you are ejected, so keep a few pies in your inventory. For the Hallowed Sepulchre, bring some food (lobsters or better) because the sarcophagus enemies can hit you if you mistime a obstacle, and a teleport tab is wise in case you get stuck. Beyond that, the only "gear" that matters is your Graceful set and comfortable click settings — no weapon, no potion, no prayer is required for normal rooftop training.

Can I train Agility effectively on mobile?

Yes, and many players do exactly that. Rooftop courses translate surprisingly well to touchscreens because the obstacle order is fixed and predictable — you simply tap the next glowing obstacle as soon as the animation ends. The Summer Garden and Hallowed Sepulchre are harder on mobile because they demand tighter timing and more screen awareness, so save those for desktop if you can. A good mobile setup is a phone stand, the official RuneScape app, and the "tap to walk" setting disabled so obstacle taps register instantly. Treat mobile Agility as your "commute skill" — it is perfect for short sessions where you cannot focus on a boss or a complex money method. Just do not expect record XP rates; mobile is about steady progress, not speed records.

Should I train Agility before or after other skills?

The conventional wisdom is to train Agility early, ideally in your first few hundred total level, because the run-energy and shortcut benefits compound across every other activity you do. Waiting until you "have time" usually means you never do it, and then you feel the drag of slow walking during every later grind. That said, Agility is a poor first skill if you are brand new, because it gives no GP and no combat power — get a few money methods and your first protect prayers first, then knock out Agility in the background. The ideal order for most accounts is: combat basics → a money method → Agility to 70-85 (for quests) → then finish to 99 whenever you have passive time. This keeps Agility from blocking content while avoiding the trap of leaving it for last.

🎯 What To Do After Reaching Your Agility Goal

Whether you stopped at 85 for quests or pushed to 99, Agility now pays you back every day. Here is where to point that newfound mobility:

→ Step 1: Apply your Graceful set to Combat Training — run between monsters without draining energy
→ Step 2: Use your shortcuts to speed up Money Making — less walking means more GP per hour
→ Step 3: Pair Agility with Prayer for safer bossing — fast repositioning plus protection prayers
→ Step 4: Tackle a high-Agility quest like Sins of the Father to unlock the Hallowed Sepulchre
