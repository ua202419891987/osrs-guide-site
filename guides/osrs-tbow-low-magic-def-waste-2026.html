This guide gives you the exact mechanic, the Magic-level threshold, and a 10-second decision rule so you stop burning a 1.6b bow on targets that Trident of the Swamp or Sanguinesti would delete faster.

1. 🧭 The Core Misconception: Tbow Is Not a Magic Weapon

The single most expensive mistake in PvM is treating the Twisted Bow like a magic weapon. Players see "it hits mages hard" and assume it is strong against anything with low Magic defence. It is not. The Tbow is a ranged weapon — it fires arrows — and its unique passive bonus is driven entirely by the target's Magic level (or Magic accuracy, whichever is higher), not by the target's Magic defence.

This matters because of the combat triangle: ranged is strong against magic, and magic is strong against ranged. When a monster has low Magic defence, the correct response is to bring your own magic weapon (Trident, Sanguinesti, Tumeken's Shadow) — not a ranged bow. Using the Tbow there leaves its signature bonus switched off and lets a 50k GP Trident outperform a 1.6b bow.

So the reframe is simple: the Tbow is a "high-Magic-target" weapon. The moment you fight a low-Magic, low-Magic-defence monster — the kind where a magic setup would normally shine — the Tbow is the wrong tool. The rest of this guide shows you exactly when, with the numbers.

💡 The 5-Second Version

If the monster's Wiki page shows a low Magic level (under ~120) and it is weak to magic, stash the Tbow. A Trident of the Swamp or Sanguinesti will kill it faster for a fraction of the cost and zero risk of "wasting" your best weapon.

2. 🧮 How the Tbow Passive Actually Works (Real Formula)

Unlike every other bow, the Tbow has no fixed strength. Its accuracy and damage multipliers are functions of the target's Magic level. Jagex published the exact formulas (capped outside the Chambers of Xeric):

① The accuracy and damage formulas

Accuracy % = 140 + (3·M/10 − 10)/100 − ((3·M/10 − 100)²)/100, capped at 140.
Damage % = 250 + (3·M/10 − 14)/100 − ((3·M/10 − 140)²)/100, capped at 250.
Where M = the target's Magic level or Magic accuracy bonus (whichever is higher), and the "100%" point is normal — i.e. a regular bow with no bonus.

② What this means in plain English

The Tbow only becomes "better than normal" once M crosses a break-even point. Below that point it is worse than a plain bow. The two break-evens are different:

Damage breaks even at M ≈ 58, accuracy at M ≈ 122

Because damage is the less binding constraint, the real "when is Tbow worth it" line is the accuracy break-even near Magic level 122. Under that, the Tbow literally misses more than a basic bow would. Past ~150 it pulls clearly ahead, and at the 250 cap (think Commander Zilyana) it is in a league of its own.

3. 📊 The Magic-Level Threshold: When Tbow Beats Magic

Now the comparison players actually care about: Tbow (ranged) vs Trident/Sanguinesti (magic). Magic weapon accuracy is rolled against the target's Magic defence, not its Magic level. So on a low-Magic-defence target, your Trident is extremely accurate, while your Tbow gets no passive bonus (low Magic level). The crossover is therefore not a single number — it is "low Magic level and low Magic defence."

① The two-axis rule

Use both axes together:

  • Target Magic level < ~120–150 → Tbow accuracy bonus is off or marginal.
  • Target weak to magic / low Magic defence → Trident/Sang accuracy is high and damage is strong.

When both are true (the common case for Slayer and low-level bossing), magic wins. When Magic level is high (Zilyana, Vorkath, Zulrah, raids bosses), the Tbow's bonus dominates and it is unambiguously BiS.

② Why "low Magic defence" is the wrong trigger by itself

⚠️ Precision Check

A monster can have low Magic defence but a high Magic level (some use devastating magic and have high levels, just low defensive Magic stats). The Tbow cares about the level, not the defence. Always check the Magic level on the Wiki — that is the number that drives the Tbow. This is the single detail that separates a correct swap from a wrong one.

③ A practical DPS shortcut

Plug both weapons into a DPS calculator

Before a long grind, run your Tbow (dragon arrows, Rigour) and your Trident/Sang (Imbued heart / potential, Augury) through a DPS calculator against the specific monster's actual defensive stats. The calculator does the Magic-level math for you. If Sang/Tbow maths shows magic ahead by even 10%, switch — you will save hours and millions in arrows.

5. 💰 Blowpipe & Cheaper Options for Low-Def Targets

Sometimes the right answer is not even magic — it is the Toxic Blowpipe. On very low-defence targets (think many Slayer monsters, Aviansie, low-level bosses), the blowpipe's raw attack speed and accuracy beat both a passive-less Tbow and a slower magic weapon, at a tiny fraction of the cost.

① Blowpipe vs Tbow on low defence

The blowpipe attacks every 3 ticks with high accuracy and a strong poison. Against a target with low Ranged defence and low Magic level, it simply out-cycles a Tbow that is getting no passive bonus. This is why experienced irons and mains keep a blowpipe for exactly these grinds.

② Trident of the Seas vs Swamp

The Trident of the Seas (Charged) is the budget magic option — no unsired, no quest wall — and is plenty for the low-Magic-defence targets above. The Sanguinesti and Tumeken's Shadow pull ahead when you also want lifesteal or a big magic strength bonus, but for pure "delete this low-def monster," a Seas Trident is the value pick.

③ Opportunity cost you are ignoring

Every Tbow arrow on the wrong target is wasted GP

Dragon arrows cost real money per shot. Spending them on a target where the Tbow is weaker than a blowpipe is double waste: you lose the DPS and burn expensive ammo. Swap to magic or blowpipe and save the arrows for the raids boss where they actually pay off. The Araxxor guide is a good example of a target where the style choice (and ammo) swings kill time by minutes.

6. 🎯 How to Decide in 10 Seconds

You do not need a spreadsheet mid-grind. Use this flow:

① The 10-second checklist

  1. Open the monster's Wiki page. Read its Magic level.
  2. Under ~120? The Tbow gets no accuracy bonus — strongly consider swapping.
  3. Is the monster weak to magic (low Magic defence)? If yes, Trident/Sang wins.
  4. Is it very low defence overall? Blowpipe may win outright.
  5. Magic level 150+ and ranged-weak? Tbow stays — it is BiS.

② The "keep Tbow" shortlist

Hold the Tbow for: Commander Zilyana (Magic 250), Vorkath, Zulrah, the Chambers of Xeric bosses (Olm etc.), TzKal-Zuk, and any high-Magic-level raid content. These are exactly the fights where the passive bonus justifies the price.

③ When in doubt, calculate

One calculator run beats an hour of guessing

If a target sits in the grey zone (Magic ~120–160), do not guess — run both setups through a DPS calculator with the real defensive stats. The 60-second check prevents a 60-minute session of sub-optimal DPS. The God Wars Dungeon guide covers several bosses where this exact call changes your kill count per hour.

7. ❓ Tbow Weapon-Choice FAQs

Does the Tbow scale with the target's Magic defence?

No. This is the #1 misconception. The Tbow's accuracy and damage scale with the target's Magic level (or Magic accuracy, whichever is higher) — never its Magic defence. A monster with low Magic defence but a high Magic level can still be a great Tbow target; a monster with low Magic level is where the Tbow falls flat.

At what Magic level does the Tbow become worth using?

Accuracy breaks even at about Magic level 122, damage at about 58. In practice, treat ~120–150 as the "worth it" threshold: below that the Tbow often underperforms a basic bow on accuracy, and a magic weapon or blowpipe will out-DPS it. Above 150 it pulls clearly ahead.

Why is Trident better than Tbow on Aviansie?

Aviansie have very low defence and a low Magic level, so the Tbow gets essentially no passive bonus (accuracy under 100%). Their Magic defence is also low, so a Trident of the Swamp or Sanguinesti lands almost every hit with strong magic damage. Magic wins on both accuracy and speed — for a weapon that costs pocket change next to a Tbow.

Is the Blowpipe ever better than the Tbow?

Yes, on very low-defence targets where the Tbow receives no passive bonus. The blowpipe's 3-tick attack speed and high base accuracy let it out-cycle a bonus-less Tbow, and it uses cheap darts instead of dragon arrows. It is the go-to for many Slayer tasks and low-level bossing.

Should I use Tumeken's Shadow instead of Trident?

Shadow has a much higher magic strength bonus, so on magic-weak targets it can out-DPS Trident significantly — but it is vastly more expensive and the passive only matters against targets with some Magic defence to overcome. For low-Magic-defence trash, a Trident of the Seas is the better value; bring Shadow when you want maximum magic DPS and can afford the risk.

Does the Tbow's arrow type change the threshold?

Dragon arrows give the highest base damage and are what the formula numbers assume. Using weaker arrows lowers your floor but does not change the Magic-level break-even — the passive bonus is independent of arrow tier. Always use dragon arrows on true Tbow targets; on low-Magic targets the arrow cost is itself a reason to switch away.

Can I just use the Tbow everywhere to simplify?

You can, but you are leaving DPS and GP on the table on every low-Magic target, and burning dragon arrows you could save for raids. Keeping a Trident/Sang and a blowpipe in your bank is the difference between "I have a Tbow" and "I use the right weapon every time."
