Mobile Overheating & Mid-Raid Crashes: Prevention & Fixes (2026)

Updated August 19, 2026  |  15 min read  |  By Yanchun Wu

Your phone gets hot, OSRS drops frames, and then you crash or disconnect halfway through a raid. This is thermal throttling plus memory pressure, and it is almost always preventable. This guide covers the exact settings, battery behavior, cooling, and cleanup that keep you in the fight — plus a pre-raid checklist you can run in two minutes.

📅 Updated: August 2026 — Mobile · 15 min read
🛡️ Level: All
🆕 NEW

A phone crashing in the middle of a raid is rarely "the OSRS app is broken." It is your device getting too hot, the chip slowing itself down to avoid damage (thermal throttling), and the game either stuttering into a disconnect or being killed by the OS for using too much memory. The mobile best settings guide has the full settings tour; this page focuses on the heat-and-crash problem specifically and how to stop it before a raid.

If the app will not even open or shows a black screen, that is a different fault — see the Android black screen fix or the iOS black screen fix. If you get kicked to the login screen repeatedly, the can't-login guide is the right stop. This page is for the "it runs, then heats up and dies" case.

💡 Quick Verdict (read this first)

The single biggest cause of mid-raid mobile crashes is heat. Lower the in-game graphics to Low/Medium, turn battery saver OFF while playing (it throttles the CPU), keep the phone on a passive cooler or in front of a fan, and close every other app first. Those four actions prevent the vast majority of raid crashes. Memory cleanup and a stable connection handle the rest. Do the pre-raid checklist every time and you will stop dying to your own phone.

1. 🔥 Why Phones Overheat in OSRS

Understanding the mechanism is what lets you actually prevent it instead of just hoping. A phone is a tiny, sealed computer with no fans — when the chip works hard, heat builds, and the only relief is slowing the chip down.

① Thermal throttling: the silent killer

When a phone's chip hits roughly 40–45°C internally, it automatically reduces clock speed to generate less heat. In OSRS that shows up as frame drops, then input lag, then a disconnect or a hard crash. The game did not "break" — your phone deliberately slowed itself to avoid cooking the battery. The hotter it gets, the more it throttles, in a downward spiral that ends in a crash mid-fight.

② Why OSRS is oddly heavy on phones

OSRS looks simple, but the mobile client still renders a live world, processes other players, runs the camera, and holds a growing cache on storage. In busy spots (raids, GE, world events) the GPU and CPU both work, and a phone with no active cooling hits thermal limits fast — especially a thin flagship that prioritizes slimness over散热.

③ Two failure modes you will see

🌡️ Know which one you have

  • Slow-then-disconnect: FPS drops, then you rubber-band and get kicked. This is throttling + network timing — heat slowed the chip so packets arrived late.
  • Hard crash to home screen: the app vanishes with no error. This is usually memory pressure (OS killed it) or the OS's thermal shutdown — heat plus RAM full.

④ The heat spiral, visualized

💡 The core rule

You cannot stop a phone from generating heat, but you can slow the climb: less GPU work (lower settings), less CPU work (no background apps), and more heat leaving the device (cooling). Do all three and the spiral never reaches the crash zone. That is the whole strategy of this guide.

2. 🎚️ Graphics & Quality Settings

The fastest lever you have is the in-game graphics setting. Every step down reduces GPU work and therefore heat. You do not need max settings to raid — you need stability.

① The exact settings to use for raids

🎚️ OSRS Mobile → Settings → Graphics

  • Graphics detail: Low or Medium for raids/bossing. High is for screenshots while skilling.
  • Display mode: Resizable is fine; avoid maxing resolution beyond your screen.
  • Translucent hold-to-drop: off (minor GPU save).
  • Ground decorations: off in busy areas.
  • Entity hider: enable to hide other players' equipment in crowds.
  • Brightness: leave at default; do not crank it (some panels raise GPU load).

② Brightness and "screen-on" heat

The screen is a major heat source on a phone. Max brightness in a raid pushes both the display and the battery hard. Use auto-brightness or a moderate manual level, and if you are somewhere you can, dim it — your eyes and your thermals both win.

③ FPS limit on mobile

Some mobile builds let you cap FPS. If yours does, set it to 30 for raids. Chasing 60 FPS on a phone doubles GPU heat for almost no gameplay benefit in OSRS, where the world ticks at 0.6s anyway. A steady 30 is smoother in practice than a throttled 60 that collapses to 10.

④ When to use Low vs Medium

Your device Recommended setting Why
Flagship (last 2 years) Medium Handles it with light cooling
Mid-range / 3+ years old Low Stays cool, avoids throttling
Any phone in a thick case Low + cooling Case traps heat; compensate
Long raids (20+ min) Low Sustained load needs headroom

3. 🔋 Battery & Power Mode

This is the most misunderstood part. The "battery saver" mode players enable to "save power" is exactly what makes OSRS crash — because it throttles the CPU, which is the opposite of what a live game needs.

① Turn battery saver OFF while playing

🔋 What to do

  • Android: Settings → Battery → Power saving / Battery saver: OFF during raids. Also set "Performance" or "High performance" mode if your brand has it (Samsung: Device care → Battery → set to "High performance").
  • iOS: Settings → Battery → Low Power Mode: OFF. It caps CPU and network, which causes exactly the mid-raid stutter.

Counter-intuitive but true: a phone on battery-saver runs the chip slower to save energy, which makes OSRS stutter and disconnect. You want the chip fast; you manage heat with cooling instead (Section 4), not by slowing the CPU.

② Keep it charged, but not at 100% baking

Playing while plugged in keeps the battery from draining, but a phone charging and running a game gets hottest of all — the battery heats from charging while the chip heats from the game. Best practice:

  • Start the raid at ~80% and play on battery if your phone stays cool enough.
  • If you must plug in for a long session, use a low-watt charger (5–10W) and keep the phone on a cooler; avoid fast-charging (20W+) during play.
  • Never play while wireless-charging — that is the worst heat trap of all.

③ Background app refresh & battery optimization

Tell the OS not to optimize OSRS away, but also stop everything else from waking up:

📱 Stop background drain

  • Android: Settings → Apps → OSRS → Battery → set to "Unrestricted" (so it is not killed) but also disable "Allow background activity" for every other app.
  • iOS: Settings → General → Background App Refresh → OFF globally during play.
⚠️ "Optimized charging" can lie to you

Some phones pause charging at 80% "for battery health" — great for the battery, annoying mid-raid if it then discharges while throttled. If you raid for an hour plugged in, prefer a steady partial charge over fast-charge-then-bake. Heat, not charge cycles, is what kills phone batteries and causes crashes.

4. ❄️ Physical Cooling & Case

Since the phone cannot cool itself, you cool it. This single category prevents more raid crashes than any setting.

① Passive cooling you already own

  • A small USB fan blowing across the back of the phone drops surface temp by several degrees — enough to stop throttling.
  • A phone stand + desk fan works the same.
  • A cooling pad / heat-sink case (passive metal back) helps on long sessions.
  • Even propping the phone up (not in your hand) helps — your hand is a warm blanket.

② Active coolers (best for serious raiders)

Bluetooth/USB-C phone cooling fans (the snap-on Peltier kind) are the gold standard for mobile OSRS raiding. They pull the chip below the throttle threshold so FPS stays flat for the whole fight. If you raid often on mobile, this is the highest-ROI purchase you can make — cheaper than a new phone and more effective than any setting.

③ Take the case off

🧊 Case off = heat out

Thick cases (especially rubber/leather) trap heat. For a raid, remove the case and set the phone on a cool surface (a tile, a metal stand, even a (sealed) ice pack underneath a cloth). Never put the phone directly on ice — condensation can kill it. Cloth barrier only.

④ Environment matters

  • Do not raid in direct sun or a hot car — ambient heat removes your only cooling margin.
  • Air-conditioned rooms are ideal; the cooler the air, the lower the throttle point stays unreached.
  • If outdoors, shade the phone and use a fan; a phone in sunlight hits thermal limits in minutes.

5. 🧹 Memory & Background Apps

The second crash cause is the OS killing OSRS for using too much RAM. Phones have limited memory; if it fills, the game you are actively playing gets terminated — a hard crash to home with no warning.

① Close everything before you raid

🧹 Pre-raid memory sweep

  • Android: open recent apps and swipe away everything — especially Chrome, YouTube, Discord, Spotify, Maps, social apps.
  • iOS: swipe up from the bottom and close all background apps (double-tap home button on older models).
  • Leave only OSRS running.

② Free up storage (cache pressure)

If your phone storage is nearly full, the OS has nowhere to page and OSRS can stutter or crash. Keep at least 2–3 GB free. The OSRS cache grows over time; clear it periodically:

🗑️ Clear OSRS cache (mobile)

Android: Settings → Apps → OSRS → Storage → Clear cache (not "Clear data" — that logs you out).
iOS: delete and reinstall the app to reset the cache (your account is safe — it is server-side).

③ Restart the phone before a big raid

A fresh reboot clears accumulated memory leaks from days of use and gives OSRS the cleanest RAM slate. If you raid daily, reboot before your main session — two minutes that prevents a costly disconnect.

④ Watch RAM-hungry settings

High graphics settings consume more RAM as well as GPU. The same "Low/Medium" advice from Section 2 protects memory too. Entity hider and ground-decoration-off also reduce the live object count the app must hold in RAM.

6. 🛡️ Prevent Mid-Raid Disconnects

A disconnect is not always heat — sometimes it is the connection dropping at the worst moment. Combine the thermal fixes above with these connection habits.

① Stable connection > fast connection

  • Wi-Fi over cellular when possible, but a strong cellular signal beats flaky Wi-Fi. Test both.
  • Stay near the router; do not raid from the garden through two walls.
  • Disable Wi-Fi assist / Cellular handoff that swaps networks mid-fight — that swap is a guaranteed disconnect.

② Background disconnect protection

Phones aggressively drop idle or backgrounded apps from the network. Make sure OSRS is not being put to sleep:

🔒 Keep OSRS awake

  • Android: enable "Pause app activity if unused" OFF for OSRS; lock the app in recent-tasks (tap the app icon → "Lock").
  • iOS: ensure Low Power Mode is off (Section 3) and the screen stays on (Auto-Lock → 5 min+ or Never during a raid).

The dedicated mobile disconnect / background fix and the background disconnect prevention guide go deeper on OS-level app-standby settings that vary by brand.

③ Avoid the screen-lock trap

If Auto-Lock kicks in mid-raid, the client pauses and you can get disconnected from a group. Set Auto-Lock to the longest option (or use a "stay awake while charging" developer toggle on Android) during raids. Just remember to re-enable it after so your phone is not left on forever.

④ Reconnect fast after a drop

If you do disconnect, the raid boss does not wait. Reopen OSRS immediately; the client usually drops you back near where you were (your raid instance persists briefly). The faster you are back, the less your team suffers. Keep the app icon on your home screen, not buried in a folder, for speed.

💡 Tell your team

If you know your phone runs hot, warn your raid group that you may need a 10-second cool-down between waves. A prepared team covers you; a surprised one wipes. Communication beats a mystery disconnect every time.

7. 🧪 Pre-Raid Checklist (2 Minutes)

Run this every time before a raid. It compresses the whole guide into a routine. Do it from muscle memory and mid-raid crashes basically stop.

① The checklist

✅ 2-Minute Pre-Raid Routine

  1. Reboot the phone (or at least close all background apps).
  2. Set graphics to Low/Medium; enable entity hider.
  3. Turn battery saver / Low Power Mode OFF; set performance mode.
  4. Remove the case; place phone on a cool surface or snap on a cooler.
  5. Point a fan at the back (or use an active cooler).
  6. Connect to the strongest, most stable network; disable network handoff.
  7. Set Auto-Lock to long/never; lock OSRS in recent tasks.
  8. Confirm at least 2–3 GB storage free; clear cache if low.
  9. Start at ~80% battery; use a low-watt charger only if needed.
  10. Open OSRS, let it sit 30s to settle, then enter the raid.

② If it still crashes: escalate

Still crashing after checklist? Then the limit is… Move to
Hot + throttled even with cooler Hardware thermal ceiling Play shorter sessions; consider a cooler or a different device
Hard crash, phone cool Memory (OS killed it) Close more apps, free storage, reboot, lower settings
Disconnect, not crash Network Router guide + background-disconnect guides
Won't open at all App fault Android / iOS black-screen fix

③ Long-term: is your phone just too old?

If even a cooler + Low settings cannot get you through a 20-minute raid without throttling, the device has hit its thermal ceiling for OSRS. Options: raid on the official client via a tablet (larger, better-cooled), tether a phone to a PC and play the desktop client, or accept shorter sessions. 8. ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Why does my phone only crash mid-raid and not while skilling?

Raids push the GPU and CPU hard for 20+ minutes straight, so heat builds until the chip throttles and the app crashes or disconnects. Skilling is lighter load and shorter bursts, so the phone never reaches its thermal limit. The fix is exactly the cooling + lower-settings routine in this guide — raids are the stress test that exposes heat problems.

Should I use battery saver to stop overheating?

No — that makes it worse. Battery saver slows the CPU, which causes OSRS to stutter and disconnect, and it does not actually cool the phone. Cool the phone physically (fan/cooler, case off) and keep the chip running at full speed. Heat is managed by removing it, not by slowing the chip that is generating it.

Is it safe to play while charging?

Mostly, but charging while gaming is the hottest state a phone reaches. Use a low-watt charger, keep the phone on a cooler, and avoid fast or wireless charging during play. Starting at ~80% and playing on battery is cooler; plug in only for long sessions, and cool aggressively then.

Does a phone cooler actually help OSRS?

Yes, noticeably. An active snap-on cooler keeps the chip below the throttle temperature, so FPS stays flat through a whole raid instead of decaying into a crash. For frequent mobile raiders it is the best investment you can make — cheaper than a new phone and more reliable than settings alone.

My phone stays cool but OSRS still crashes. Why?

That is memory pressure, not heat. The OS is killing OSRS because RAM is full. Close every other app, free up storage to 2–3 GB, reboot before raiding, and lower graphics (which also uses less RAM). If it still crashes cool, the device may simply be short on RAM for modern OSRS — see the escalation table.

Will lowering graphics make me worse at the game?

No. OSRS ticks at 0.6 seconds regardless of your FPS; you do not need 60 FPS to raid well, you need a stable 30 and no disconnects. Low/Medium settings keep you in the fight, which is worth far more than prettier trees. Turn settings up only for screenshots while skilling.

Why do I disconnect exactly when the screen locks?

Auto-Lock pauses the client and the OS drops the network session, so you get disconnected from the raid. Set Auto-Lock to the longest option (or "Never" via a developer stay-awake toggle on Android) during raids, and lock OSRS in your recent-tasks so the OS does not standby it. The background disconnect prevention guide covers brand-specific standby settings.

Android or iOS — which overheats less for OSRS?

It depends on the model, not the OS. Thinner flagships of either brand throttle sooner; larger phones and tablets cool better. The settings and cooling advice here applies to both. iOS Low Power Mode and Android Battery Saver are the equivalent traps to avoid. Pick the device with the better sustained thermal behavior, not the bigger benchmark number.

Can I prevent the disconnect by using mobile data instead of Wi-Fi?

Use whichever is more stable, not inherently one or the other. A strong 5G/4G signal can beat flaky Wi-Fi, but Wi-Fi near the router is usually steadier. The real disconnect triggers are network handoff (swapping Wi-Fi/cellular mid-fight) and the OS putting OSRS to sleep — disable handoff and keep the app awake, and either connection works.

How often should I clear the OSRS cache on mobile?

Every few weeks, or whenever you notice stutter or storage getting low. Clearing cache (not data) is safe and logs you back in fast; it removes accumulated temporary files that can contribute to memory pressure. On iOS, deleting and reinstalling the app resets the cache — your account is server-side, so you just sign in again.
