1. What Is Group Ironman (GIM)?

Group Ironman, almost always shortened to GIM, is a cooperative game mode in Old School RuneScape where a small team of two to five players progresses together under the same ironman restrictions. Everyone in the group is an ironman: you cannot use the Grand Exchange, you cannot trade with non-ironman players, and almost everything you own must be gathered by you or your group. What makes GIM different from a solo ironman is that the group shares a single central bank, called shared group storage, that every member can deposit into and withdraw from. That shared bank is the heart of the mode and the reason GIM can feel dramatically faster and more social than playing alone.

When you create or join a GIM team, each player still has a personal bank for items they want to keep private, but the group storage acts like a communal vault. One person farms a thousand potatoes, another grinds bones for prayer, a third kills bosses for rare drops, and all of it flows into the shared storage so the whole team can use it. A fresh account can be kitted out in gear they could never have afforded to grind themselves for weeks, simply because a teammate already did the work and dropped it in the group bank. This is the single biggest advantage of GIM: progress is multiplied by the number of active, organized players, not just summed.

🎯 The Three Core Rules of Group Ironman

  • No Grand Exchange: you cannot buy or sell on the GE. Every coin, bar, log, and potion must be earned by the group.
  • No trading outside the group: dropping items to strangers or using the GE is blocked. Your economy is fully internal.
  • Group size 2 to 5: you play with a fixed, named roster. Leaves and kicks are permanent and limited, so choose carefully.

The mode was designed for players who love the ironman self-sufficiency but do not want to do every single boring grind themselves. In a well-run group, the person who hates farming can specialize in combat, while the person who loves skilling can feed the team supplies. Because OSRS has hundreds of hours of repetitive content, splitting that content across five people is one of the most efficient ways to experience the whole game without burning out. GIM is not easier than a solo ironman in terms of total work required — the total work is similar — but the per-player workload drops sharply when roles are divided well.

💡 Is GIM right for you?

If you enjoy coordinating with friends, dislike repeating grinds you have already done, and want a shared sense of progression, GIM is excellent. If you prefer total independence or get frustrated waiting on others, a regular ironman may suit you better. We cover the full comparison in section 6.

One subtle but important point: group members are not forced to be online at the same time. GIM is asynchronous cooperation. You can log in at 2 a.m., dump your day's loot into shared storage, and log off, while a teammate on the other side of the world wakes up, grabs what they need, and pushes their own goals. The shared bank is always there, working even when individuals are offline. This makes GIM far more forgiving for adults with busy schedules than many players expect.

2. Creating a Group & How Shared Storage Works

Creating a Group Ironman is simple, but the decisions you make in the first five minutes shape your entire experience, so do not rush. From the account creation screen or the ironman tutor near the start area, you choose Group Ironman instead of standard or hardcore ironman. You then name your group. The group name is permanent and public, so pick something your whole team is happy to be associated with for the life of the account. Once the group exists, you receive a group invitation code that you share with the friends you want to recruit.

The person who creates the group becomes the group owner (also called the founder). The owner has special permissions: they can invite new members (up to the 2-5 cap), approve join requests, and — importantly — they are the only one who can initiate a kick or a leave. Every member, including the owner, can leave the group, but leaving or being kicked is permanent: you cannot rejoin the same group, and your account converts to a solo ironman if you leave. This permanence is why GIM drama can be brutal, and why we dedicate a whole section later to mistakes. For now, the key takeaway is that the roster you build in week one is essentially final.

🎯 Step-by-Step: Create Your GIM Team

  • Agree on the roster first. Talk to 1 to 4 real friends who will actually play. Empty slots can be filled later, but a flaky group is worse than a small committed one.
  • One person creates the group and sets the permanent name.
  • Share the invite code privately. Members enter the code on their own account to join.
  • Set expectations in voice chat or a group chat before anyone trains: goals, roles, and activity level.
  • Make your first group-storage deposit together so everyone learns the system immediately.

How Shared Group Storage Actually Works

Shared group storage is a bank interface accessible to every member from anywhere in the world through the group storage icon, or by visiting the group storage chest in your group's designated location. Think of it as one giant extra bank tab that everyone shares. When you deposit an item, it leaves your personal bank and enters the group pool. When a teammate withdraws it, it moves into their personal bank. There is no "owner" of an individual item once it is in the pool — it belongs to the group.

This has powerful consequences. A bosser who gets a rare drop can drop it straight into shared storage, and the skiller who needed that exact item for a quest can grab it instantly instead of grinding for it. A farmer who harvests a huge herb crop can leave potion ingredients for the whole team. Because withdrawals are instant and unlimited (you are only limited by what is physically in the storage), the shared bank removes the single biggest bottleneck of solo ironman: waiting on your own slow grinds before you can do the content you actually want.

💡 Use personal bank for private items

Keep your privately-owned untradeable gear, clues you are saving, and anything you are not ready to share in your personal bank. Shared storage is for the communal economy. Being clear about what is "group" versus "mine" prevents resentment later.

There are a few mechanics worth knowing early. Group storage has a capacity measured in item slots, and while it is generous, a hoarder can fill it. More importantly, some items behave specially: certain untradeable items cannot be placed in shared storage at all, and a handful of boss drops are "owner-locked" for a short period after being obtained so they cannot be immediately laundered. None of this should block a beginner — just know that not literally everything can be pooled. The practical routine is simple: at the end of every session, empty your useful surplus (extra food, spare gear, spare supplies) into shared storage so the next teammate benefits.

A smart habit is to treat shared storage like a supply line, not a dumpster. Label nothing (the game does not let you), but establish a mental convention: food and basics go in loose, while rare or contested items get discussed in chat before withdrawal if your group agrees. Many successful groups adopt a simple rule — "take what you need, replace what you can" — which keeps the bank healthy without micromanagement. The groups that struggle are the ones where one person treats the shared bank as their personal hoard and another treats it as an all-you-can-eat buffet with no contribution.

3. Role Division — Play to Your Strengths

The reason GIM beats solo ironman is not magic — it is specialization. Five people each doing the part of the game they enjoy or are good at will always out-produce five people each trying to do everything. Before anyone trains past the early levels, sit down as a group and agree on rough roles. Roles are not rigid cages; you can overlap and switch, but having a default focus prevents duplicated effort and wasted hours. The four classic GIM roles are skillers, bossers, farmers, and crafters (often called crafters/suppliers).

Skillers — The Backbone of the Bank

Skillers focus on the gathering and production skills: Woodcutting, Fishing, Mining, Smithing, Cooking, Firemaking, Runecrafting, Crafting, and Construction. In a GIM, the skiller's job is to generate the steady stream of raw materials and basic gear that everyone else consumes. A dedicated skiller can get the whole group plank-and-log stocked for Construction, keep everyone fed with cooked fish, and supply ores and bars for smithed armor. Because these grinds are often the most repetitive part of ironman, letting one or two players who actually enjoy them handle it frees the rest of the team for content they find fun. If you are patient and like watching numbers go up, you are a skiller.

Bossers — The Rare-Drop Engine

Bossers push combat stats and fight bosses for rare drops: armor, weapons, supplies, and valuable untradeables that cannot be bought. In GIM, bossers are the reason the group suddenly has Barrows sets, a Toxic Blowpipe, or a full set of Bandos. A bosser's drops flow into shared storage and equip the entire team. The trade-off is that bossers depend on the skillers and farmers to keep them supplied with food, potions, and ammo. A group with strong bossers but no supply line stalls; a group with strong suppliers but no bossers never gets top-tier gear. The two roles are symbiotic, which is exactly the point of the mode.

🎯 Example 5-Person Role Split

  • Player 1 — Skiller: Woodcutting, Fishing, Cooking, Firemaking.
  • Player 2 — Miner/Smith: Mining, Smithing, Runecrafting for runes.
  • Player 3 — Bosser: Melee bossing, Barrows, God Wars.
  • Player 4 — Farmer/Crafter: Farming, Herblore, Crafting, fletching.
  • Player 5 — Flex/Raider: Slayer, raids, fills gaps wherever the group is short.

Farmers & Crafters — The Supply Chain

"Farmer" in GIM usually means the Farming skill specifically, plus the Herblore and Crafting pipeline that turns farm output into potions and gear. A farmer keeps the group in herbs, logs (via tree runs), and food, while a crafter turns those herbs into potions, leather into armor, and gems into jewelry. In practice many groups merge farmer and crafter into one role because Farming and Herblore feed each other so directly. This role is quietly the most valuable in the mid-game: a steady potion supply is what lets bossers actually attempt high-level content. If you enjoy passive, log-in-do-a-tree-run gameplay, this is your lane.

💡 Overlap is fine, rigidity is not

Roles are a planning tool, not a contract. A bosser can still do birdhouse runs; a skiller can still camp a boss occasionally. The goal is to avoid five people all grinding the same early skill and none grinding the others. Default focus, flexible execution.

How Roles Accelerate Everyone

Here is the concrete math of why division works. Suppose your group needs 1,000 cooked lobsters, a set of Rune armor, 50 prayer potions, and a Dragon Scimitar. Solo, one player must fish, cook, mine, smith, farm, brew, and boss — probably 40+ hours of mixed grinding. In a group, the skiller cooks the lobsters in an evening, the miner supplies the smith, the farmer brews the potions, and the bosser farms the scimitar drop — and each person did only the part they are built for. Everyone reaches "geared and ready" in a fraction of the time. Shared storage is the delivery system; roles are the factory layout. Together they are what make GIM the fastest ironman experience in the game.

If you are unsure which role fits you, ask yourself what you would do if no one was watching: the player who logs in just to do birdhouses and herb runs is a farmer; the one who immediately wants to kill something is a bosser; the one happy to afk fish while watching a show is a skiller. Match the role to the fun and the group will sustain itself for years. For the combat side of your role, our Combat Training Beginner Guide walks through the early levels every bosser needs.

4. First 2-Week Group Goals (Checklist)

The first two weeks of a GIM decide whether the group survives or quietly dies. Momentum is everything. Below is a concrete checklist your group should aim to clear in the opening fortnight. You do not need to finish all of it, but the more you complete together, the stronger your foundation. The theme is simple: get everyone survivable, get the supply line running, and unlock the content that makes GIM fun.

Week 1 — Foundation

🎯 Week 1 Collective Checklist

  • All members: complete the early quest line (Cook's Assistant, Sheep Shearer, Restless Ghost, Gertrude's Cat, Knight's Sword for smithing XP).
  • All members: reach 40+ combat stats (Attack, Strength, Defence, Hitpoints, Ranged or Magic) so basic bossing is possible.
  • Skiller: stock 1,000+ cooked trout/lobster in shared storage for the team.
  • Miner/Smith: produce a full set of Rune or Adamant armor for each member from shared storage.
  • Farmer: start herb and tree runs; establish a daily birdhouse run routine.
  • Group goal: unlock the Skills Necklace and Ring of Dueling teleports via Fremennik or desert tasks for faster movement.

Notice that week one is mostly about enabling rather than achieving. The single most important early unlock for any ironman, including GIM, is convenient teleportation. Without it, every gathering trip is slow and every death is a long walk. Spend group time knocking out the easy quest and diary requirements that grant jewelry teleports. A ring of dueling alone (ferrying you to the PvP arena and the digsite) saves uncountable hours across five players.

💡 Quest together, even solo

You cannot share quest progress, but you can coordinate which quests everyone does so the group unlocks the same teleports and gear roughly together. Sync your quest lists in chat each night.

Week 2 — First Real Content

🎯 Week 2 Collective Checklist

  • Bosser: farm Barrows sets (at least one full set in shared storage) and a Dragon Scimitar from Draynor.
  • All members: reach 43 Prayer via Prayer Training so Protect prayers unlock for bossing safety.
  • Farmer/Crafter: brew a stock of Combat Potions and Prayer Potions for the team.
  • Group goal: complete Waterfall Quest and Tree Gnome Village for instant Attack/Strength/HP boosts.
  • Money: establish a group beginner money-making method (e.g., temporary event shops, clue scrolls, or resource selling internally) for bonds if needed.
  • Milestone: first group boss kill of a mid-tier boss (e.g., King Black Dragon or Giant Mole) using shared supplies.

By the end of week two, a healthy GIM should have a shared bank that looks like a real economy: stacks of food, multiple armor sets, a pile of potions, some rares, and a clear schedule of who is doing what next. If your shared storage is empty and everyone is off doing their own thing with no coordination, that is the early warning sign of the mistakes we cover next. The groups that thrive treat week two's boss kills as group victories, not individual loot screenshots.

5. Common GIM Mistakes That Wreck Groups

Most Group Ironman groups do not fail because the mode is hard. They fail because of people problems that were predictable from day one. If you internalize the five mistakes below, your group will outlast the vast majority that quit within a month. These are ordered roughly by how often they actually kill groups.

Mistake 1 — Kicking and Leaving Over Drama

The permanent nature of kicks and leaves is the deadliest feature of GIM. Because leaving converts you to a solo ironman and you can never rejoin, a single heated argument can end a friendship-shaped account forever. The fix is boring but effective: agree on a conflict policy before you start. Decide that kicks only happen for confirmed inactivity or genuine betrayal, never in the heat of the moment. Many groups add a "24-hour cool-down rule" — any kick decision must be discussed and waited a day. The owner should be the most level-headed player, not just the first one to click create.

Mistake 2 — Hoarding the Shared Bank

Hoarding is the silent group-killer. One member treats shared storage like a personal vault, withdrawing everything and depositing nothing, while others contribute and watch their surplus vanish. Resentment builds, contributors stop contributing, and the supply line collapses. Prevent it with a stated norm: "take what you need, replace what you can." Some groups go further and set soft limits (e.g., "no more than 200 of any food out at once without asking"). The point is not to be strict — it is to make the expectation explicit so a hoarder stands out early and can be talked to before it festers.

⚠️ Red flag to watch for

If a member goes silent for a week AND has emptied shared storage of valuables, treat it as a warning sign. Communicate early. A quick "hey, everything okay?" message resolves more group deaths than any loot strategy.

Mistake 3 — No Agreed Plan or Goals

A GIM with no plan is just five solo ironmen who happen to share a bank. Without shared goals, people drift: one rushes bosses, another quits skilling, and the roles you set in section 3 evaporate. The cure is a written (or at least chat-pinned) group plan: this week's targets, who is doing what, and the next big unlock you are all working toward. Ten minutes of planning per session keeps five people pulling the same direction. Groups that "just wing it" are the ones that quietly stop logging in together.

Mistake 4 — Mismatched Activity Levels

Nothing breeds resentment like three players grinding daily while two log in once a week and still withdraw from the shared bank. Before forming the group, be honest about how many hours per week each person can really play. A group of two hardcore and three casual players can work if the casuals are happy being supporters (farmers, skillers with passive gains) rather than front-line bossers. The mismatch is fatal only when it is hidden. Talk about real availability up front.

💡 Use async play to your advantage

GIM does not require synchronized play. A casual player who does daily tree runs and birdhouses still contributes enormous value to a hardcore bossing core. Frame roles around real life, not ideal life.

Mistake 5 — Skipping the Boring Unlocks

Beginners often rush straight to bossing and skip the teleports, quests, and diaries that make everything faster. A GIM that ignores easy unlocks stays slow and feels like a worse solo ironman. The discipline to spend week one on quests and teleports (section 4) is what separates a smooth group from a frustrated one. If your group is bored of questing, split it: two quest while three skill, then swap. Keep the plan moving without forcing everyone through the same dull content simultaneously.

6. GIM vs Regular Ironman — Which Should You Pick?

The final question every new ironman faces: do you go Group, or do you go solo? There is no universally correct answer — only the one that matches your playstyle and social situation. Below is an honest comparison to help you decide, plus guidance on when to pick each.

When to Pick Group Ironman

Choose GIM if you have two to four friends who will actually play and you enjoy coordinated progress. GIM is objectively the fastest way to experience mid-to-late game content because the workload is split. You will get to try bosses, raids, and build a big base far sooner than solo. It is also simply more social and more fun for many players — sharing a loot drop with four friends feels better than a silent lone grind. If your friends are reliable and you like the idea of a shared bank, GIM is the better pick. For long-term supply planning once you are established, our Ironman Lategame Supply Guide covers what a mature group bank should look like.

When to Pick Regular Ironman

Choose a solo ironman if you value total independence, have an unpredictable schedule, or do not trust anyone else with your progress. Solo ironman has zero people-risk: no kicks, no hoarders, no drama. The cost is that every grind is yours alone, so reaching late game takes longer and some content (like group-only raids requiring a team) needs you to find temporary teammates outside your account. If you have been burned by flaky groups before, or you just like doing things your own way, solo is the safe, calm choice.

🎯 Quick Decision Rule

  • Pick GIM if: you have 1-4 reliable friends, enjoy teamwork, and want faster access to late-game content.
  • Pick solo iron if: you play alone by preference, have a chaotic schedule, or want zero social risk.
  • Pick Hardcore iron: only if you love permadeath pressure and accept the group/solo trade-offs above.
💡 You can start solo, then... no

Be aware: you cannot convert a solo ironman into a Group Ironman later, and you cannot convert GIM back except by leaving (which makes you solo permanently). The choice is effectively final at creation, so decide with your friends before anyone clicks.

Many experienced players recommend this pragmatic path: if you are unsure, start with a regular ironman to learn the game's systems and confirm you enjoy ironman pace, then invite close friends to a fresh GIM once you know you are committed. That way you never risk a permanent group decision on a mode you have not tried. Either way, the skills you build — efficient skilling, boss mechanics, resource management — transfer perfectly between the modes, so nothing is wasted.

7. Group Ironman FAQs

Can a Group Ironman have just 2 members?

Yes. The group size range is 2 to 5, so a duo GIM is fully supported and quite popular among couples or close friends. Two committed players still get the shared-storage advantage, though the workload split is less dramatic than with five. You can also start with two and invite up to three more later, as long as you stay within the 2-5 cap and the owner sends invites.

What happens if I leave my GIM group?

Leaving is permanent. Your account immediately becomes a standard solo ironman, you lose access to the shared group storage, and you can never rejoin the same group. Any items left in shared storage stay with the group. Because of this, only leave if you are certain you no longer want to play with the team. Kicks work the same way from the owner's side — the removed player becomes a solo ironman.

Can I trade items to non-group players in GIM?

No. Like all ironmen, Group Ironman accounts cannot use the Grand Exchange and cannot trade with players outside the group. Your entire economy is internal to the 2-5 person roster and the shared storage. This is what keeps the mode self-sufficient and is non-negotiable — it is the core ironman rule.

Do all group members have to be online at the same time?

No, and this is one of GIM's biggest strengths. The shared storage is always available, so you can deposit loot and log off while a teammate in a different time zone withdraws what they need later. GIM is asynchronous cooperation — you coordinate through the bank and chat, not by forcing synchronized play sessions. This makes it ideal for adults with busy schedules.

Can I convert my existing solo ironman into a Group Ironman?

No. Account type changes of that kind are not allowed. A solo ironman cannot become a GIM, and a GIM cannot become a solo ironman except by leaving (which permanently converts the leaver to solo). You must create a brand-new account and choose Group Ironman at creation if you want the mode. Plan the decision with friends before anyone starts.

What is the best group size for a new GIM?

For most new groups, three to four active players is the sweet spot. Two can feel thin if one goes inactive, and five is harder to coordinate and increases people-risk. Three or four gives strong role coverage (skiller, bosser, farmer/crafter, flex) while keeping communication manageable. Prioritize reliable activity over a high headcount — a committed trio beats a flaky five.
