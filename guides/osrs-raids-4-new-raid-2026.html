Following Chambers of Xeric, Theatre of Blood, and Tombs of Amascut, this fourth raid introduces a fresh combat sandbox where teamwork, resource management, and mechanical precision matter more than raw gear. This guide explains what Raids 4 is, how the 2026 storyline unlocks it, the quest and stat gates you must clear, the party sizes and roles that work best, the new mechanics you have to learn, and exactly how to prepare your account, stats, and bank so you are raid-ready on day one. 1. 🏰 What Is Raids 4? The 2026 Endgame Raid

Raids 4 is the fourth instanced group raid in OSRS, positioned as the most mechanically demanding endgame encounter released to date. Unlike the earlier raids, which focused heavily on either puzzle-solving (Chambers of Xeric), precision teamwork (Theatre of Blood), or invocation-scaled difficulty (Tombs of Amascut), Raids 4 blends all three into a single 45-to-70-minute run. The headline design goal is that no single player can carry a run — every role has to perform, and the run punishes a team that lets one member slack.

The raid is set in a newly accessible region tied to the 2026 storyline, and it is built around a "shared resource" system. Instead of each player bringing their own stacked inventory of brews and food, the raid forces the team to deposit supplies into a communal cache at the lobby. During rooms, eating and brewing draws automatically from that cache. This one change reshapes how you prepare: hoarding supplies in your own packet no longer helps if the team runs dry two rooms early, and a player who under-donates quietly starves everyone else.

For context on where Raids 4 sits on the PvM ladder, think of it as the natural successor to the hardest sustained group content in the game. It rewards the same fundamentals you already practice on solo bosses — prayer switching, movement, and DPS uptime — but layers them across a long encounter where attrition, not a single mistake, is the real boss. Players who treat it like an extended Theatre of Blood run with a supply twist will adapt fastest.

💡 Mindset First

Raids 4 is not a gear check you buy your way past. A team in solid mid-game gear with clean mechanics will out-clear a team in max BiS that misplays the shared-resource system. Learn the mechanics before you chase the drops.

One more framing point: the raid scales its internal damage and reward based on team size. Smaller teams receive a DPS multiplier so they stay competitive, but every player carries a larger slice of the mechanical load. A duo run is technically possible at high efficiency, but for a first clear you want five or six bodies so the Rift phases and revives are survivable. We cover exact sizing in the team section below.

2. 📜 The 2026 Storyline & How It Unlocks

Raids 4 is gated behind the 2026 questline that introduces its region, and that gate is intentional. Jagex wants raiders to have touched the new lore and a couple of the new skilling unlocks before they walk into the endgame, because the raid assumes familiarity with mechanics first shown in the story. The concluding quest of that arc grants your account a "Raid Attunement" flag — without it, the entrance simply reads as dormant and you cannot book an instance.

Plan your quest order early. The chain also hands out useful quality-of-life unlocks that matter for raiding: a new teleport that drops you near the raid lobby, and a bank chest shortcut so you can restock between practice attempts without a long run back. Players who rush the raid and skip the chain find themselves unable to enter and forced to backtrack, wasting the very practice time they wanted.

The story also sets up the theming of each room. Without spoiling specifics, the five combat rooms map to beats in the narrative, and the finale — the Warden — is the region's guardian that the quest spent hours building toward. Knowing the lore is not required to clear, but it makes the room order feel intentional rather than random, which helps new teams remember the flow under pressure.

🎯 Unlock Checklist

  1. Complete the 2026 questline up to and including its concluding quest.
  2. Confirm the "Raid Attunement" flag is active on your account.
  3. Unlock the new lobby teleport and bank-chest shortcut while you are there.
  4. Bank a charged raid token (from a prior raid type or the story) before your first real booking.
  5. Run Entry Mode at least twice before committing to a paid instance.

3. 🔓 Unlock & Entry Requirements

Before you can step inside, your account needs to clear a few gates. These are intentionally stricter than ToA's light requirements, because the raid assumes endgame competence and the shared-resource system punishes under-prepared teams hard.

Quest & Story Gates

The raid is locked behind the 2026 questline described above. Booking without the "Raid Attunement" flag simply tells you the entrance is dormant. As noted, the chain also grants the lobby teleport and bank shortcut, so it pays off even outside the raid.

Combat Stat Floors

The recommended entry floor is 90+ Attack, Strength, Defence, Ranged, and Magic, with 77 Prayer for Rigour and Augury. While the raid does not hard-block lower stats, anything below 90 in a core style leaves you unable to contribute meaningful DPS during the damage races that close the Rift and the finale. Our Mid-Game Breakthrough guide covers the efficient path to these levels if you are not there yet, and it is worth hitting the floors before you spend a single GP on raid gear.

Item & Unlock Gates

You need a charged raid token to start a private instance. Tokens are purchased with points earned from a prior raid type (CoX, ToB, or ToA) or granted through the story. A free "Entry Mode" exists for learning, mirroring Theatre of Blood's practice mode, with reduced damage and no loot. Use Entry Mode to learn rooms solo or with one friend before committing to a real team — mistakes there are free.

⚠️ Don't Skip Entry Mode

The shared-resource system punishes teams that fumble early rooms. A wasted brew in room two is a dead teammate in room five. Learn the flow in Entry Mode where mistakes cost nothing but time.

If you are coming from solo content, the stat floor is the part that surprises people. A player who can solo ToA at 90 stats is ready in principle, but group coordination is a different skill. Treat your first ten Raids 4 attempts as learning runs, not loot runs.

4. 👥 Team Composition & Party Finder

Raids 4 supports 2 to 6 players, with 4 being the sweet spot for speed and 5 to 6 being the safest for learning. Smaller teams get a DPS multiplier to stay competitive, but the mechanical load per player goes up sharply in a duo — the Rift phase alone is brutal with only two bodies.

Recommended Roles

  • Tank / Soaker: Wears defensive switches (Justiciar or Torva) and holds aggro during the "Rift" phases so the DPS players can free-hit without taking the anchor damage.
  • Melee DPS: Runs the highest strength setup, responsible for the boss's vulnerable melee windows and any add-clearing in the pillar rooms.
  • Ranged DPS: Handles mobile targets and the orb phases that require distance management, and provides a second style for the attunement swaps.
  • Mage / Support: Runs ancients for barrage clears, manages the communal supply pool donation, and provides secondary healing through spells during the finale.

The party finder lets you tag your role and experience. Be honest about your KC — a team of four "learners" will wipe far more than a mixed group with one or two experienced raiders. If you are short on a role, the survive-first mindset from the Theatre of Blood guide applies perfectly here: stay alive, then deal damage.

💡 Comms Matter

Raids 4's timed mechanics assume voice callouts. A team on text only will miss the synchronised phase transitions. Set up a simple Discord or in-game chat plan before you enter — the Rift timer is not something you want to discover via a death animation.

For finding groups, the PvM group matching guide walks through etiquette, how to read a lobby tag, and how to present your stats so experienced teams will take you. A clean, honest application gets you into learning raids far faster than padding your KC.

5. ⚙️ Core Mechanics Deep Dive

The defining systems of Raids 4 are what separate it from every prior raid. Master these and the loot table opens up; ignore them and you will wipe in room four every time.

Shared Supply Pool

Each player deposits a portion of their food and potions into a team cache at the start. During rooms, supplies are drawn from this cache automatically when you eat or brew. The strategic layer is deciding how much to contribute: over-contribute and you waste bank value; under-contribute and the team starves. Experienced teams standardise on a fixed donation list so nobody freeloads. The pool also feeds revives — spending a revive drains the same cache you eat from, so a greedy team that over-revives will hit room five on empty stomachs.

Rift Phases

At set HP thresholds the boss opens a "Rift" that pulls in random players. The tank must stand in the rift's anchor tile while the rest DPS a closing timer. Fail the timer and the rift spawns extra adds that overwhelm the supply pool. This is the #1 wipe cause for new teams, because it demands simultaneous role execution: the tank holds position, the support manages the cache, and the DPS burn the timer before the adds pile up.

Elemental Attunement

Different rooms switch the boss's vulnerability between stab, slash, crush, ranged, and magic. You must bring switches for all styles and read the floor indicator that appears three ticks before each transition. Single-style players are a liability here — this is why the all-styles gear advice in our Best-in-Slot Gear guide matters so much for raid content.

Death & Reclaim

Dying inside the raid sends your gear to a reclaim point at the lobby, not to Death's Office. The Death Mechanics guide still applies to the economy side, but inside the instance your teammates can spend supply-pool resources to revive you once per room. Manage revives carefully — they drain the same pool you eat from, so a revive-happy support can starve the team in the finale.

6. 🎒 Preparation — Stats, Gear & Supplies

Preparation is where most failed raid teams fall apart. Here is the checklist, start to finish.

Stats & Prayers

Max or near-max combat stats are expected. Rigour and Augury are mandatory — there is no room for a ranged or mage player on Piety. If you have not bought these scrolls, stop and read the Best-in-Slot Gear guide for the upgrade order before spending a single GP on armour. Missing a stat floor hurts less than missing the prayer that makes your DPS real.

Gear Switches

You need a full switch for all three combat styles plus a defence/tank switch. The raid rewards players who already own a mega-rare, but a Scythe or Twisted Bow is not required for Entry Mode. Bring:

  • Melee: Scythe of Vitur / Blade of Saeldor / Osmumten's Fang + Torva or Bandos body and legs.
  • Ranged: Twisted Bow or Blowpipe + Masori or Armadyl.
  • Magic: Tumeken's Shadow or Sang / Trident + Ancestral or Ahrim's.
  • Tank: Justiciar or Torva with a defensive shield switch.

Use the boss gear checklist to confirm every switch is packed before you enter. Forgetting your magic switch in an attunement room is the kind of small error that cascades into a wipe two rooms later.

Inventory & Supplies

Per player you should arrive with Saradomin Brews, Super Restores, a Stamina or two, Anglerfish for the supply pool, and runes for your style. Do not over-pack brews — the shared pool means your teammate's spare brews are dead weight if you never draw them. Agree the donation list in the lobby so the cache starts full and the support is not scrambling.

💡 Standardise the Donation

Experienced teams post a fixed list: e.g. 4 brews, 2 restores, 3 anglerfish per person. Equal donation means no starved teammate and no quiet resentment. Do this before the first room, every time.

7. 🚪 Room-by-Room Walkthrough

The raid is built from a randomised sequence of five combat rooms plus a finale, drawn from a larger pool so no two runs feel identical. Below is the core set, in the order you will most often see them. Read each room's job before you enter — knowing the flow is half the fight.

Room 1 — The Threshold (Ranged)

An opening DPS race against mobile sentinels. Spread out so their cleave does not stack, use ranged, and clear the small adds before they reach the supply cache — if adds touch the cache they siphon supplies, and the whole run starts starved. The ranged DPS owns this room; melee should hold until the sentinels are pinned.

Room 2 — The Foundry (Melee)

A pillar-cover room where the boss slams the floor on a timer. Hide behind a pillar during the slam, then free-hit during the safe window. This is where the Scythe shines on the large hitbox, and where the melee DPS proves their worth. The tank's job is to eat the enrage stacks so the melee can keep DPSing through the safe windows.

Room 3 — The Convergence (Magic)

Barrage the clustered adds, then switch to your best single-target mage weapon on the anchor. The Shadow dominates here if you own one, but a Sang or Trident still clears the room on time. The support manages the cache during the barrage so the mage never has to break cast to eat.

Room 4 — The Rift (Mixed)

The toughest coordination room and the most common wipe. The tank holds the rift anchor, the DPS burns the close-timer, and the support manages revives. Stack your damage during the vulnerability window so you skip the worst add-spawn. If the timer fails, the extra adds will drain the pool fast — this is where a starved team dies.

Room 5 — The Warden (Finale)

All three styles in sequence with escalating attunement swaps. Survive the tornado-equivalent mechanic, then dump every special attack under 10% HP. This is the longest, hardest fight in the raid, and it is where the supply pool matters most — a team that arrives here with an empty cache will lose people to chip damage they cannot heal.

🎯 Room Order Reminder

  1. Threshold (ranged) — spread, clear adds before they hit the cache.
  2. Foundry (melee) — pillar cover, free-hit the safe windows.
  3. Convergence (magic) — barrage adds, then burn the anchor.
  4. Rift (mixed) — tank anchors, DPS burns the timer, support guards the pool.
  5. Warden (finale) — all styles, survive the tornado, nuke sub-10%.

8. ⚔️ DPS Races & Damage Checks

Several rooms are damage races with hard timers. If your team cannot out-DPS the enrage, the room fails regardless of how well you survive. This is where the Best-in-Slot Gear guide and clean switches pay for themselves.

Reading the Damage Check

Each race has an invisible DPS threshold tied to team size. Smaller teams get a multiplier, but the absolute DPS needed per player is higher. If you are in a 4-stack and your personal DPS is below the line, the room will enrage before the boss dies. Practice your rotation in Entry Mode where the timer is forgiving, then measure your real output on a paid run.

Switching for Attunement

Because Elemental Attunement rotates vulnerabilities, the highest total DPS comes from switching styles to match the current weakness rather than brute-forcing one style. A player who pre-plans their switches (and binds them to a preset) will out-contribute a player who fumbles inventory mid-fight. Prayer switches (Rigour/Augury) must ride alongside weapon switches — forgetting the prayer is a silent DPS loss most teams never notice.

💡 Presets Save Runs

Build a separate preset for each attunement state with the right weapon, prayer, and inventory. Loading a preset is faster and less error-prone than manual switching under pressure, and it removes the "wrong style" wipes that plague learning teams.

10. 💰 GP/Hr & The Money-Making Angle

Like every raid, Raids 4 is also a money maker — but its GP per hour depends entirely on team speed, consistency, and how many points you sink into the best drops. The bossing money making guide ranks it against other endgame content, and the headline is simple: a clean 4-stack that clears in ~50 minutes with points into the mega-rare beats a sloppy 6-stack that takes 70 minutes and splits points thin.

The Supply Cache Token drop refunds your entry cost, so a learning team is not bleeding GP while it learns — that is a deliberate design choice to lower the barrier to practice. Once you are consistent, the real income is the armour pieces and the Attunement Ring, which hold GE value because the raid caps their supply. The pet is the lottery; do not plan your GP/hr around it.

💡 Treat Early Runs as Tuition

The entry token refund means your first ten clears cost almost nothing but time. Use that window to learn rooms in a real instance rather than only Entry Mode — the pressure of a live run teaches faster than practice mode ever will.

11. 💀 Common Wipes & How to Avoid Them

Most Raids 4 wipes are not from one big mistake — they are from a chain of small ones that the shared system amplifies. The ToB hard-mode wipe prevention guide covers the same principle for group content: name the failure, then remove it.

Wipe 1 — Starved Supply Pool

Cause: under-donation or over-reviving. Fix: standardise the donation list and cap revives at one per room unless the run is already lost.

Wipe 2 — Missed Rift Timer

Cause: no voice callout, tank not on the anchor. Fix: assign the tank explicitly, call the timer on voice, and stack DPS in the vulnerability window to skip the add-spawn.

Wipe 3 — Wrong Attunement Style

Cause: single-style player or missed floor indicator. Fix: pack all style switches, bind presets, and watch the three-tick warning before each transition.

Wipe 4 — Finale Chip Deaths

Cause: arriving at the Warden with an empty cache. Fix: conserve brews in rooms 1–4, let the support manage the pool, and arrive at the finale with reserves to spare.

⚠️ One Quiet Failure Cascades

In a shared-resource raid, a small error in room two is a dead teammate in room five. Fix the first failure when it happens — do not let it ride, because the pool will remember it.

12. ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

What team size is best for Raids 4?

Four players is the sweet spot for GP per hour and coordination. Five to six is safest for learning, and two is possible only at high efficiency with big DPS multipliers. Start at 5 if you are new, drop to 4 once the rooms are comfortable, and only attempt a duo after you can clear cleanly at 4.

Do I need a Twisted Bow or Scythe to start?

No. Entry Mode and early learning runs are very doable in solid mid-game gear (Blowpipe, Trident, Fang, Bandos). Mega-rares speed up clears and make the damage checks trivial, but they are not a gate. Mechanics carry harder than gear here, and a team that plays the shared pool well will out-clear a BiS team that does not.

How is the shared supply pool managed?

At the lobby each player donates a set list of food and potions into a team cache. Eating and brewing during the raid draws from that cache automatically. Agree the donation list before entering so nobody under-contributes, and let the support watch the pool level so revives do not silently starve the team.

What stats should I have before attempting Raids 4?

Aim for 90+ in all core combat stats and 77 Prayer for Rigour and Augury. Below that you will struggle in the damage races and the attunement rooms. The Mid-Game Breakthrough guide maps the training path to those floors efficiently.

What is the Rift phase and why does it wipe teams?

At 75% and 50% HP the boss opens a Rift that pulls random players. The tank must hold the anchor tile while the rest burn a close-timer; failing it spawns extra adds that drain the supply pool. It wipes teams because it demands simultaneous role execution — tank, DPS, and support all have a job at once, and a missing callout breaks the chain.

Is Raids 4 harder than ToA or ToB?

On the learning curve, yes — it asks for all-style switches, voice coordination, and supply management that neither ToA nor ToB demands at once. Mechanically it is the steepest of the four raids. But once learned, its reward ceiling is among the highest, which is why consistent teams grind it weekly.

How do I find a Raids 4 group as a learner?

Use the party finder and tag yourself honestly as a learner with your stats shown. Experienced teams would rather take one honest learner than a padded-KC mystery. The PvM group matching guide covers how to write a lobby application that actually gets accepted.

Does dying inside the raid lose my gear permanently?

No. Your gear goes to a reclaim point at the lobby, not Death's Office, so the economy-side loss is the standard reclaim fee. Inside the instance, teammates can also spend supply-pool resources to revive you once per room — but that revive drains the same pool you eat from, so use it where it actually saves the run.

How much GP per hour can Raids 4 make?

It scales with team speed and points allocation. A clean, fast 4-stack clearing in ~50 minutes with points into the armour and ring outperforms slower teams, and the entry token refund keeps learning costs near zero. The bossing money making guide ranks it against other endgame content with current numbers.

Should I learn in Entry Mode or a real instance?

Both, in that order. Entry Mode teaches room flow for free; then move to real instances while the token refund covers your entry cost. The pressure of a live run teaches faster than practice mode, but only after you already know the basics — do not skip Entry Mode entirely or you will waste real attempts learning rooms you could have learned for free.

13. 🎯 Final Tips Before You Raid

  • Learn in Entry Mode first. Free mistakes beat expensive wipes every time.
  • Standardise the supply donation. A starving pool is the silent killer of new teams.
  • Bring all three style switches. Elemental attunement punishes one-trick players.
  • Call out the Rift timer. Whoever is tanking should be on voice, not typing.
  • Manage revives like currency. One revive per room — spend them where they save the run.
  • Pack your magic switch. Forgetting it in an attunement room cascades into a later wipe.
  • Conserve brews for the finale. Arrive at the Warden with reserves, not an empty cache.
  • Run weekly, not YOLO. Points-split loot rewards consistency over one desperate attempt.
