1. Herblore Basics — Cleaning Herbs

Herblore is one of the slowest and most expensive skills in Old School RuneScape, but it is also one of the most useful. Almost every serious PvM encounter, every raid, and most boss trips rely on potions that you brew with this skill. At its core, Herblore is a two-step process. First you obtain a grimy herb, usually by farming, monster drops, or buying from the Grand Exchange. Then you clean the herb by clicking it, which turns a grimy guam leaf into a clean guam leaf and awards a small amount of experience. After that you combine the clean herb with a secondary ingredient and a vial of water to create an unfinished potion, and finally add a third ingredient to finish the potion.

Cleaning herbs is the entry point of the skill and the part that frustrates most new players. When you first click a grimy herb it shows a small animation and the herb becomes "clean". The experience reward for cleaning is tiny compared to actually making the potion. For example, cleaning a grimy guam leaf gives only 2.5 experience, but making a guam potion (unfinished plus eye of newt) gives 50 experience in total. This means cleaning alone is a terrible way to train. You should never sit and clean herbs for XP unless you have no other option. The realistic role of cleaning is simply to prepare herbs you will later turn into potions, and the XP from cleaning is a small bonus on top of the much larger XP from mixing.

How cleaning works and what affects it

Every herb type has a "grimy" and a "clean" version. The grimy version is what drops from monsters and what you harvest from a farming patch. Cleaning requires no tools, no level other than the level needed to use the herb in a potion, and no special location. You can clean herbs anywhere in Gielinor. The main factors that matter are the price difference between grimy and clean herbs, and your ability to buy clean herbs directly to skip cleaning entirely.

🎯 The Three Core Actions of Herblore

  • Clean — turn grimy herb into clean herb (small XP, prepares for mixing).
  • Make Unfinished (Unf) — add clean herb to a vial of water; this is where most profit methods live.
  • Finish — add the secondary ingredient to complete the potion and bank the big XP and value.

One important detail that intermediate players often miss is that buying clean herbs directly from the Grand Exchange is almost always more efficient than buying grimy and cleaning them yourself. The GP you "save" by cleaning is usually smaller than the time you waste, and time is the real currency when you are pushing toward 99. The only exception is if you are ironman and must supply yourself, or if grimy herbs are dramatically cheaper than clean ones because of a market dip. As a general rule, treat cleaning as a preparation step, not a training method.

Tools and locations that help

You do not need any special tool to clean herbs, but a few quality-of-life upgrades make the grind far less painful. The Herb sack (purchasable from the farming guild after 58 Farming) stores up to 30 of each herb type and lets you note grimy herbs from a farmer, which is a huge convenience for ironmen. The Botanist's mask from the Temple Trekking and Burning of acy scenes gives a small chance to clean herbs instantly in batches and provides noted herbs. For pure speed, many players use the Make-X interface and let the game auto-mix while they do something else, because Herblore is one of the most click-light skills once set up.

💡 Tip — Skip cleaning with the GE

Buy clean herbs directly from the Grand Exchange whenever the grimy-to-clean margin is under a few coins. You will pay a tiny premium but save enormous amounts of time, and time saved is XP-per-hour gained. Reserve cleaning for herbs you farm yourself or receive as drops.

Herb tiers and where they come from

Herbs scale in value and usefulness. Low-tier herbs like Guam, Marrentill, Tarromin, and Harralander are cheap and used for low-level potions and quests. Mid-tier herbs like Ranarr, Toadflax, and Irit are the workhorses of money making because they feed potions that are always in demand: Ranarr for Prayer potions, Toadflax for Saradomin brews, Irit for super attack potions. High-tier herbs like Avantoe, Kwuarm, Cadantine, Lantadyme, Dwarf weed, and Torstol are expensive and used in the strongest potions. The single most important herb for PvM is Ranarr, because Prayer potions are consumed constantly in every boss kill. If you want a deeper foundation on how Prayer fits into your boss trips, read our Prayer Training Beginner Guide 2026 to understand why Prayer potions are worth brewing in bulk.

2. Profit Methods — Make/Unf & Potion Flipping

Herblore has a reputation for being a money sink, but that reputation is only half true. If you train blindly by buying finished potions or by making the most expensive potions at every level, you will burn millions of GP. If you train intelligently, you can actually make money while leveling, or at least cut your total cost dramatically. The secret is understanding the "make/unf" economy and knowing which potions are always in demand.

What "Make/Unf" means and why it prints GP

An "unf" is an unfinished potion: a clean herb dropped into a vial of water. The make/unf method works because of how the Grand Exchange prices herbs versus unfinished potions. In many cases, the combined cost of a clean herb plus a vial of water is LOWER than the street price of the unfinished potion. When that gap exists, you can buy clean herbs and vials, make them into unfinished potions, and sell the unf potions for a profit, while also collecting the XP from making the unf. This is one of the few skilling methods in the game that is both profitable and gives experience.

The classic example is the Ranarr potion (unf). Ranarr weeds are expensive, but the demand for Prayer potions is enormous, so Ranarr unf always sells quickly. A player with a modest bank can buy clean ranarr, buy vials of water, make ranarr unf, and sell them, often turning a small profit per potion while banking Herblore XP. The same logic applies to Toadflax unf (for Saradomin brews) and Irit unf (for super attack). The margin changes daily with the GE, so you must check live prices before committing. Never assume a margin exists; verify it.

🎯 The Make/Unf Profit Loop

  • Step 1: Check GE price of clean herb + vial of water vs price of the unf potion.
  • Step 2: If unf price > herb + vial cost, buy the inputs and make unf in bulk.
  • Step 3: Sell the unf potions; collect XP and any GP margin.
  • Step 4: Reinvest profit into the next herb tier as you level.

Potion flipping for steady income

Beyond make/unf, you can flip finished potions. Potions like Prayer potions, Saradomin brews, Super restores, and Overloads are consumed by thousands of players every day. Their prices fluctuate with the price of raw herbs and with the release of new content. A player who watches the market can buy potions when herb prices dip and sell when they rise. This is not pure Herblore training, but it is a legitimate way to fund your 1-99 journey. If you want a broader framework for turning skilling into cash, our Money Making Beginner Guide 2026 covers the Grand Exchange flipping mindset that makes this work.

The "buy unf, finish, sell" variant

An even more advanced variant is to buy unfinished potions from other players, finish them with the secondary ingredient, and sell the finished potion. Sometimes the secondary ingredient is cheap enough that finishing an unf and selling the potion yields more than selling the unf directly. This is common with potions whose secondaries are abundant, such as the red spiders' eggs used in Prayer potions or the snape grass used in Prayer potions and super restores. The math must be checked constantly because margins are thin and competition is fierce.

💡 Tip — Use the margin, not the fantasy

Make/unf only works when the margin is real. Always subtract the GE tax (1% on trades above 100 GP) and your time. If the margin is under a few GP per potion after tax, skip it and just buy the finished potion to train, because your time is worth more than the tiny profit.

Which potions are always profitable to make

The potions with the most stable demand are the ones used in PvM: Prayer potions (Ranarr), Saradomin brews (Toadflax + nest), Super restores (Snapdragon), and combat super potions (Irit, Kwuarm, Cadantine, Lantadyme, Dwarf weed). Because these are consumed constantly in Chambers of Xeric, Theatre of Blood, and Tombs of Amascut, their unf versions almost always carry a premium. A smart intermediate player times their Herblore training around these potions, both to save money and to stock their own bank for bossing. Remember that making potions for your own use is itself a saving: a Prayer potion you brew costs less than one you buy, and you gain XP doing it.

3. The 1-99 Efficient Training Route

This section is the heart of the guide: a level-by-level route that minimizes cost and maximizes XP per hour. The philosophy is simple. Early levels are cheap and fast if you use the right potions. Mid levels are where you should lean on make/unf and the most efficient potion per XP. Late levels are expensive, so you either accept the cost or use alternative XP like the Nightmare Zone, Garden of Tranquility, and the Dragon Slayer II reward to skip the worst gaps. Below is a practical route that assumes a member account with a reasonable bank.

Levels 1-38 — Cheap foundation

From level 1, make attack potions (Guam + eye of newt) all the way to 12-ish if you want, but the smarter move is to rush to higher potions quickly because low-tier potions give terrible XP per herb. A common efficient start is to make strength potions (Tarromin + limpwurt root) from 12, then restore potions (Harralander + red spiders' eggs) from 21, then prayer potions (Ranarr + snape grass) from 25. Prayer potions are the workhorse: they give decent XP and you will use them forever. Pushing from 25 to 38 on prayer potions is cheap relative to what comes later and stocks your bank with a potion you need anyway.

Levels 38-72 — The profitable middle

At 38 you unlock super strength potions (Kwuarm + limpwurt root). At 45 you unlock super attacks (Irit + eye of newt) and at 48 super antidotes and super defences (Cadantine + white berries) at 66. The 45-66 band is where make/unf shines: Irit unf, Kwuarm unf, and Cadantine unf all have steady demand. Many players push from 38 to 72 almost entirely on super attack, super strength, and super defence potions, because the XP per potion is solid and the potions sell well. Around 63 you unlock super restores (Snapdragon + snape grass), one of the best all-around potions and a strong training choice through the 70s.

🎯 Efficient Route Snapshot

  • 1-25: Attack / Strength / Restore potions (cheap, quest-reward boosted).
  • 25-38: Prayer potions (Ranarr) — bank them for bossing.
  • 38-66: Super attack / strength / defence via make/unf.
  • 66-85: Super restores and anti-venom+ where useful.
  • 85-99: Overloads (96) and extreme potions for max efficiency.

Levels 72-90 — Super restores and brews

Once you can make super restores (63) and Saradomin brews (Toadflax + nest at 81), you have the two most important raid potions. These are excellent training potions because they are expensive enough to give good XP but always in demand, so the loss is small. Pushing 72 to 90 on a mix of super restores and brews is a standard efficient path. Note that Saradomin brews require a bird's nest or crushed nest, which adds cost, so weigh the XP against the price. Many players prefer super restores here because snape grass is relatively cheap and the XP is strong.

Levels 90-99 — The endgame push

The final stretch is where cost explodes. At 90 you can make super combat potions (Super attack + Super strength + Super defence), a popular and decent-XP choice. At 96 you unlock overloads, the crown jewel of Herblore and the main reason many players chase 99. From 96 to 99, making overloads is the obvious route if you can afford it, because the XP per potion is the highest in the skill and the potions are the most valuable in the game. If overloads are too expensive, extreme potions (made from avantoe, dwarf weed, lantadyme, etc.) are a cheaper bridge, though they give less XP and are less useful.

💡 Tip — Use quest and mini-game XP to skip dead zones

Several quests and activities give Herblore XP that can skip the worst low-XP gaps. Druidic Ritual is required and teaches the skill. Dream Mentor gives a large chunk and is required for Dream herbs. Garden of Tranquility grants a helpful boost. The Nightmare Zone and Herb Boxes from the Tithe Farm and Farmer's Affinity reward points are also efficient. Use these to leap past expensive level bands rather than grinding them with GP.

Alternative: the "lamp and book" route for ironmen

Ironmen cannot buy herbs freely, so their route is different. They farm herbs with Farming, collect drops, and use herb boxes from Tithe Farm, anonymous noteable herb sources, and reward lamps from Entrana and Tempest content. The efficient ironman path is to pair Herblore with Farming so that every herb harvested becomes a potion, and to prioritize the potions needed for the next boss unlock. For a main account, the GE route above is fastest; for ironman, self-sufficiency is the only route, so plan your herb farms around Ranarr, Toadflax, and Snapdragon.

Whatever route you choose, the key insight is that Herblore cost is dominated by the last 10-15 levels. The 1-85 band can be done relatively cheaply or even at a small profit via make/unf, but 85-99 is where the millions disappear. Budget accordingly, and consider stopping at 96 (overloads) if 99 is out of reach, because 96 already unlocks the most important endgame potion.

Reading the cost table correctly

The single biggest cost driver is the herb price. Ranarr, Snapdragon, and Toadflax dominate your spend. Third, brew potions you would buy anyway (Prayer, brews, restores) so the "cost" is really a discount versus buying them finished. For most PvM players, 96 is the real finish line.

If your bank is strained, stop at 96. You unlock overloads, the single most impactful Herblore reward, and save 60-100M GP that would otherwise go into the 96-99 push. You can always finish 99 later when richer. This is the most common "smart stop" in the skill.

5. Key Potion Unlocks by Level

Knowing which potion unlocks at which level lets you plan your route and your bank. Below are the potions that matter most for an intermediate player, focusing on the ones used in real gameplay rather than every obscure brew. The levels listed are the Herblore level required to make the potion.

Early unlocks (1-30)

At level 1 you make attack potions (Guam + eye of newt), boosting attack by 3 plus 10%. At 12, strength potions (Tarromin + limpwurt root) boost strength by 3 plus 10% — a staple for early melee training, which pairs well with the progression in our Combat Training Beginner Guide 2026. At 21, restore potions (Harralander + red spiders' eggs) restore depleted stats. At 25, the critical prayer potion (Ranarr + snape grass) restores Prayer points — essential for every boss and every long trip. At 30, energy potions and defence potions arrive, useful but not core.

Mid unlocks (38-66)

At 38, super strength potions (Kwuarm + limpwurt) boost strength by 5 plus 15% — a major melee upgrade. At 45, super attack (Irit + eye of newt) and super defence (Cadantine + white berries at 66) form the classic super set. At 48, anti-fire potions (Lantadyme + dragon scale dust) become available, needed for many dragon bosses. At 55, fishing potions and at 63 super restores (Snapdragon + snape grass) restore all stats including Prayer — the most versatile potion in the game. At 66, super defence completes the super trio used in super combat potions later.

🎯 The "Must-Have" Potion Checklist

  • Prayer potion (25) — non-negotiable for any PvM.
  • Super attack / strength / defence (45/38/66) — the combat super set.
  • Super restore (63) — restores everything, including Prayer.
  • Saradomin brew (81) — the tanking potion for raids.
  • Overload (96) — the endgame power potion.

Late unlocks (70-99)

At 70, super antipoison and antidote+ help with venom. At 78, super energy (Avantoe + mort myre fungus) for run restoration. At 81, Saradomin brew (Toadflax + crushed nest) is the premier defensive potion, absorbing damage while boosting defence and strength at the cost of other stats. At 86, anti-venom+ (Super antipoison + antidote+) grants full venom immunity. At 90, super combat potion combines the three supers into one dose-efficient bottle. At 96, overload (see next section) and at 99 you can make super combat+ variants and the strongest extremes. The 90-99 band is where the real power — and the real cost — lives.

💡 Tip — Brew what you fight with

Do not train on potions you will never use. If you are a melee bosser, prioritize prayer, super combat, brews, and overloads. If you are a ranged or magic PvMer, the same core set applies because overloads and brews are style-neutral. Tailor your route to the content you actually do, and sell or skip the rest.

6. Overloads & Dream Herbs

Overloads are the reason many players grind Herblore to the endgame. An overload is a stacked super potion that temporarily boosts your attack, strength, defence, ranged, and magic by 5 plus 15% (and 13% for magic) above your base level, for six minutes, with the boost refreshing every 15 seconds so it never decays during combat. It is the single most powerful consumable in the game and is required or strongly expected for high-level PvM such as the Theatre of Blood, Tombs of Amascut, and Chambers of Xeric efficiency runs.

How to unlock overloads

Making overloads requires 96 Herblore and completion of the Dream Mentor quest, which itself requires 85 Herblore among other stats. The quest teaches you the dream herb mechanic: a second, rare set of herbs grown only in the patch in the dream within the Meilyr section of Prifddinas after Plague's End, or more commonly obtained as drops. To make an overload you first create the five super potions (super attack, super strength, super defence, super ranging, and super magic), then combine them with a clean torstol and a death ruin at 96 Herblore. The torstol is the dream herb component that makes overloads so expensive.

🎯 Overload Recipe (96 Herblore)

  • Super attack (4) + Super strength (4)
  • Super defence (4) + Super ranging (4)
  • Super magic (4) + Clean torstol
  • Add death rune to finish → Overload (4)

Dream herbs explained

"Dream herbs" refers to the herbs associated with the Dream Mentor and Prifddinas herb system. The most relevant is torstol, the highest-tier herb, which grows in the Prifddinas herb patch (a "dream" patch unlocked after Plague's End) and is also dropped by high-level monsters. Torstol is used for overloads and for zanadar potions and super combat variants. Because the Prifddinas patch can be protected with ultracompost and a harralander or payment, ironmen often farm their own torstol there to avoid the Grand Exchange price. For mains, the GE torstol price is the dominant overload cost factor.

Overload variants and the 99 push

At 96 you make standard overloads. At 98 you can make overload+ (using a super combat potion plus the supers and torstol), and at 99 you unlock the holy overload and elder overload variants when combined with other skills, plus overload salves for raids. The difference between 96 and 99 is meaningful but not massive for casual players; the 96 overload already provides the core boost. This is why the "stop at 96" advice in the cost section is so popular. The extra levels mainly let you make the plus variants and save a tiny amount of inventory space in raids.

💡 Tip — Bank overloads before big raids

Because overloads are expensive and time-consuming to make, many players brew a large batch at once (the Herblore skill is click-light with Make-X) and store them for raid teams. If you are in a clan or raid group, offering to supply overloads is both a service and a way to justify the GP spend. Pair overloads with prayer potions and brews for a complete endgame inventory.

Extreme potions as a bridge

If overloads are out of budget, extreme potions (made from avantoe, dwarf weed, lantadyme, and torstol at 80+) are a cheaper bridge. They give a smaller boost than overloads but far more than supers, and they are useful for players who are close to 96 but not there yet. Extreme attack, extreme strength, extreme defence, extreme ranging, and extreme magic each require a high-tier herb and a secondary, and they are a reasonable "affordable endgame" step. Once you hit 96, however, overloads strictly replace them, so do not over-invest in extremes if 96 is near.

In summary, the dream-herb path — clean torstol, complete Dream Mentor, and reach 96 — is the capstone of an OSRS Herblore journey. It is the most expensive goal in the skill, but it delivers the single biggest practical payoff: the ability to attempt and efficiently clear the hardest PvM content in the game. If you treat 96 as your target and use the cost-saving levers from section 4, the overload dream becomes achievable for any determined intermediate player.

7. Herblore FAQs

Is Herblore worth training to 99 in 2026?

Yes, for any player who intends to do mid-to-high-level PvM. Potions are consumed in every boss kill, raid, and slayer task, and the cost of buying them forever exceeds the one-time cost of training the skill. Even stopping at 96 for overloads pays for itself quickly if you boss regularly. For pure skillers who never PvM, the value is lower, but Prayer potions alone justify reaching at least level 25-38.

What is the fastest way to get from 1 to 99?

Speed is dominated by GP. With an unlimited bank, making overloads from 96-99 is the fastest XP per action, but it costs tens of millions. A balanced fast route uses quest XP to skip early levels, make/unf through the middle, super restores and brews in the 70s, and overloads at the end. Expect 1-99 to take anywhere from a few focused sessions to a couple of weeks depending on your budget and how much make/unf profit you capture.

Can I make money with Herblore instead of losing it?

Partially. The make/unf method can be profitable on high-demand herbs like Ranarr, Toadflax, and Snapdragon when margins are positive, and brewing your own potions saves the markup you would pay at the GE. However, the late game (85-99) is almost always a net loss because overload and extreme ingredients are expensive. Treat Herblore as cost-efficient rather than a primary money maker; pair it with the broader strategies in our Money Making Beginner Guide 2026.

Do I need to clean herbs myself?

No. Buying clean herbs from the Grand Exchange is usually more time-efficient than cleaning grimy ones, because cleaning gives almost no XP and wastes clicks. Clean herbs yourself only when you farm them or receive them as drops, or if you are an ironman supplying your own bank. For mains chasing 99, skip cleaning and buy clean.

What quests give Herblore XP I should do first?

Start with Druidic Ritual (required, teaches the skill, small XP), then Recruitment Drive and Nature Spirit for early boosts. The big one is Dream Mentor (85 Herblore requirement, huge XP, unlocks overloads and dream herbs). Garden of Tranquility and The Hand in the Sand also help. Using these to skip expensive level bands is the cheapest XP in the game.

Should I stop at 96 or push to 99?

For most players, 96 is the smart stop: it unlocks overloads, the most important reward, and saves 60-100M GP versus 99. Push to 99 only if you want the max cape, the plus-variant overloads, or the achievement. The combat benefit of 96-99 is real but small compared to the cost, so bank the savings unless overloads are trivially affordable for you.
