OSRS Chain Bans Explained: When One Ban Hits Your Other Accounts (2026)

You logged in, and two of your accounts are gone instead of one. This guide explains exactly how Jagex connects accounts, which offenses actually spread to your other characters, and the honest, rules-compliant way to recover a clean account that got swept up in a false association. We do not teach cheating, botting, RWT, or detection evasion — this is for innocent and wrongly-linked players only.

1. 🔗 What a "Chain Ban" Actually Means

There is no special "chain ban" button at Jagex that automatically nukes every account you own. What players call a chain ban is the situation where multiple accounts under the same owner receive a punishment after one of them is banned — most often for Real World Trading (RWT) or major macroing — because Jagex's systems associate those accounts with each other.

Understanding this distinction matters, because it changes how you respond:

🧠 The Core Idea

  • One account is banned for a serious rule break (e.g., RWT, botting).
  • Jagex's detection links that account to others you own through shared signals (covered in Section 2).
  • Those linked accounts are then reviewed and may also be actioned — sometimes at the same time, sometimes weeks later.
⚠️ The Honest Reality

If you personally broke the rules on one account, it is within Jagex's rules for the linked accounts to be actioned too. This guide exists to help players who were wrongly associated — for example, a clean Main caught because it shared a home IP with a friend's RWT account, or an innocent alt flagged by coincidence. It is not a workaround for genuine offenses, and we will not tell you how to hide activity.

2. 🕵️ How Jagex Links Accounts Together

Jagex does not publish the exact weighting of its detection systems, but the signals it uses to associate accounts are well established from official statements, support replies, and years of player experience. Below are the four main ways accounts get connected in Jagex's eyes.

① 🌐 Shared IP / Network

Accounts that regularly log in from the same IP address or the same local network (same home WiFi, same VPN exit node, same dorm) are treated as potentially related. This is the most common source of false links — families, housemates, and internet cafés put many unrelated players on one IP.

② 💳 Payment & Billing Information

The strongest association signal is shared payment data: the same credit card, PayPal, Apple/Google account, or bond-purchase history across characters. If you buy membership or bonds for several accounts with one card, Jagex can tie them together with very high confidence.

③ 💻 Hardware / Device Fingerprints

Jagex can recognise the machine you play on through client and system data. Playing multiple accounts on the same PC, especially the same installed client, creates a hard link between them. This is why "I only checked my alt on this laptop once" still associates the two accounts.

④ 🔐 Jagex Account Grouping (Migration)

Since the introduction of the Jagex Account (email-based login), you can group several characters under a single login. By definition, every character inside one Jagex Account is explicitly linked — they share one email, one 2FA, and one recovery path. If one character in the group breaks the rules, the others are, in Jagex's view, the same owner's accounts.

💡 Why This Matters

If your accounts are spread across different logins and different payment methods, a problem on one is less likely to pull the others in. If they are all bundled into one Jagex Account and one card pays for all of them, a serious ban on one is far more likely to be treated as "your accounts" plural. See our account-linking prevention guide for the compliant way to keep things clean.

Not every ban spreads. The table below shows, based on Jagex's published rules and long-standing enforcement patterns, which offenses typically pull in linked accounts and which tend to stay contained.

📌 The Pattern

Chaining is driven by economy crimes (RWT) and automation (botting), because those are the cases where multiple accounts are genuinely involved. A single account that broke a different rule, with no shared gold or botting, is far less likely to take its siblings down.

4. 🆘 Your Clean Main Got Swept Up — Step-by-Step Recovery

If your Main (or any account you know is clean) was actioned because of an association with another account, stay calm and work the official process. Do not create throwaway accounts to "test" logins — that only muddies the picture.

Step 1: Read the exact ban reason on every account

Log in to the Jagex Support / Account Management page for each affected character and note the stated reason (RWT, macroing, etc.) and the date. You cannot appeal well until you know what each account is accused of.

Step 2: Appeal each account SEPARATELY

Every character is appealed on its own appeal. There is no "appeal my whole account group at once" form. Submit an honest appeal for the clean account specifically, explaining why it is not part of the offense.

Step 3: Stop sharing the risky environment while appealing

If the link came from a shared PC, shared WiFi, or a friend's RWT, avoid logging the clean account in from that same device/network until the appeal is resolved. This prevents the "still associated" signal from reinforcing itself.

Step 4: Gather proof of legitimate ownership

Collect what shows the clean account is yours and was used normally: purchase receipts for that account, the creation date you remember, characters/items you trained legitimately, and any 2FA/recovery setup. See Section 5 for how to present it.

Step 5: Wait for the appeal window, then follow up once

Jagex asks you to wait for the appeal to be reviewed rather than spamming. If it is rejected and you have new evidence, send one clear follow-up. Mass-appealing the same account repeatedly can work against you.

5. 📝 Building an Appeal That Proves Your Innocence

A good appeal is honest, specific, and evidence-backed. Jagex reviewers look for a coherent story, not for clever wording. Lying in an appeal is itself a violation and can extend or deepen a ban.

⑤ 🗂️ What evidence to gather

  • Membership / bond receipts tied to the clean account (email confirmations, bank statements with the amount).
  • Account creation details you genuinely remember (approximate date, original email, first stats trained).
  • Legitimate progress proof — bosses killed, quests done, levels achieved through normal play.
  • The IP/device context — if you shared a network with the offending account, explain the real-world relationship (housemate, family, campus WiFi) honestly.

⑥ ✍️ How to write the appeal

✅ A Clear Appeal Structure

  1. State the account (login / character name) and the ban reason you were given.
  2. Say plainly that this account did not RWT / bot — no hedging.
  3. Explain the link if you know it (shared WiFi with a friend, one PC used by siblings, etc.).
  4. Attach the evidence you gathered above.
  5. Ask specifically for a manual review of this individual account.

⑦ 🚫 What NOT to do

  • Do not lie about what an account was used for.
  • Do not submit the same appeal 20 times — it does not speed anything up.
  • Do not pay third-party "unban" services — they are scams and can cost you more accounts.
  • Do not ask Jagex to "just unban everything" — appeal the clean account on its merits.

6. 🔐 Jagex Account Migration & the "Group" Logic

Migrating to a Jagex Account is genuinely good for security — it adds proper 2FA, removes the old character-name login, and protects against hijacking. But it also makes the ownership link explicit.

SetupAssociation StrengthChain-Ban Risk if One Breaks Rules
Several characters in ONE Jagex AccountExplicit (same login)Higher — they are "your accounts" by definition
Separate logins, separate cardsWeak / noneLower — only shared IP/device would link them
One card pays for several loginsStrong via billingMedium–High — payment ties them
💡 Migration Is Still Recommended

We are not telling you to avoid Jagex Accounts — they are the safer login. The point is simply to understand that grouping characters makes them formally "yours together." If you play clean, that is a non-issue. For the official migration steps and FAQ, read our Jagex Account FAQ.

7. 🧭 Honest Note: If You Actually Broke the Rules

We will say this plainly, because honesty is the whole point of this site. If you did buy gold, sell items for real money, or run a bot on one of the accounts, then actioning the linked accounts is within Jagex's published rules. No appeal trick changes that, and we will not help you disguise it.

The constructive path is the same one every long-term player eventually takes:

🛡️ Play Fair From Here

  • Accept the result on the account that broke the rules.
  • Keep any genuinely clean account separate (different device/network where possible) and play it honestly.
  • Learn the actual rules from the official Jagex Account & rules pages.
  • Protect what you have with 2FA and the anti-scam habits in our Account Security & Anti-Scam Guide.

8. ❓ Chain Ban FAQs

Will my Main be banned just because my brother got banned on our shared home WiFi?

Not automatically. Shared IP alone is a weak link and is a common source of false associations — families and housemates share networks all the time. If your Main has no RWT or botting history, appeal it honestly and explain the shared network. Jagex's systems weigh multiple signals, not IP alone.

Does Jagex ban every account on the same IP?

No. An IP is one signal among several. Internet cafés, universities, and households put many innocent players on one address. A ban spreads based on corroborating links (payment, device, gold movement, Jagex Account grouping) — not IP by itself.

I bought gold once years ago and now an account is banned — is that a chain ban?

That is an RWT ban on the account that received or bought the gold, and RWT is exactly the offense most likely to chain to linked accounts. We don't help disguise past RWT. If a different, clean account got swept in, appeal it on its own merits as described in Section 4.

Can I appeal a chain ban if I did nothing wrong?

Yes. Innocent accounts are meant to be restored through the normal appeal process. Appeal the clean account separately with specific evidence (Section 5). If the only reason it was actioned was an association with another account, a clear, honest appeal is your best route.

How long does a ban appeal take?

Timelines vary and Jagex does not guarantee a fixed window. Appeals are reviewed in order; sending one calm, complete appeal and waiting is more effective than repeated messages. Check the support centre for your ticket status rather than creating new accounts.

Will filing an appeal make things worse?

A truthful appeal will not make a clean account's situation worse. What can hurt is lying in the appeal or using ban-evasion tactics. Be honest, stick to the facts, and avoid anything that looks like trying to circumvent the system.

My alt was banned for RWT but my Main is clean — will my Main survive?

If your Main has no RWT/botting involvement and isn't hard-linked (e.g., same Jagex Account or same payment moving gold between them), it is much more likely to be fine. If it does get actioned, appeal it as a clean account. The key question Jagex asks is whether this account broke the rules.

Does migrating to a Jagex Account increase my chain-ban risk?

Grouping several characters under one Jagex Account does make the ownership link explicit, so a serious ban on one becomes "your accounts." But migration is still the right move for security. If you play clean, the link is irrelevant. The risk only matters if one of the grouped characters actually breaks the rules.

Is using a VPN safe? Will it cause a chain ban?

A VPN is allowed, but shared VPN exit nodes are used by many players and can trigger login checks. VPNs don't cause chain bans by themselves. If a VPN login gets blocked, see our VPN Login Blocked Fix — and never use a VPN to dodge a ban.

Can I pay Jagex (or someone else) to unban my account?

No — and anyone offering to "unban" your account for money is running a scam. Jagex does not sell un-bans. The only legitimate route is the official appeal system. Protect yourself with the tips in our Anti-Scam Guide.
