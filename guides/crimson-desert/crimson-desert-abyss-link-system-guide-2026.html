What Abyss Link Actually Is

Per the official 2.00.00 patch notes (client version 1.000.484, released August 25, 2026), Crimson Desert added a new Abyss Link skill resource system. That is the full extent of what the patch notes state about it in plain terms: it exists, it is a resource tied to skills, and it arrived alongside the rework that made skill progression resettable per character.

Everything beyond that is inference, and we are going to be explicit about where the line sits. This is a brand-new system with almost no third-party coverage — most Crimson Desert guides currently online were written against the 1.x build and do not mention Abyss Link at all. Rather than fill the gap with confident invention, this guide separates the three tiers of knowledge:

  • Confirmed (official): stated in the 2.00.00 patch notes.
  • Reported (community): repeated by players on Steam and Reddit, not yet corroborated by Pearl Abyss.
  • Unverified: the mechanic is still being tested by the community; we say so instead of guessing.
The one-sentence version: Abyss Link is the resource layer that sits alongside Abyss Artifacts, and it is the reason the 2.0 patch could afford to wipe everyone's skill tree — the progression you rebuild into is now tracked differently than it was in 1.x.

The practical consequence you already experienced: on your first launch after updating, all existing skill progress was reset and every Abyss Artifact you had spent was refunded in full (official). That wipe was the migration step into the new system.

Why One Character's Skills Feed the Other Two

This is the single most-asked question about Abyss Link on the Steam boards — usually phrased as "how does abyss link work" — and it is the part where we have to be most careful.

What we can say with confidence is the shape of the design. Crimson Desert lets you play as Kliff, Damiane and Oongka within one save. Before 2.0, the three shared HP, stamina and spirit; per the official patch notes, as of 2.0 they no longer do. A system called "Link" that arrives in the same patch as the removal of shared pools is clearly doing the work that shared pools used to do: it provides a roster-level progression thread that is not the same thing as a per-character stat pool.

Where we stop: the exact transfer rule — how much resource one character's unlock grants the others, whether it is a flat amount or a percentage, and whether it applies to every node or only certain categories — is still being verified by the community. The official notes confirm the system exists but do not publish the formula. We will update this section when a reliable figure is established.

Players report observing that investment on one character produces visible resource changes on the others, which is consistent with a link mechanic. What players do not yet agree on is whether the transfer is symmetrical, whether it is capped, and whether it can be gamed by unlocking cheap nodes repeatedly. Treat all three as open questions.

How to Check Your Current Abyss Link Progress

Because the system is new, the in-game UI for it is not something players have learned to read yet. Here is the safest procedure, using only steps that follow from confirmed behaviour.

1

Open the skill menu on each character separately

Since pools are now per character, the menu you see for Kliff is not the menu you see for Damiane. Switch characters and read each one rather than assuming a shared total.

2

Record your Abyss Artifact total before and after any purchase

Note the number, buy one node, note it again. This is the only reliable way to observe what a single purchase costs and what it credits elsewhere.

3

Switch to another character and re-read the resource display

If a purchase on Kliff changes anything visible on Damiane or Oongka, that delta is your Abyss Link transfer. Log it — the community needs consistent observations.

4

Do not mass-spend until you have done the above once

Artifacts are finite and the refund was a one-time migration event. There is no second wipe coming to give them back.

If you were wiped and have not rebuilt yet: run this four-step observation once with a single cheap node before you commit your refund. Five minutes of measurement prevents a rebuild you cannot undo for free.

Per-Character Reset vs. Full Reset

Per the official 2.00.00 patch notes, skill progression is now reset per character. This is the most immediately useful part of the whole 2.0 rework, and it is worth understanding precisely.

ActionScopeWhen you want it
Per-character resetOne character's tree onlyA quest forces a character you have not invested in
Full resetAll three treesYou want to re-plan the entire roster from scratch
The 2.0 migration wipeEverything, once, automaticallyNot a choice — it already happened on first launch

The clearest use case reported by the community is Chapter 8. Players report that the Lucian Bastier encounter forces you to play Damiane, while "all Abyss Artifacts are usually invested in Kliff by Chapter 8." Under the old global-reset rule, fixing that meant gutting Kliff to fix Damiane. Under per-character reset, you rebuild Damiane alone and Kliff never notices.

What per-character reset does NOT mean

It does not mean the characters share a pool again — HP, stamina and spirit remain separate per character (official). It means the reset operation is scoped, not the resources. Do not read "reset per character" as a reversal of the stat split.

What the Community Has Not Confirmed Yet

We would rather tell you what we do not know than invent a number you will act on. The following are open as of August 31, 2026:

1. The transfer formula

Whether one character's unlock grants a flat resource amount, a percentage of cost, or a category-gated credit is unverified. No official figure has been published.

2. Caps and diminishing returns

Whether Abyss Link accumulation is capped per character or per save is unverified. If you are planning a long-horizon spend, assume a cap may exist and keep a reserve.

3. Interaction with the refund

The 2.0 wipe refunded spent Abyss Artifacts in full. Whether Abyss Link resources were also reset, preserved, or set to a baseline is not stated in the official notes.

4. Whether reset costs anything

The notes confirm per-character reset exists but do not state its price. Verify in-game before resetting repeatedly.

A note on sources: a document circulating online claiming to be a Crimson Desert "1.000.302" patch notes listing locations such as "Macduff" and "Pywel Gates" is fabricated — those do not exist in the game. Do not trust any Abyss Link figure traced back to it.

Practical Rules While the System Settles

Given how much is still unconfirmed, here is the conservative play. None of it depends on the open questions above.

  • Buy on your main first. Concentrating investment is correct whether or not the link transfers value, because it is correct on its own terms.
  • Keep a 30% artifact reserve. Kliff received 8 new skills in 2.0 and several are traversal tools you will want immediately.
  • Reset narrowly, not globally. Per-character reset means you never need to nuke a working tree to fix a forced-quest character.
  • Log your observations. Buy one node, check all three characters, write down the delta. That is how the formula gets solved, and it costs you one node.
  • Do not trust pre-2.0 build tables. They assume shared stat pools that no longer exist.

If your goal is a clean start rather than a repair, note that 2.0 also added a New Game Plus mode that carries over currency once you clear the full main story — see our Endgame Guide.

How Abyss Link Interacts With the Rest of 2.0

Abyss Link did not arrive alone. The same patch, per the official notes, delivered: 8 new Kliff skills, the per-character reset rework, the HP / stamina / spirit split, a New Game Plus mode with currency carryover, a reworked Golden Star fight now split into distinct phases, a new flying mount control option under Others > Input (Camera or Manual), and new German, French, Spanish, Japanese and Brazilian Portuguese voiceovers plus an Arabic interface option.

The through-line is that 2.0 restructures how progression is stored and scoped. Abyss Link is the new storage layer; per-character reset is the new scoping rule; the wipe was the migration. Once you see it that way, the patch stops looking like a punishment and starts looking like groundwork.

FAQ

What is Abyss Link in Crimson Desert?

A new skill resource system added in update 2.0, per the official 2.00.00 patch notes. It sits alongside Abyss Artifacts as part of how skill progression is tracked.

Is Abyss Link officially confirmed?

Yes — it is listed in the official 2.00.00 patch notes (version 1.000.484, August 25, 2026). The official notes confirm the system exists but do not publish its transfer formula.

Why did my skill tree get wiped?

Per the official patch notes, all existing skill progress resets on the first launch after updating, and spent Abyss Artifacts are refunded in full. It is the migration into the new system, not data loss.

Why does one character learning a skill give resources to the others?

Players report observing cross-character resource changes, which is consistent with a link system. The exact transfer rate is still being verified by the community and is not stated in the official notes.

Can I reset just one character?

Yes. Per the official 2.00.00 patch notes, skill progression is now reset per character.

Does per-character reset undo the stat split?

No. HP, stamina and spirit remain separate per Kliff, Damiane and Oongka. Per-character reset scopes the reset operation, not the pools.

How do I see my Abyss Link progress?

Read the skill menu per character, record your Abyss Artifact total before and after a purchase, then switch characters and re-read it. The delta is your transfer.

Is Abyss Link capped?

Unverified. The official notes do not state a cap. Keep a reserve rather than assuming unlimited accumulation.

Is resetting free?

The official notes confirm per-character reset exists but do not state a cost. Check in-game before resetting repeatedly.

Should I wait to spend until the system is fully understood?

No — but spend conservatively. Buy on your main, measure one node first, and hold roughly 30% of your artifacts in reserve.
