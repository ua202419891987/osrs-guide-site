Why the Community Calls Him Crimson Desert's Malenia

The comparison gets made constantly, and it is more precise than it sounds. The reference is not "a hard boss" — it is a specific design pattern: a fight where the two obvious strategies both fail. Play passively and you never deal damage while the boss's pressure compounds. Play aggressively and you feed the mechanic that heals or extends him.

Players describe the Forgotten General in exactly those terms. Dedicated videos covering only this one fight have accumulated view counts in the tens of thousands, which is a strong signal of how many people get stuck here rather than how strong the boss is numerically.

Sourcing note: Anything marked players report or community reports comes from Steam Community discussions, Reddit, and community video coverage — not from Pearl Abyss. Only items marked official come from the Pearl Abyss 2.00.00 patch notes (client version 1.000.484, August 25, 2026). We do not invent move names, damage figures, or exact health values.

Three things make the fight feel like this:

  • A summon mechanic. He brings phantom knights into the arena, which converts a clean duel into a crowd-control problem mid-fight.
  • A pressure curve rather than a fixed pattern. His aggression appears to respond to what you do, so memorising a single rotation is not enough.
  • Tight punish windows. The gap between his attacks rewards exactly one or two hits — not the full combos most players are used to landing by this point.
The reframe that unlocks the fight: stop thinking "aggressive or defensive." Think alternating. This boss is designed around a rhythm switch, and the players who beat him are the ones who change tempo on cue rather than committing to one speed.

Phantom Knights — What Changed in 2.0

This is the most time-sensitive part of the guide, and the reason a pre-2.0 walkthrough will mislead you.

The Forgotten General summons phantom knights during the fight. According to community reports, before update 2.0 those phantoms could hit the player when they should not have — the adds were functioning as a hazard aimed at you rather than as a mechanic to be managed. That made the encounter feel arbitrary, because damage was arriving from a source the fight did not appear to be asking you to respect.

Community reports say this was addressed in the 2.0 update, so the phantoms no longer produce that unfair friendly-fire-style damage. We attribute this to community reporting rather than quoting a patch line we cannot verify, because we only present official text we can actually source.

Practical consequence: if your muscle memory from a pre-2.0 attempt is "stay as far from everything as possible," you can now be more deliberate. Plan a route that puts the phantoms where you want them rather than fleeing the entire arena.

How to handle the phantoms now

  • Do not ignore them and do not tunnel them. They are a positioning problem, not a DPS check. Kill one when it is free; otherwise route around.
  • Keep the phantoms between you and nothing. Never let one sit behind you — being hit from off-camera is how this fight kills people.
  • Use the summon animation as a breath. Summoning is a commitment for him. If you can read it, it is a window to reposition or heal.
  • Verify the fix for yourself. If you are still taking unexplained damage from phantoms, note your client version and check our Known Bugs & Fixes Guide.

Everything 2.0 changed across the game is catalogued in our 2.0 Enhanced Update Guide.

The Aggression Switch: Why Both Styles Get You Killed

This is the heart of the fight. The Forgotten General is built so that a constant tempo loses. Here is what each failure looks like and what to do instead.

Failure mode A — the passive player

You keep your distance, wait for a big opening, and chip when it appears. What actually happens: you never create enough pressure to force a recovery animation, the phantom count stays high, and you slowly lose the stamina and food war. Crimson Desert has no potions, so a long low-damage attempt is a guaranteed loss — you will run out of food before you run out of health bar. Signs you are here: the fight lasts forever, your food is half gone, and his health barely moved.

Failure mode B — the aggressive player

You commit to full combos and try to overwhelm him. What actually happens: you are caught mid-animation by the fast pressure chain, you take damage during your own recovery frames, and — worst — you are attacking when you should be repositioning around the phantoms. Signs you are here: you die fast, and your deaths feel sudden rather than gradual.

The correct pattern — alternating tempo

Default to neutral: stay just outside his range, hold your stamina, watch. When a committed heavy lands and he enters recovery, switch up for exactly two hits. The instant he begins a new string, switch back down to neutral and retreat to space. The switch is the skill. Not attacking fast, not attacking slow — changing at the right moment.

1

Neutral — hold space, hold stamina

Stay outside his comfortable range. Keep all phantoms in your field of view. Bank at least one roll's worth of stamina at all times.

2

Switch up — two hits, no more

Only after a committed attack enters recovery. Two hits. Then stop, whether or not it feels like enough. The fight is won by repeating this, not by extending it.

3

Switch down — reset before he starts

Disengage the moment he begins a new string, not after you finish your own animation. Your recovery frames are his opening.

4

On the switch tell — full reset

When you read the stance-shift pause, stop entirely. Back off, re-centre, re-check the phantoms, then learn the new tempo before attacking again.

Abyss Core Setup for This Fight

Your Abyss Core configuration matters more here than on most bosses, because the fight is a stamina and survivability exam rather than a burst-DPS test. Players report that the biggest mistake is stacking pure offensive cores and then dying with a full damage build.

1Stamina pool / stamina recoveryTempo switching costs rolls and sprints constantly; dry stamina ends attempts
2Survivability / damage reductionNo potions exist — every point of mitigation saves food you cannot replace mid-fight
3Stance or guard breakCreates the long recovery windows that make two-hit punishes safe
4Single-target damageUseful but lowest priority — you already have enough if your weapon is upgraded

Rewards, Unlocks and What Comes Next

The Forgotten General is a fundamentals gate. Clearing him without relying on the phantom bug means your dodge input, stamina management, and tempo discipline are all working — and almost every fight after this one is built on those three things.

  • Restore your supply first. Food is your only healing and this fight is expensive. Rebuild the stack before the next boss rather than arriving short. Our Healing & Food System Guide covers the no-potion economy.

FAQ

Why is he called the Malenia of Crimson Desert?

Because both obvious strategies fail. Passive play never creates punish windows and you lose the food war; aggressive play gets you caught mid-animation. The fight requires alternating tempo instead.

Do the phantom knights still damage the player after 2.0?

Community reports say the pre-2.0 behaviour where phantoms could hit the player was fixed in the 2.0 update. We attribute this to community reporting, not to an official patch line we can quote.

Should I kill the phantoms or ignore them?

Neither extreme. Treat them as a positioning problem: kill one when it is free, otherwise route so they stay in front of you. Never let one sit behind you.

How many hits should I take per opening?

Two, maximum. The punish windows are deliberately tight and a third hit is usually what gets you killed during your own recovery frames.

What Abyss Core setup works best?

Stamina first, survivability second, stance break third, raw damage last. With no potions in the game, mitigation is worth more than burst damage here.

I keep getting hit from behind — what am I doing wrong?

You are losing track of the phantoms. Reposition to open ground on the summon tell and keep every add inside your camera view.

My parry keeps failing on certain attacks. Why?

Some attacks use a deliberate delay to catch parries thrown on the wind-up. Roll those instead of parrying; see Unparryable Attacks Guide.

Did update 2.0 make this boss easier?

The official 2.00.00 notes we can verify do not list a tuning change to this encounter, but they do confirm the global skill reset and Abyss Artifact refund, plus the new Abyss Link resource. Community reports credit 2.0 with fixing the phantom damage issue.

Is this the hardest boss in Crimson Desert?

It is the most discussed, not necessarily the hardest. Later walls tend to be supply and super-armour problems rather than tempo problems.

What should I practise before retrying?

Tempo switching. Deliberately practise attack-two-then-disengage against ordinary enemies until it is automatic. The boss fight is that habit under pressure.
