Crimson Desert 1.15 Patch Issues: HDR, DLSS & AMD Driver Crashes Explained

🔧 Technical & Fixes

Patch 1.15 was supposed to be the stability update. Instead it washed out HDR, silently disabled DLSS Frame Generation, and turned AMD's 26.6.2 / 26.6.3 drivers into a crash generator. Here is exactly which game version and driver combination still works — tested, not guessed.

"I spent two evenings reinstalling drivers before realising the game version was the variable, not the driver."

🎮 Patch Triage · 8 min read · Updated August 2026

⏱️ Quick Summary — 30-Second Read

  • 📌 1.15 is a regression patch: HDR output, DLSS Frame Generation and AMD driver stability all got worse than 1.14.
  • 📌 Safest combos: 1.15 + NVIDIA studio driver with Frame Gen off, or 1.14 + AMD 26.5.x.
  • 📌 AMD 26.6.2 / 26.6.3 crash on shader compile and weather transitions — clean-uninstall back to 26.5.1.
  • 📌 HDR fix: disable in-game HDR, use Windows Auto HDR, or cap peak brightness at 1000 nits.
  • 📌 Linux / Proton: every patch resets launch options; Steam Deck stays uncertified at 15–30fps.
  • 📌 Rollback works: disable auto-update, then use the depot downgrade steps in section 7.

1. What 1.15 Actually Changed

1.15 touched the renderer's tone-mapping pass, updated the upscaling plugin stack (DLSS/FSR/XeSS), and adjusted shader precompilation. Those three changes are the exact source of the three biggest PC complaints.

The detail most coverage misses: 1.15 does not break everyone equally. Whether you get the HDR washout, the Frame Generation dropout, or a hard crash depends on the combination of game build and GPU driver version. That is why the same "fix" works for one player and does nothing for another — and why we built a matrix instead of a checklist.

⚠️ Before You Reinstall Anything

Don't verify files, reinstall the game, or wipe your driver until you've checked the matrix below. Most 1.15 reports are driver-pairing problems, not corrupted installs.

3. HDR Broken After 1.15

After 1.15, HDR looks flat and grey, black levels lift, and the peak-brightness slider seems to do nothing. The game now applies an SDR curve before handing the frame to the HDR pipeline on some display configurations.

① Turn off in-game HDR, use Windows Auto HDR

Set the game to SDR output, then enable Auto HDR in Windows. This bypasses the broken tone-mapping pass and is the most reliable fix right now.

② If you keep native HDR, cap peak brightness

Set peak brightness to 1000 nits even on a brighter panel. Higher values push the broken curve into visible highlight clipping.

③ Re-run calibration after every launch

1.15 doesn't always reload your saved HDR calibration. Open Settings → Display → HDR Calibration, nudge a slider and confirm to force the profile to re-apply.

④ Disable your panel's own HDR tone mapping

Some TVs apply dynamic tone mapping on top. Turning it off removes the double-processing that makes the image look washed.

4. DLSS Frame Generation Not Working

Frame Generation is either greyed out, silently disabled, or enabled but delivering no gain. The upscaling plugin stack changed in 1.15 and the capability check now fails in several ordinary situations.

🖥️ Frame Generation Recovery Checklist

  1. Check the pairing. Frame Gen needs DLSS upscaling active, V-Sync off and exclusive fullscreen. 1.15 no longer warns you — it just greys the option out.
  2. Avoid alt-tab. Alt-tabbing with Frame Gen on can disable it for the whole session. Restart instead of toggling.
  3. Cap frames above your refresh rate. A cap at or below refresh discards generated frames, so Frame Gen appears to do nothing.
  4. Enable Reflex. Without it the latency penalty makes Frame Gen feel broken even when it works.
  5. Prefer the Studio branch. Game Ready 580.xx is where the greyed-out bug reproduces most often on 1.15.
💡 Honest Verdict

On 1.15, treat Frame Generation as optional. DLSS Quality without Frame Gen is stable across every NVIDIA row in the matrix.

5. AMD 26.6.2 / 26.6.3 Crashes

This is the hard failure. On AMD 26.6.2 and 26.6.3, 1.15 crashes during shader compilation on first launch and later hangs during weather transitions — sandstorms are the most reliable repro. 26.6.3 adds a driver timeout (TDR) on top.

① Clean-uninstall the current driver

Use AMD's cleanup utility or DDU in safe mode. Installing over 26.6.x leaves the broken shader cache behind and the crash persists.

② Install 26.5.1 and block auto-update

26.5.1 is the last branch that survives a full session on 1.15. Turn off automatic driver updates so 26.6.x isn't silently reinstalled.

③ Delete the shader cache

After the swap, clear the DirectX shader cache and the game's local shader folder. Let it recompile once, undisturbed.

④ Disable AMD overlays and tuning

Turn off Anti-Lag, Radeon Chill, Radeon Boost and the overlay. Each hooks the present path 1.15 changed and can turn a stutter into a crash.

⚠️ Don't Undervolt While Testing

An undervolted or overclocked card produces crashes identical to the 26.6.x bug. Reset to stock while you diagnose.

If it crashes when a quest cutscene loads rather than during weather, that's a different problem — see our quest softlock fix guide before blaming your GPU.

Keep a text file with your exact launch options and Proton version. Paste them back after every patch — that alone resolves most "it stopped launching on Linux" reports.

7. How to Roll Back Safely

If your matrix row says ❌ and you can't change drivers, rolling back to 1.14 is the pragmatic move. It's reversible and doesn't touch your save data.

🔁 Rollback Steps

  1. Back up your saves first — copy the save folder somewhere outside the game directory.
  2. Stop auto-update. Set the title to "Only update this game when I launch it", then launch offline.
  3. Downgrade the build via the console depot download for the 1.14 manifest, or restore your own pre-patch backup.
  4. Stay offline — an older build may refuse current services, so treat 1.14 as a single-player fallback.
  5. Re-enable updates once a 1.15.x hotfix turns your matrix row green.
⚠️ Save Compatibility Goes One Way

A save touched by 1.15 may not load on 1.14. Keep a separate copy of your pre-1.15 save and use that on the older build.

Rolling back also reverts 1.15's input-layer tweaks. Binding profiles don't survive a downgrade cleanly, so re-check custom movement keys against our WASD rebind bypass guide.

8. FAQs

Is 1.15 worth installing right now?

On NVIDIA yes — just leave Frame Generation off. On AMD 26.6.x no: roll the driver back to 26.5.1 first, or stay on 1.14.

Will a hotfix fix HDR?

Probably, but there's no confirmed date. Auto HDR is the safer default — treat native HDR as broken until the patch notes say otherwise.

Why does my friend on the same GPU have no crashes?

Almost always a different driver branch. Compare exact version numbers, not GPU models — that's the point of the matrix.

Can I play on Steam Deck if I lower everything?

Not meaningfully. Even at lowest preset with FSR performance mode, 15–30fps makes reactive combat unreliable. Remote play from a desktop is the workable route.

Do I need to delete my shader cache after every patch?

Not every patch, but yes after a renderer change like 1.15 or any driver swap. A stale cache is the most common cause of "I fixed the driver and it still crashes".
