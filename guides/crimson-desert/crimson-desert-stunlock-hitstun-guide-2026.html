Why "I Got Hit, Now I Roll Away" Fails Here

Every soulslike trains the same reflex: you eat a hit, the flinch ends, you roll out, you reset. In Crimson Desert that reflex gets you killed, and the community has been saying so loudly for months. Three separate large Steam discussion threads — running at roughly 289, 98, and 20 replies — have carried the same complaint from around three months ago right up to now, with players reporting that they simply cannot dodge out of a hit once it lands.

One widely echoed community report puts it plainly: "you cant really dodge like in soulsgames - you get stunlocked, trown around." The spelling is the player's; the sentiment is the thread's. Another cluster of reports describes the same thing from the boss side — being caught by one attack and then held through a full sequence with no window to act.

The important thing to understand is that this is not one single bug and it is not purely the game's fault. Community reports consistently point at three stacked causes, and only one of them is fully under your control. This guide separates them so you can stop dying to the part you can fix.

The one-line fix: stop mashing. Crimson Desert appears to buffer inputs made during hitstun and release them the instant control returns — which means the roll you "queued" while being thrown around fires straight into the next hit instead of away from it.

What Actually Causes Hitstun and Chain Control

Chain control in Crimson Desert is best understood as three separate states that players often describe with the same word. Separating them is what makes the problem solvable.

The three states players call "stunlock"

  • Repeated light hitstun. A multi-hit attack — a drill, a flurry, a spinning sweep — re-applies a small flinch faster than it decays. You are technically never "stunned"; you are permanently one frame into a flinch.
  • Launch / knockdown. A single heavy blow converts you into an airborne or prone state with its own recovery animation. During this you have no control at all, and it is the state players describe as being "trown around."
  • Pin against geometry. A wall, a rock, a prop, or a ledge. Your recovery animation plays but your displacement is blocked, so the follow-up hit lands for free.

The second and third states are where the deaths actually happen. The first is survivable if you stop inputting; the second and third kill you because the recovery animation has no cancel and your queued input is waiting behind it.

Why your input makes it worse

Players across the Steam threads report the same pattern: they mash evade while being hit, and the character does nothing — then performs a single dodge at the worst possible moment, directly into the follow-up. Whether or not the game is formally buffering inputs, the practical lesson reported over and over is that input made during hitstun is input wasted or input weaponized against you.

The habit to break: mashing evade is not "trying to escape." In this game it is pre-loading an animation you cannot cancel, timed by the game instead of by you.

Hold vs Tap — Evasive Roll vs Side Step

This is the single highest-value mechanic in this guide, and it is the one most players report discovering far too late. Your evade input in Crimson Desert produces two different moves depending on how you press it.

InputMoveWhat it's forWhy it fails as an escape
Tap (quick press, release)Side stepRepositioning inside a fight, baiting a whiff, staying close for a punishShort displacement — it does not clear sweeping or drilling attack arcs
Hold (press and keep holding)Evasive rollEscaping hitstun, leaving an attack arc, creating real distanceLonger recovery — never roll back into a boss that is still swinging

The Excavator / quarry drill threads are the clearest evidence of how much this matters. Players there report being stun-locked "into an endless amount of hits until he stops drilling" — and in the same breath report that their dodge only ever produced a side step, never a roll. They were tapping. Tapping gave them the shortest possible displacement, which kept them inside the drill's arc, which re-applied the flinch, which kept them tapping.

The tell: if you are dying inside an attack that "should" have missed, film yourself. Nine times out of ten the character is doing the small hop, not the full roll. That means you are tapping.

One officially listed wrinkle to check first

Before you blame your own hands, check your settings. Pearl Abyss's 1.18.02 Known Issues list includes an issue where, after setting Evasion Control to "Hold", the default dodge key from before rebinding still functions. If you rebound dodge and feel like the game is ignoring half your inputs, test the original key before you retrain your muscle memory.

The First Frame After Hitstun Ends

Escaping chain control is a five-step sequence, and steps one and two are the ones everybody skips. Run it in this order.

1

Stop inputting entirely

The moment you realize you are being hit, take your hands off. Let the full knockback or flinch play out. This is the hardest step and the most important one — you are sacrificing the illusion of control for an actual escape window.

2

Identify the first actionable frame

Watch for your character settling into a stance from which an idle or a move could begin. That settle is the first frame the game will accept input again. Do not guess it — learn to see it. It is earlier than you think and much later than your panic says.

3

Hold evade — do not tap

On that frame, press and hold. You want the evasive roll, not the side step. A side step here reproduces the exact loop that killed you.

4

Roll away from the attacker's facing, not sideways

Rolling laterally keeps you inside sweeping and drilling arcs. Roll along the attacker's facing axis — usually straight back, or through and behind them if the move has a committed recovery. Creating distance is the goal; punishing is not.

5

Only now, spend the window

Once you have distance, that is your window to eat, re-buff, or reposition. Crimson Desert has no potions — healing is food, and boss fights are attrition. Our Healing & Food System Guide covers how much to carry.

Do not attack on the recovery frame. The most common way players re-enter the chain is landing one greedy hit instead of rolling. Damage you deal while stunned is not damage you get to keep.

Landing Recovery After Being Launched

Being launched is its own problem with its own timing, and community reports on it are consistent enough to build a method around.

While airborne

Do not spam. Players report that inputs made during the airborne portion fire on landing and eat the follow-up, exactly like inputs made during grounded hitstun. Use directional influence to steer where you come down — away from the attacker, and critically, away from walls.

On the landing frame

Treat the landing exactly like the end of a grounded hitstun: one held evade input, timed to the moment your character touches down and can act. Rolling out of the get-up is the difference between a launcher being a scare and a launcher being a death.

If you landed against geometry

Priority changes. A wall defeats the roll, so your first job is to get off the wall — roll along it or perpendicular to it, then re-assess. If a boss is drilling you into a rock face, the mistake happened before the launch: you were standing with your back to geometry. Fight in the open whenever the arena allows it.

Arena rule: before any boss pull, look behind you. If there is a wall, a cliff, or a prop within a roll's distance, relocate the fight. Geometry is the silent partner in almost every stunlock clip.

Three Drills to Build the Habit

Drill 1 — The no-mash challenge

Pick a mid-tier enemy you can comfortably beat. Fight it under one rule: the instant you are hit, you stop touching the controls until your character fully settles. You will lose the habit of panic-inputting faster than any amount of reading.

Drill 2 — Roll-only escapes

Same enemy, added rule: every escape must be a held evade. If you see the small side step, you failed the rep. The goal is to make "hold" the default under pressure so that it survives contact with a real boss.

Drill 3 — Wall awareness

Before every pull in an open-world camp, name your escape lane out loud. If you cannot name one, reposition. This turns the geometry problem from something you notice while dying into something you notice while walking up.

FAQ

Why can't I dodge after being hit in Crimson Desert?

Community reports across three large Steam threads say the recovery window does not behave like a soulslike's, and that inputs made during hitstun appear to be buffered and released the moment control returns — firing your roll into the next hit. The practical fix is to stop inputting until your character settles, then hold evade once.

Is stunlock a bug that Pearl Abyss is going to fix?

We have not seen an official statement confirming a fix, so treat it as a system to play around rather than a bug waiting on a patch. Nothing in the official 2.00.00 patch notes (1.000.484) addresses it.

What is the difference between tapping and holding the dodge button?

Tapping produces a short side step; holding produces a full evasive roll with real displacement. Players in the Excavator drill threads report only ever getting the side step, which keeps them inside the attack arc. If you are trying to escape, hold.

Why does my dodge sometimes not respond at all?

Check your keybindings first. Pearl Abyss's 1.18.02 Known Issues list includes an issue where, after setting Evasion Control to "Hold", the pre-rebind default dodge key still works. Test the original key before assuming the input is being dropped.

How do I get out of the Excavator drill combo?

Create distance with a held evasive roll and do not re-enter until the drill visibly ends. Tapping keeps you in the arc. Our Excavator Boss Guide covers the full cycle.

Should I attack right after escaping?

No. Roll first, then decide. Attacking on the recovery frame is the most commonly reported way players re-enter the chain and lose the health they just spent escaping to protect.

What do I do after being launched into the air?

Steer your landing away from the attacker and away from walls, then hold evade on the landing frame to roll out of the get-up. Do not mash during the airborne portion — reports consistently say buffered inputs fire on landing and eat the follow-up.

Does armor or poise stop stunlock?

Players report gear matters, but no loadout removes the need for the escape timing. Armor stat visibility is its own known complaint — see our Armor Stats & Research Guide.

Why do I get chained against walls specifically?

A wall blocks your roll's displacement while the recovery animation still plays, so follow-ups land free. Always check what is behind you before a pull and relocate if there is geometry within a roll's distance.

Did the 2.0 update change any of this?

The official 2.00.00 notes confirm that skill progress was reset on first launch with Abyss Artifacts refunded, and that skill progression is now reset per character. That means defensive skills may need re-purchasing — see our 2.0 Enhanced Update Guide.
