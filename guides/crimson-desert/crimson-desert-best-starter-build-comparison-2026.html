Why Your Starter Build Decides Your First 20 Hours

Crimson Desert does not lock you into a class the way a traditional MMO does, but the character you pick at the start shapes your opening act more than any single gear drop. Kliff, Damiane, and Oongka begin with different baseline stats, a different signature weapon, and a different pacing curve. Choosing badly does not soft-lock the game — you can respec and regear later — but it costs you hours of backtracking, wasted materials, and a rougher early game than necessary.

This comparison is built for three reader types: the solo player who wants to survive the open world alone, the co-op player who wants to pull their weight in a duo or squad, and the min-maxer who is already thinking about level 50 endgame scaling. We rank all three starters against those goals with concrete numbers, not vibes.

Quick verdict: Pick Kliff if you play solo and hate dying. Pick Damiane if you mostly play co-op or want the safest climb. Pick Oongka only if you are confident in melee timing and want the highest skill ceiling.

One caveat before the rankings: Crimson Desert's "starter" is closer to a soulslike class than an MMO lock-in. You keep your character, but respecs are cheap and gear is swappable. So treat the comparison below as "what makes your first 20 hours smoother," not "who you are forever."

Kliff — Mercenary Bruiser Build

Kliff is the mercenary bruiser: high poise, a two-handed greatsword style, and a kit built around trading blows and winning. His starting gear favors heavy armor and slow, telegraphed swings that hit brutally hard when they connect.

Core stats at level 1

  • Vitality: 14 (highest of the three)
  • Strength: 16 (highest of the three)
  • Agility: 9 (lowest — you will not outrun anything)
  • Focus (magic): 8

Recommended early skill order

Prioritize the Guard Break tree first. It lets you stagger humanoid enemies and open them for a critical riposte, which is the single biggest damage multiplier in the early game. Second, take Poise Recovery so you can tank a hit and keep swinging. Save the mobility nodes for later — Kliff does not need them to clear the starting region.

Mistake to avoid: Do not dump points into Agility early. Kliff's whole identity is "hit harder than they can heal." A fast but weak Kliff is strictly worse than a slow, terrifying one.

Best early weapon

Keep the starter greatsword until you can craft the Reinforced Cleaver from the blacksmith at the first major town. It trades a little range for a massive poise bonus. Pair it with the heaviest armor you can still dodge-roll in (see our Mid-Game Gear Guide for the exact weight breakpoint).

Damiane — Guardian Support Build

Damiane is the guardian: a shield-and-spear kit with the best defensive toolkit and surprisingly strong co-op utility. She starts a little slower than Kliff on raw DPS but makes up for it with sustain and crowd control.

Core stats at level 1

  • Vitality: 13
  • Strength: 12
  • Agility: 12 (balanced)
  • Focus (magic): 13 (highest of the three — she can run light buffs)

Recommended early skill order

Take the Bulwark line first for a stance that cuts incoming damage by roughly 35% while held. Then grab Warding Light, a focus spell that drops a small AoE heal-over-time your co-op partner can stand in. In solo play this same spell keeps you alive through long boss fights without chugging potions.

Co-op note: Damiane is the best "I am playing with a friend" pick by a wide margin. Her Warding Light + Bulwark combo lets a squishy Oongka player delete bosses they could never solo.

Best early weapon

The starting spear is genuinely good — it has reach and a fast poke that interrupts enemies. Upgrade it along the Hooked Spear path. Do not switch to greatswords on Damiane; you lose her only mobility advantage (the spear thrust dash) for almost no gain.

Oongka — Berserker DPS Build

Oongka is the berserker: dual-wield, aggression, and the highest potential DPS of the three — but only if you can land parries and dodge-counters consistently. In the hands of a new player he is the riskiest starter; in the hands of someone who has finished the game once, he is the fastest clearer.

Core stats at level 1

  • Vitality: 11 (lowest — you will die in 2–3 hits)
  • Strength: 13
  • Agility: 15 (highest — insane dodge distance)
  • Focus (magic): 10

Recommended early skill order

Max Flurry first — the dual-wield combo that stacks a bleed debuff. Then take Counter Stance, which turns a perfect dodge into a massive riposte window. Skip defense entirely until late game; Oongka's survival is his dodge, not his HP.

Honest warning: If you are still learning Crimson Desert's parry timing (covered in our Parry & Dodge Guide), Oongka will feel miserable for the first ten hours. That is by design.

Best early weapon

Dual short blades from the starting kit, upgraded toward the Twin Fang path. The bleed stacks are what make Oongka scale — a single big weapon defeats the purpose.

Common New-Player Build Mistakes

1. Spreading stats evenly

A "balanced" Kliff is a weak Kliff. Commit to his identity (VIT/STR) or reroll. Crimson Desert rewards specialization far more than hybridization in the early game.

2. Skipping the signature weapon path

Each starter's starting weapon has a dedicated upgrade tree that scales with their kit. Switching to a "cooler" weapon you found often means losing synergy nodes.

3. Building Oongka like a tank

Oongka's low vitality is intentional. If you pour points into VIT you get a slow, weak character. Embrace the dodge or pick Kliff instead.

4. Ignoring co-op synergy

Damiane players who skip Warding Light waste 40% of her value. If you queue co-op, the support nodes are not optional.

For boss-specific prep before you commit to a build, see our full Boss Guide and the Staglord prep walkthrough.

First 10 hours — what to actually do

  • Kliff: Clear the starting valley, pour every point into VIT/STR, craft the Reinforced Cleaver, then run the Staglord prep walkthrough before the first boss.
  • Damiane: Rush the Bulwark node, keep Warding Light on cooldown in every fight, and upgrade your spear before your armor — your defense is the stance, not the plate.
  • Oongka: Spend the first five hours ONLY practicing parry timing on weak enemies. Do not fight the first boss until Counter Stance is maxed; the DPS gap before that is brutal.

Co-op Role Split (If You Play With a Friend)

The fastest way through Crimson Desert's hardest content is a Damiane (support) + Oongka (DPS) duo, or a Kliff (frontline) + Damiane (support) trio. Avoid two Oongkas — if both whiff a parry on the same pull, the run ends. A Kliff tanking while Damiane heals and Oongka deletes the boss is the gold-standard trio for Staglord and beyond.

Pick order: Whoever is newer should play Damiane. The support kit is forgiving and the team survives mistakes either way. For co-op setup details, see our Co-op Multiplayer Guide.

When Builds Converge Late Game

By level 40 the starter gap narrows. Kliff's poise edge matters less once everyone wears tier-3 armor; Oongka's bleed scales hardest into raid bosses with huge HP pools. If you started Kliff for safety and now want speed, respec toward a hybrid — the shrine cost is trivial next to an hour of wipes. The "wrong" starter only costs your first region's pace, never the whole game.

Final Verdict

If you read nothing else: Kliff for solo safety, Damiane for co-op peace of mind, Oongka for mastery and speed. None of them gates your progression — the starter only shapes your first region's pace. Pick the fantasy you like, follow the allocation table above, and you will reach the endgame with any of the three. The "wrong" pick is a myth; the only real failure is building against your character's identity.

FAQ

Can I switch starter character later?

No — the starting character is locked to your save. You can respec stats and regear weapons, but you cannot become a different character. Make a new save if you want to try another.

Is Kliff strictly better for solo?

For survivability, yes. For clear speed, Oongka overtakes him once you master parries. New solo players should still default to Kliff.

Does Damiane fall off in the endgame?

Her personal DPS is lower, but her support value (Warding Light, Bulwark) stays relevant in every co-op boss. She does not "fall off" if you play with others.

What is the fastest way to learn Oongka?

Practice parry timing in the early game against weak enemies, then read our Parry & Dodge Guide. Expect a rough first 10 hours.

Should I worry about the meta build?

Only if you plan to speedrun or push the hardest endgame content. For a first playthrough, pick the character whose fantasy you enjoy — you will finish either way.

Which starter is best for co-op with a random?

Damiane. You have no guarantee a random partner knows their kit, so your healing and shielding cover their mistakes.

Do weapons scale with the starter's stats?

Weapons scale with their own upgrade tree and the relevant stat (STR for blades, etc.). The starter weapon's tree is tuned to that character's strengths.

Is there a "wrong" pick that ruins the game?

No. The only trap is building against the character's identity (e.g., tank Oongka). Otherwise every starter is viable to completion.

When should I start thinking about endgame gear?

Around the mid-game gear breakpoint. Our Mid-Game Gear Guide covers the exact moment to transition.

Can I play all three eventually?

Yes — just make separate saves. Many players keep a Kliff for solo and an Oongka for co-op speedruns.

Does difficulty scale with starter?

No — raid and enemy difficulty scale with your gear and progression, not your character. A Kliff and an Oongka face the same Staglord with the same HP.

Which starter is most fun, not most optimal?

Subjective, but Oongka has the highest skill-expression ceiling and the most satisfying perfect-parry deletes. If you value mastery over safety, he is the fun pick.
