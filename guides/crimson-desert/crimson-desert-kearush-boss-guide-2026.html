What Makes Kearush the Slayer a Wall

Kearush the Slayer is the Chapter 5 boss that the community names more often than almost any other when asked which fight stopped them. Multiple guide sites have written standalone articles about him, and the community's own summary of the encounter fits on a bumper sticker: "BRING 100+ GRILLED MEAT."

That quote is not a joke, and understanding why it is not a joke is the key to the fight. Three structural things make Kearush different from the bosses before him:

  • Three health bars. The encounter is divided into distinct segments, so a single clean run is not enough — you have to sustain a standard across all three.
  • A super-armour phase. During part of the fight he becomes effectively immune to interruption, which means your normal "stagger him and punish" habit actively wastes your stamina.
  • An arena that can eject you. The church interior has boundaries, and players report being thrown out of the arena entirely, which resets the attempt.
Sourcing note: Anything marked players report or community reports comes from Steam Community discussions, Reddit, and third-party guide sites — not from Pearl Abyss. Only items marked official come from the Pearl Abyss 2.00.00 patch notes (client version 1.000.484, August 25, 2026). We do not invent move names, damage figures, or health values.

The single most important reframe: you do not beat Kearush by out-damaging him, you beat him by out-lasting him. Every decision below follows from that.

This Is a Supply Fight, Not a Damage Race

Crimson Desert has no potions. There is no flask, no estus, no quick-heal consumable that refills at a checkpoint. Food is the only healing source in the game. That single design decision converts every long boss fight into a logistics problem, and Kearush is the first boss long enough for the logistics to actually bite.

This is why players who arrive with a strong weapon and fifteen pieces of meat lose to players who arrive with a mediocre weapon and a full larder. The strong-weapon player dies at the third health bar with an empty inventory. The prepared player wins slowly.

The mental model: treat Kearush as three separate fights back to back, each with its own food allowance. If you spend bar two's allowance surviving bar one, you have already lost bar three — even if bar one went well.

What to cook, and why cooked matters

Raw ingredients heal poorly or not at all. The community's consensus is specifically about grilled meat — cooked food heals substantially better than raw, which is why the "100+ grilled meat" figure gets repeated so often. Cook everything before you walk in; do not plan to cook mid-fight. How Much Food to Actually Bring

Rather than quoting a number you cannot verify, here is the method the number comes from. Work it out once for your own build and you will never under-pack again.

The supply formula

Step 1 — Count how many hits you take per health bar in a practice run where you are not trying to win. Write it down.
Step 2 — Estimate how many food items one heal animation's worth of recovery covers per hit taken. On a long fight, assume two items per significant hit.
Step 3 — Multiply by three (three health bars), then multiply by three again (you will need roughly three attempts to learn the phase transitions).
Step 4 — Add 50% on top for the final winning attempt, because the winning run is always the longest one.

Run those numbers honestly and you land in the same territory the community jokes about — a stack measured in dozens, not units. The "100+ grilled meat" figure that players repeat is not exaggeration for a first clear; it is the arithmetic of a three-bar fight with no potions.

Do not farm this by grinding one enemy. Build a repeatable cooking loop instead. Our Resource Gathering Routes guide covers routes that pair hunting with ingredient pickup, and Camp Farm Guide covers producing ingredients rather than chasing them.

Three Health Bars — Phase by Phase

We will not give you fake health thresholds. Instead, here is how to identify each transition in the moment and what changes when it happens.

1

Bar one — learn, do not burn

Treat the first bar as reconnaissance. Your goal is to identify his two or three basic strings and leave the bar with your food nearly untouched. If you have spent a third of your supply by the end of bar one, reset the attempt. Resetting early costs you two minutes; continuing costs you the whole stack.

2

Bar two — the super-armour bar

This is where most runs die. He gains super armour and stops being interruptible. Your instinct will be to hit him harder to break through; that instinct is wrong and it is the reason people run out of both stamina and food here. See the next section for the correct response.

3

Bar three — the attrition bar

By now you are low on stamina food and probably damaged. Shorten your aggression to single hits. The temptation in the final bar is to rush the finish; the third bar punishes rushing more than the first two combined. One hit per opening, every time.

How to recognise a transition: watch for the stance-shift pause at mid-range and for the moment he stops flinching when you hit him. Either of those is your cue to back off rather than press. Transitions are free healing windows if you respect them.

Super Armour: What to Do Instead of Hitting

Super armour means your hits do not interrupt him. Continuing to swing into it is the most expensive mistake in the fight: it costs stamina, it costs animation commitment you cannot cancel, and it produces no stagger to show for it.

Your three correct options during super armour

Option A — Break his stance instead of his health

Super armour resists interruption, not posture. Attacks that target stance or guard are the intended answer. If your build has a stance-breaking or guard-breaking option, that is the tool for this window. Check what your weapon offers in our Weapons & Gear Guide.

Option B — Wait for the window and punish the recovery

Super armour is a phase, not a permanent state. It ends. Disengage, keep your stamina full, and punish the recovery animation when it closes. This is slower and it is also how the fight is designed to be won.

Option C — Spend the phase on logistics

Use the immune window to heal, re-buff, and reposition toward the arena centre. You are going to be unable to do damage anyway; make the time productive instead of draining your stamina bar for nothing.

The hard rule: never enter a super-armour phase with less than half stamina. If you do, back off and recover before engaging — the phase will still be there when you are ready, and it costs nothing to let it run while you catch your breath.

Church Space, Ring-Out and Positioning

The church arena is the third difficulty multiplier. Players report being thrown out of it and having to restart entirely, which turns a mechanical problem into a morale problem.

  • Fight toward the centre, not away from the boss. Players drift backward under pressure and end up with their back to a wall or an edge. Make a conscious rule: every dodge ends with a step back toward the middle.
  • Treat knockback attacks as repositioning events. The wide sweep is not just damage, it is displacement. Roll into it rather than away so the knockback pushes you inward.
  • Never block the grab. Guards do not stop grabs and grabs are what eject you. Roll backward twice instead.
  • Use the pillars deliberately. Interior geometry can break line of sight and buy a heal animation — but do not let it trap you against a wall.
  • Watch the camera at the boundary. Lock-on can drift toward the arena edge; release and re-acquire when you are near a wall.

If you are still being chain-hit rather than out-positioned, the problem is your dodge input rather than your spacing — see our Stun-Lock & Hitstun Guide. FAQ

Why does everyone say to bring 100+ grilled meat?

Because Crimson Desert has no potions — food is the only healing source — and Kearush has three health bars. The number is the arithmetic of a long fight with no refillable healing, not an exaggeration.

Is raw meat enough?

No. Cooked food heals substantially better than raw, which is why the community specifies grilled meat specifically. Cook everything before the attempt; you cannot cook mid-fight.

How do I deal with the super-armour phase?

Stop attacking. Super armour means your hits will not interrupt him, so swinging only drains stamina. Either use a stance/guard-breaking option, wait out the phase and punish the recovery, or spend the window healing and repositioning.

What happens if I get thrown out of the church?

Players report that being ejected from the arena ends the attempt and forces a restart. Prevent it by never blocking the grab and by always ending your dodges with a step toward the arena centre.

Should I use a shield?

A shield helps with the fast pressure strings, but it will not save you from grabs and it drains stamina you need for rolling. Use it to absorb the first hit of a chain, then roll the rest.

How many punish hits should I take per opening?

One in the third bar, two at most in the first. Kearush's follow-ups are fast enough that a full combo will get you caught mid-animation.

My damage feels too low — is my build wrong?

Possibly, and check the 2.0 reset first. The official 2.00.00 patch notes confirm all skill progress was reset with Abyss Artifacts fully refunded, so if you have not rebuilt since August 25 your build is likely incomplete.

Can I leave and come back with more food?

Yes, and you should. Resetting early to restock is far cheaper than pushing a doomed attempt and losing a full stack. Run a gathering route rather than grinding a single enemy.

Is Kearush harder after update 2.0?

The official 2.00.00 patch notes did not list a tuning change to this encounter. What changed is your build — the skill reset and the Abyss Link resource mean pre-2.0 guides may no longer match your character.

What should I do right after beating him?

Rebuild your spent skill points, restock before the next chapter, and check our Boss Guide for what comes next. Chapter 5 gear breakpoints are covered in the Mid-Game Gear Guide.
