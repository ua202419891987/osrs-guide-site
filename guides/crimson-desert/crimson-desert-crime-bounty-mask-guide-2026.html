The Crime System Nobody Explains

Crimson Desert runs a full crime and bounty simulation underneath its open world, and it tells you almost nothing about it. There is no crime tutorial, no clear on-screen warning before an action flags you, and no obvious indicator of how much heat you are carrying. You find out the way most players do: you take something, and suddenly every guard in the province is chasing you across the map.

The system is not broken — it is strict. Community reports and multiple beginner-focused write-ups converge on the same description: once you commit a crime, guards pursue persistently, over a wide area, and for a long time. Players have described being hunted across an entire province after a single theft.

Sourcing note: The crime, bounty, and mask system is not documented in official patch notes. What follows reflects community reports and player testing, consolidated from the sources we could verify. We have deliberately avoided publishing specific bounty values, decay timings, or penalty amounts because we could not verify them. Wherever that is the case, this guide gives you a way to test it yourself instead.

The good news is that the system has one dominant counterplay, and it is simple enough to turn into a habit: equip a mask before you commit the crime. Everything else in this guide is either setup for that habit or damage control for when you forget it.

What Actually Counts as a Crime

Before you can avoid committing crimes, you need to know what the game considers one. Players report three main categories.

Theft

Taking items that do not belong to you. This includes containers, goods on stalls, and objects inside buildings you have not been given access to. Theft is the most common trigger by far, largely because it is the easiest to commit by accident — see the lock-on section below.

Attacking people who are not hostile

Striking a non-hostile NPC, and in many reports, striking a guard at all, registers as a crime. The ambiguity here is dangerous: in a crowded settlement, a wide swing that clips a bystander is enough. This is why area-of-effect and sweeping weapon attacks are a genuine liability inside towns.

Forcing things open

Community reports describe forced entry — prying open a locked container or a locked door — as a criminal act in its own right, separate from whatever you take afterwards. In other words, the act of forcing is the crime; the loot is a second offence stacked on top.

Verify your own boundary: If you want to know exactly which interactions flag you, run a controlled test. Save first. In a settlement, interact with one container of each type — an obviously owned chest, a stall good, a container you have permission for — and check your status after each one. Restore your save and repeat with a mask equipped. That single experiment teaches you more than any guide can.

How Bounty Accumulates and Decays

The mental model most players bring from other open-world games — "I committed a crime in this town, so this town's guards are angry" — is the wrong one for Crimson Desert. Community reports consistently describe the response as province-wide and persistent, not local and temporary.

What players report about accumulation

  • It stacks. Repeat offences appear to build on each other rather than reset. A second theft is treated as worse than a first one.
  • Scope is regional, not local. The phrase used repeatedly in community discussion is that the whole province turns on you, not just the settlement where the crime happened.
  • Guards are persistent. Reports describe pursuit that does not simply time out while you stand nearby. Waiting it out in the same area is generally not a viable strategy.

What we can and cannot tell you about decay

Bounty does appear to reduce over time under some conditions, but we have not verified a specific rate, a specific cooldown, or a specific "cleared" threshold, and we are not going to publish a number we cannot stand behind. Here is how to measure it yourself:

1

Establish a baseline

Commit one minor crime in a settlement you can leave quickly. Note the guard behaviour immediately: how many engage, how far they follow, whether they disengage at a boundary.

2

Leave the area completely

Travel to a different region and stay away. Community reports suggest distance matters, so test whether being out of the province changes anything when you return.

3

Return at intervals

Come back after a short absence, then a longer one, and compare guard reaction each time. Record roughly how much time was needed before you were ignored. That is your own decay figure — measured on your save, on your version.

4

Repeat with a mask

Run the identical test with a mask equipped before the crime. The difference between the two results is the entire argument for wearing one.

The Mask — Put It On Before You Steal

This is the solution the community keeps repeating, and it is the one the game never surfaces: wear a mask before you commit the crime. The logic is straightforward and matches how disguise works in most open-world crime systems — if the guards cannot identify you, the crime does not attach to you.

Timing is everything

The mask has to be on before the act, not after. Equipping it while guards are already chasing you does not undo the identification that already happened. Make it the first step of your routine, not the emergency response.

Where to get one

Rather than send you to a specific vendor we cannot confirm, here is the reliable approach: check general goods and armour vendors in larger settlements, check quest rewards that involve disguise or infiltration, and check enemies and containers associated with bandit or outlaw content. Compare mask items in your inventory and look for any description or stat that references concealment or identity.

Make it a loadout slot

Keep a mask in your inventory permanently and equip it before entering any settlement where you intend to take things. Treat "mask on" the same way you treat "weapons out" when approaching a fight — a deliberate pre-action check.

Verify the mask works on your save: Save, commit a crime without a mask, note the guard response, reload, put the mask on first, and commit the identical crime. The difference in response tells you exactly how much protection the mask is giving you. Do this once and you will never skip the step again.

Lock On Before You Interact

Here is the detail that separates players who never get a bounty from players who constantly wonder why they have one: lock on to your target before you press the interact button.

In a busy settlement, dozens of objects sit within interact range — stall goods, crates, doors, containers, and NPCs, all overlapping. Without a lock-on, the game picks for you, and what it picks is frequently not what you meant. You reach for a door and take a merchant's goods instead. You thought you were opening a chest you owned and you forced one you did not.

Community reports identify this as one of the most common sources of accidental crime — players committing offences they never intended, and then being baffled by the province-wide response.

The habit to build

  • Lock on first, then interact. Every time, in any crowded interior. The extra half-second is cheap.
  • Confirm the prompt text before you commit. Read what the interaction actually says rather than assuming. If the prompt indicates taking rather than opening, that is your warning.
  • Sheathe before you walk through a market. An accidental swing in a crowd is a crime in its own right. Putting the weapon away removes the possibility entirely.
  • Never use wide sweeping attacks indoors. If a fight does break out in a settlement, switch to single-target attacks so you do not clip bystanders.
The compounding mistake: Accidental theft is bad enough. Accidental theft without a mask is the full punishment. Since accidental crimes are the ones you cannot plan for, the mask needs to be on during ordinary exploration, not only during planned heists.

Escaping a Bounty Once You Have One

So you forgot the mask and the province is hunting you. The instinct — fight your way out — is usually the worst available option, because every guard you strike stacks more offences onto the existing bounty.

Escape priorities, in order

  • Stop attacking. The fastest way to turn a survivable bounty into a permanent one is to keep fighting. Break contact; do not win the fight.
  • Leave the province, not the street. Because the response appears to be regional, putting real distance between you and the region is more reliable than hiding in an alley.
  • Use vertical space and your mount. Guards on foot lose you quickly if you take to rooftops or ride out. Mounts are also the safest way to survive the drops you will take while fleeing — see our Fall Damage Guide.
  • Do not fast travel into the same region. Arriving in a settlement mid-bounty just restarts the pursuit with a fresh group of guards.
  • Equip the mask anyway. It will not clear what already happened, but it prevents the next interaction from adding more.

If none of that works, the honest answer is to reload an earlier save. Crimson Desert saves generously enough that a reload is almost always cheaper than a twenty-minute chase across a province.

Why New Players Almost Always Get Burned

This system is close to a guaranteed bad experience for new players, and it is worth being precise about why — because every one of these reasons is a design gap rather than a player failure.

1. There is no tutorial

The game never introduces crime, bounty, or disguise. A new player's first theft is also their first lesson, and the lesson is delivered by the entire province attacking them.

2. The punishment is disproportionate to the feedback

A misclick produces the same response as a planned heist. There is no graduated warning — a small accidental theft can produce the full response.

3. The counterplay is invisible

The mask is the answer, and nothing in the game tells you it exists as a mechanic. You have to already know to look for it.

4. The trigger is an everyday input

Interacting with the world is something you do constantly. When a routine input can silently flag you, the failure rate over a long session approaches certainty.

The practical fix is a short pre-settlement checklist. Before you enter any town: mask on, weapon sheathed, lock-on ready. Three habits, and the crime system stops being a threat at all.

FAQ

What counts as a crime in Crimson Desert?

Community reports identify three main categories: theft of items you do not own, attacking non-hostile NPCs or guards, and forcing open locked containers or doors. Forcing appears to be an offence in its own right, separate from whatever you take.

Do I need to wear a mask before or after committing a crime?

Before. Equipping a mask once guards are already pursuing you does not undo the identification that already happened. Make it part of your pre-action routine.

Why are guards chasing me across the whole province?

Community reports consistently describe the response as province-wide and persistent rather than local and temporary. This is the single most complained-about aspect of the system.

How do I avoid stealing things by accident?

Lock on to your target before pressing interact. In crowded settlements the game will otherwise pick for you, and what it picks is often not what you meant.

Where do I get a mask?

Check general goods and armour vendors in larger settlements, disguise- or infiltration-related quest rewards, and bandit or outlaw associated enemies and containers. Compare mask descriptions for any concealment or identity wording.

How long does a bounty last?

We have not verified a specific decay rate and will not publish one. Test it yourself: commit one minor crime, leave the province, and return at intervals to compare guard response.

Should I fight the guards or run?

Run. Every guard you strike adds more offences to the existing bounty. Break contact and put distance between you and the region rather than winning the fight.

Does putting on a mask clear my existing bounty?

No. It prevents further identification but does not undo what already happened. Equip it anyway so the chase does not get worse.

Can I fast travel away from a bounty?

Avoid fast travelling into the same region — you arrive into a fresh group of guards and restart the pursuit. Put real distance between you and the province instead.

Is the crime system bugged?

Community consensus is that it is strict rather than broken. It punishes accidental crimes as hard as deliberate ones, which is why the mask and lock-on habits matter so much.
