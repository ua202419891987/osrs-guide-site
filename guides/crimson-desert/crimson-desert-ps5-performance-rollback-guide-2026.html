"My PS5 ran better before 1.13." If you have said that out loud lately, you are not imagining things. A flood of players report the same: the update fixed some bugs, but the game feels laggy, stutters in towns, and drops frames in fights that used to be rock solid. The top searches right now are "frame drops after patch", "update made it laggy", and "how to revert" — all the right questions.

🔧 1. What 1.13 Actually Changed on PS5 — The Perf Regression Explained

Before we talk fixes, it helps to understand why your PS5 suddenly feels worse. Patch 1.13 was primarily a content and balance update, but every patch that touches a game this complex also changes the console build. On PS5, three things in 1.13 appear to have hurt frame rate for a meaningful slice of players.

First, 1.13 increased ambient NPC and wildlife density in major hubs. Uzkigrad's marketplace and crossroads camps now spawn more actors with richer animation — great for atmosphere, terrible for the GPU when the camera sweeps a crowd. Second, the patch revised the resolution scaler to ramp up more aggressively than 1.12, pushing frame time up. Third, players report 1.13 changed shader compilation, causing hitches on first region entry — a "shader stutter" largely absent before.

Pro Tip: The regression is not uniform. Base PS5 (disc or digital) reports the worst drops; PS5 Pro owners with extra GPU headroom feel it far less. If you are on a base PS5, the Section 3 settings are your best friend.

None of this means 1.13 is "broken" — it fixed real issues like better co-op netcode and two fewer crash bugs. But the performance cost was not free; Pearl Abyss traded headroom for visual and content gains. The good news: much of that headroom is reclaimable through settings, because the console can still hit your 1.12 numbers. You are not stuck with a worse game.

⚠️ 2. Can You Roll Back a Console Patch? (Honest Answer)

Here is the question everyone types into search: "how to revert." Let us be direct. On PlayStation 5 you cannot uninstall or roll back a game update. PlayStation makes patches mandatory and permanent for the installed copy. The moment 1.13 finished downloading, your 1.12 files were overwritten. There is no "uninstall 1.13" button, and Sony offers no consumer version archives.

So if "rollback" means "get 1.12 back on this console," the honest answer is no — not without jailbreaking, which we will not walk through and advise against. But "rollback" in the practical sense — getting your performance back — is absolutely achievable. Think of it as rolling back the settings and state the patch worsened, not the patch itself.

① The Workarounds That Actually Work

🛠️ Reclaim FPS Through Settings (Recommended)

This is the primary path and recovers most lost frame rate. Switching to Performance Mode, disabling motion blur, and lowering crowd density (Section 3) typically restores your 1.12 smoothness. It does not literally uninstall 1.13, but it delivers the result you want: a stable, playable game.

🧹 Clear Cache / Rebuild Database (Safe, Section 5)

1.13's shader changes can leave stale cache that causes stutter on some consoles. A safe database rebuild + cache clear removes those and often kills the "first time in a region" hitches. Your saves are untouched — the closest thing to a clean rollback of state you can do.

🕹️ Wait for 1.14

A second PSN account still auto-updates, so that is not a real escape. The only true engine-level fix is a developer patch. Reporting the regression (Section 8) is what makes 1.14 prioritize perf. Until then, settings are your lever.

Warning: Ignore any "downgrade tool" or "1.12 PKG file" on random forums. On modern PS5 firmware these are scams at best, account-bans or malware at worst. There is no legitimate consumer rollback. Stick to the safe steps in this guide.

🎚️ 3. Settings That Restore FPS — Mode, Motion Blur, Crowds

This is the meat of the recovery. The single biggest lever is the graphics mode. Crimson Desert on PS5 ships with two: Resolution Mode (prioritizes 4K-ish checkerboard, targets 30–40fps with dips) and Performance Mode (drops internal resolution, targets a steadier 60fps). If 1.13 made your game laggy, you were likely in Resolution Mode or the scaler nudged up. Switch to Performance Mode first — it alone recovers a huge chunk of the regression.

Beyond the mode, three toggles matter most. Motion blur is GPU-expensive and costs more during fast camera moves after 1.13 — turn it off. Crowd/ambient density is the direct culprit behind hub stutter; dropping it from High to Medium or Low removes most marketplace drops. Volumetric fog and SSR are secondary but worth lowering if you are short of 60. None meaningfully hurt how the game looks in normal play.

1

Open Settings → Graphics

From the pause menu, go to Settings, then the Graphics tab. Confirm which mode you are in — the patch may have reset options to defaults on first launch.

2

Set Mode to "Performance"

Select Performance Mode. On a base PS5 this targets 60fps with dynamic resolution — the single highest-impact change for the 1.13 regression.

3

Disable Motion Blur, Lower Crowd Density

Turn Motion Blur Off. Set Crowd/Ambient Density to Medium (or Low in Uzkigrad). Optionally drop Volumetric Fog to Medium. Apply and reload the area if prompted.

📺 4. Reconstruction & VRR Tricks (If Your TV Supports Them)

If you have a modern TV or monitor, two free multipliers survive 1.13: VRR (Variable Refresh Rate) and a clean reconstruction pipeline. VRR is the underrated hero. Even if Crimson Desert is not hitting a locked 60, a VRR display smooths the dips so they are barely perceptible. On a base PS5 the game outputs 60Hz; with VRR on in both PS5 settings and your TV's Game Mode, the panel refreshes in sync with the console's actual FPS, killing the "juddery laggy" feeling 1.13 introduced.

To use it: on your PS5, go to Settings → Screen and Video → Video Output → VRR and set it to Automatic (or On). Make sure your TV is in its dedicated Game Mode and that the HDMI port supports HDMI 2.1 (most 2021+ TVs do). If your display does not support VRR, you can still help by forcing the PS5 to 120Hz output even though the game runs lower — some panels handle 120Hz input better and reduce processing lag. Experiment; results vary by TV brand.

② Reconstruction Settings

Crimson Desert uses a checkerboard/temporal reconstruction to hit high resolutions. After 1.13 the scaler ramps resolution higher, which hurts FPS. You cannot disable reconstruction, but pairing Performance Mode with VRR lets the console spend less time fighting for pixels. If your TV supports it, enable "adaptive sync" or "game optimizer" mode — it complements VRR and cuts input lag, making the patch feel less sluggish at the same FPS.

Important: VRR only helps if BOTH the PS5 and the display agree. If your TV reports "VRR unsupported," the PS5 setting does nothing. Check your TV's spec sheet for HDMI 2.1 / VRR before blaming the patch.

🧹 5. Clearing Cache / Rebuild Database on PS5 (Safe Steps)

One of the most effective "soft rollbacks" for the 1.13 stutter is a PS5 database rebuild and cache clear — a built-in, Sony-supported routine. It does not delete your games, saves, or profiles. It reorganizes the filesystem and clears temporary cache that 1.13's shader recompile left in a messy state — exactly why some players see hitches "the first time in each region."

1

Power Down Fully

From the control center, select Power → Turn Off PS5. Wait until the light is fully off. Do not use Rest Mode.

2

Boot Into Safe Mode

Hold the console power button (not the controller) until you hear a second beep — about 7 seconds. Connect a controller via USB and press PS.

3

Choose "Clear Cache and Rebuild Database"

In Safe Mode, pick option 5, "Clear Cache and Rebuild Database." It runs a few minutes. When done, restart and launch the game.

Pro Tip: After the rebuild, the first launch recompiles shaders — expect a longer load the very first time. That is normal and one-time; afterward, the region-entry hitches 1.13 introduced should be greatly reduced.

For most players this step, combined with the Performance Mode switch from Section 3, returns the game to its pre-1.13 feel. It is safe, reversible, and free — there is no reason not to try it before waiting on a patch.

📣 8. Reporting the Regression So It Gets Patched

The final, arguably most important, step: make sure Pearl Abyss knows 1.13 hurt PS5 performance. Patches are prioritized by signal volume. If only a few complain, it gets buried; if hundreds report the same regression with data, 1.14 puts a perf pass at the top. You have three channels, and using all three takes five minutes.

1

Use the In-Game Report Tool

Pause → System → Send Feedback. Pick "Performance," note your platform, and describe it: "FPS dropped after 1.13, especially in hubs; fixed via Performance Mode + cache rebuild but engine fix needed."

2

File a Support Ticket

Through the official support site, attach your PS5 model. Tickets are tracked internally and weigh more than forum posts for patch prioritization.

3

Upvote Community Reports

On the official Discord and boards, find existing "1.13 PS5 performance" threads and upvote. Consolidated threads beat scattered posts. See our Known Bugs & Fixes guide to check if your symptom is tracked.

Pro Tip: When you report, include base PS5 vs PS5 Pro, your TV model, and whether VRR is on. That hardware context lets the devs reproduce the regression instead of dismissing it as "user setup."

❓ FAQ — PS5 Performance & Rollback Questions

Can I uninstall or roll back the 1.13 update on PS5?

No. PS5 patches are mandatory and overwrite the previous version on install, so there is no consumer revert to 1.12. The practical "rollback" is restoring performance via settings (Performance Mode, lower crowd density) and a safe cache/database rebuild — both in this guide. Avoid third-party "downgrade" tools; they are scams or bans.

Should I use Performance Mode or Resolution Mode after 1.13?

For a base PS5, Performance Mode is the clear winner — it targets 60fps and recovers most of the regression. Resolution Mode looks sharper but drops into the 30s in hubs and feels like the "laggy after update" complaint. PS5 Pro owners can often run Performance Mode and still get near-4K.

Why does Crimson Desert feel worse after the update?

1.13 raised ambient NPC/wildlife density, made the resolution scaler more aggressive, and changed shader compilation — all of which raise console load. The result is frame drops in crowds, shader hitches on first region entry, and a sluggish feel. The settings and cache steps here reverse most of it.

Does VRR actually help with the 1.13 frame drops?

Yes, if your display supports it. VRR syncs the panel's refresh to the console's actual output, smoothing dips so they are barely visible. It will not raise average FPS, but it removes the judder that makes the patch feel bad. Enable it in PS5 Settings → Screen and Video → Video Output → VRR, plus your TV's Game Mode.

Is rebuilding the PS5 database safe? Will I lose saves?

Yes, it is safe and Sony-supported. A rebuild and cache clear does not delete games, profiles, or saves (backed up to PS Plus cloud and local storage). It only reorganizes the filesystem and clears temporary cache — exactly what fixes the 1.13 shader-entry hitches. Always power off fully first, then boot into Safe Mode.

When will Pearl Abyss fix the 1.13 PS5 performance?

No official date, but the regression is widely reported and the studio ships patches fast (1.10 through 1.13 landed within months). Reporting it via the in-game tool, support tickets, and community threads raises the odds that 1.14 includes a perf pass. Until then, the settings here keep the game fully playable.

Motion blur off — does it really help FPS on PS5?

It helps more after 1.13 than before, because the patch increased camera-move cost for post-processing. Turning it off is a medium-impact gain and costs nothing visually in normal play. Combined with Performance Mode and lower crowd density, it is part of our "Stable 60" preset.

My PS5 Pro is fine but my friend's base PS5 struggles — why?

The PS5 Pro has roughly 1.6x the GPU and better reconstruction, so it absorbs 1.13's extra load without obvious drops. A base PS5 has no such buffer, so the same patch pushes it past its comfort zone in hubs and fights. Base PS5 players should follow Performance Mode + low crowd density most aggressively.

Should I reinstall the game to fix the lag?

Rarely necessary, and it costs a long download. Start with the cache/database rebuild (Section 5) and the settings changes (Section 3) — those fix the vast majority of 1.13 regressions. Only reinstall if you see corrupted textures or repeated crashes afterward, which would point to a bad install rather than the patch.

Will the settings that fix 1.13 hurt visuals too much?

Minimally. Performance Mode trades some resolution sharpness (partly recovered by VRR and reconstruction), motion blur off is barely noticeable, and lower crowd density mainly affects busy marketplaces. None of it changes gameplay, story, or combat feel — players just notice the smoother frame rate.
