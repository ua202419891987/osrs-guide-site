Lost After the Tutorial? Your First Hour in Pywel (No Spoilers, Just Direction)

⚔️ Early Game Guide

Finished the opening and now staring at an open world with zero idea what to do? Here's exactly where to point Kliff next — without ruining any story beats.

🧭 1. You're Not Lost — The Game Opened Up on Purpose

If you just finished the opening, you likely thought: "I beat the tutorial and now I'm lost," or "the game just dropped me in the world and I have no clue what to do first." Take a breath — that disorientation is not a bug, and no quest is missed. Crimson Desert hands you the reins once the prologue ends, and Pywel unfolds with little signposting.

Where many RPGs keep a glowing path for ten hours, Crimson Desert drops Kliff into a lived-in, politically messy region and trusts you to find your next move. That silence can feel like a softlock; it isn't. The systems you need are a short walk away — head toward the nearest stone walls.

Think of it this way: the tutorial taught you how to fight and move; hour one teaches you where you belong. Everything orbits your company's home base. Learn "the fortress is my anchor, the contract board is my to-do list, the training yard is my lab, and the blacksmith is my workshop," and the open world stops feeling threatening.

Pro Tip: The first thing you should do after gaining control is open your map (see the FAQ for the key). Even if most of it is fogged out, Uzkigrad fortress is always visible. Walk that way and the rest of this guide becomes a walking tour.

The good news is that hour one is low-stakes by design. You aren't expected to clear a region, beat a boss, or lock in a build yet. Just orient yourself, meet the people who matter for the next forty hours, and pick up the core loop: contract, train, upgrade, explore, advance. Do those five things and you played hour one perfectly.

🏰 2. Step 1: Reach Uzkigrad Fortress & Meet Your Company (The Hub)

Uzkigrad fortress is the beating heart of your early game and almost always the nearest major landmark after the tutorial. Head there on foot — the walk teaches you the starting biome's layout and reveals a few collectibles. As you approach, you will see weathered stone walls, a gate, and your company's scattered camp.

Inside the fortress you meet the core cast: the quartermaster (storage), blacksmith (upgrades and repairs), weapon merchant (training yard), and contract officer (jobs). None are decoration — each is a system gateway. Talk to all once; most hand you a starter reward and reveal their function.

1

Walk to Uzkigrad and clear your intro dialogue

Approach the gate, watch the short arrival beat, and speak with each company member flagged with a marker. Your storage chest, contract board, training yard, and blacksmith all become accessible here. Goal: Confirm you can open the map, see the fortress as a fast-travel point, and locate every key NPC.

The fortress also unlocks your company storage, which matters more than it looks. Early on you loot more than you can carry, and Pywel's weight system slows Kliff when overburdened. Drop excess gear into storage to stay light and mobile — it is the difference between sprinting into a fight and waddling.

Pro Tip: Open your quest log and make sure the opening story objective is "tracked." A tracked quest paints a direction on your compass — the single best anti-lost tool in the game. If your compass shows nothing, you are not tracking a quest yet.

Spend a minute inside the walls looking around. The fortress is compact, and every building connects to a system you use constantly. Treat this like a tour of your toolbox — you should leave knowing where to go for a repair, a job, a weapon test, and a place to stash loot.

📜 3. Step 2: Hit the Contract Board — Pick "Wolves at the Gate"

The contract board is your quest giver, gold engine, and pacing tool in one wooden slab, usually in Uzkigrad's central courtyard near the contract officer. Contracts are Crimson Desert's side quests, framed as paid mercenary work — which fits Kliff perfectly. They range from monster hunts to deliveries to recon.

For your first hour, the board offers a clearly beginner-friendly hunt called "Wolves at the Gate." A small wolf pack has been harassing the fortress outskirts and someone needs them dealt with. This contract is tuned to be winnable with your starting gear — the ideal first real fight, no story boss pressure.

2

Accept "Wolves at the Gate" and track it

Read the contract text (it sets up a bit of local flavor), accept it, and track it in your quest log so the compass points you to the den. Goal: Leave the fortress with a live objective and a clear direction. Do not grab five contracts yet — one is enough to learn the loop.

Why this contract instead of random exploring? It teaches the core loop in a safe sandbox: travel to a marker, read the environment, fight a manageable group, loot, and return for a reward. That loop is most of what you will do all campaign, so learning it cleanly makes later contracts feel familiar.

Warning: Do not ignore the contract board for hours thinking the main story will carry you. Contracts are where you earn gold for upgrades and materials for repairs. A player who skips them early hits a gear wall and wonders why fights feel unfair. The board is your economy, not optional side content.

If "Wolves at the Gate" isn't on the board when you arrive, pick the lowest-threat monster hunt and track it. The principle is identical: choose the gentlest contract, learn the loop, bank the reward. Come back for spicier work once your weapon is upgraded and your style is locked in.

🗡️ 4. Step 3: Test Every Weapon at the Training Yard (Risk-Free)

Before you march out to fight those wolves, do yourself a favor: visit the training yard, usually tucked behind the barracks near the weapon merchant. It is a sandbox where you can swing every weapon type with zero consequences — no enemies, no durability loss, no story pressure.

Crimson Desert does not lock you into a class, which is liberating and also paralyzing. Your starting longsword and shield are perfectly good, but you might fall for the greatsword's cleave, the bow's reach, or the twin-blade's speed. Hour one is the cheapest time to find out, with no sunk skill points or upgraded weapon.

⚔️ Sword + Shield (The Safe Default)

Balanced speed and defense with a shield that bails you out while learning enemy patterns. If unsure, this is the answer — it trades burst for survival, exactly what hour one wants.

🏹 Bow (The Patient Pick)

Great for scouting and plinking weak enemies from range, but punishing if something closes the gap. Keep it as a secondary early rather than your main damage source.

🔨 Greatsword / Two-Handed (The High-Reward Gamble)

Enormous damage and crowd-clearing swings, but slow recovery — one mistimed heavy leaves you exposed. Fun, but not the kindest first weapon if your dodge timing is shaky.

Spend ten minutes here. Swing each weapon, feel the combo weight, and notice which makes you smile. Equip that as your main and walk out the gate. You aren't committing forever — respecs exist later — but a weapon that fits your hands beats any stat bonus.

Pro Tip: Use the yard to practice the light-light-heavy combo and one parry against a dummy. The muscle memory carries straight into "Wolves at the Gate" and spares you a bloody first swing.

🔨 5. Step 4: Visit the Blacksmith — First Upgrade & Repair Durability

The blacksmith is one of the most important NPCs in Uzkigrad, and hour one is the right time to introduce yourself. Two services matter immediately: upgrades and repairs. Both are cheap this early and have an outsized impact on how fights feel.

Your starting weapon can usually take one upgrade level right away, and a single upgrade is surprisingly noticeable — hits connect harder and enemies stagger sooner. You earn the gold from intro contracts and looting, so there is rarely a reason to skip it.

3

Upgrade your main weapon once and repair everything

Talk to the blacksmith, apply your first available upgrade to the weapon you chose at the training yard, and repair any gear showing worn durability. Goal: Leave the fortress with a +1 weapon and full-condition armor before fighting.

Repairs deserve a mention because durability is easy to forget. As you fight, gear wears down and performs worse — weaker hits, less protection. The HUD only warns when low, so it's easy to ignore until a weapon breaks mid-swing. A quick repair before every expedition prevents a mid-fight disaster.

Warning: Do not ride a single weapon into the ground hoping to upgrade later. Durability affects damage output directly, so a "free" fight that wrecks your sword isn't free once the repair bill and lost effectiveness stack up. Repair early, repair often.

While at the anvil, glance at the upgrade material requirements. Knowing what the blacksmith needs later — ores, monster parts, coin — lets you loot with purpose. Those wolf pelts from "Wolves at the Gate" feed early upgrade trees. Loot with intent and your next upgrade comes faster.

🗺️ 6. Step 5: Explore the Immediate Region (Landmarks, Hidden Cave, NPC Lore)

With a tracked contract, a tested weapon, and a fresh upgrade, you are finally ready to step outside. But "explore" here doesn't mean "wander to the map's edge." Hour one exploration means the immediate region around Uzkigrad — the farmland, treeline, wolf den, and points of interest the game gently nudges you toward.

The ring around the fortress is dense with low-threat content: a few collectible pages that build the lore, an NPC or two with dialogue that humanizes Pywel, and — in most playthroughs — a hidden cave with a minor mini-boss and a worthwhile chest. None of it is required, but all of it rewards curiosity at little risk.

4

Clear "Wolves at the Gate," then poke at one nearby landmark

Fight the wolf pack at the marked den, loot the bodies, and on your way back investigate one glowing point of interest or the cliff-face cave. Goal: Complete your first contract and discover at least one landmark for the map.

Exploration is also how the map fills in. The mini-map uses fog-of-war, so walking an area reveals it permanently. That makes hour-one wandering productive — every road and hill is cartography you'll rely on later. Treat the starting region like a sandbox for your own sense of direction.

Pro Tip: If the hidden cave's mini-boss feels too tough, leave. Bookmark it and return after a level or two — the cave is a bonus, not a gate. Forcing it in hour one is how players get discouraged.

🎯 7. Step 6: Advance the Main Story & Allocate Your First Skill Point

Once the wolves are dealt with and you have a feel for the region, nudge the main story forward. The early beats unlock systems and deliver your first meaningful currency: skill points. You'll typically earn your first by clearing the opening story step after the fortress intro, possibly a second from a related beat.

Open your skill menu (see the FAQ for the key) to see a node-based tree with branches for stamina, health, spirit, and each weapon family. The key rule for hour one: do not sit on skill points. Unspent points do nothing, so put your first into the cluster matching your weapon.

5

Push the story to its first milestone and spend your point

Follow the tracked main objective to its first checkpoint, accept the skill point reward, and immediately open the skill tree to invest it in your weapon's starting node. Goal: Reach the first story milestone and allocate at least one skill point with purpose.

Allocating thoughtfully sets the tone for your build. If you chose sword and shield, reinforce that line; if you fell for the greatsword, feed its branch. Spreading points thin across unrelated trees is the classic hour-one trap — it makes you a master of nothing.

Pro Tip: The story's first milestone also confirms your company's next move. Pay attention to the cutscene — it reframes your "lost" feeling into a clear mission. That narrative clarity is the real reward of step six.

🚫 8. What NOT to Do in Hour 1

Knowing what to skip is as valuable as knowing what to do. Hour one is a foundation, not a finale, and a few missteps can sour the opening. Here is what to avoid.

① Don't Over-Level or Over-Explore Distant Regions

Pywel's scaling is unforgiving: ride too far from Uzkigrad and you meet enemies that ignore your armor and delete your health bar. If basic foes one-shot you, retreat, finish local contracts, upgrade, and return later. Exploration beyond your level is a death spiral.

② Don't Hoard Healing Items "For Later"

Veterans often stockpile consumables and never use them. Healing isn't scarce here — you can buy and craft more. If a fight is rough and you have a draught, drink it. Dying to save a potion is false economy; use your tools.

③ Don't Skip the Training Yard

It is tempting to rush out, but ten minutes with every weapon spares you from a style that fights your instincts. The yard is free and safe — the best hour-one time investment. Skipping it makes players blame the game for a poor weapon fit.

④ Don't Neglect Stamina Habits

Even in easy wolf fights, keep a sliver of stamina in reserve. Spamming until empty leaves you unable to block or dodge, and that habit becomes a liability against real enemies. Breathe between combos; hour one is where that discipline is cheapest to learn.

Warning: Do not try to parry every attack. Some moves simply are not parryable and must be dodged. Attempting a parry on an unparryable slam leaves you wide open. Watch for the visual tell (a brief golden shimmer) and dodge when you don't see it.

If you avoid those four habits, hour one becomes a confident on-ramp rather than a stumble. You walk into hour two with a weapon you like, gear upgraded and repaired, a map partly filled, and a clear build direction.

❓ FAQ — First Hour Questions Answered

Where do I go after the tutorial ends?

Head to Uzkigrad fortress — your company's home base and the nearest landmark, always visible on your map. Walk there to unlock the contract board, training yard, blacksmith, and storage. That fortress anchors your early game.

What is the contract board and why does it matter?

A posting in Uzkigrad where the contract officer lists paid mercenary jobs — hunts, deliveries, recon. It is your quest giver, gold source, and pacing tool, and it funds upgrades and repairs. Start with "Wolves at the Gate."

Can I skip the training yard and go straight to fighting?

You can, but don't. The yard lets you test every weapon risk-free before spending skill points, so you pick a style that fits your instincts. Ten minutes there saves hours of regretting the wrong weapon.

How do I open the map, and is it fully revealed?

Press the map key (default M — see the Controls & Keybindings Guide). The map uses fog-of-war, so explore to reveal it. Uzkigrad is always visible, which is why walking toward it is your safest first move.

Where is my company, and what do they do?

Stationed at Uzkigrad. Inside you meet the quartermaster (storage), blacksmith (upgrades and repairs), weapon merchant (training yard), and contract officer (jobs). Each is a system gateway; talking to all once spares later menu confusion.

Should I explore the open world right now?

Explore the immediate region — farmland, treeline, wolf den, landmarks, the hidden cave. Don't roam to distant high-level areas; Pywel's scaling punishes you. Stay near Uzkigrad until your gear and level are stronger.

When do I get my first skill point, and where do I spend it?

Usually earned by clearing the opening story step after the fortress intro, possibly a second from a related beat. Spend it in the skill menu (default K) on the first node of your chosen weapon's branch. Unspent points do nothing.

My gear is breaking — what do I do?

Visit the Uzkigrad blacksmith and repair. Durability directly affects damage and protection, and the HUD only warns when low, so repair before every expedition. Early repairs are cheap and better than a weapon breaking mid-fight.
