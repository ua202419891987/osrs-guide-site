Crimson Desert Chinese Localization Errors: Mistranslation Cheat Sheet

🌐 Localization & Reference

If you play Crimson Desert in Simplified Chinese and keep buying the wrong dye, missing crafting materials, or wondering why two different items share the same name — you are not misreading anything. The Chinese localization has systematic naming errors, and this is the cross-reference table Pearl Abyss never shipped.

"I wasted four hours farming for a material that the Chinese UI had already renamed to something else."

📖 Localization Reference · 8 min read · Updated August 2026

⏱️ Quick Summary — 30-Second Read

  • 📌 Dye names are the worst offenders: orange and orange-red are used interchangeably, so the colour you buy is not the colour you get.
  • 📌 Recipe material names don't match inventory names — the same item appears under two different Chinese labels.
  • 📌 ATAG duplication: UI tags render the translated string twice, making tooltips unreadable in places.
  • 📌 Chinese voice acting is flat — switch voice to Korean or English with Chinese subtitles for better delivery.
  • 📌 Practical fix: play with English item names + Chinese subtitles, and use the cheat sheet below when following any guide.
  • 📌 Community patches exist but carry risk — section 7 covers what is safe and what is not.

1. Why the Chinese Build Breaks Guides

The Simplified Chinese localization was clearly produced in parallel streams: item names, recipes, UI strings and subtitles were each handled separately and never reconciled against one glossary. The result isn't "bad Chinese" — the sentences read fine. The problem is inconsistency: one English source term ends up with two or three Chinese renderings depending on which screen you're on.

That matters, because almost every guide, wiki entry and video uses English item names. When your inventory calls the item something else and the crafting window calls it a third thing, you can't follow instructions at all. New players hit this hardest, which is why this pairs with our beginner completion guide.

⚠️ Not a Bug You Can Fix in Settings

There's no toggle for "consistent item names". The only reliable workaround is knowing which terms are wrong — which is what the tables below are for.

2. Dye & Colour Name Errors

Dyes fail most visibly, because you pay for a colour and receive a different one. Orange and orange-red / vermilion are collapsed into overlapping labels, and several "crimson" variants reuse the same characters.

💡 Buy by Swatch, Not by Name

Always preview the dye on your gear before confirming the purchase. The swatch is generated from the actual colour value, so it is the one part of the UI that cannot lie to you.

3. Recipe & Material Mismatches

The second systematic failure: a recipe lists a material under one Chinese name while your inventory and the vendor use another. Players routinely conclude they're missing an ingredient that's already in their bag.

This compounds fast — duplicated stacks under different labels eat warehouse slots. Our storage and economy guide covers auditing and consolidating them before they cost you money.

⚠️ Don't Sell "Duplicates"

Two stacks with different Chinese names may be one item shown twice, or two genuinely different items. Compare icon and tooltip stats before selling either.

4. UI, Tooltips & ATAG Duplication

Beyond naming, the Chinese build has a rendering defect: inline UI tags (the ATAG markup used for keybind icons and emphasis) get processed twice, so the translated fragment appears duplicated inside the tooltip.

🧩 What ATAG Duplication Looks Like

  1. Doubled words in tooltips — the same characters repeat right after the tag, e.g. a keybind hint printed twice.
  2. Overflowing tooltip boxes — duplicated text pushes real content out of frame, hiding stat lines.
  3. Broken keybind prompts — a raw tag or placeholder shows instead of the button glyph.
  4. Truncated quest objectives — the duplicate eats the character budget and the objective gets cut off.

Workaround: English UI, Chinese subtitles

Set interface to English and subtitles to Chinese if your build allows split settings. You lose nothing narratively and every tooltip becomes readable and guide-compatible.

Workaround: widen the UI scale

A slightly larger UI scale gives the duplicated string room, stopping the overflow from hiding stat lines. It doesn't remove the duplicate but makes tooltips usable.

5. Chinese Voice Acting Problems

The Chinese dub draws consistent criticism for flat, read-aloud delivery — lines are accurate but performed without emotional range, and timing often mismatches the animation. Combat barks in particular land after the animation finishes.

💡 The Cross-Reference Trick

English voice with Chinese subtitles gives you both sides of the glossary at once. When a proper noun sounds different from the subtitle, you've just found another mistranslation.

6. The Bilingual Play Workflow

This setup removes almost all localization friction without patching anything:

🛠️ Recommended Setup

  1. Interface: English. Item names, recipes and tooltips become guide-compatible and ATAG duplication largely disappears.
  2. Subtitles: Chinese. You keep full story comprehension where it matters.
  3. Voice: Korean or English. Better performance than the Chinese dub; English doubles as a name cross-reference.
  4. Keep the cheat sheet open so you can translate terms from Chinese-language videos instantly.
  5. Verify by icon. Icons and stat lines are language-independent — treat them as the source of truth.

Any patch can reset language settings to system default. If your interface flips back to Chinese after an update, that's the same config-reset behaviour documented in the 1.15 patch issues guide — re-apply settings before assuming something broke.

7. Community Translation Patches

Player-made corrective translation packs circulate for most Pearl Abyss titles. They typically ship a corrected string table fixing dye and material naming. Weigh this honestly before installing one.

⚠️ Risk Checklist

Modifying game files can trip integrity checks and may put your account at risk on online-connected features. Text-only string replacements are lowest-risk, but "low risk" isn't "no risk" — no fan patch is officially supported.

① Prefer string-table-only packs

Avoid anything replacing textures, font binaries or executables. A legitimate translation fix only touches localization data.

② Back up the original files

Copy the untouched localization files before overwriting so you have a clean baseline to restore.

③ Expect to reinstall after every patch

Updates overwrite localization data. If broken names return, your patch was replaced — nothing is corrupted.

④ Report the errors upstream

Send the official feedback channel a screenshot and the exact string. Naming inconsistencies are cheap to fix once documented.

8. FAQs

Are these errors going to be fixed officially?

Localization glossary passes usually land in larger content patches rather than hotfixes. There is no announced date, so plan around the workaround rather than waiting.

If I switch the interface to English, do I lose my Chinese save?

No. Language is a display setting only. Your save, progress and settings are untouched by the switch, and you can flip back at any time.

Why do two items have exactly the same Chinese name?

Because two distinct English source terms were mapped to one Chinese string. Distinguish them by icon, rarity colour and the stat block — never by name alone.

Does the ATAG duplication affect gameplay or just looks?

Indirectly, yes — truncated quest objectives and hidden stat lines mean you decide with missing information. That's why the English-UI workaround is worth doing.

Can I use a fan patch on console?

No. Console builds don't allow file replacement. Use the English-interface workaround, which works on every platform.
