OSRS Budget Gear 2026 — Best Value Gear for Every Budget Tier

You do not need a billion GP to enjoy Old School RuneScape. Some of the most satisfying kills in the game — Vorkath, Zulrah, the God Wars Dungeon, and entry-level raids — are cleared comfortably on a tight budget. The trick is not buying the most expensive gear; it is buying the right gear at the right price.

1. 💡 Why Budget Gear Is a Skill, Not a Shortcut

New and returning players often assume that "budget" means "weak." That is wrong. OSRS combat math rewards a few specific slots far more than others, and most of those high-impact slots have cheap or free best-in-class options. A player in a 30M GP setup who understands how gear stats work will out-DPS a player in 300M GP of random gear every single time. Budget gearing is not a compromise you suffer through until you are rich — it is the core discipline that separates efficient players from ones who bleed gold.

The single most important concept is DPS per GP. Imagine two upgrades: a weapon that costs 5M and raises your DPS by 20%, versus a cape that costs 50M and raises your DPS by 2%. The weapon is ten times more efficient. Budget gearing is simply the discipline of always buying the high-efficiency item and skipping the low-efficiency one until you genuinely have cash to burn. This mindset is what lets a mid-game account punch far above its bank value.

Another reason budget gear matters: you die in OSRS, and death in the Wilderness or a risky PvM trip means losing your kit. A 30M setup stings far less than a 1B setup when a PKer catches you. Learning the game in budget gear also teaches you mechanics instead of letting expensive gear carry you — a habit that pays off massively at endgame, where every mistake is punished regardless of what you are wearing. If you want a broader PvM foundation before you start spending, read our PvM beginner guide to understand the fights your gear is meant to clear.

💡 The 80/20 Rule of OSRS Gear

Roughly 80% of your practical DPS comes from about 20% of your gear budget: your weapon, your amulet, and a few free unlocks. Spend there first, and the rest of your slots can stay cheap for a very long time. Once the weapon and neck are handled, armor is mostly defense and style bonuses that matter far less than players think.

2. 🎁 The Free Gear You Must Get First (0 GP)

Before you spend a single coin, go earn these items. Every one of them is free, and together they replace hundreds of millions of GP in "starter" purchases. Skipping them is the most common budget mistake, and it is the reason so many players feel poor even with a 100M bank. Think of this list as your Tier 0 foundation — it does not change no matter how rich you get.

If you are below 70 Prayer, train it — the passive DPS from Piety alone is worth more than most paid gear, and Rigour/Augury turn ranged and magic from "okay" into "elite." 📋 Ranged budget rule

Get the Blowpipe and Anguish before you buy any Armadyl armor. The weapon and neck give far more DPS than the body and legs. Blessed D'hide plus Barrows Gloves is a perfectly fine placeholder while you save for the Armadyl set.

If you are tight on GP and want to do the most content fastest, level Magic and run the Trident + Ahrim's + Occult kit. It is the cheapest of the three styles to take from zero to "clears real bosses," and it stays relevant far longer than most players expect.

6. 📈 Step-by-Step Budget Upgrade Path (0 to 200M GP)

This is the heart of the guide: a single, ordered path you can follow from a fresh account to a 200M bank. Each step is numbered and ranked by DPS-per-GP, so you always know the next best purchase. Do not skip steps, and do not jump ahead to "cool" gear — the order is what makes it efficient.

① Earn the free Tier 0 kit (0 GP)

Complete the quests and minigames in Chapter 2. Cost: zero. Impact: equivalent to a ~150M bank. Until this is done, buying anything is a mistake because the free items already occupy your highest-value slots.

② Buy your first real weapon (~5M–14M)

Pick one style and buy its budget weapon: Toxic Blowpipe (~5M) for ranged, Trident of the Swamp (~4M) for magic, or Abyssal Whip (~1.5M) / Osmumten's Fang (~14M) for melee. This is the single biggest DPS jump available to a new account and it unlocks real PvM money.

③ Get the matching zenyte amulet (~0.6M–13M)

Occult Necklace (~600K) for magic, Amulet of Fury then Torture (~1.5M then ~10M) for melee, Necklace of Anguish (~13M) for ranged. The neck is your second-highest DPS slot after the weapon, so it comes before any armor.

④ Buy Rigour + Augury scrolls (~2.6M total)

One-time purchases of roughly 1.3M each. They boost every ranged and magic hit you ever make by ~25%. Nothing else in this entire path comes close to this efficiency, and they benefit you for the life of the account. Piety you should already have free from quests.

⑤ Boot trio: Primordial + Pegasian + Eternal (~42M total)

One-time upgrades for all three styles. You will never replace them. Buy them as a bundle once you have steady PvM income, because they quietly boost every future setup you build.

⑥ Bandos Chestplate + Tassets (~48M)

Your first "real" melee armor. Buy Tassets first (the +2 Strength upgrade over the free Torso); the Chestplate only matches the Torso's +4 Strength and mainly adds defense. Wait on this until after steps 2–5.

⑦ Ancestral Hat + Bottom (~150M) and Sanguinesti (~70M)

Your first real magic armor and lifesteal weapon, bought only when you are doing CoX mage regularly or want longer trips. These sit at the top of the budget ladder and mark the transition into genuine endgame gear.

Notice what is not on the list until the very end: Masori, Torva, and the mega-rare weapons (Twisted Bow, Tumeken's Shadow). Those belong in the 200M+ tier covered in our best-in-slot guide. Buying them early is the classic way to waste a mid-game bank. 7. 💰 Money-Making-to-Gear Routes & Common Mistakes

Gear is only as good as the GP that bought it. The most efficient budget players do not "save up" passively — they run specific money makers that fund specific upgrades. Below are three routes that map directly onto the upgrade path above.

RouteTarget GearEst. GP/HrRequirements
Vorkath (Blowpipe setup)Fang, Anguish, Torso~2–3MFree kit + Blowpipe
Zulrah (mixed setup)Occult, Trident, Ahrim's~2–4MFree kit + a few switches
God Wars DungeonBandos Tassets, Armadyl~1.5–3MFang or Blowpipe
Slayer (high-level tasks)All budget kits~1–2M85+ Slayer, free kit

The fastest loop is simple: earn the free kit, buy one weapon, then immediately use that weapon at Vorkath or Zulrah to fund the next upgrade. Our money-making beginner guide lists the exact methods and requirements for each route, and our what-to-buy-first guide helps you prioritize the first million. If you want a tighter plan for your first big purchases, that guide pairs perfectly with the numbered path in Chapter 6.

⑧ Common budget mistakes to avoid

  • Buying Bandos Chestplate before Tassets. The chestplate has the same +4 Strength as your free Fighter Torso. Tassets (+2) are the real upgrade. Buy Tassets first.
  • Buying Primordial Boots before Torture/Anguish. Boots give a tiny Strength bonus; zenyte amulets give far more DPS for less money.
  • Skipping the free unlocks. A player in full Bandos but still using a Dragon Scimitar and Mystic boots is leaving more DPS on the table than the Bandos added.
  • Chasing armor over weapons. A Fang with monk robes out-DPSes a Dragon Scimitar with Torva. Weapons first, always.
  • Spreading GP across all three styles. Max one style at a time. Melee first (most content), then ranged, then magic.
  • Buying luxury armor "because it looks done." Masori and Torva feel like progress but add little until your weapon and neck are already best-in-slot.
⚠️ Don't Finance Gear with Bonds You Can't Afford to Lose

If you are grinding toward a big purchase, keep your gold in the bank, not on your character in risky content. Die once in the Wilderness with a Fang and you are back to square one. Use the money routes above to grow the bank steadily instead of risking it.

8. ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Can I really do bossing on a budget setup?

Yes. Vorkath, Zulrah, GWD bosses, and entry-level CoX/ToA are all clearable with the sub-50M setups above. The difference versus best-in-slot is usually a few percent DPS and a longer trip — not a hard wall. Move up only when the content demands it, and not before.

What is the single best budget upgrade under 20M?

Osmumten's Fang (~14M) for melee, the Blowpipe (~5M) for ranged, and the Trident of the Swamp (~4M) for magic. Each is a massive power spike over starter weapons and stays useful for hundreds of hours. Pair any of them with the free Fighter Torso, Fire Cape, and the right zenyte neck.

Is the Fighter Torso really better than buying Bandos?

For Strength bonus they are equal (+4). The Torso is free; Bandos costs ~27M and adds defense, not Strength. Until you care about defense (tank roles, the Inferno, or learning raids), the Torso is strictly better value. Get Tassets later for the +2 Strength upgrade over the Torso.

Should I buy Ancestral or save for Tumeken's Shadow?

Save for the Shadow if you mage seriously. Ancestral adds about +2% magic damage per piece; the Shadow multiplies your total magic damage bonus from gear by a far larger factor. Budget mage players should stay on Ahrim's until they can afford the Shadow, then buy Ancestral only as a comfort upgrade.

Do I need Rigour and Augury if I mostly melee?

Not for melee — Piety (free from quests) covers melee. But Rigour and Augury cost only about 1.3M each and permanently boost your ranged and magic. Since you will eventually do ranged and magic content (Zulrah, CoX, Slayer), buy them early; they are the most efficient GP you will ever spend.

What should I buy first with my first 5M GP?

A weapon for your chosen style. With ~5M you can buy a Toxic Blowpipe (ranged) or a Trident of the Swamp (magic) outright, or save a little more for Osmumten's Fang (melee). Do not spend it on armor or a cape — your free unlocks already cover those slots better than anything 5M buys.

Is Blessed D'hide fine instead of Armadyl?

Yes, for budget ranged. Blessed D'hide is dramatically cheaper and the DPS loss versus Armadyl is small until you also own the Blowpipe, Anguish, and Archers (i). Buy Armadyl only after those three are in your bank — that is when the armor upgrade actually matters.

How do I fund upgrades without risky Wilderness content?

Use safe PvM: Vorkath, Zulrah, God Wars Dungeon, and high-level Slayer all pay 1.5–4M GP per hour on budget kits with zero risk of PKers. Our money-making guide ranks the safest, highest-GP methods so you can grow your bank without ever stepping into the Wilderness.

When should I stop budget gearing and go best-in-slot?

When your bank passes ~200M and you are doing content where the last few percent DPS matter (high-enrage ToA, hard-mode raids, speedrunning). Until then, the budget and mid-game tiers clear everything. Our BiS guide is the natural next read once you cross that threshold.

✅ Final Tips

  • Earn the free gear first. Torso, Barrows Gloves, Fire Cape, Ava's Assembler, Imbued God Cape, and Neitiznot Faceguard replace hundreds of millions in purchases.
  • Weapons and amulets before armor. The DPS-per-GP curve falls off a cliff after your weapon and neck. Spend there.
  • Use the DPS calculator. Before any purchase, model it. If an upgrade is under ~3% DPS for its price, skip it and save.
  • One style at a time. Depth beats breadth. A great melee kit opens more content than three mediocre kits.
  • Run the money-to-gear loop. Your first weapon funds every later upgrade through safe PvM — let the game pay for your gear.
