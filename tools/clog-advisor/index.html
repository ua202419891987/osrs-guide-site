How the Collection Log Advisor works

The Old School RuneScape collection log has thousands of slots spread across hundreds of pages, and almost every existing tool treats it as a static checklist. A checklist tells you what is missing. It never tells you what to do about it. This advisor is built to answer the harder question that actually costs players time: given your current combat and Slayer levels, the gear you can realistically bring, and the number of hours you genuinely play each day, which collection log page is the best use of your next session?

Every recommendation comes out of the same three-step calculation. First, each remaining item is treated as an independent drop with probability p = 1/rate per kill, so the chance of having it after k kills is 1 − (1−p)^k. Second, the tool computes the median kill count for a single item as ln(0.5) / ln(1−p) — the point where half of all players have it and half are still dry. Median is used instead of "expected" kills on purpose: the mean is dragged upwards by the unlucky tail, which is why the wiki's 1-in-512 feels so different from what people actually experience. Third, for a whole page it multiplies the per-item probabilities together and solves numerically for the kill count where the chance of having everything crosses 50%, and again where it crosses 90%.

Ranking is then done on expected new log slots per hour, calculated as the sum of 1 − (1−p)^kph across every item you still need, where kph is your kills per hour after being scaled down for your gear tier. This is deliberately not a naive sum of drop rates: getting the same item twice in an hour still only lights up one slot, and the formula accounts for that. If you switch the preference dropdown, the same underlying numbers get re-weighted for GP efficiency, rare-drop chasing, or Ironman utility instead.

Level requirements are enforced rather than ignored. A source you cannot enter yet is either hidden or flagged with exactly how many Combat or Slayer levels you are short, because a recommendation you cannot act on is worse than no recommendation. Gear tier feeds directly into kills per hour, so a budget setup produces slower and more honest estimates than a maxed one instead of quoting you speedrunner rates.

Finally, a note on data integrity. Every drop rate built into this tool is one we are confident about. Sources where the per-item weighting could not be verified — Theatre of Blood, Tombs of Amascut, Barrows, Wintertodt, Tempoross, Zalcano and The Nightmare among them — were left out entirely rather than filled in with a plausible-looking guess. A planner that quietly invents numbers is worse than useless, because you would never know which line to distrust.

Common questions

Do I have to upload my collection log?

No. There is no account, no plugin and no upload. If you want personalised remaining-slot maths, type your owned-slot counts into the optional progress panel and they stay in your browser's local storage.

Why is the top pick sometimes a boss I already farm?

Because slot rate rewards pages with several relatively common uniques. Dagannoth Kings, for example, has seven items around 1/128 — that is far more log progress per hour than a single 1/5000 chase, even if the chase is worth more GP.

Is this against Jagex's rules?

No. It is a web page that does arithmetic. It never touches the game client, reads memory, or automates anything, which puts it in the same category as any wiki calculator.

What does "90% within X hours" mean?

It is the point where nine out of ten players in your exact situation would have finished the page. The gap between that and the median number is the honest size of the dry-streak risk you are signing up for.
